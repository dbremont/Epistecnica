# Representation Problem:  Video (Data)


> **Domain:** Video is a sequence of images or frames that, when played in rapid succession, create the illusion of motion. It is a visual medium that captures and displays moving images and often includes accompanying audio, such as speech, music, or sound effects.
> 

> Videos are a common form of multimedia content and are used for various purposes, including entertainment, education, communication, and documentation.
> 

## Video Data

Key characteristics of a video include:

1. **Frames**: A video is made up of individual frames, which are static images displayed one after the other. When played at a high frame rate, these frames create the appearance of continuous motion.
2. **Motion**: Videos capture and convey movement, whether through recorded actions, scenes, or events.
3. **Duration**: Videos can vary from concise clips to full-length movies or documentaries. The duration of a video is determined by the number of frames and the frame rate.
4. **Audio**: Many videos include synchronized audio, such as dialogue, music, or ambient sound, to enhance the viewer’s experience and provide context.
5. **Resolution**: Videos have a specific resolution, defining each frame's dimensions (e.g., 1920x1080 pixels for Full HD). Higher-resolution videos offer greater visual detail.
6. **Compression**: Videos are often compressed to reduce file size while maintaining acceptable quality. Standard video compression codecs include H.264, H.265 (HEVC), VP9, and more.
7. **Container Format**: Videos are stored in formats like MP4, AVI, MKV, and MOV. These formats can contain video and audio streams, subtitles, and metadata.
8. **Playback**: Videos can be played on various devices, including computers, smartphones, tablets, televisions, and dedicated media players. Video players and software applications open and play video files.
9. **Editing**: Video editing software allows users to manipulate and enhance videos by cutting, trimming, adding effects, and combining clips.

## Representation Systems

> **Video Representations**: Video data is represented using codecs like H.264, H.265 (HEVC), VP9, etc., encode frames and audio together.
> 

> **Video representations** refer to how video content can be encoded, compressed, and stored.
> 

These representations are used in various video formats and codecs. Here is a list of common video representations:

- **Raw Video**: Uncompressed video data that retains the original quality but requires significant storage space and bandwidth.
- **YUV/YCbCr**: A color representation that separates brightness (luma, Y) from chrominance (chroma, U, and V), which allows for efficient compression.
- **RGB**: A color representation of each pixel by its red, green, and blue values.
- **H.264 (AVC)** is a widely used video compression standard that uses various techniques to achieve high compression while maintaining good quality.
- **H.265 (HEVC)** is a successor to H.264 that offers better compression efficiency and improved video quality.
- **VP9**: An open-source video codec developed by Google, known for its high compression efficiency.
- **AV1** is an open-source video codec developed by the Alliance for Open Media (AOM), designed for efficient compression and improved quality.
- **MPEG-2**: A video compression standard commonly used in DVDs and older broadcast television.
- **MPEG-4**: A video compression standard that includes interactive multimedia and streaming features.
- **VC-1**: A video compression standard developed by Microsoft and used in Windows Media Video (WMV) files.
- **Theora**: An open-source video codec based on the VP3 codec.
- **Dirac**: An open and royalty-free video compression format developed by the BBC.
- **ProRes**: A high-quality video codec developed by Apple, commonly used in professional video production.
- **DNxHD/DNxHR**: A codec family developed by Avid for high-quality video editing.
- **XDCAM**: A series of professional video codecs developed by Sony for various applications.
- **CineForm**: A codec known for its use in professional video production and editing.
- **Uncompressed 4:4:4**: A high-quality, uncompressed video representation used in professional video editing.
- **Motion JPEG**: A video compression format that treats each frame as a separate JPEG image.
- **AVC-Intra**: An intra-frame video codec used in professional video production.
- **WebM**: A container format that uses VP8 or VP9 video codecs and Vorbis or Opus audio codecs for web video.
- **AVC Ultra**: A video codec standard used in Panasonic’s professional camcorders.
- **XAVC**: A video codec developed by Sony for high-definition and 4K video recording.
- **AVC Long GOP**: A variant of H.264 designed for efficient compression with some loss of quality.
- **H.263**: A video compression standard used in video conferencing and early web video streaming.
- **WMV (Windows Media Video)**: A family of video codecs developed by Microsoft.
