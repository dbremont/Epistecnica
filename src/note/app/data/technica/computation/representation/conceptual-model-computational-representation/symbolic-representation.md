# Symbolic Representation


> Computing using symbols.
> 

> **Symbolic Computation** is concerned with algorithmic manipulations of **symbolic objects**. These can be objects in formal language, such as formulas or programs; algebraic objects, such as polynomials or residue classes; or geometric objects, such as curves or surfaces. Research in symbolic computation combines advanced mathematics with advanced computer science for computing and the development of algorithms. It is applied in various fields of science and engineering, such as the analysis of finite element methods, chemical reaction networks, wireless communication systems, statistical physics, robotics, and geometric modeling.
> 

> Underlying every programming language is a model of a computing system that is program control. Some models are pure abstractions; some are represented by hardware, and others by compiling or interpreting programs.
> 

> **Symbolic Representations**: In symbolic computing, expressions, equations, and variables are represented symbolically, allowing manipulation and analysis of mathematical and logical expressions.
> 

> **Symbolic representations** **do not equate** to **semantics**; while symbols may convey information, the meaning and interpretation of that information rely on the context, rules, and understanding inherent in the system or language being used.
>

## On the Benefits of Symbolic Representations

**What is the best way to represent a concept?**

Different representations can facilitate different computations; for example, a vector representation of words can give you similar, analogous, and antonymous computations for (almost) free, etc.

**Symbolic representations** offer several benefits over other representations, such as numerical or graphical representations. Some of the main benefits of symbolic representations include:

- **Flexibility**: Symbolic representations can represent a wide range of concepts and relationships, making them highly flexible and adaptable to different problems.
- **Precision**: Symbolic representations can accurately represent concepts and relationships, allowing for accurate analysis and manipulation.
- **Abstraction**: Symbolic representations can abstract away details irrelevant to the problem at hand, allowing for higher-level reasoning and analysis.
- **Generalization**: Symbolic representations can generalize across different instances of a problem, allowing for insights and solutions to be applied across various situations.
- **Machine Reasoning**: Machines can process and manipulate Symbolic representations, allowing for automated reasoning and analysis.
- **Expressiveness**: Symbolic representations can express complex concepts and relationships concisely and expressively.

Symbolic representations are a powerful tool for representing and reasoning about abstract concepts and relationships, making them useful in various fields, from mathematics and computer science to natural language processing and engineering.

## Representation with Symbolic Systems

> In symbolic systems, entities and concepts are represented using symbols, such as letters, numbers, or other abstract objects. These symbols are manipulated using formal rules and operations.
> 

Symbolic systems can model a wide range of things, including but not limited to:

- **Mathematics**: Symbolic systems are particularly well-suited for modeling mathematical concepts, such as equations, functions, and geometric shapes. They can perform operations such as simplification, differentiation, and integration.
- **Logic**: Symbolic systems can model logical concepts, such as propositional logic, predicate logic, and modal logic. They can be used for tasks such as deductive reasoning, automated theorem proving, and logic programming.
- **Natural language**: Symbolic systems can model the structure and meaning of natural language, such as sentences and phrases. They can be used for tasks such as text parsing, semantic analysis, and machine translation.
- **Computer systems**: Symbolic systems can model the behavior of computer systems, such as hardware designs and software programs. They can be used for formal verification, model checking, and testing.
- **Physical systems**: Symbolic systems, such as mechanical and electrical circuits, can model physical systems. They can be used for simulation, control, and optimization tasks.

Symbolic systems can model various things, and their flexibility and power make them useful in many fields, including mathematics, computer science, engineering, and linguistics.

## List of Symbolic Representations

**Symbolic representation** refers to using symbols or abstract representations to represent real-world objects, concepts, or ideas. Here are some examples:

1. **Numerals**: In mathematics, digits like 0, 1, 2, 3, etc., represent numbers (e.g., 1234).
2. **Letters**: Using alphabets like A, B, C, etc., to represent sounds and form words in language.
3. **Musical Notation**: Using symbols like notes, rests, and clefs to represent musical sounds and rhythm.
4. **Chemical Formulas**: Chemical symbols like H, O, C, etc., represent elements and compounds (e.g., H2O for water).
5. **Logic Symbols**: Using symbols like ∧, ∨, ¬, etc., to represent logical operations (AND, OR, NOT) in logic and computer science.
6. **Mathematical Symbols**: Using symbols like +, -, ×, ÷, etc., to represent mathematical operations.
7. **Cartographic Symbols**: Using icons or symbols to represent various geographical features on maps (e.g., a small house symbol for a residential area).
8. **Traffic Signs**: Using symbols and colors to represent different instructions and warnings for drivers on the road.
9. **Astrological Symbols**: Using symbols to represent planets, zodiac signs, and other celestial objects in astrology.
10. **Gestures in Sign Language**: Using specific hand movements and positions to represent words and concepts in sign language.
