# Quantum Framework

   : Electromagnetic Framework (Electromagnetic%20Framework%2022dc0f5171ec806dba2fd78026e678a2.md), Relativistic Quantum Framework (Relativistic%20Quantum%20Framework%2022dc0f5171ec80238434e10b4e503fc7.md)

> **Quantum mechanics** is a theoretical framework and set of principles used to model and predict physical behavior at microscopic scales.
> 

> A **quantum mechanical system** is a physical system whose behavior is governed by the principles of quantum mechanics, characterized by wavefunctions, quantized observables, and probabilistic outcomes resulting from the superposition and entanglement of quantum states.
> 

## Learning Guide

| **Level** | **Target Understanding** | **Time Estimate** | **Prerequisites** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 🟢 **Conceptual Intro** | Basic ideas (superposition, uncertainty, entanglement, etc.) without much math | **1–2 months** | High school algebra & interest |
| 🟡 **University-Level (Undergrad Physics Major)** | Full mathematical treatment (Hilbert spaces, operators, Schrödinger eq.) | **6–12 months** | Calculus, linear algebra, differential equations, classical mechanics |
| 🔵 **Graduate-Level (Advanced QM)** | Relativistic QM, spin systems, perturbation theory, path integrals, scattering, QFT intro | **1–2 years** | Undergrad QM + complex analysis, group theory, statistical mechanics |

## Guiding Problems

- How states of a system are represented
- How observables are measured
- How systems evolve in time
- How measurement affects systems

## 🔧 What does this *framework* include?

- **State representation** (wavefunctions, Hilbert space)
- **Observables** as operators (momentum, energy, etc.)
- **Measurement postulates**
- **Schrödinger equation** (time evolution)
- **Superposition, entanglement, uncertainty**
- **Probabilistic predictions** (Born rule)
- **Quantization** means that certain physical quantities (like energy, angular momentum, or charge) can only take **discrete values**, not any value on a continuous spectrum.

## 🧪 **History**

> What were the first systems and phenomena that escaped classical explanation?
> 

### **Blackbody Radiation**

- **Problem**: Classical physics predicted the **ultraviolet catastrophe** — that energy would diverge to infinity at short wavelengths.
- **Resolution**: Max Planck (1900) introduced **quantized energy levels** (E = nhν), laying the groundwork for quantum theory.

### **Photoelectric Effect**

- **Problem**: Light below a certain frequency, regardless of intensity, could not eject electrons from metal surfaces — classical wave theory couldn't explain this.
- **Resolution**: Albert Einstein (1905) proposed that light comes in **quanta (photons)** with energy $E = h\nu$, earning the Nobel Prize.

### **Atomic Spectra**

- **Problem**: Atoms emitted **discrete lines**, not continuous spectra — classical electrodynamics couldn’t explain stable orbits or energy levels.
- **Resolution**: Niels Bohr (1913) introduced **quantized orbits** for electrons in the hydrogen atom.

### **Electron Diffraction**

- **Problem**: Electrons, considered particles, were observed to **diffract like waves** when passed through crystals or slits.
- **Resolution**: Confirmed **wave–particle duality** (de Broglie, 1924; Davisson–Germer experiment, 1927).

### **Heat Capacity of Solids**

- **Problem**: Classical models (like the Dulong–Petit law) failed to predict the low-temperature behavior of solids.
- **Resolution**: Quantum models (Einstein, Debye) explained how **vibrational modes are quantized**.

## Formalism

| **Formalism** | **Core Concepts** | **Mathematical Tools** | **Strengths / Use Cases** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Schrödinger (Wave Mechanics)** | Wavefunction $\psi(x,t)$ evolves in time | Schrödinger equation, differential equations, Hilbert space | Intuitive; good for bound states, atoms, simple quantum systems |
| **Heisenberg (Matrix Mechanics)** | Observables evolve, states fixed | Operators, commutation relations, matrices | Natural in quantum optics, scattering theory; time evolution of observables |
| **Dirac (Abstract Hilbert Space)** | States and observables in abstract vector space | Bras/kets, linear algebra, spectral theory | Unifies Schrödinger and Heisenberg; used in general formulations |
| **Feynman (Path Integral)** | Sum over all possible paths between initial and final state | Functional integrals, action principles | Natural for quantum field theory, statistical physics, and systems with symmetries |
| **Density Matrix Formalism** | Describes mixed states (not just pure ones) | Operators, trace, statistical mechanics | Essential for open systems, decoherence, quantum information |
| **Wigner–Weyl (Phase Space)** | Quasi-probability distributions in phase space | Wigner functions, Moyal brackets | Bridges quantum and classical mechanics; used in quantum optics and semiclassical methods |
| **Quantum Logic / Algebraic Formalism** | Logical structure of quantum propositions | C*-algebras, von Neumann algebras, lattice theory | Foundations, quantum information, general systems (esp. in QFT) |
