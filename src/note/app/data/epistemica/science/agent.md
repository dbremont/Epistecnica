## Replicator Dynamics

**Replicator dynamics** is a mathematical framework used in evolutionary game theory to model the evolution of populations of agents who interact with each other in repeated games. The framework assumes that agents have different strategies or behaviors and that these strategies have different payoffs or benefits in the game.

In **replicator dynamics**, the relative frequency of each strategy in the population changes over time as agents interact and receive payoffs. Successful strategies in the game tend to increase in frequency, while less successful strategies tend to decrease in frequency. The system's dynamics can be described using a set of differential equations that capture each strategy's frequency change over time.

The concept of **replicator dynamics** has been used to study a wide range of social and biological phenomena, including the evolution of cooperation, the spread of cultural traits, and the dynamics of infectious diseases. The framework has also been used to analyze the emergence and stability of different social norms and institutions.

**Replicator dynamics** is a valuable tool for understanding the evolution of complex social systems, as it allows researchers to model the interactions and feedback between agents and their environment. By exploring the dynamics of different strategies and their outcomes, researchers can gain insights into the factors that promote or hinder the emergence of cooperative or competitive behaviors in social and economic systems.
