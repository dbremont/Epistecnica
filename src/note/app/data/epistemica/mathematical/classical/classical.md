# Classical Mathematics

> **Classical mathematics** is the mathematical tradition of classical antiquity — the Greek and Roman world from roughly the sixth century BCE to the fifth century CE — together with the elementary toolkit that the classical curriculum built on it: arithmetic, geometry, algebra, and equation solving.

> Its characteristic form is the **geometric proof**: a claim is established by construction and deduction from stated axioms, not by symbolic manipulation alone. Its central works are Euclid's *Elements*, Archimedes' treatises, Apollonius' *Conics*, and Diophantus' *Arithmetica*. Its practical side is the arithmetic, surveying, and computation that the classical world used and transmitted.

## The classical/modern boundary

> Classical mathematics is not simply "elementary mathematics", and it is not defined by the absence of abstraction: Euclid's *Elements* is an axiomatic system, and Archimedes' method of exhaustion is a rigorous limit argument. The boundary is historical and methodological. The classical tradition works by geometric construction and axiomatic proof on the objects of the Greek and Roman world; modern mathematics adds algebraic notation, the function concept, and the abstract structures that generalize the classical objects. The elementary algebra, equation, and computation notes in this scope are the symbolic after-life of the classical toolkit, not the Greek corpus itself.

> The same boundary separates the classical scope from [Abstract Algebra](note.html?n=epistemica/mathematical/structure/algebra/algebra.md): the classical scope transforms a given expression or figure; the structural scope studies the system that licenses the transformation. It also separates the classical scope from [Computer Science](note.html?n=epistemica/epistemic/discipline/computer-science.md): the classical scope executes the computation; the theory of what can be computed at all belongs to logic and computability.

## Scope note

> The scope is a *reading* of existing branches, not a new container. The equation and number branches stay in place because they also carry non-classical members — differential equations, Diophantine equations, numeral systems — and this note links them rather than duplicating them. The algebra note moved here from `structure/algebra/` because the user's request names classical algebra as part of the scope; `structure/algebra/` remains the abstract and structural branch.
