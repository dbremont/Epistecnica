# Division

> A Note on the  analysis of the **`division functional (/)`**.
> 

> **Interpretation**: A division represents a relation between two quantitative; the  relation is relative in nature; and can have many equivalent. (A:  B) → (How many A : Per 1B)
> 

> **Technique: Division** represents the process of **partitioning a quantity into equal parts,** or **determining how many times one number (the divisor) fits into another (the dividend).**
> 

> **Note:** Division is one of the most powerful tools in mathematics, as it captures  a **kind relationship  between** two numbers - shared by a class of pairs numbers also.
> 

> Division expresses the **relative magnitude** of one quantity with respect to another by normalizing one quantity by the other.
> 

## Formulation

Mathematically, given: $a \div b = c$:

- **a (dividend)**: The number being divided.
- **b (divisor)**: The number by which the dividend is divided.
- **c (quotient)**: The result of the division.
- **(remainder)**: The part of the dividend that is left over after division, satisfying $0 \le r < b$, so that  $a = b \cdot c + r$.

## Interpretations

> Canonical Interpretation: `10 / 2; 10 repartido entre dos - por cada 1; 10 repartido de a (dos);`.  `4 / 0.5` ?
> 

- **Quotient as a Count (Measurement Interpretation)**: The result tells **how many times** the divisor fits into the dividend.
- **Quotient as a Share (Partition Interpretation)**: The result represents **how much each group gets** when dividing equally.
- **Quotient as a Ratio**: Division expresses a relationship between two numbers.
- **Quotient as a Rate**: The result represents a rate of change or speed.
- **Quotient as Scaling (Multiplicative Inverse Interpretation)**:  Division by a number is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal.
- **Quotient as a Slope (Linear Interpretation)**: In graphs, division can represent a slope or rate of change.
- **Quotient as a Conversion Factor**:  A **conversion factor** is a fraction that equals **1**, where the numerator and denominator are **equivalent quantities in different units**.

## Ratio (**Razón**)

> **Ratio**: A ratio is a comparison of two quantities expressed as a fraction, showing how many times one quantity contains the other.
> 

> See more: [Ratio](note.html?n=epistemica/mathematical/ratio.md).

> Note: A ratio usually does not have dimensions - or type  -  but this is almost by convention.
> 

## Rate (Tasa)

> Etymology: From Latin `Pro rata → Rata.`
> 

> **Rate**: A rate is a measure of how one quantity changes relative to another, often expressed as a ratio per unit of time or space (e.g., speed as distance per time).
> 

> See more: [Rate (mathematics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_(mathematics)) and [Tasa (matemáticas)](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasa_(matem%C3%A1ticas)).

## Proportion (**Proporción)**

> **Proportion**: A proportion is an equation that expresses two ratios as being equal, showing the relative relationship between two sets of quantities.
> 

> See more: [Proportionality](note.html?n=epistemica/mathematical/proportionality.md).

## Connotation

> 
- **Speed** up refers specifically to an increase in performance or efficiency, typically measured in time. It's often used in computational contexts (e.g., "This optimization results in a 2x speed up").
- **Performance Improvement**:  Expresses the relative reduction in execution time.

## Case Studies

### **The Mysterious Mixture**

You have two solutions of hydrochloric acid:

- **Solution A** is 40% acid.
- **Solution B** is 60% acid.

You want to make **100 liters** of a **54% acid** solution. How many liters of each solution should you mix to achieve this?

### **The Shrinking Rectangle**

> A rectangle has length $L$ and width $W$. If the length is increased by 20% and the width is decreased by 20%, by what percentage does the area change?
> 

### **The Speeding Train**

> A train travels **300 km** in **5 hours**. If it travels at a constant speed, how long will it take to travel **420 km** at the same speed?
> 

### **The Mysterious Salary Adjustment**

> Alice and Bob work at a company. Alice earns **20% more** than Bob. After a salary adjustment, Bob’s salary increases by **25%**, and Alice’s salary decreases by **10%**. Who now earns more, and by what percentage?
> 

### The Bat and Ball Problem

> A stopper and a bottle together cost **$2.10**.
The bottle costs **$2.00** more than the stopper.
What is the cost of the stopper?
>

### **Machine Problem**

> A machine can make 5 widgets in 5 minutes.
How long would it take 100 machines to make 100 widgets?
> 

### **Painting Problem**

> If it takes 3 painters 3 hours to paint 3 rooms,
how long will it take 6 painters to paint 6 rooms?
> 

### **Rope Problem**

> A rope is cut into two pieces. One piece is three times as long as the other.
The total length is 48 meters.
How long is the shorter piece?
> 

### **Egg Problem**

> You buy a dozen eggs for $1.20.
You break 3, eat 4, and cook 5.
How many eggs do you have?
