# Maya Number System

 : Indo-Arabic Numeral (Indo-Arabic%20Numeral%20a3d9c8f22c444f89a7daf097cc1d29d3.md), Numeric System (Numeric%20System%201d6c0f5171ec80899160d6587a6a9e3e.md)

> The **Maya number system** is a sophisticated **vigesimal (base-20)**, **positional system** with only two primary symbols (dots and bars), an early concept of **zero**, and vertical representation. It was primarily used for **astronomy**, **timekeeping**, and complex mathematical calculations, and its principles significantly contributed to the Maya civilization's ability to track long cycles of time and understand the cosmos.
> 

## **Base-20 (Vigesimal System)**

- The Maya number system is **vigesimal**, meaning it is based on **20** rather than 10 (like the decimal system). Each position in a number corresponds to powers of 20.
    - The first place represents  $20^0 (1)$,
    - The second place represents $20^1 (20)$,
    - The third place represents  $20^2 (400)$, and so on.

## **Positional Notation**

- Like the modern decimal system, the Maya number system uses **positional notation**. The value of a digit depends on its position in the number.
- The Maya system was vertical, with each level representing higher powers of 20.

## **Two Main Symbols**

> Note: With these two symbols, the atomic numbers are represented. This is an example of a non-positional numeric encoding.
> 
- The Maya number system used **two symbols**:
    - A **dot (•)** represents the number **1**.
    - A **bar (—)** represents the number **5**.
- Numbers from 1 to 19 are created by combining dots and bars. For example:
    - **1 dot (•)** = 1
    - **1 bar (—)** = 5
    - **1 bar (—) and 1 dot (•)** = 6
    - **3 bars (—) and 2 dots (••)** = 17

## **Vertical Representation**

- Maya numerals were usually written **vertically**, with the bottom level representing the lower values (like units) and the higher levels representing higher powers of 20.
- This vertical format emphasized the positional aspect of the system.

## **Use of Zero**

- The Maya were one of the first civilizations to have a concept of **zero**. They used a **shell symbol** to represent zero, which was important for their calendar and mathematical computations.
- Zero was used as a placeholder, especially in the context of the **Long Count calendar** and to mark empty places in the number system.
