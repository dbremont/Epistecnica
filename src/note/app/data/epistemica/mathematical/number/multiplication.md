# Multiplication

> In this note we will explore the operation of **multiply**.

## Formulation

> Multiplication is a **mathematical binary operation** that combines two quantities (factors) to produce a third quantity (their product), according to specified mathematical rules.

> For natural numbers, multiplication can be introduced as repeated addition. More generally, multiplication is defined according to the algebraic structure in which it operates and need not be reducible to repeated addition.

## Which Concepts Allow Us to Interpret \(a \times b\)?

| Interpretation | Description | Example | Subinterpretations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ratio-Based Multiplicative Interaction (Aggregation)** | Combining quantities through a multiplicative relationship between factors to determine an aggregate quantity. | 3 groups of 4 apples produce 12 apples. | Repeated Addition; Equal Groups; Rectangular Arrays; Cartesian Combination; Spatial Composition; Proportional Dependency; Multiplicative Scaling |
| **Pure Multiplicative Structure** | Multiplication operates on mathematical elements to produce another mathematical element, without requiring an interpretation in terms of aggregation, repeated quantities, or physical proportional relationships. | \(2 \times 3 = 6\) Prime Factorization; |  Product of Algebraic Expressions; Multiplication of Matrices; Multiplication of Complex Numbers; Polynomial Multiplication; Product of Functions |

## Does a Multiplicative Structure Always Require a Certain Kind of “Ratio-Based Relationship Between the Factors” In Order to Have 'Representational Adequacy' ?

> ...

> **Ratio as a multiplicative relationship**: One quantity is specified relative to another (e.g., 3 columns per row).

> **Multiplicative Relationship**: A relationship between quantities or dimensions in which the magnitude or structure of one is conditioned by the multiplicity of another, such that their joint consideration determines a product.

> **Note:** Multiplicative interactions are grounded by ratios.

> **Note:** When representing non-natural elements, such as through factorization, ratio-based multiplicative structures are not required.

## What properties of a phenomenon, relationship, or problem make multiplication an appropriate mathematical representation?

> They must embody a particular kind of **multiplicative structure**.

| Multiplicative structure | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Cartesian-product structure** | Elements are formed by independently selecting one element from each of several sets. | 3 shirts × 4 trousers = 12 outfits. |
| **Repeated-group structure** | A quantity consists of a number of groups, each containing the same number of elements. | 4 groups × 5 objects. |
| **Scaling structure** | One quantity is proportional to another through a multiplicative factor. | Doubling the length of a rectangle. |
| **Compositional structure** | Successive transformations or factors combine multiplicatively. | Successive magnification factors. |
| **Rate structure** | A quantity is determined by multiplying a rate by an appropriate extent. | Distance = speed × time. |
| **Combinatorial structure** | The number of possible configurations is obtained by multiplying the number of choices at successive stages. | Counting paths through a decision tree. |
| **Measure-product structure** | A multidimensional measure is obtained from the product of measures along independent dimensions. | Rectangle area = length × width. |
