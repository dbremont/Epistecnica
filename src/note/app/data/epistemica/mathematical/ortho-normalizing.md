# Ortho-Normalizing


Its task is simple: ortho-normalizing vector sets.

Its applications are endless: matrix decompositions, eigenvalue problems, and numerical linear algebra…

Applications of Ortho-normalizing:

**Orthonormalizing** a set of vectors means converting the set of vectors into an orthonormal basis, which has many valuable applications in mathematics, physics, and engineering. Here are some examples:

- **Linear Algebra**: Orthonormal bases are instrumental in linear algebra, providing a convenient way to represent vectors and matrices. An orthonormal basis is a set of vectors that are all orthogonal to each other and have a length of 1. By expressing a vector as a linear combination of orthonormal basis vectors, it is possible to calculate its projection onto any given vector in the basis.
- **Signal Processing**: In signal processing, orthonormal bases often transform signals from one domain to another. For example, the Fourier and wavelet transforms use orthonormal bases to represent signals in their frequency or time-frequency content.
- **Quantum Mechanics**: In quantum mechanics, orthonormal bases represent quantum states. In this context, the orthonormal basis vectors are called wave functions, and they represent the probability amplitudes of finding a particle in a particular quantum state.
- **Computer Graphics**: Orthonormal bases are used extensively in computer graphics to calculate lighting and shading effects. By representing the orientation of surfaces in orthonormal basis vectors, it is possible to calculate the angle between the surface and a light source and determine the amount of reflected light.
- **Optimization**: Orthonormalization is also used in optimization problems to simplify the problem and improve convergence. By ortho-normalizing a set of vectors, it is possible to remove redundant information and reduce the number of variables that need to be optimized.
