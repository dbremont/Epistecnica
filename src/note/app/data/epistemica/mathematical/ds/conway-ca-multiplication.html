Conway Multiplier

live note interactive #cellular-automata #dynamical-systems #computation

In this note we compute a product the way a dynamical system does — not by an arithmetic operator but by letting a cellular automaton fire, count, and settle; the number that survives is A·B.

Formulation

What epistemic element type does this instance belong to?

Concrete Epistemic Artifact — a computed product about one operand pair — produced by applying an Epistemic Operator (a Game-of-Life machine), and usable as an Epistemic Standard (product-by-count completion criterion). Schema: Philosophia Artium Epistemicarum et Operis.

What is this epistemic instance?

The Conway Multiplier computes A·B by running Conway's Game of Life (B3/S23): A Gosper glider guns each emit exactly B gliders — a delay line of kill-order gliders terminates every gun after B firing periods — so the product appears, in unary, as A·B gliders counted live by eater detectors. Type: Computational Procedure — a dynamical realization of integer multiplication within the epistemic toolbox.

What grounds this reading?

It resolves the epistemic gap of arithmetic in a dynamical substrate — transforming an operand pair into a product through local cellular dynamics alone, with no arithmetic operator. It preserves the multiplicative relation between operands (and commutativity up to the machine's symmetry); it destroys the operands' numeric encoding (they survive only as gun and delay counts) and the machine's intermediate trajectory (glider timings and positions are discarded once counted).

Machine

  1. A Gosper glider guns fire a glider every 30 generations — each gun is a unary “1-generator”.
  2. Kill-order gliders are placed on a parallel diagonal at flight distance ⌀7.5·B⌁+7 cells. At glider speed (c/4) each arrives after ≈ 30·B generations — exactly B firing periods.
  3. Each strike destroys its gun cleanly (verified collision), so every gun fires exactly B gliders.
  4. A independent guns × B gliders each = A·B gliders in total — the product, in unary — streamed into the detectors (pale) and counted live.

Every glider you count is a real Game of Life collision. The machine was verified by exhaustive simulation: all 100 single-lane configurations (B = 0…99) and multi-lane checks up to 12 × 99 produce exact products.

Diagnostic questions

What happens if the machine's assumptions are violated?

If an operand falls outside its calibrated range (A > 12 or B > 99), the delay-line geometry no longer matches the gun period and kills miss: the count under- or over-shoots the product. If lanes are placed too close, glider streams interfere and detectors count collisions that are not product events.

What invariant does this tool rely on most critically?

The constant glider speed c/4 and the clean-kill collision. Everything reduces to the calibrated identity between delay distance, arrival time, and the gun's 30-generation period — break the period or the collision and the multiplication becomes a coincidence.

Can two different instances produce indistinguishable artifacts?

Yes. The product scalar alone is ambiguous: 3×7 and 7×3 (and any factorization of 21) yield the same surviving count, and differently wired machines that happen to emit 21 gliders are indistinguishable from the artifact. The count certifies the product, not the machine that produced it.

How robust is the reading to noise?

Not robust. A single stray cell can disturb a gun, disrupt a kill, or leak a glider into a detector, and the unary count has no error correction: one spurious transit corrupts the result. The empirical sweeps hold only because the simulation is exact and the world is closed with a kill-border.
