> A **`dynamical systems`**  is a field in mathematics that develop tools to study **`dynamical systems`**  is a mathematical model that describes th**e evolution** of a set of variables **over time**, where the **future state** depends on the **current state** and is **governed by a set of equations or rules**.
> 

> A  [Nonlinear system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_system) is a [system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System) in which the change of the output is not [proportional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportionality_(mathematics)) to the change of the input.
> 

> Using a term like **nonlinear science** is like referring to the bulk of zoology as the study of non-elephant animals.  - [Stanisław Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanis%C5%82aw_Ulam).
> 

## Formulation

> Note: Basic Definition for a Continuous System.
> 

A **dynamical system** is often defined by a set of **differential equations**, such as: $\frac{dx}{dt} = f(x, t)$

Where $x(t)$ is the state of the system at the time $t$.

- The **initial condition** (e.g.,  $x(0) = x_0$) specifies where in the state space the system starts.
- The solution $x(t)$ tells us **how the system evolves** from that initial state.

## List of Frameworks to Study Dynamical Systems

Here’s a table summarizing various dynamical systems frameworks and their key characteristics:

| **Framework** | **Description** | **Key Applications** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Linear Dynamical Systems** | Systems described by linear differential or difference equations, where the output is a linear function of the input. | Control theory, signal processing, economic modeling |
| **Nonlinear Dynamical Systems** | Systems where the relationship between variables is nonlinear, often leading to complex behavior like chaos. | Climate modeling, population dynamics, engineering systems |
| **Discrete Dynamical Systems** | Systems where the state evolves in discrete time steps, often described by difference equations. | Computer science, population models, economic cycles |
| **Continuous Dynamical Systems** | Systems described by continuous time evolution, typically modeled by differential equations. | Physics, biology, engineering |
| **Stochastic Dynamical Systems** | Systems that incorporate randomness, where future states depend on both deterministic rules and probabilistic components. | Financial modeling, genetic algorithms, queuing theory |
| **Deterministic Dynamical Systems** | Systems where future states are determined entirely by the current state, with no randomness involved. | Classical mechanics, celestial mechanics, electrical circuits |
| **Hamiltonian Systems** | A special class of dynamical systems governed by Hamilton's equations, conserving energy over time. | Quantum mechanics, classical mechanics, celestial dynamics |
| **Lagrangian Systems** | Systems described by the Lagrangian function, focusing on energy dynamics and used in deriving equations of motion. | Mechanics, theoretical physics, robotics |
| **Cellular Automata** | Discrete models where space and time are divided into cells that evolve according to simple rules, leading to complex behavior. | Computational biology, physics, theoretical computer science |
| **Ergodic Theory** | Studies statistical properties of dynamical systems, particularly those that are invariant over time under certain conditions. | Statistical mechanics, thermodynamics, probability theory |
| **Chaos Theory** | Focuses on the behavior of dynamical systems that are highly sensitive to initial conditions, leading to seemingly random states. | Meteorology, ecology, financial markets |
| **Control Systems** | Dynamical systems designed to maintain desired outputs despite changes in inputs, often using feedback mechanisms. | Engineering, robotics, automated systems |
| **Bifurcation Theory** | Analyzes changes in the qualitative or topological structure of a dynamical system as a parameter is varied. | Nonlinear dynamics, climate modeling, engineering |
| **Complex Systems** | Studies systems with many interconnected parts that exhibit emergent behavior not predictable from the properties of individual parts. | Social sciences, biology, economics |
| **Network Dynamics** | Focuses on the behavior of dynamical systems represented by networks or graphs, analyzing how connections influence the system. | Epidemiology, social networks, neuroscience |
| **Subshift of Finite Type (SFT)** | A subshift is a model in dynamical systems where a sequence of symbols is generated by applying a set of rules to neighboring symbols, and it satisfies certain constraints on admissible patterns or configurations. |  |
| **Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs)** | Equations involving functions and their derivatives to model the change in a system over time. | Population dynamics, mechanical systems, electrical circuits |
| **Partial Differential Equations (PDEs)** | Equations involving partial derivatives of functions with multiple variables, often used for spatially distributed systems. | Heat distribution, fluid dynamics, wave propagation |
| **Difference Equations** | Equations defining a sequence recursively by relating each term to its predecessors, used for discrete systems. | Economic models, population models, digital signal processing |
| **State Space Models** | Mathematical models that represent a physical system as a set of input, output, and state variables related by first-order differential equations. | Control systems, signal processing, econometrics |
| **Chaos Theory** | … | … |
