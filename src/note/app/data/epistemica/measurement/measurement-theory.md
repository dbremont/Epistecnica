# Measurement Theory


> **Measurement** is the process of assigning numbers or symbols to attributes of objects or phenomena **in such a way that** the structure of empirical relationships among those attributes is preserved in the numerical representation.
> 

> **Measurement Theory** is a formal framework for understanding how **quantitative representations** of empirical phenomena are constructed, validated, and interpreted. It's a foundational area that intersects **philosophy of science**, **mathematics**, **psychometrics**, **physics**, and **statistics**.
> 

> **Empirical Relational System:** A set of empirical objects and their observable relations (e.g., "longer than", "heavier than").
> 

> **Numerical Relational System:** A set of numbers and their mathematical relations (e.g., ≥, +, ×).
> 

QA:

- When is it meaningful to **assign numbers** to attributes of **objects or phenomena**?
- Can an alternative procedure be used to assign numerical values to the attributes of objects or phenomena?

## Measurement

A **measurement** is a function $M: E \rightarrow N$, where:

- $E$ is an **empirical relational system** (objects and their observed relations),
- $N$ is a **numerical relational system** (numbers and their mathematical relations),
- and $M$ **preserves the structure** of relations: If $a \sim_E b$, then $M(a) \sim_N M(b)$.

## 🧠 Foundational Frameworks in Measurement Theory

- **Representational Measurement Theory**
    - Founded by Suppes, Krantz, Luce, Tversky
    - Uses **axiomatic systems** to describe when numerical representations are valid
    - Goal: Construct representation and uniqueness theorems
- Classical (Physical) Measurement Theory
    - Emphasizes empirical procedures of **concatenation** and **comparison**
    - Rooted in physics (e.g., length measurement by placing rods end-to-end)
