# de Finetti Interpretation


> De Finetti argued that **probability does not exist in the external world** as an objective property. Instead, it is a **subjective concept** that reflects an individual's degree of belief or uncertainty about an event.
> 

> **"Probability does not exist."**: This famous statement  encapsulates de Finetti's view that probability is not an objective feature of the world but a subjective tool for expressing uncertainty.
> 

> **"The only relevant thing is uncertainty—the extent of our own knowledge and ignorance."**: De Finetti emphasized that probability is about quantifying our lack of knowledge, not describing the world.
> 

## **Coherence**

> 
- De Finetti emphasized that rational beliefs must be **coherent**, meaning they must satisfy the **axioms of probability**:
    - **Non-negativity**: Probabilities must be non-negative.
    - **Normalization**: The probability of all possible outcomes must sum to 1.
    - **Additivity**: The probability of mutually exclusive events must equal the sum of their individual probabilities.
- If an individual's beliefs violate these axioms, they are **incoherent** and can be exploited through a **Dutch Book** (a set of bets that guarantees a loss).

## **Exchangeability**

> 
- De Finetti introduced the concept of **exchangeability** to bridge subjective probability and statistical inference. A sequence
of events is exchangeable if the probability of any sequence depends
only on the number of successes and failures, not on their order.
- **De Finetti's Theorem**: If a sequence of events is exchangeable, it can be represented as a
mixture of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) sequences.
This theorem connects subjective beliefs to the frequentist notion of
probability.
