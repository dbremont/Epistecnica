# Theory of Statistics

 : Statistics (Statistics%20c9d6a53f36a8494eae934a1a14f969da.md), Cramér–Rao Lower Bound (CRLB) (Cram%C3%A9r%E2%80%93Rao%20Lower%20Bound%20(CRLB)%20270c0f5171ec8038973bc8ee4b89bfec.md), Fisher Information (Fisher%20Information%204cefa509a9c94f7b912cfbdc477adbec.md)

> In this note, we explore the theory of statistics, a rigorous foundation for statistical tools and practice in general.
> 

> **Statistics provides the principles and tools** for drawing valid conclusions from data, just as logic provides the principles for valid reasoning.
> 

> Statistical tools are usually developed to solve practical problems and are validated to ensure reliability and accuracy. The theoretical validity of many statistical methods is often justified using convergence results and asymptotic probability arguments, such as the law of large numbers or the central limit theorem.
> 

## Key Ideas

| **Organizing Idea** | **Description** | **Role in Foundations** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Uncertainty and Randomness** | Data and phenomena are inherently variable; probability models quantify this. | Provides a formal framework to reason about variability in data and outcomes. |
| **Estimation and Inference** | Using data to estimate unknown parameters and make conclusions about populations. | Ensures conclusions from samples are justified and accounts for uncertainty. |
| **Sampling and Representativeness** | Understanding how samples reflect populations; concepts like bias and consistency. | Explains when and why sample statistics approximate population quantities. |
| **Modeling and Abstraction** | Creating simplified representations of complex phenomena through statistical models. | Clarifies assumptions, limitations, and consequences of modeling choices. |
| **Probability Foundations** | Rigorous definitions of probability, expectation, and variance, often via measure theory. | Ensures consistency, generality, and correctness of statistical reasoning. |
| **Decision and Loss** | Formalizing rational decisions under uncertainty, often via loss functions. | Provides principles for making optimal or informed decisions based on data. |
| **Asymptotics and Convergence** | Large-sample theory explaining why methods “work” in practice (e.g., CLT, LLN). | Offers guarantees for estimators, tests, and procedures as sample size grows. |
| **Validity and Robustness** | Conditions under which methods produce reliable results; sensitivity to assumptions. | Ensures statistical procedures remain trustworthy and interpretable. |

## Problem Space

> What kinds of questions does the foundation of statistics answer?
> 

> The foundations of statistics do not directly answer applied statistical questions. Instead, they provide the conceptual and mathematical framework to reason about **how statistical tools work, why they are valid, and under what conditions conclusions are justified**.
> 

These meta-questions include:

- What assumptions underlie a statistical method, and are they reasonable?
- How do sample-based estimates relate to population quantities?
- Why do certain procedures converge to correct answers as sample size increases?
- How can uncertainty be quantified and rigorously represented?
- What principles ensure that inferences and decisions based on data are valid?

## Toolbox

- **Analysis** – Tools for studying limits, continuity, and convergence.
- **Linear Algebra** – Methods for working with vectors, matrices, and linear transformations.
- **Probability** – Concepts for modeling uncertainty and randomness.
- **Differential Equations** – Techniques for describing dynamic systems and change.
- **Measure Theory** – Foundations for rigorously defining probabilities, integrals, and distributions.
