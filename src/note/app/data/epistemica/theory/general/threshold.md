# Threshold

## Critical Mass

> **Critical Mass** represents the threshold or point at which a system undergoes a qualitative change or significant shift in behavior due to the accumulation of a sufficient quantity of certain elements or factors.
> 

Related Concepts:

- **Tipping Point:** The critical point at which a small change or addition can lead to a significant and irreversible transformation.
- **Adoption Curve:** A graphical representation showing the rate at which the population adopts new technology or innovation, often depicting critical mass as a significant point.
- **Network Effect:** The phenomenon where the value of a product or service increases as more people use it, creating a positive feedback loop that can lead to critical mass.
- **Phase Transition:** The critical point at which a substance changes state, from a liquid to a gas, analogous to crucial mass in complex systems.

Models:

- **Threshold Models in Epidemiology:** Describes the critical mass of infected individuals needed to sustain an epidemic outbreak, often modeled using concepts like the primary reproduction number (R₀).
- **Innovation Diffusion Models:** Explore the spread of  innovations through a population, with critical mass representing the point at which a significant portion of the population adopts the 
innovation (e.g., Rogers' Diffusion of Innovations theory).
- **Network Effects Models:** Investigate how the value of a product or service increases with the number of users, reaching critical mass when the network effects become significant.
- **Adoption Curve Models:** Describe the rate at which a new  product or technology is adopted by a population with critical mass  indicating widespread adoption (e.g., the adoption curve in technology 
diffusion).
- **Olson's Logic of Collective Action** Explores the conditions under which group members mobilize and coordinate their efforts to achieve a common goal, reaching critical mass for effective collective action.
- **Resource Mobilization Theory** examines how social movements mobilize resources, supporters, and public opinion, reaching a critical mass to exert influence and bring about change.
- **Threshold Models of Opinion Dynamics:** Study how individuals change their opinions based on the influence of others,  reaching a critical mass where a tipping point is reached.
- **Bank Run Models:** Investigate situations where a critical mass of individuals withdrawing funds from a bank triggers a cascade effect, potentially leading to a systemic banking crisis.
- **Spatial Voting Models:** Analyze the spatial distribution of voters' preferences and how a critical mass of support can determine electoral outcomes.
- **Bass Diffusion Model:** Describes how new products or technologies are adopted in a population, emphasizing the critical mass of adopters influencing others to adopt.

## Critical Mass

> **Critical mass** refers to the minimum threshold of adoption or participation required for a phenomenon, such as a technology or social movement, to sustainably achieve widespread acceptance or impact.
>
