## Limitations

> While Bayesian epistemology provides a structured framework for updating beliefs, its limitations include:
> 
1. **Dependence on Prior Beliefs:** The choice of prior probabilities can heavily influence outcomes, and selecting an appropriate prior can be subjective or difficult, especially in the absence of strong prior knowledge.
2. **Complexity in Modeling Real-World Evidence:** Real-world evidence is often messy, uncertain, and not always quantifiable, making it challenging to apply Bayesian methods in certain contexts.
3. **Cognitive Limitations:** Human beings may not always be able to update beliefs in a purely rational, Bayesian manner, as cognitive biases, heuristics, and emotional factors often influence belief formation and revision.
4. **Inability to Handle All Types of Knowledge:** Bayesian epistemology is better suited for situations where probabilities can be defined, but it struggles with qualitative, ambiguous, or highly subjective forms of knowledge.
5. **Over-Reliance on Consistency:** The assumption that beliefs should always be updated consistently might be unrealistic in the face of new, radically different evidence that forces a complete overhaul of existing beliefs.
