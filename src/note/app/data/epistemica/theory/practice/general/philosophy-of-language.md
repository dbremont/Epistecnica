# Philosophy of Language

> The **philosophy of language** is the study of the nature, meaning, and use of language, exploring fundamental questions about communication, semantics, and linguistic representation.
> 

> Name:  a referential  / representational token.
> 

> Language is a **framework** or **set of conventions** that, like a coordinate system for spatial representation, allows us to r**epresent the world** and **communicate** our mental representations.
> 

> Language is a complex system made up of many interconnected parts that work together to create new  **`conventional linguistic system`** to represent and communicate. Each **`linguistic convention`** represents a state of this system, and every new state is a slightly modified version of the previous one.
> 

Levels of Language:

- **Linguistic Act**: A **linguistic act** refers to an action performed through language that conveys meaning or intention,
- **Speech Act**:  A **speech act** is an utterance that performs a specific communicative function, shaped by the speaker's intention and the context in which it occurs. Understanding speech acts is essential for analyzing language use, as it highlights how meaning is constructed and conveyed in communication beyond mere words.
- **Conversational Act**: A conversational act is the bringing about of an effect. A conversational act is the bringing about of the intented effect, which is the standard effect for the kind of speech act that the speaker is performing.

Evaluate Language:

- **Evaluative language** is used to express emotions, make recommendations, or state moral judgments.
- Evaluative language is called "evaluative" because it involves making evaluations or judgments about something.

## Names

> 

Here’s a summary of the **theory of names** as articulated by several prominent philosophers, including John Stuart Mill, Gottlob Frege, and John Searle, among others:

| **Philosopher** | **Key Ideas on Names** |
| --- | --- |
| **John Stuart Mill** | Mill proposed that names function as **connotative** terms that signify a particular object or person. He distinguished between **denotation** (the actual object) and **connotation** (the attributes associated with that object). He argued that names are meaningful in their reference and that they denote objects directly. |
| **Gottlob Frege** | Frege introduced the distinction between the **sense** (Sinn) and **reference** (Bedeutung) of names. According to Frege, the sense of a name is the way in which the reference is presented; it is the cognitive value associated with the name. Frege emphasized that two names can refer to the same object but have different senses (e.g., "the morning star" vs. "the evening star"). |
| **Bertrand Russell** | Although not mentioned initially, Russell's theory of descriptions also contributes to the understanding of names. He argued that many names do not refer to specific objects but rather function as **descriptions** that can be analyzed to understand their meaning. For Russell, the meaning of a name could be derived from the definite description it expresses. |
| **John Searle** | Searle focused on the **performative aspect** of language and proposed that names are not merely labels but play a role in speech acts. He distinguished between **referring** and **predicating** in his discussions of names, emphasizing how they function within sentences to convey meaning and perform actions. He also explored the concept of **reference** in terms of social and linguistic conventions. |
| **Saul Kripke** | Kripke challenged the descriptivist view (like that of Frege and Russell) with his theory of **rigid designation**. He argued that names refer directly to objects in all possible worlds, rather than through descriptions. For Kripke, a name's reference is fixed at the time of naming and does not change based on different contexts or descriptions. |

## Meaning

> Meaning: Meaning is used.
> 

> **"Significado"** se refiere al contenido o la esencia que se transmite a través de un signo, palabra o símbolo.
> 

> **"Significado"** proviene del latín "significatum," que se compone de "signum," que significa "signo," y "facere," que significa "hacer," lo que sugiere que el significado se refiere a la esencia o el contenido que un signo indica o representa.
