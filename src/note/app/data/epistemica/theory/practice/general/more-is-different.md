# More Is Different

## More is Different

"More is different" is a phrase coined by the physicist Philip Anderson in his seminal 1972 paper of the same title. It suggests that as systems become more complex, new emergent properties and behaviors arise that cannot be understood or predicted by simply studying the individual components or their interactions.

In science and complex systems, "more is different" emphasizes that the whole is greater than the sum of its parts. Even if we understand the behavior of individual components, their interactions can lead to novel phenomena that cannot be explained solely by examining the parts in isolation.

This concept is fundamental in fields such as condensed matter physics, where complex materials exhibit emergent properties such as superconductivity or magnetism that cannot be explained by studying the behavior of individual atoms. It also applies to many other disciplines, including biology, sociology, and economics, where complex systems exhibit behaviors that arise from interactions between many individual entities.

In summary, "more is different" highlights the importance of studying systems holistically and recognizing that complex systems often exhibit emergent properties that cannot be understood by studying their components alone.
