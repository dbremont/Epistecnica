# Argumentation

> **“Argumentar"** proviene del latín *argumentare*, que significa "dar pruebas" o "probar". Esta, a su vez, se deriva de *argumentum*, que significa "prueba" o "razón". En su etimología, se refleja la idea de presentar razones o evidencias para sustentar una afirmación o punto de vista.
> 

> **Sofisma**: Argumento falso o capcioso que se pretende hacer pasar por verdadero.
> 

> **Tu quoque** (/tjuːˈkwoʊkwi, tuːˈkwoʊkweɪ/; Latin Tū quoque, for “you also”) is a discussion technique that intends to discredit the opponent’s argument by attacking the opponent’s own personal behavior and actions as being inconsistent with their argument, therefore accusing hypocrisy. This specious reasoning is a special type of ad hominem attack. The Oxford English Dictionary cites John Cooke’s 1614 stage play The Cittie Gallant as the earliest use of the term in the English language. “Whataboutism” is one particularly well-known modern instance of this technique.
> 

> **Whataboutism** or whataboutery (as in “what about…?”) denotes in a pejorative sense a procedure in which a critical question or argument is not answered or discussed, but retorted with a critical counter-question which expresses a counter-accusation. From a logical and argumentative point of view, it is considered a variant of the tu-quoque pattern (Latin ‘you too’, the term for a counter-accusation), which is a subtype of the ad-hominem argument.
> 

> A **syllogism** is a form of deductive reasoning where a conclusion is drawn from two premises, each sharing a common term with the conclusion.
> 

Arguments can be used to (reasons):

- Persuasion
- Justification
- Explanation

Argument Structure:

1. A series of sentences, statements, or propositions.
2. Some of these serve as the premises.
3. One of them is the conclusion.
4. The premises are intended to provide reasons for the conclusion.

How to detect arguments?

- Look for a conditional structure `if premises then conclucion.` , or an equivalent structure.
- We have too look for conclucion markers and reason markers.
    - Look at the `marker` (connector) and see if the piece after that connector is a premise or a conclucion; that will determine the type of marker.
    - **Reason markers**, such as "because," "for," "as," and "since," indicate that the sentence following them is a reason or premise.
    - **Conclusion markers**, such as "so," "therefore," "thus," and "accordingly," indicate that the sentence following them is a conclusion, while the preceding sentence is a premise.

> 
1. Do a close analysis
2. Get down to basics
    1. remove al excess verbiage

3. sharpen edges
    1. clarify where needed
    2. break up where possible without distortion
4. Organize parts
    1. Divide the argument into sub-arguments
5. Fill in gaps: Are they some hidden premisses?

## Evaluation

- Evaluate the premises
- Evaluate the reasoning chain
- Seeing this; we have a recursive structure; this is called the **`The Problem of Skeptical Regress`**
- Look for problems
    - Circular reasoning
    - Infinite argument
    - Authority based arguments (Assuring)
- **Validity**: Is not possible for the premises to be true and the conclusion to be false.  {Premises} → {Conslusions}  Argument validity refers to a property of an argument where, if the premises are true, the conclusion must necessarily follow and also be true. In other words, a valid argument has a logical structure such that it is impossible for the premises to be true and the conclusion false at the same time. Validity focuses on the form of the argument rather than the actual truth of the premises or conclusion.
    - How to check the validity of an argument?  If the conclusion is false; can the premises be true;  and viceversa.
    - If the conclusion is true; can the premises be false; and viceversa.
- Soundness: A **sound argument** is an argument that is both **valid** (its logical structure ensures that if the premises are true, the conclusion must be true) and has **true premises**. In other words, a sound argument guarantees the truth of its conclusion because it is logically valid and based on factually correct premises.

## Dialectic

> [**Dialectic**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialectic) is a method of dialogue or reasoning that seeks to uncover truth through the exchange of logical arguments, often involving the opposition of different viewpoints to arrive at a synthesis.
