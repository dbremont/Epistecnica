# Adaptation

> Systems adapt to changes in their environment or internal conditions.
> 

> In technical terms, **adaptation** refers to a **dynamic, feedback-driven process by which a system modifies its internal structure, parameters, or behavior in response to changes in its environment or internal state**, with the objective of maintaining or improving performance, stability, or fitness according to some evaluation criterion.
> 

> **Note**: Structural modifications within a system may arise from intentional design or as emergent consequences of decentralized, non-conscious dynamics.
> 

> Biology, Society (System) : Mechanism (Variation + Selection aka Natural Selection) in Biological Adaptation.
> 

### 1. **In Control Theory**:

> Adaptation is the process by which a control system alters its parameters in real time to maintain desired performance in the presence of unknown or time-varying system dynamics and disturbances. This is formalized in adaptive control schemes (e.g., Model Reference Adaptive Control, Self-Tuning Regulators), where parameter estimation and control law update are integral parts of the system.
> 

### 2. **In Evolutionary Biology**:

> Adaptation denotes a heritable trait or process arising through natural selection, where phenotypic modifications increase the organism’s reproductive fitness in a specific environment. It is the outcome of selection acting on genetic variation, formally modeled using fitness landscapes and population genetics.
> 

### 3. **In Machine Learning**:

> Adaptation is the modification of a model's parameters or structure to reduce generalization error under distributional shift. Examples include domain adaptation, where a model trained on a source domain is adapted to perform well on a different, target domain, typically via transfer learning, fine-tuning, or distribution alignment techniques.
> 

### 4. **In Neuroscience**:

> Neural adaptation refers to a decrease in neuronal response to a sustained or repeated stimulus, governed by synaptic plasticity mechanisms (e.g., short-term depression, long-term potentiation) and used to optimize information processing.
> 

### 5. **In Social Systems (Societal Adaptation)**

> Societal adaptation is the process by which social systems—comprising institutions, cultural norms, economic structures, and governance mechanisms—reconfigure themselves in response to internal dynamics or external perturbations (e.g., environmental, technological, economic, or demographic changes) to preserve systemic functionality, stability, and resilience.
>
