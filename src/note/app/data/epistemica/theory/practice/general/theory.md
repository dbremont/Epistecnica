# Theory

> A **theory** is a structured system of ideas that explains, organizes, or predicts phenomena. It’s more than just a guess—it has internal logic, a set of assumptions, and often some grounding in evidence or observation.
> 

> A theory is a coherent explanatory framework.
> 

## Aspect

| Aspect | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **General framework** | Provides a broad way of understanding a domain (e.g., evolution, relativity). |
| **Objects of study** | Concerns specific entities, processes, or relationships. |
| **Claims and principles** | Built from claims, axioms, or hypotheses defining how its objects behave or relate. |
| **Explanatory power** | Explains *why* things happen, not just that they happen. |
| **Predictive capacity** | Allows anticipation of outcomes in new situations. |
| **Testability** | Can be challenged, tested, and refined, especially in scientific contexts. |

## Etymology

> The word **“theory”** comes from the **Greek word *theoria* (θεωρία)**, which originally meant **“contemplation, speculation, a looking at, a viewing”**. It is derived from the verb ***theorein* (θεωρεῖν)**, meaning **“to look at, to observe, to contemplate”**, which itself comes from ***theoros* (θεωρός)**, meaning **“spectator” or “observer”**.
> 

> So etymologically, a **theory** is fundamentally **an act of observing or contemplating something**, rather than necessarily being a practical or applied tool. Only later in Latin (*theoria*) and Medieval Latin did it take on the sense of **a systematic framework of ideas or principles that explain a piece of reality - or a set of observations**.
> 

## Taxonomy

| Type | Description | Instance(s) | Tags |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Descriptive Theory** | Describes phenomena without necessarily explaining causes or mechanisms. | Early astronomy catalogues, Darwin’s notes on finches | #Observation #Documentation #Phenomenon |
| **Explanatory Theory** | Explains *why* phenomena occur by identifying mechanisms or principles. | Germ Theory of Disease, Theory of Evolution | #Causality #Mechanism #Understanding |
| **Predictive Theory** | Allows forecasting of outcomes in new or unseen situations. | Newtonian Mechanics, Quantum Electrodynamics | #Forecasting #Modeling #Future |
| **Effective Theory** | Provides an approximate or domain-limited explanation valid under certain conditions. | Thermodynamics (macroscopic), Effective Field Theory in physics | #Approximation #DomainSpecific #Practical |
| **Prescriptive Theory** | Provides guidance on how things *ought* to be. | Utilitarianism, Rawls’ Theory of Justice | #Ethics #Guidance #Prescriptive |
| **Mathematical Theory** | Axiomatic system of concepts, definitions, and theorems. | Number Theory, Probability Theory | #Axiomatic #Logical #Abstract |
| **Social/Interpretive Theory** | Framework for understanding human meaning, culture, or interaction. | Symbolic Interactionism, Critical Theory | #Culture #Interaction #Interpretive |

## Evaluation

> How to evaluate a theory?
> 

| **Criteria** | **Description** | **Tag(s)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-Consistency | Free from internal contradictions; assumptions and conclusions align logically. | Logic, Validity |
| Predictive Power | Generates testable, novel, and accurate predictions. | Science, Forecasting |
| Explanatory Power | Provides clear, deep, and unifying explanations of observed phenomena. | Understanding, Clarity |
| Empirical Adequacy | Fits with observed data and experimental results. | Evidence, Data |
| Scope | Addresses a wide range of phenomena without ad hoc fixes. | Breadth, Generality |
| Simplicity / Parsimony | Explains much with few assumptions (Occam’s Razor). | Elegance, Economy |
| Falsifiability | Can be tested and, in principle, proven wrong by evidence. | Testability, Scientific Method |
| Coherence | Integrates with or logically relates to other established theories. | Integration, Consistency |
| Practical Utility | Provides tools, guidance, or applications for solving real-world problems. | Application, Usefulness |
| Fruitfulness | Stimulates new research, questions, and discoveries. | Creativity, Progress |
