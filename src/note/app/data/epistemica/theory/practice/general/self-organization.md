## Term Linguistics

> How can we characterize the linguistic  of the term  **‘Organization’**? By **organization** is usually meant **structural-functional coherence**—a system in which parts interact  in meaningful ways in order to achieve certain ends, maintain stability - sustain certain ‘functionality’. By this we can infer - that at some level ‘Organization’ is a epistemic  notion  that is in the eye of the beholder.
> 

> How can we characterize the linguistic  of the term  **‘Self-Organization’**? By self-organization we mean - something that has auto-organized - that is transition from (dis-organization - not functional coherence) to organization (functional coherece).
>

## List of Self-Organization Systems

> List of Self-Organizing Systems Examples.
> 

| **Self-Organization Phenomenon** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Crystallization** | The process where atoms or molecules arrange themselves in a regular, repeating pattern to form a crystal. |
| **Pattern Formation** | The emergence of regular, repeating patterns in systems initially lacking structure, like the stripes on a zebra or spots on a leopard. |
| **Convection** | The spontaneous circulation of fluids driven by temperature or density differences, leading to complex patterns and structures. |
| **Swarm Behavior** | Collective behavior of individuals, such as flocking birds or schooling fish, arising from simple local interactions. |
| **Neural Network Formation** | The spontaneous organization of neurons in the brain into complex networks underlying cognitive processes like learning and memory. |
| **Self-Assembly** | The spontaneous formation of complex structures or materials from simple building blocks, such as lipid bilayers in cell membranes. |
| **Evolution** | The adaptive process of biological populations, driven by natural selection and other mechanisms. |
| **Social Self-Organization** | The emergence of social norms, organizations, and institutions from the interactions of individuals and groups in society. |

## Self-Organizing Computation

| **Method** | **Description** | **Example** | **Type of Emergence** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cellular Automata (CA)** | A grid-based model where cells evolve according to simple rules based on their neighbors. The system self-organizes into complex patterns. | Conway's **Game of Life** | Weak Emergence |
| **Agent-Based Models (ABM)** | Simulates individual agents that interact based on simple rules. Collective behavior emerges from these interactions. | **Boids** (flocking behavior) | Weak Emergence |
| **Swarm Intelligence** | Inspired by collective behavior in natural systems (e.g., ants, bees), this method uses decentralized agents to solve optimization or search problems. | **Ant Colony Optimization (ACO)** | Weak Emergence |
| **Self-Organizing Maps (SOM)** | A type of artificial neural network that maps high-dimensional data to lower dimensions, preserving the topological properties of the input data. | **Kohonen Networks** | Weak Emergence |
| **Artificial Life (ALife)** | Simulates life-like processes in a computational environment. It explores self-organization and adaptation in systems that exhibit biological processes. | **Langton's Ant** | Weak Emergence |
| **Evolutionary Algorithms** | Simulates the process of natural selection where solutions evolve over generations based on fitness evaluations. | **Genetic Algorithms (GA)** | Weak Emergence |
| **Neural Networks (NN)** | Systems of interconnected neurons that can learn complex patterns through training. The network self-organizes to represent and solve tasks without explicit programming. | **Deep Learning Networks** | Weak to Strong Emergence |
| **Genetic Programming (GP)** | An evolutionary algorithm where programs evolve over time to solve problems by generating solutions that improve through selection and mutation. | Evolving code for problem-solving | Weak Emergence |
| **Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)** | Optimizes a problem by simulating a group of particles that explore the solution space and share information to find optimal solutions. | Finding the optimal path in a maze | Weak Emergence |
| **Lattice Gas Automata (LGA)** | A model for simulating fluid dynamics where particles interact according to simple rules, leading to complex behaviors like turbulence and fluid flow. | Simulating fluid dynamics | Weak Emergence |
| **Complex Systems Simulations** | Modeling complex systems with interacting components that lead to emergent properties like pattern formation or phase transitions. | Ising model (magnetism) | Weak Emergence |
