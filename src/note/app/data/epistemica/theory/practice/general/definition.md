# Definition

> This note aims to explore the highly abstract operation of definition, which serves as a fundamental mechanism for establishing meaning, constraining interpretation, and enabling systematic reasoning within formal, scientific, and conceptual frameworks.
> 

> A **definition** is a formal operation within a representational system that establishes a *mapping* between a **definiendum** (the symbol, sign, or linguistic/conceptual entity to be defined) and a **definiens** (the structured set of terms, rules, or primitives already available within the system).
> 

> A **definition** is a stipulative operator that introduces or fixes a symbol’s admissible use within a **formal or conceptual system** by establishing a mapping to preexisting structures, thereby constraining interpretation, ensuring operational closure, and enabling systematic reasoning without adding contingent empirical content.

## Formulation

**Syntactic Function**:

- A definition introduces or fixes the admissible use of a symbol in a formal language by stipulating its equivalence or correspondence to an expression constructed from preexisting elements.
- Formally, if $L$ is a formal language, a definition is a rule $d: \Sigma \to \mathcal{E}(L)$, where $\Sigma$ is a set of symbols and $\mathcal{E}(L)$ is the set of permissible expressions in $L$.

**Semantic Function**:

- A definition constrains the interpretation function II of a language, i.e., it determines the mapping from syntactic objects to semantic domains.
- In model-theoretic terms, a definition enriches the structure $\mathcal{M} = (D, I)$, where $D$ is the domain and $I$ the interpretation, by specifying $I(s) = v$ for some symbol $s$.

**Epistemic Function**:

- A definition does not assert contingent truth but instead *stipulates* conventions that establish the identity conditions of a concept.
- In terms of information theory, it introduces no new empirical information but reorganizes the coding scheme for subsequent inferences.

**Ontological Role**:

- A definition can be intensional (fixing the sense or conceptual boundaries of a term by specifying necessary and sufficient conditions) or extensional (fixing its referent set or denotation within a given universe of discourse).
- More abstractly, it constitutes a *constraint on admissible states* of a symbol’s use within a semiotic system.

**Operational Closure**:

- In mathematics and formal sciences, a definition ensures closure under substitution: if a:=ba := b, then any valid transformation on bb may be applied to aa without loss of consistency.
- Thus, definitions act as **mappings preserving invariants** across syntactic and semantic layers.

## **Definitional Strategy**

> What is a **Definitional Strategy?** A **Definitional Strategy** is a systematic approach or method used to define a concept, term, or object within a formal, scientific, or conceptual system. It specifies **how the meaning of a term is fixed** by choosing a particular way to relate the term (definiendum) to other concepts, structures, or instances (definiens).
> 

| **Method** | **Description** | **Case** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Intensional** | Specifies necessary and sufficient conditions that an object must satisfy to fall under the defined term. Focuses on the *conceptual essence* rather than listing instances. | “A triangle is a polygon with three straight sides.” |
| **Extensional** | Defines a term by enumerating all members of its reference set. Focuses on the *instances* rather than properties. | “The planets in our solar system = {Mercury, Venus, Earth, …}.” |
| **Operational** | Defines a concept in terms of a measurable procedure or operation. Ensures reproducibility in empirical or experimental contexts. | “Temperature is defined as the reading of a calibrated thermometer under standard conditions.” |
| **Stipulative** | Introduces a new term or convention for a specific purpose. May not have prior usage. | “Let an X-point be any intersection of two functions in this discussion.” |
| **Recursive (Constructive)** | Defines a term by referring to simpler or previously defined elements in a constructive manner. Common in mathematics and computer science. | “A natural number is either 0 or the successor of a natural number.” |
| **Analytic (Definition by Analysis)** | Defines a concept by decomposing it into constituent properties or relations. Often used in philosophy or formal semantics. | “Knowledge = justified true belief.” |
| **Via Negatiava** | A **via negativa definition** is a way of defining something **by stating what it is *not*** rather than what it *is*. | “God is that than which nothing greater can be conceived” (defined by negating imperfection) or “Health is the absence of disease.” |

## Defining

> How to define things?
> 

> To **define** something is to establish a precise mapping between a **term (definiendum)** and a **conceptual or formal structure (definiens)** that fixes its meaning within a given system of thought.
> 

Process:

1. **Identify the Universe of Discourse**
    - Specify the domain in which the definition operates (mathematics, physics, law, ordinary language, etc.).
    - This prevents category mistakes (e.g., defining “energy” differently in physics vs. economics).
2. **Choose the Definitional Strategy**
3. **Ensure Non-Circularity and Clarity**
    - A definition should not use the term being defined (avoid circularity).
    - It should reduce the new term to already-understood primitives.
4. **Test for Coherence**
    - Within the formal system, check whether the definition preserves logical consistency and closure.
    - Within empirical sciences, check whether the operational definition is reproducible.
5. **Distinguish Definition from Assertion**
    - A definition does not assert contingent truth (it does not say *what is the case in the world*).
    - It stipulates *how a term will be used*, providing a framework for later truth-claims.

## ❓ QA

### When philosophers or logicians say that a definition does not assert contingent truth, what exactly do they mean?

> A definition is a **stipulation about meaning**, not a **truth claim about reality** that might be true or false depending on circumstances.
> 

In a formal sense:

- A definition **restricts the mapping** between a symbol (definiendum) and an expression/structure (definiens).
- It is **conventional, not empirical**: you could stipulate different definitions (e.g., some mathematicians define 1 as “prime” in alternative systems), and both are valid within their respective systems.
- Thus, definitions **don’t add empirical knowledge**, they **shape the representational framework** in which empirical or logical truths are later evaluated.
