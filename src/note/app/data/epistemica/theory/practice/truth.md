# Truth

Related: [Epistemology](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/general/epistemology.md), [Ontology](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/general/ontology.md), [Explanation](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/explanation.md)

> A statement is **true** if and only if it **corresponds** to a state of affairs in the world.
> 

> La palabra **"verdad"** proviene del latín ***veritas***, que a su vez deriva del adjetivo ***verus***, que significa "verdadero" o "real".
> 

> Real:  Cosa de la existencia → *Realis* deriva de ***res* (cosa, objeto, entidad)**.
> 

> Truth is a property of reality itself, not of our interpretation of it.
> 

## Theories of Truth

| **Theory of Truth** | **Key Definition** | **Proponents** | **Strengths** | **Criticisms** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Correspondence Theory** | A statement is true if it corresponds to objective reality. | Aristotle, Bertrand Russell, Tarski | Intuitive; aligns with scientific realism. | Requires a clear definition of "correspondence"; challenged by skepticism. |
| **Coherence Theory** | A statement is true if it coheres with a system of beliefs. | Hegel, Bradley, Brandom | Works well in mathematics/logic. | May allow internally consistent but false systems. |
| **Pragmatic Theory** | A statement is true if it is useful or works in practice. | C.S. Peirce, William James, Dewey | Emphasizes practical verification. | Relativistic; conflates truth with utility. |
| **Deflationary (Redundancy) Theory** | "True" is a logical device, not a substantive property. | Frege, Ramsey, Quine | Avoids metaphysical commitments. | May oversimplify truth’s role in discourse. |
| **Semantic Theory (Tarski)** | Formal definition: "S is true iff p" (Convention T). | Alfred Tarski | Precise for formal languages. | Limited to structured, non-natural languages. |
| **Consensus Theory** | Truth is what is agreed upon by a group or community. | Habermas, Putnam (sometimes) | Useful in social epistemology. | Risks relativism (e.g., "majority decides truth"). |
| **Performative Theory** | Calling something "true" is a speech act, not a description. | Strawson, Austin | Explains how we use "true" in language. | Doesn’t define truth conditions. |
| **Alethic Pluralism** | Different domains (science, ethics) have different truth criteria. | Crispin Wright, Lynch | Flexible; accommodates diverse fields. | Lacks a unified account of truth. |
