# Category

Related: [Ontology](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/general/ontology.md)

> In this note, we will explore the **philosophical dimension** of the term *“Category”*; specifically, we will examine the doctrine of the Categories.
> 

> In general, a **category system** is a structured framework used to represent the hierarchy of concepts through which reality is described.

## Category System Space

> Which are the **classification**?
> 

| **Category System** | **Description** | **Characterization** | **Limitation(s)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Aristotelian** (cf. Categories by Aristotle) | A classification of the fundamental modes in which being can be said or predicated. | Ontological–logical system; enumerates basic types such as substance, quantity, quality, relation, place, time, etc. Grounded in metaphysical realism. | Assumes categories reflect the structure of reality itself; limited adaptability to modern scientific ontology; lacks formal derivation principle. |
| **Kantian** (cf. Critique of Pure Reason by Immanuel Kant) | A table of pure concepts of the understanding that structure possible experience. | Epistemological–transcendental system; categories are a priori conditions for cognition (e.g., causality, unity, plurality). Derived architectonically from logical forms of judgment. | Applies only to phenomena (not things-in-themselves); contested derivation method; historically situated cognitive model. |
| **Scholastic(Medieval)** | Development and refinement of Aristotelian categories within theological-metaphysical frameworks. | Ontological hierarchy integrated with Christian metaphysics; emphasis on substance–accident distinction. | Dependent on theological commitments; limited by pre-scientific cosmology. |
| **Hegelian** (cf. Science of Logic by Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel) | Dynamic, dialectical development of categories through logical self-movement. | Processual and developmental; categories evolve via contradiction and synthesis. | Highly abstract; controversial logical method; difficult formalization. |
| **Phenomenological** (e.g., Edmund Husserl) | Categories as structures of intentional consciousness. | Descriptive–eidetic; grounded in lived experience rather than metaphysical enumeration. | Methodologically demanding; bracketing assumptions debated. |
