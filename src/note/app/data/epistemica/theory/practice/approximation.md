# Approximation

> Truth … is much too complicated to allow anything but approximations. - John von Neumann
> 

> Learn things quickly, note your confusions, and correct them later when the Anki cards show up again. Let yourself learn approximate models of reality.
> 

The [**approximation model of reality**](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/B7P97C27rvHPz3s9B/gears-in-understanding) is a method that is commonly used in many fields, including science, engineering, and economics. The idea behind this method is to create a simplified model of a complex system that captures the most important aspects of the system while ignoring irrelevant details. This allows researchers to analyze the system in a more manageable way and to make predictions about how it will behave under different conditions.

One of the main advantages of the **approximation model** of reality is that it can simplify the analysis of complex systems, which can be very difficult or impossible to analyze directly. By creating a **simplified model**, researchers can gain insights into the behavior of the system that would be difficult to obtain otherwise. Additionally, the **approximation model** can be used to make predictions about how the system will behave under different conditions, which can be useful for designing experiments or making decisions.

However, there are also some limitations to the **approximation model of reality**. One potential issue is that the simplified model may not accurately capture all the important aspects of the system, which can lead to inaccurate predictions or conclusions. Additionally, the simplified model may be based on assumptions or approximations that are not completely valid, which can further limit its accuracy.

**Approximation models** offer us a reduced representation of reality, so the inferences from them are only valid in a limited context.

Overall, the **approximation model** of reality can be a useful method for analyzing complex systems, but it is important to be aware of its limitations and potential sources of error. It is often necessary to combine this method with other analytical or empirical methods to fully understand the behavior of a complex system.
