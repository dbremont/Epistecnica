> It looks like you're referring to **Roger Bacon** (not "Robert"), a 13th-century philosopher and Franciscan friar often called *Doctor Mirabilis* ("Wonderful Teacher").
> 

> His epistemology (theory of knowledge) was groundbreaking for the medieval period, blending empiricism, rationalism, and a critique of scholastic methods. Here’s an analysis of his key ideas:
> 

### **Roger Bacon’s Epistemology**

1. **Empirical Foundations**
    - Bacon emphasized **observation and experimentation** as crucial for true knowledge, prefiguring later scientific empiricism.
    - He argued that direct experience (*experientia*)—not just textual authority—was necessary to verify truths.
2. **Critique of Scholasticism**
    - He attacked the overreliance on tradition, dogma, and the *argument from authority* (e.g., blindly citing Aristotle or Church fathers).
    - Advocated for **mathematical and scientific methods** to correct errors in theology and natural philosophy.
3. **Role of Reason and Mathematics**
    - While valuing empiricism, Bacon also stressed **rational deduction** and the use of mathematics to interpret nature.
    - Saw math as the "alphabet of philosophy," essential for astronomy, optics, and physics.
4. **Four Causes of Error** (*Opus Majus*)
    
    Bacon identified obstacles to knowledge:
    
    - **Submission to faulty authority** (e.g., uncritical acceptance of ancient texts).
    - **Habitual thinking** (repeating unexamined traditions).
    - **Popular prejudice** (relying on crowd opinions).
    - **False displays of knowledge** (ignorance disguised as wisdom).
5. **Theology and Science**
    - Believed science and theology could harmonize, as both revealed divine truth.
    - Argued that studying nature (e.g., optics, alchemy) deepened understanding of God’s creation.
