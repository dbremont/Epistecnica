# Whitehead’s  Method

Related: [Leibniz Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/leibniz-method.md)

> aka: Philosophy of Organism.
> 

> Whitehead’s system is **metaphysical**, **ontological**, and **cosmological** in scope. At its heart is the idea that **reality is fundamentally made of processes, not static substances**. He replaces the traditional metaphysical unit ("substance") with a new concept: the **actual occasion**.
> 

## Structure

| Component | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Ontology** | Reality consists of *actual occasions* and *eternal objects*. |
| **Cosmology** | The universe is a web of related processes unfolding in time. |
| **Epistemology** | Knowledge is participatory; subject-object dualism is dissolved. |
| **Theology** | God is embedded in the processual fabric of reality, not outside it. |
| **Ethics/Aesthetics** | Emphasis on harmony, intensity, novelty—beauty as the telos of reality. |

## Ontological Framework

| Ontological Role | Whitehead's Replacement |
| --- | --- |
| **Substance** (static being) | **Actual Occasion** (unit of becoming) (events) |
| **Property** (inherence) | **Prehension** (relational grasping) |
| **Form** (essence) | **Eternal Object** (pure potential) |
| **Cause** | **Creative Advance** (novelty through concrescence) |

| **Category** | **Definition** | **Role in Ontology** | **Key Relations** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Actual Occasion** | A unit of becoming; an event of experience. | The most basic real entity; reality is composed of them. | Prehends others; undergoes concrescence; forms nexūs. |
| **Prehension** | A process of relational grasping of another entity (positive or negative). | Mechanism of interaction; replaces classical causality. | Links actual occasions to each other and to eternal objects. |
| **Concrescence** | The internal process by which an actual occasion becomes a unified whole. | Explains how an entity integrates influences into a singular experience. | Composes prehensions into satisfaction; guided by initial aim. |
| **Satisfaction** | The final phase of an actual occasion; a completed, determinate state. | Marks the completion of concrescence; enters objective reality. | Becomes datum for future prehensions. |
| **Eternal Object** | Pure potential; abstract form (like a Platonic form). | Provides qualitative definiteness and possibility for novelty. | Ingresses into actual occasions; housed in God’s primordial nature. |
| **Nexus** | A group of actual occasions in mutual prehension. | Explains enduring patterns and compound individuals. | Forms societies; has structured relations. |
| **Society** | A structured nexus with a common defining characteristic. | Explains stable entities (organisms, objects, etc.) in terms of process. | Requires repetition of pattern across occasions. |
| **Creativity** | The universal principle of becoming and novelty. | Ultimate metaphysical principle; enables process and change. | Expressed in every actual occasion; foundational. |
| **God (Primordial)** | The repository of all eternal objects; provides the "initial aim." | Source of order and possibility; grounds potentiality. | Related to all actual occasions; does not determine, but lures. |
| **God (Consequent)** | The evolving aspect of God that prehends the world. | Participates in the world’s becoming; feels all actual occasions. | Accumulates all value and becomes the most complete experience. |

## Comparison

| Classical Ontology | Whitehead's Ontology |
| --- | --- |
| Substance (Aristotle) | Actual Occasion (Process) |
| Properties/Accidents | Prehensions and Eternal Objects |
| Static Being | Dynamic Becoming |
| Linear Time | Creative Advance |
| Efficient Causality | Internal Relations, Prehensions |
| God as Unmoved Mover | God as Lure and Participant |

## Influences

- Plato (especially via *Forms*)
- Leibniz (monads → actual occasions)
- Hume and empiricism
- Bergson (emphasis on time and becoming)
- Mathematics and physics (especially relativity and quantum theory)\

## Influenced

- Charles Hartshorne, John Cobb (Process Theology)
- Gilles Deleuze (especially via Bergson and relational metaphysics)
- Environmental philosophy
- Systems theory and complexity science
- Ecological theology
