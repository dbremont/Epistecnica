# Hume’s System

Related: [Philosophical System](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/philosophical-system.md)

> David Hume (1711–1776) developed a highly influential system of thought that touched epistemology, metaphysics, ethics, and philosophy of mind. Here’s a **technical** summary of his major ideas:
> 

### 1. **Empiricism**

- **Premise**: All knowledge derives from sensory experience.
- **Technical distinction**:
    - **Impressions**: Immediate, vivid sensory experiences (e.g., the feeling of heat).
    - **Ideas**: Faint copies of impressions used in memory and imagination.
- **Claim**: Complex ideas are built from simple impressions by association.

---

### 2. **Theory of Ideas (Copy Principle)**

- **Copy Principle**: Every idea is a copy of some prior impression.
- **Consequences**: If we cannot trace an idea back to a sensory impression, it is likely meaningless (anticipating logical positivism).

---

### 3. **Causality (Problem of Induction)**

- **Critique**:
    - We have no rational, a priori access to causality itself; we only observe constant conjunctions (e.g., event A is regularly followed by event B).
    - The idea of necessary connection is a psychological habit, not a logically justified inference.
- **Induction Problem**:
    - Justifying inductive reasoning (predicting the future based on past experience) requires assuming the future will resemble the past — an assumption that itself cannot be justified by reason.

---

### 4. **Skepticism**

- **Moderate Skepticism**:
    - Radical doubt is not livable in practice, but philosophically, certainty is unattainable for most matters outside immediate experience and tautological reasoning (e.g., mathematics).
- **Radical Skepticism (in theory)**:
    - Even the existence of the external world, the self, or causality is not provable by reason alone.

---

### 5. **Theory of the Self**

- **Bundle Theory**:
    - There is no persistent "self" as a substance.
    - The self is a collection ("bundle") of perceptions in flux — feelings, thoughts, sensations — tied together by memory and causal relations.
    - **No metaphysical identity** persists through time.

---

### 6. **Ethics and Moral Sentimentalism**

- **Moral judgments** are not derived from reason but from sentiment (feelings).
- Reason discovers facts; sentiment determines values (the "is-ought" gap).
- Moral distinctions are grounded in human emotions like approval (virtue) and disapproval (vice), not in objective rational properties.

---

### 7. **Is-Ought Problem**

- **Observation**: Philosophers often move from statements about what *is* (facts) to what *ought* to be (values) without justification.
- **Result**: Normative claims cannot be logically derived from purely descriptive premises.

---

### 8. **Religion and Miracles**

- **Critique of Religious Belief**:
    - Belief in miracles is irrational because miracles, by definition, violate the uniform laws of nature, which have overwhelming empirical support.
    - Testimony supporting miracles is never strong enough to overcome our established belief in natural laws.

---

### 9. **Political Philosophy**

- **Conservatism of Institutions**:
    - Political institutions and practices evolve through historical trial and error, not from rational design.
    - Government is justified not by theoretical consent but by its practical ability to maintain order and stability.
