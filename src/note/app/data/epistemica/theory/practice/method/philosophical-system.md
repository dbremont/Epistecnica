# Philosophical System

Related: [Hume’s System](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/hume-system.md), [Descartes’s Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/descartes-method.md), [Bacon's Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/bacon-method.md), [Ibn al-Haytham's Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/ibn-al-haytham-method.md), [Wittgenstein’s Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/wittgenstein-method.md), [Aristotle's Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/aristotle-method.md), [Spinoza’s Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/spinoza-method.md), [Heidegger’s Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/heidegger-method.md), [Hegel’s Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/hegel-method.md), [Berkeley's Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/berkeley-method.md), [Nietzsche’s Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/nietzsche-method.md), [Kant’s Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/kant-method.md), [Leibniz Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/leibniz-method.md), [Schopenhauer's Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/schopenhauer-method.md), [Russell’s Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/russell-method.md), [Husserl’s Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/husserl-method.md), [Mileto’s Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/mileto-method.md), [Cusa’s Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/cusa-method.md), [Event](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/ontology/event.md)

> A **philosophical system** is a **structured, coherent set of ontological, epistemological, ethical, and/or metaphysical claims**, organized according to a set of foundational principles and governed by internal logical relations, which aims to offer a comprehensive and unified account of reality, knowledge, value, or existence.
> 

> “Es gibt zwei Gefahren: die eine, daß man kein System hat, die andere, daß man sich an sein System hält.” — Friedrich Schlegel, *Athenaeum Fragments* , c. 1798
> 

> There are two dangers: one is that you have no system; the other is that you adhere to your system.
— Friedrich Schlegel, Athenaeum Fragments, c. 1798
> 

## Characterization

> Characterizing a philosophical system usually means: clearly identifying its **structure, content, methods, and goals**.
> 

> **Note:** This is a structured comparison guide for philosophical systems, focusing specifically on their **metaphysical (ontological)** and **epistemological** foundations. It provides key criteria to analyze how different systems conceptualize reality and the nature, sources, and limits of human knowledge.
> 

| **Dimension** | **Criterion** | **Description** | **Guiding Questions** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Metaphysics** | **Ontological Commitment** | What kinds of entities are said to exist fundamentally? | Are substances, processes, structures, or relations primary? |
|  | **Substance Theory** | How are objects or substances conceived? | Is there one substance, many, or none? Are substances material or immaterial? |
|  | **Modality** | How does the system treat necessity and possibility? | Are there necessary beings or truths? How are possible worlds handled? |
|  | **Causality** | What is the nature of causation? | Is it mechanical, teleological, occasional, internal, or denied? |
|  | **Time and Space** | How are time and space understood ontologically? | Are they absolute, relational, emergent, or illusions? |
|  | **Mind–Body Relation** | How are mental and physical substances related? | Dualism, monism, idealism, materialism, panpsychism? |
|  | **Unity and Plurality** | Is reality one or many? | Is the world ultimately a unity, plurality, or structured field? |
|  | **World Structure** | How is the cosmos organized? | Is there hierarchy, harmony, randomness, or dynamic structure? |
| **Epistemology** | **Source of Knowledge** | Where does knowledge come from? | Sense data, reason, intuition, revelation, construction? |
|  | **Theory of Truth** | What makes a belief true? | Correspondence, coherence, pragmatism, constructivism? |
|  | **Justification** | How are beliefs justified? | Foundationalism, coherentism, reliabilism, infinitism? |
|  | **Innate Ideas** | Are some ideas present in the mind from birth? | Are concepts acquired, built-in, or structured a priori? |
|  | **Limits of Knowledge** | What are the constraints on what can be known? | Is skepticism embraced or rejected? Are there unknowables? |
|  | **Role of Reason vs. Experience** | What is the balance between rational and empirical sources? | Rationalism, empiricism, synthesis? |
|  | **Degrees of Knowledge** | Is knowledge binary or graded? | Does it include intuition, opinion, belief, certainty? |
|  | **Knowledge Objects** | What can be known? | Are we limited to phenomena, or can we know noumena, forms, God? |

|  | **Limits of Knowledge** | Knowledge limited to eternal Forms and their relations | Knowledge is progressive, limited only by the stage of Spirit’s development | Knowledge limited to self and its positing | Some truths are innate and certain; others empirical and uncertain |
|  | **Reason vs. Experience** | Primacy of reason | Reason is dialectical and self-developing | Primacy of reason as self-positing | Primacy of reason |

|  | **Knowledge Objects** | Forms, eternal truths | Absolute Spirit, self and world unified | The self and its relation to the not-self | Ideas, God, self, and clear & distinct concepts |
