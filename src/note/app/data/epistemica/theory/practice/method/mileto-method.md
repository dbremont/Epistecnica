# Mileto’s Method

Related: [Philosophical System](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/philosophical-system.md)

> Thales of Miletus's philosophical system posits that water is the fundamental substance (arche) underlying all reality, marking a revolutionary shift from mythological to naturalistic explanations of the cosmos.
> 

> This thinker represents a huge cognitive phase transition from irrational thinking to naturalism.
>

| Aspect | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Nucleus (Deepest Essence) 🧬** | The deepest essence of Thales's philosophy is his material monism - the idea that all reality originates from and is constituted by a single substance: water. |
| **Evidentia (Evidence) 📊** | The evidence for Thales's philosophy comes primarily from Aristotle's accounts in Metaphysics and other works by later philosophers who referenced his ideas. There are no surviving primary texts by Thales himself. |
| **Telos (Intent) 🎯** | Thales aimed to provide a natural explanation for the universe and its phenomena, moving away from mythological accounts toward rational, observable principles. His approach sought to identify the fundamental substance underlying all reality. |
| **Gnosis (Understanding) 🧠** | To understand Thales's philosophy, one must grasp the concept of arche (first principle) and his material monism. This requires understanding the transition from mythological to natural explanations in ancient Greek thought. Previous knowledge of presocratic philosophy and the Milesian school helps contextualize his ideas. |
| **Epignosis (Application) 🛠️** | Thales's approach to finding fundamental principles influenced subsequent scientific and philosophical inquiry. His mathematical contributions (Thales's theorem) have practical applications in geometry. His method of seeking natural explanations for phenomena laid groundwork for scientific methodology. |
| **Relatio (Connections) 🔗** | Thales's ideas influenced other Milesian philosophers (Anaximander, Anaximenes) and later pre-Socratics. His material monism connects to later philosophical traditions seeking fundamental substances or principles. His mathematical work connects to the development of Greek mathematics. |
| **Idoneitas (Suitability) 📐** | Thales's philosophy was suitable for its time as it represented a significant shift from mythological to natural explanations. However, by modern standards, his specific claim about water as the fundamental substance is seen as overly simplistic. |
| **Limites (Limits) ⛔** | Thales's philosophy was limited by the scientific knowledge of his time. His explanations, while naturalistic, were based on limited observation and lacked experimental verification. The scope of his inquiry was broad but not systematically developed. |
| **Contextus (Context) 🌍** | Thales lived in Miletus, a prosperous Greek city in Ionia (modern-day Turkey) during a period of cultural and intellectual flourishing. This context allowed for the development of new ideas that moved beyond traditional mythological frameworks. |
| **Dynamis (Evolution) 🌱** | Thales's ideas evolved through his successors in the Milesian school, with Anaximander proposing the apeiron (boundless) as the arche, and Anaximenes suggesting air as the fundamental substance. His approach influenced the broader development of pre-Socratic philosophy. |
| **Critica (Critique) 🔍** | Even in antiquity, Thales's water principle was questioned. Aristotle noted that water itself requires explanation and cannot be the ultimate principle. Later philosophers developed more complex metaphysical systems in response to perceived limitations in Thales's approach. |
| **Verbum Usus (Language Use) 🗣️** | The concept of arche (beginning, principle, origin) was central to Thales's philosophy and became a key term in pre-Socratic thought. His use of naturalistic language to describe the cosmos represented a shift from mythological discourse. |

## **Philosophical System**

> Characterizing a philosophical system usually means: clearly identifying its structure, content, methods, and goals.
> 
> 
> **Note:** This is a structured comparison guide for philosophical systems, focusing specifically on their **metaphysical (ontological)** and **epistemological** foundations. It provides key criteria to analyze how different systems conceptualize reality and the nature, sources, and limits of human knowledge.
> 

| **Dimension** | **Criterion** | **Description** | **Guiding Questions** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Metaphysics** | **Ontological Commitment** | Thales committed to material monism, positing that water is the fundamental substance of reality. | Are substances, processes, structures, or relations primary? For Thales, a material substance (water) is primary. |
|  | **Substance Theory** | Thales proposed a single substance (water) as the underlying reality of all things. | Is there one substance, many, or none? One substance (water). Are substances material or immaterial? Material. |
|  | **Modality** | Thales's views on necessity and possibility are not well documented in surviving sources. | Are there necessary beings or truths? Unknown. How are possible worlds handled? Not addressed. |
|  | **Causality** | Thales seems to have viewed causality in naturalistic terms, with transformations of water explaining natural phenomena. | Is it mechanical, teleological, occasional, internal, or denied? Likely natural/mechanical, though not fully developed. |
|  | **Time and Space** | Thales believed the Earth floats on water, suggesting a conception of space as containing substances. | Are they absolute, relational, emergent, or illusions? Not systematically addressed, but his cosmology suggests a relational view. |
|  | **Mind–Body Relation** | Thales's statement that "all things are full of gods" suggests a form of hylozoism, where matter has life or mind. | Dualism, monism, idealism, materialism, panpsychism? A form of materialism with hylozoistic elements. |
|  | **Unity and Plurality** | Thales maintained that reality is ultimately unified, with all things being modifications of water. | Is the world ultimately a unity, plurality, or structured field? Unity, with water as the unifying substance. |
|  | **World Structure** | Thales viewed the cosmos as consisting of the Earth floating on water, with celestial bodies also made from water or its transformations. | Is there hierarchy, harmony, randomness, or dynamic structure? A simple structure with Earth at center floating on water. |
|  | **Change** | Thales explained change as transformations of the fundamental substance (water) into different forms. | How does change occur? Through transformations of water into various states and combinations. |
| **Epistemology** | **Source of Knowledge** | Thales appears to have valued observation and reason, as evidenced by his astronomical observations and mathematical work. | Sense data, reason, intuition, revelation, construction? Likely a combination of observation and reason. |
|  | **Theory of Truth** | Thales's views on truth are not explicitly documented, but his approach suggests correspondence between ideas and observed reality. | Correspondence, coherence, pragmatism, constructivism? Likely correspondence, given his empirical approach. |
|  | **Justification** | Thales seems to have justified his claims through observation and rational explanation, though the exact method is unclear. | Foundationalism, coherentism, reliabilism, infinitism? Unclear, but likely based on observational foundations. |
|  | **Innate Ideas** | Thales's views on innate ideas are not documented in surviving sources. | Are concepts acquired, built-in, or structured a priori? Unknown. |
|  | **Limits of Knowledge** | Thales appears confident in human ability to understand natural phenomena through observation and reason. | Is skepticism embraced or rejected? Rejected. Are there unknowables? Not addressed. |
|  | **Role of Reason vs. Experience** | Thales balanced observation (e.g., of natural phenomena) with reason (e.g., in his mathematical work). | Rationalism, empiricism, synthesis? A precursor to empiricism with rational elements. |
|  | **Degrees of Knowledge** | Thales's views on degrees of knowledge are not explicitly documented. | Does it include intuition, opinion, belief, certainty? Unknown. |
|  | **Knowledge Objects** | Thales focused on understanding the natural world, including astronomical phenomena and mathematical principles. | Are we limited to phenomena, or can we know noumena, forms, God? Focused on natural phenomena. |
