# System

Related: [Level of Organization](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/ontology/level-of-organization.md)

> In this note, we explore a highly abstract concept—a system—as a mechanism to organize reality into a comprehensible and manageable form, one that is representable and thus rendered tractable.
> 

> The idea of a system is **one instance of the abstract mechanism** that we have to represent (both  dynamics, state) a subset of reality.
> 

> A system is essentially a technique used by observers to designate a target—or targets—of study, enabling representation and modeling by structuring its components, interactions, and boundaries in a way that can be analyzed and understood, ultimately delimiting a tractable target.
> 

> A quasi-system is **an entity or phenomenon treated as a system for purposes of observation and analysis, despite having incomplete, ambiguous, or loosely defined boundaries and interactions**.
> 

> Note: **The concept of a system has its limits**; it is merely one technique for structuring our representation of reality. It is best suited for contexts where boundaries can be reasonably defined, interactions are relatively stable, and the overall purpose or function of the components can be clearly identified.
>

## Ontology

> How should we think about the Concept of System?
> 

> Systems are **epistemic tools**, not ontologically primary entities. Reality itself consists of entities, interactions, and processes; the system is a **highly effective abstraction** that allows us to perceive order, model behavior, and intervene effectively.
> 

> It is **universal in concept** but **particular in instantiation**: many concrete entities can be modeled as systems, but no single one is the “system itself.”
> 

> A **system is a filter of reality**: a conceptual lens that selects, organizes, and emphasizes certain entities, relationships, and processes while ignoring others. It does not exist as a **thing in itself**, but as an abstraction that allows us to focus on patterns, structure, and dynamics within a chosen subset of the world.
>

> How should we model a complex system—by examining the particular behaviors of individual components, or by abstracting to general patterns across many cases?
> 

> **Instance-Based Level Modelling** refers to the practice of understanding complex systems by grounding analysis in the concrete behaviors and properties of individual entities, emphasizing particularity and emergence over abstraction and generalization.
> 

> **Aggregate-Level Modelling** refers to the representation of complex systems through generalized, collective properties, privileging abstraction, statistical regularity, and top-down explanation over the particularity of individual entities or interactions.
> 

> **Note:** Once an aggregate-level model is constructed and the **system’s statistical profile** is established, we can say that a typical or **exemplar instance is expected to exhibit certain properties**. However, this does not eliminate variation—individual instances may still deviate significantly from the average due to contextual or stochastic factors.
> 

> *Instance-level thinking asks, ‘What happened this time?’ Aggregate thinking asks, ‘What happens on average?’*
> 

> *You don’t understand a drug by studying one patient, just as you don’t understand a lottery by studying one draw.*
> 

> **Note:** *"Probability" here refers to **inherent randomness** (aleatory uncertainty) or **epistemic uncertainty** (lack of knowledge), depending on the system.*

### State

> The **state of a system** refers to a specific condition or configuration of the system's variables or parameters at a given moment in time.
> 

> **State Space**: The set of all possible states that the system can occupy.
> 

> **Phase space** is a multidimensional space in which all possible states of a system are represented, with each dimension corresponding to one of the system's variables, such as position and momentum.
> 

> **Absorbing States** States from which the system cannot leave once entered.
> 

> See more in [State](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/state.md).
> 

### State Descriptor

> A **state descriptor** is a (possibly many-to-one) mapping $d: \mathcal{S} \rightarrow \mathcal{Y}$  from the system’s state space $\mathcal{S}$ to a representation space $\mathcal{Y}$, such that $d(s)$ encodes selected, possibly lossy, structural or functional properties of the underlying state $s \in \mathcal{S}$ without constituting a generative coordinate of the system’s phase space dynamics; equivalently, it is an epistemic projection or feature functional over state space used to summarize, classify, compare, or control states with respect to analytically or normatively relevant criteria.
> 

> See more in [State Descriptor](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/general/state-descriptor.md).

### Dynamics

> Dynamics describe **how a system evolves over time**, including the rules, forces, or processes that govern transitions between states.
> 

| **Aspect** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Deterministic dynamics** | The system’s next state is fully determined by its current state and rules. |
| **Stochastic dynamics** | Transitions between states are probabilistic, incorporating aleatory or epistemic uncertainty. |
| **Time-dependence** | Dynamics may be **continuous** (smooth changes over time) or **discrete** (stepwise changes). |
| **Feedback loops** | Outputs of the system can influence its own future behavior, leading to positive or negative reinforcement. |
| **Equilibria** | States or configurations where the system tends to settle, including stable, unstable, or metastable states. |
| **Emergent behavior** | Complex patterns arising from simple interactions among components, not predictable from individual parts alone. |
| **Path-dependence** | The system’s trajectory depends on its history, not just its current state. |

## Limits

> Which are the limits of the idea of a ‘System’?
> 

| **Limit Category** | **Challenge** | **Implication** | **Case(s)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Boundaries & Scope** | Defining what is inside vs. outside the system | Boundaries are arbitrary; changing them changes system behavior | Modeling a city transport network might ignore regional migration flows |
| **Complexity** | Systems have many interacting components | Beyond a certain point, interactions are intractable or unpredictable | Ecosystems with nonlinear feedback are hard to predict precisely |
| **Observer Dependence** | System definition depends on observer perspective | A system is a construct, not an absolute entity | Economists see “economy” as agents and markets; ecologists see “ecosystem” |
| **Dynamics & Change** | Systems evolve over time | Static models cannot capture emergent behavior or transitions | Company org chart fails to reflect informal networks or culture shifts |
| **Reductionism vs. Holism** | Focus can be on parts or whole | Neither alone fully explains system; trade-off between detail and context | Knowing all genes doesn’t predict organismal behavior |
| **Modeling & Representation** | Systems require models | Models simplify reality; cannot fully capture it | Climate models capture trends but not every local weather event |
| **Uncertainty & Openness** | Systems interact with environment | External influences limit predictability and control | Financial markets affected by unpredictable geopolitical events |
| **Ethical & Epistemic Limits** | System perspective influences interventions | Oversimplification may ignore human values, norms, and agency | Treating a community solely as a “resource network” may overlook rights |

> 

> System - A structured set of interconnected components with defined boundaries and interactions, used to **explain, model, predict, or control phenomena**.

### **What other effective abstractions exist for organizing reality?**

> – For instance, categories, networks, fields, processes, patterns, … — how do these compare to “systems”?
> 

| **Abstraction Type** | **Definition** | **Comparison to Systems** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Categories (Classes)** | Grouping entities based on shared properties or features. | Simpler than systems; focus on **static classification**, not interactions or dynamics. Useful for taxonomies, ontologies, or sorting phenomena. |
| **Networks (Graphs)** | Entities (nodes) connected by relationships (edges). | Emphasizes **interconnections**. Systems are often representable as networks, but networks may not include dynamics, boundaries, or functions explicitly. |
| **Fields (Continua)** | Continuous spaces or regions with properties defined at each point (e.g., electromagnetic fields, temperature fields). | Focused on **gradients and distributions**. Systems may exist within fields, but fields abstract over discrete components. |
| **Processes (Flows)** | Sequences of events or transformations over time. | Focused on **temporal dynamics**. Systems often embed processes but also include structure, boundaries, and interactions beyond just sequences. |
| **Patterns (Regularities)** | Recurring forms, structures, or behaviors. | Often **emergent from systems**, but can be abstracted independently. Patterns reveal **higher-level regularities** without specifying components explicitly. |

### Is Systems Thinking a Scam?

> So ‘systematics’ or ‘systems thinking’ may be the wrong label, because forcing reality into a system without clear criteria can lead to distorted or superficial understanding.
> 

> Labeling something as a system without clear criteria or rigorous delimitation risks creating superficial or misleading understanding rather than genuine insight.
> 

### When people refer to the ‘Economic System,’ are they referring to a set of ideals or the economic reality?

> People usually refer to a **set of ideas or conceptual models** rather than economic reality itself. However, in some contexts, the term can also denote the actual functioning of the economy.
