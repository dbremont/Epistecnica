# Monism

Related: [Ontology](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/general/ontology.md), [Spinoza’s Method](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/method/spinoza-method.md), [Dualism](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/practice/general/dualism.md)

> In philosophical terms, **monism** is the ontological view that reality is fundamentally unified and composed of one substance or principle. It opposes **dualism**, which posits two distinct and separate substances (e.g., mind and body, or the material and the immaterial). Monism asserts that everything that exists can ultimately be reduced to a single essence or underlying substance. This view can be further categorized into different types, depending on the nature of the substance or principle proposed.
> 

## Taxonomy

| **Type of Monism** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Material Monism (Physicalism)** | All phenomena, including mental phenomena (e.g., consciousness), are reducible to physical processes. |
| **Idealist Monism** | Reality is fundamentally mental or spiritual, with the physical world being either an illusion or a manifestation of a deeper, non-material consciousness or thought. |
| **Neutral Monism** | The fundamental substance of reality is neither purely material nor purely mental, but something more fundamental that can give rise to both. |
| **Substance Monism** | All reality consists of one kind of substance, which is singular and indivisible. |

## List of Conceptual Adaptations

- **Spinoza's Substance Monism**: Spinoza’s philosophy asserts that there is only one substance, which he identified as God or Nature. All things in the universe, including human beings, are merely modes or attributes of this single substance.
- **Berkeley's Idealism**: Berkeley posited that only minds and ideas exist, and that physical objects are merely ideas in the mind of God or in the minds of individuals.
- **Russell's Neutral Monism**: Bertrand Russell proposed that both physical and mental phenomena are manifestations of a more basic "neutral" substance.

## Why Monism?

- **Mind-Body Problem**: Monism challenges dualism’s separation of mind and body. While dualists argue that mental and physical realms are distinct, monists seek to explain consciousness as an aspect of a single substance.
    - **Physicalist Monism**: Attempts to explain mental phenomena as reducible to physical brain states.
    - **Idealist Monism**: Proposes that all physical phenomena are manifestations of an underlying mental or spiritual reality.
- **Ontological Economy**: Monism aims to simplify the ontology of the universe by positing a single substance or principle, thereby avoiding the complexities and divisions introduced by dualism. This is often aligned with **Occam's Razor**, which favors simpler, unified explanations over complex, multiple entities.
- **The Problem of Universals**: Monism often addresses the relationship between individual objects and universal properties. For instance, in idealist forms of monism, all particular instances of experience may be seen as expressions or modes of a single underlying essence.
