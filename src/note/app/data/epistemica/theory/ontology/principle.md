# Principle

Related: [Property](note.html?n=epistemica/theory/ontology/property.md)

> In this note let’s explore the term `Principle`.
> 

> 
> 
> 
> `Principle` designates something that is fundamental or prior—a starting point, basis, or origin. In `practice`, however, `principles` can have different degrees of fundamentality. “First principle” therefore marks a principle with the highest relevant priority: a principle prior to other principles.
> 

## Origin

> *principium* ← *princeps* = **first, foremost**
> 

> Aristotle's concept of **ἀρχή (*archē*)** means something like **origin, beginning, first point, or governing principle**. A *first principle* (*archē*) is therefore a point of explanation that is **not itself derived from a prior explanatory principle within the relevant system**.
> 

> **Principle:** a fundamental basis.
> 

> **First principle:** a fundamental basis that serves as a starting point for deriving or explaining something else.
> 

## Typology

> To develop a deeper understanding of principles, they should be differentiated according to the fundamental dimensions of human practice: **the epistemic and the technical**.
> 

### Epistemic Practice

> It is a proposition or basis from which other propositions can be explained, justified, or derived.
> 

### Technical Practice

> In technical practice, a first principle is a fundamental basis from which the organization or operation of a technical system is determined.
> 

## Principle Catalog

> **Philosophical principles** are the most abstract principles: they fundamentally ground our epistemology, support logical inference, provide the basic structure of reality, and even underpin normative frameworks.
> 

| Category | Principle | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logic and Reasoning | Principle of Non-Contradiction (PNC) | A statement cannot be both true and false at the same time in the same respect. Formally: ¬(P ∧ ¬P) |
| Logic and Reasoning | Principle of Identity | Each thing is identical to itself. Formally: ∀x (x = x) |
| Logic and Reasoning | Principle of the Excluded Middle | Every proposition is either true or false; no middle option exists. Formally: P ∨ ¬P |
| Logic and Reasoning | Principle of Sufficient Reason (Leibniz) | Everything must have a reason or cause. |
| Logic and Reasoning | Principle of Parsimony / Ockham’s Razor | Do not multiply entities beyond necessity; prefer simpler explanations. |
| Metaphysics and Ontology | Principle of Causality | Every effect has a cause; events are connected in a causal chain. |
| Metaphysics and Ontology | Principle of Continuity | Changes in reality occur gradually, not in sudden leaps (classical version). |
| Metaphysics and Ontology | Principle of Persistence / Identity over Time | Objects persist as the same entity through time under certain conditions. |
| Metaphysics and Ontology | Principle of Potentiality and Actuality (Aristotle) | Distinguishes what a thing can become vs. what it actually is. |
| Metaphysics and Ontology | Principle of Individuation | What makes an entity uniquely itself and distinct from others. |
| Epistemology / Knowledge | Principle of Empiricism | Knowledge arises from sensory experience. |
| Epistemology / Knowledge | Principle of Rationalism | Knowledge arises from reason and innate ideas. |
| Epistemology / Knowledge | Principle of Fallibilism | No belief is beyond potential revision; all knowledge is provisional. |
| Epistemology / Knowledge | Principle of Coherence | Truth of a proposition depends on its consistency with other beliefs. |
| Epistemology / Knowledge | Principle of Correspondence | Truth corresponds to reality; propositions must match facts. |
| Epistemology / Knowledge | Principle of Evidential Support | Beliefs should be proportional to available evidence. |
| Ethics / Action | Principle of Autonomy | Rational agents should self-govern. |
| Ethics / Action | Principle of Non-Maleficence | Do not harm others. |
| Ethics / Action | Principle of Beneficence | Act to benefit others. |
| Ethics / Action | Principle of Justice / Fairness | Equal treatment and distribution of benefits/burdens. |
| Ethics / Action | Golden Rule / Principle of Reciprocity | Treat others as you wish to be treated. |
