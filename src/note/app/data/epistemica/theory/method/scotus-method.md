# Scotus's Method

> John Duns Scotus’ philosophical system is a rigorous scholastic metaphysics centered on the univocity of being, the formal distinction, and haecceity as the principle of individuation, emphasizing God's absolute freedom and a nuanced synthesis of faith and reason.

## ❓ QA

### What is *haecceity* and why is it important?

> *Haecceity* (thisness) is the principle that individuates an entity beyond its essence, making it this particular individual rather than any other. It is central to Scotus’ account of individuality.
> 

### How does Scotus differ from Aquinas metaphysically?

> Scotus emphasizes the univocity of being (one concept applies equally to God and creatures), while Aquinas stresses analogy of being. Scotus also introduces formal distinctions and haecceity to explain individuation, which Aquinas handles differently.
> 

### What is the formal distinction?

> A middle ground between purely conceptual and real distinctions; it is a real distinction between aspects that are inseparable in reality but can be conceptually distinguished.
