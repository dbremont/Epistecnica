## Second-order Cybernetics

> **Second-order cybernetics** is the study of *systems that observe and are observed by themselves*. It goes beyond traditional cybernetics, which focuses on the regulation and control of systems (such as machines or organizations) by external feedback. Second-order cybernetics introduces the idea that **the observer is part of the system** being observed, and that the system cannot be fully understood without considering the role of the observer in its construction and behavior.
> 

> **Second-order cybernetics** is the study of systems that include the observer within their framework, emphasizing **self-regulation**, **self-observation**, and **feedback loops**. It seeks to understand not only the system's behavior but also how the system and the observer co-evolve, with an emphasis on how knowledge is constructed through interaction rather than just discovered.
> 

Key concepts in second-order **cybernetics** include:

1. **Self-reference**: The system is both the subject and the object of observation. The observer and the system are intertwined, and the system’s behavior is shaped by how it perceives itself and the world around it.
2. **Autopoiesis**: Proposed by Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela, autopoiesis describes the ability of a system to create and maintain itself through internal processes. An autopoietic system is self-organizing, self-referential, and autonomous, often in reference to its internal states, behaviors, and feedback loops.
3. **Circular causality**: Second-order cybernetics emphasizes **feedback loops**, where the output of a system is fed back as input, potentially influencing its future states. Unlike linear causality, circular causality reflects the idea that systems are influenced by the interaction of components that do not have a simple cause-and-effect relationship.
4. **Observer-participation**: In second-order cybernetics, the observer is not a passive entity but an active participant in the system’s operation. The way an observer perceives, interacts with, and constructs the system affects both the observer and the system itself. This interaction creates a **co-evolutionary process** between the system and the observer.
5. **Constructivism**: A foundational assumption in second-order cybernetics is that knowledge and reality are **constructed** by the observer. The observer doesn’t simply perceive an external reality but actively contributes to the creation of meaning through their observations, beliefs, and actions.

## Viable System Model (VSM)

> **Viable System Theory** is a model of the organizational structure of any autonomous system capable of producing itself, which describes a system in terms of necessary regulatory and operational components needed to adapt to and survive changes in its environment.
> 

| **System** | **Description** | **Function** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **System 1** | **Operational Units** | The primary units perform the organization's basic operations. Each unit is semi-autonomous and responsible for delivering specific outputs or services. |
| **System 2** | **Coordination** | Ensures the harmonious operation of System 1 units by providing coordination and conflict resolution. Manages the interactions and synergies between operational units. |
| **System 3** | **Control** | Provides resource allocation, monitoring, and control over the operational units. Ensures that the organization is functioning efficiently and effectively. |
| **System 3*** | **Audit** | A subset of System 3 focuses on monitoring and auditing the activities of the operational units to ensure compliance and performance standards. |
| **System 4** | **Intelligence** | Focuses on external environments and future planning. Responsible for adapting the organization to environmental changes, innovation, and strategic development. |
| **System 5** | **Policy** | Sets the organization's overall goals, values, and direction. Ensures a balance between internal and external focus and maintains the organization's identity and cohesion. |
