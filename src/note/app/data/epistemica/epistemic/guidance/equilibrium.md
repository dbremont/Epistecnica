## **🌪️Non-equilibrium**

> **Out-of-equilibrium systems** (also called **non-equilibrium systems**) are physical, chemical, biological, or social systems that are not in **thermodynamic equilibrium**—that is, they do not satisfy the condition where all macroscopic flows (e.g., heat, matter, momentum) and driving forces are balanced or static.
> 

> Out-of-equilibrium systems are crucial in understanding **complex adaptive systems**, **nonlinear dynamics**, and **emergent behavior**. They are often modeled with tools from **non-equilibrium thermodynamics**, **stochastic processes**, and **dynamical systems’ theory**.
> 

> A system that is not in equilibrium and undergoes continuous change.
> 

Key Characteristics:

1. **Energy or Matter Flow:** These systems are typically **open**, exchanging energy and/or matter with their environment.
2. **Time Dependence:** Their macroscopic properties **change over time**, often exhibiting **transient**, **fluctuating**, or **self-organizing** behavior.
3. **Irreversibility:** The dynamics are **irreversible**; they generate entropy and evolve along a time-asymmetric path.
4. **Far-from-equilibrium Regimes:** Some systems are not just slightly perturbed from equilibrium but operate **far from equilibrium**, which allows for **emergent phenomena** like pattern formation, turbulence, or life.

## 📘Case Study

- **Physical systems:** A flame, atmospheric convection, or a stirred fluid.
- **Biological systems:** Living cells, which maintain structure through constant energy input (e.g., ATP metabolism).
- **Chemical systems:** Reaction-diffusion systems like the Belousov–Zhabotinsky reaction.
- **Social systems:** Economies or societies adapting under continuous external and internal pressures.
