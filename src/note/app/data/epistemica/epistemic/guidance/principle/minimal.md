# Minimal Principle

> Mother Nature usually finds the simplest solutions.

> **Note on scope:** Saying that nature tends to find the simplest solutions to problems does not mean that nature optimizes everything. Optimization at the level of a part does not necessarily imply optimization at the level of the whole. In complex systems, redundancy and other forms of apparent inefficiency may be essential for robustness, resilience, and overall system performance.

## Minimal Principle Space

> Which minimal principles can be used to ground explanations in reality?

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| Least Action Principle | Physical trajectories make the action stationary under appropriate conditions. |
| Principle of Stationary Action | Physical systems evolve according to equations derived from the stationarity of an action functional. |
| Principle of Least Constraint | In constrained mechanical systems, motion minimizes a measure of deviation from unconstrained acceleration. |
| Minimum Energy Principle | Under appropriate constraints, stable equilibrium corresponds to a minimum of the relevant energy. |
| Minimum Potential Energy Principle | Equilibrium configurations of appropriate mechanical systems minimize potential energy. |
| Maximum Entropy Principle | The equilibrium statistical description maximizes entropy subject to specified constraints. |
| Minimum Helmholtz Free Energy Principle | At fixed temperature and volume, equilibrium minimizes Helmholtz free energy. |
| Minimum Gibbs Free Energy Principle | At fixed temperature and pressure, equilibrium minimizes Gibbs free energy. |
| Principle of Increasing Entropy | The total entropy of an isolated system does not decrease in spontaneous processes. |
| Principle of Stationary Entropy Production | Near equilibrium, certain systems under fixed constraints exhibit minimum entropy production in the linear regime. |
| Principle of Least Resistance | In some physical contexts, flows preferentially follow paths of lower resistance, subject to the governing dynamics and constraints. |
| Principle of Minimum Dissipation | Certain physical systems settle into states that minimize a suitable dissipation functional under specified constraints. |
| Principle of Locality | Physical interactions are local in the sense specified by the relevant physical theory. |
| Principle of Causality | Physical effects are constrained by causal relations, including the causal structure of spacetime. |
| Principle of Relativity | The laws of physics take the same form in the reference frames specified by the relevant theory of relativity. |
| Principle of Equivalence | In general relativity, local gravitational effects are equivalent to those of acceleration under specified conditions. |
| Principle of Symmetry | Physical laws or systems exhibit invariance under specified transformations. |
| Principle of Least Symmetry Breaking | In some contexts, symmetry breaking selects among previously equivalent configurations; this is not a universal minimum principle. |
| Principle of Conservation | Certain physical quantities remain constant under specified conditions, often as a consequence of symmetries or dynamical laws. |
| Principle of Energy Conservation | The total energy of an isolated system is conserved under the applicable physical laws. |
| Principle of Momentum Conservation | Total momentum is conserved in an isolated system when the relevant symmetries and conditions hold. |
| Principle of Angular Momentum Conservation | Angular momentum is conserved when the system has the appropriate rotational symmetry and isolation conditions. |
| Principle of Correspondence | A newer physical theory recovers the successful predictions of an older theory within the older theory's domain of applicability. |
| Principle of Superposition | In linear systems, combinations of solutions can also be solutions; quantum states also obey superposition rules. |
| Principle of Least Time (Fermat's Principle) | Light follows paths for which optical travel time is stationary under the relevant conditions. |
| Principle of Virtual Work | Mechanical equilibrium can be characterized by vanishing virtual work for all admissible virtual displacements. |
| Principle of Minimum Free Energy | At equilibrium, the appropriate thermodynamic free-energy potential is minimized under the relevant constraints. |
| Principle of Maximum Entropy Production | A proposed principle that certain nonequilibrium systems maximize entropy production under specified constraints; its general validity remains unsettled. |
| Principle of Natural Selection | Heritable variations that affect reproductive success change in frequency across generations. |
| Principle of Competitive Exclusion | Species competing for the same limiting resource cannot generally coexist indefinitely under idealized conditions. |
| Principle of Energy Minimization in Biology | Biological structures may occupy configurations that reduce certain energetic costs, subject to biological constraints; this is not a universal law of life. |
| Principle of Least Effort | Organisms and other systems may favor lower-effort strategies when alternatives achieve comparable outcomes; its scope depends on the system. |
| Principle of Allometry | Biological traits scale with organism size according to characteristic relationships that vary across traits and taxa. |
| Principle of Homeostasis | Biological systems regulate internal variables within viable ranges despite external disturbances. |
| Principle of Self-Organization | Order can emerge from local interactions and system dynamics without centralized external control. |
| Principle of Emergence | System-level properties can arise from interactions among components and may not be straightforwardly attributable to isolated components. |
| Principle of Criticality | Some systems operate near critical points where small changes can produce large-scale responses; this is not universal across systems. |
| Principle of Minimum Entropy Production in Near-Equilibrium Systems | Under particular linear-response conditions, stationary states can minimize entropy production subject to fixed constraints. |
| Principle of Computational Efficiency (Chomsky)| The idea that aspects of language structure and use may reflect constraints favoring computationally efficient operations. Chomsky and collaborators have explored this perspective in relation to the design of the human language faculty, including the Strong Minimalist Thesis and principles of efficient computation.|
| Occam's Razor (Epistemic Practice)| Prefer explanations that make fewer assumptions when they explain the evidence equally well.|
| Principle of Explanatory Parsimony (Epistemic Practice)| Minimize the assumptions required to explain a phenomenon while preserving explanatory adequacy. |
| Principle of Ontological Parsimony (Epistemic Practice)| Avoid positing more kinds of entities than are required by the explanation. |
| Principle of Theoretical Parsimony (Epistemic Practice)| Minimize the complexity of a theory while preserving its relevant explanatory and predictive capabilities. |
| Principle of Methodological Parsimony (Epistemic Practice)| Prefer less complicated investigative procedures when they achieve the required epistemic objectives. |
| Principle of Minimum Commitment (Epistemic Practice)| Adopt the least committal conclusion consistent with the available evidence. |
| Principle of Minimum Description Length (Epistemic Practice)| Minimize the combined description length of a model and the data encoded using it. |
| Principle of Minimum Sufficient Representation (Epistemic Practice)| Seek the smallest representation that retains the information necessary for a specified epistemic task. |
| Principle of Minimum Sufficient Explanation (Epistemic Practice)| Seek the smallest explanatory structure that adequately accounts for the target phenomenon. This is a useful general formulation, rather than a universally established principle under this name. |
| Principle of Minimum Assumption (Epistemic Practice)| Minimize unsupported assumptions introduced in constructing an explanation or inference. |
| Principle of Minimum Information Commitment (Epistemic Practice)| Avoid introducing information or distinctions not warranted by the evidence or required by the task. |
| Principle of Minimum Model Complexity (Epistemic Practice)| Select the least complex model that achieves the required empirical adequacy. |
| Principle of Minimum Sufficient Evidence (Epistemic Practice)| Seek the smallest body of evidence sufficient to support a specified conclusion at the required confidence level. |
| Principle of Minimum Regret in Inquiry (Epistemic Practice)| Select an investigative strategy that minimizes the maximum potential loss associated with an incorrect epistemic decision. |
| Principle of Minimum Divergence (Epistemic Practice)| When revising a probability distribution under new constraints, minimize its divergence from a reference distribution, subject to those constraints. |
| Bayesian Occam Principle (Epistemic Practice)| Bayesian model comparison can favor models that concentrate predictive probability on the observed data rather than distributing it across many unsupported possibilities. |
