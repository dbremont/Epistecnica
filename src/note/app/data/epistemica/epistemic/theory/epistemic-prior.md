# Epistemic Prior

> An epistemic prior (aka inductive bias) is a **modeling practical assumption (not affirmation)** about the underlying segment of reality that **can informs** the selection, solution, and validation of the resulting epistemic artifact, as well as the epistemic tools (representational forms, etc.) used to derive it.
> 

> **Note:** This is term is a new re-used - not related with Bayesian Epistemology, but a useful bridge between practical epistemology and reality.
> 

## Is modeling possible without representational priors, and if not, how must priors be structured to ensure well-posed, identifiable, and generalizable inference?

> Modeling without representational priors is formally ill-posed because the data-to-hypothesis mapping is underdetermined, and therefore any viable modeling framework must embed structured priors—expressed as constraints on hypothesis space, inductive biases over representations, and regularity assumptions on the data-generating process—that jointly ensure existence and stability of solutions (well-posedness), uniqueness or parameter recoverability (identifiability), and controlled out-of-sample error via inductive generalization.
> 

## If the prior is a "practical assumption," does its validity depend entirely on the **success of the artifact** (instrumentalism), or must it still maintain a degree of **isomorphism** with the underlying segment of reality to be considered a "good" prior?

> Both.
>
