# Siméon Denis Poisson

> **Siméon Denis Poisson** (21 June 1781 – 25 April 1840) was a French mathematician and physicist whose name is attached to the Poisson equation, the Poisson bracket, the Poisson distribution, Poisson's ratio, and the Poisson spot. He worked across mechanics, heat, elasticity, electricity, magnetism, and probability.

## Life

Poisson was born in Pithiviers, the son of a former soldier. He entered the École Polytechnique in 1798, where his teachers included Laplace and Lagrange, and he became a professor there in 1806. He also taught at the Faculté des Sciences; in 1812 he was elected to the Académie des Sciences, and in 1837 he was made a peer of France. He died in Sceaux in 1840.

## Mechanics and Analysis

Poisson's *Traité de mécanique* (1811) systematized the analytical mechanics of his teachers. His name is attached to the **Poisson bracket**, the algebraic structure that governs the time evolution of dynamical variables in Hamiltonian mechanics, and to the **Poisson equation** of potential theory,

\[
\nabla^2 \varphi = -\rho,
\]

which generalizes Laplace's equation to a region containing charge or mass. He also introduced the Poisson integral for harmonic functions and made important contributions to the calculus of variations and to the theory of partial differential equations.

## Physics

Poisson applied analysis to heat, elasticity, capillarity, electrostatics, and magnetism. His *Théorie mathématique de la chaleur* (1835) developed the mathematical theory of heat; his work on elasticity introduced **Poisson's ratio**, the ratio of lateral contraction to longitudinal extension in a stretched material; and his *Théorie nouvelle de l'action capillaire* (1831) treated capillarity by molecular forces.

In optics, Poisson was a defender of the particle theory. In 1818, when the Academy's prize on diffraction was judged, Poisson argued that Fresnel's wave theory implied a bright spot at the centre of the shadow of a circular obstacle — a consequence he regarded as absurd. François Arago performed the experiment and found the spot, which is now called the **Poisson spot** (or Arago spot), and the result helped establish the wave theory.

## Probability

Poisson's *Recherches sur la probabilité des jugements en matière criminelle et en matière civile* (1837) applied probability to the decisions of juries and courts. In it he introduced the distribution now called the **Poisson distribution**, the law of rare events, and he gave the phrase "law of large numbers" its name. The Poisson process, the Poisson summation formula, and the Poisson kernel all descend from this body of work.

## Legacy

Poisson's name is one of the most widely distributed in mathematical physics: the Poisson equation, bracket, integral, kernel, distribution, process, ratio, spot, summation, manifold, and algebra. He was a central figure of the French analytical school that carried the work of Laplace, Lagrange, and Fourier into the nineteenth century.
