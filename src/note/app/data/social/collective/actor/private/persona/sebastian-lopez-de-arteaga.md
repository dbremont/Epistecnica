# Sebastián López de Arteaga

> **Sebastián López de Arteaga** (Seville, 15 March 1610 – Mexico City, 1652) was a Spanish Baroque painter who moved to New Spain in 1640 and introduced the naturalist, strongly chiaroscuro Caravaggesque style there. His best-known work is *La incredulidad de santo Tomás* (c. 1643), painted in Mexico City.

## Life

Arteaga was born in Seville, the son of Sebastián López de Arteaga — who died the same year the painter was born — and Inés de los Reyes, who took charge of his upbringing. Nothing is known of his training, but on 19 April 1630 he passed the master's examination of the Sevillian painters' guild. From 1630 to 1638 he ran his own workshop in Seville, where he came to employ three apprentices. In 1638 he moved to Cádiz, and in 1640 he sailed with the fleet of viceroy Diego López Pacheco for New Spain.

The first record of his activity in Mexico, from 1642, concerns his participation in the invention and paintings of the ephemeral arch raised for the reception of viceroy García Sarmiento de Sotomayor, decorated with the fable of Prometheus, to whom the new viceroy was compared. In 1643 he requested appointment as a familiar of the Inquisition and was soon admitted as notary of the tribunal; he painted a *Crucifijo* for the tribunal by his own devotion, now preserved in the Museo de la Basílica de Guadalupe.

He died in 1652 from wounds received in a fight or sword duel. His will of 13 June describes him as «enfermo en la cama de algunas heridas» and declares him culpable; his daughters Feliciana and Dorotea were his universal heirs. Nine days later he dictated a codicil giving further explanation of the affair, which had begun with insulting words he had spoken about the birth of one Juan Manuel, an *aderezador de valonas*, and in which the landscape painter Cristóbal Daza was also involved.

## Work

Arteaga brought the naturalism and strong chiaroscuro of Caravaggism to New Spain. His religious output includes *Los desposorios de la Virgen* (Museo Nacional de Arte, Mexico City), in which Santiago Sebastián detects recollections of Raphael's painting of the same subject in the central figures, though the action is set inside the temple. He also painted portraits, among them that of archbishop Francisco Manso de Zúñiga (Metropolitan Cathedral, Mexico City) and ten portraits of the "Inquisidores Antiguos" commissioned by the Holy Office — a portrait gallery that has not survived.

From the mid-1640s his palette became lighter and he moved away from his initial Caravaggism toward landscape backgrounds and a fuller Baroque manner, in which the influence of Rubens is visible through prints. This is evident in *La caída de los ángeles rebeldes* (Hispanic Society of America, New York), painted on copper, whose group of fallen angels imitates a Lucas Vorsterman engraving after a lost painting by Rubens.
