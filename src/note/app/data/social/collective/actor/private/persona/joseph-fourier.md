# Joseph Fourier

> **Jean-Baptiste Joseph Fourier** (21 March 1768 – 16 May 1830) was a French mathematician and physicist, best known for the *Théorie analytique de la chaleur* (1822), the Fourier series, and the Fourier transform. He showed that a periodic function can be decomposed into a sum of sines and cosines, a result that became the foundation of harmonic analysis and mathematical physics.

## Life

Fourier was born in Auxerre, the son of a tailor. Orphaned young, he was educated by the Benedictines and then at the École Normale and the École Polytechnique in Paris, where he later taught. In 1798 he joined Napoleon's Egyptian expedition as a scientific adviser and served as secretary of the Institut d'Égypte. Returning to France, he was appointed prefect of the department of Isère in 1801, based in Grenoble, where he began the study of heat.

In 1811 he won the Grand Prix of the Institut for his memoir on the propagation of heat, though the committee — which included Lagrange, Laplace, and Legendre — criticized the lack of rigor in his use of trigonometric series. He published the *Théorie analytique de la chaleur* in 1822, became secretary of the Académie des Sciences the same year, and died in Paris in 1830.

## The Analytical Theory of Heat

Fourier's central problem was the conduction of heat in a solid body. He formulated the heat equation, in which the rate of change of temperature at a point is proportional to the Laplacian of the temperature, and he proposed the law of heat conduction — the heat flux is proportional to the temperature gradient — now called Fourier's law:

\[
\mathbf{q} = -k \nabla T.
\]

The mathematical difficulty was that the solutions of the heat equation are trigonometric series. Fourier argued that an arbitrary function on an interval could be expanded as an infinite sum of sines and cosines, with coefficients given by integrals of the function against those sines and cosines. The claim was controversial — Lagrange objected that it was not rigorous — but it proved to be correct under broad conditions, and it became the founding result of Fourier analysis.

## Fourier Series and the Fourier Transform

The **Fourier series** represents a periodic function as

\[
f(x) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( a_n \cos nx + b_n \sin nx \right),
\]

with coefficients obtained by integrating \(f\) against the corresponding cosine or sine. The **Fourier transform** extends the idea to non-periodic functions, replacing the discrete sum by an integral over frequencies. Together they provide a dictionary between a function and its frequency content, and they underlie signal processing, quantum mechanics, crystallography, and the theory of partial differential equations.

## The 1811 Prize and the Priority Question

Fourier's 1811 memoir was awarded the Grand Prix of the Institut, but the committee's report praised the novelty of the work while noting the lack of rigor. The episode is often cited as an example of a result that was accepted for its power before its foundations were secured. Later mathematicians — Dirichlet, Riemann, and Lebesgue — supplied the convergence theory that Fourier had asserted.

## Legacy

Fourier's name is attached to the series, the transform, the integral, the law of heat conduction, the number, and the analysis that all derive from his work. His insistence that a complicated function could be decomposed into simple periodic components changed the way mathematics, physics, and engineering treat signals, fields, and differential equations. He is not to be confused with Charles Fourier, the utopian socialist.
