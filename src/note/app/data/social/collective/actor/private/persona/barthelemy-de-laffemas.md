# Barthélemy de Laffemas

> **Barthélemy de Laffemas** (1545–1612) was a French mercantilist and royal official, one of the earliest advocates of state-sponsored manufactures in France. His *Règlement général pour dresser les manufactures en ce royaume* (1597) proposed a national programme of industrial regulation, protection, and employment.

## Life and work

Laffemas was born into a poor Protestant noble family in the Dauphiné and became a tailor and valet to Henry of Navarre, the future Henry IV. After Henry became king, Laffemas rose to become controller-general of commerce and head of the Commission of Commerce.

He argued that France should develop its own manufactures, especially silk and luxury textiles, in order to reduce imports from Italy and Flanders and to employ the poor. His programme combined protectionist tariffs, quality regulation, guild reform, and the creation of royal manufactures. The Gobelins tapestry works were established in 1603 under this policy.

His influence declined after Henry IV’s assassination in 1610, and he died in poverty. Colbert later drew on the mercantilist programme that Laffemas had helped to define.

## Selected works

- *Règlement général pour dresser les manufactures en ce royaume* (1597).
- *La Commission, édit et partie des mémoires de l’ordre et établissement du commerce général des manufactures* (1601).
