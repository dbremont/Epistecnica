## Definition and Basic Rules

> In this module, we explore the **differential operator** — its definition in terms of the **secant-line limit**, its geometric interpretation as the tangent line, and the derivative of a function at  a point $x_0$, denoted $f'(x_0)$.
> 

> The derivative at $f'(x_0)$ is the slope $m$ of the tangent line (limit of the secant line) at $x_0$,  $f(x_0 + \Delta x) - f(x_0) = m (x_0 + \Delta x - x_0)$ .
> 

> **Note:** Most functions do not have a constant slope — they are not straight lines — meaning $\Delta y \not\propto \Delta x$ globally. Instead, we work with **local slopes**: we examine the behavior near a chosen point $x_0$, compute the slope of the tangent line there, and use it as a local linear approximation. Conceptually, this amounts to partitioning the curve into infinitesimal intervals and approximating each by its tangent line — creating an **infinite collection of local linear models** that together reconstruct the function’s behavior.
> 

> **Note:** The slope represents the **rate of change** of a function. It measures how much $\Delta Y$ changes **per unit of** $\Delta X$, that is, how $\Delta Y$ is distributed across $\Delta X$ at each point of the function.
> 

> **Note:** The concept of a limit captures the idea of approaching a value through infinitely many approximations.
