# Giovanni Battista Beccaria

> **Giovanni Battista Beccaria** (3 October 1716 – 27 December 1781) was an Italian physicist and Benedictine monk, a pioneer of the study of electricity. He investigated atmospheric electricity, corresponded with Benjamin Franklin, and wrote the first systematic Italian treatises on the subject. He is not to be confused with Cesare Beccaria, the criminologist.

## Life

Beccaria was born in Mondovì, in Piedmont, and entered the Benedictine order. He studied at the University of Turin, where he later became professor of physics. His early work was on the measurement of the figure of the Earth and on astronomy, but from the 1750s he turned to electricity.

He corresponded with Benjamin Franklin and became one of the leading European students of the new science. He travelled in Italy and France, demonstrated electrical experiments, and trained a generation of Italian physicists. He died in Turin in 1781.

## Electricity

Beccaria's *Dell'elettricismo artificiale e naturale* (1753) was the first systematic Italian treatise on electricity. It distinguished artificial electricity — the charge produced by friction machines and Leyden jars — from natural electricity, the electricity of the atmosphere and of lightning. He followed Franklin's one-fluid theory and the pointed-conductor experiments, and he designed instruments for detecting and measuring charge.

His later works, *Dell'elettricismo* (1758) and *Elettricismo terrestre e atmosferico* (1775), developed the study of atmospheric electricity. He investigated the electrical state of the atmosphere, the electricity of clouds and rain, and the relation between lightning and atmospheric charge. His measurements and instruments were part of the international program that turned electricity from a cabinet curiosity into a quantitative science.

## Legacy

Beccaria's name is associated with the early experimental study of atmospheric electricity, with the Italian school of eighteenth-century physics, and with the diffusion of Franklin's ideas in Italy. His work prepared the ground for Volta's electrical instruments and for the later science of atmospheric electricity.
