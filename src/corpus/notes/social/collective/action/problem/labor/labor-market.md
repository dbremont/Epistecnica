# Labor Market

> The **labor market** is the arena in which labour is priced, allocated and governed: who works, for what wage, on what contract, and with what security.

## Domain space

- **Employment and unemployment** — the quantity of work: participation, hiring, dismissal, the natural rate.
- **Wages and distribution** — the price of work: minimum wages, wage setting, the labour share.
- **Informality** — work outside the contract and the tax base, and the transition into formality.
- **Skills** — what the workforce can do, and where education ends and the firm begins.
- **Industrial relations** — unions, collective bargaining, and the institutions that set the terms of conflict.
- **Labour mobility** — migration, commuting, and the matching of people to places.
- **Security and protection** — unemployment insurance, severance, and the trade-off between protection and hiring.

## The growth question

Labour is the input a country cannot import at scale, so the growth question is how the market converts people into capability: whether employment is formal enough to be taxed and trained, whether wages track productivity, and whether firms can hire and dismiss without being destroyed by either. A market that cannot formalise keeps its workforce unskilled and its firms small.
