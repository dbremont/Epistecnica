# Business Development Bank of Canada

> **BDC** is Canada's state development bank: a **Crown corporation** created in 1944 and headquartered in Montreal, lending to and advising small and medium enterprises — the segment the chartered banks serve least well — and investing through BDC Capital in venture and growth equity.

## What it is

| Attribute | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Legal form** | Crown corporation, wholly owned by the Government of Canada — a [Statutory Corporation](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/governance/actor/type/statutory-corporation.md) variant |
| **Mandate** | Financing, capital and advisory services for SMEs, with a complementary national purpose |
| **Instruments** | Term loans, working capital, equipment and real-estate financing, subordinated debt, venture and growth equity through BDC Capital, advisory services |
| **Accountability** | Reports to Parliament through the Minister of Innovation, Science and Industry; annual report and corporate plan |

## Why a development bank for SMEs

> The gap it fills is not profitability but **underwriting economics**. A small ticket carries the same fixed cost of assessment as a large one, and a single institution's SME book is too thin to diversify the idiosyncratic risk. A public lender can hold that risk across a portfolio larger than any one bank's SME exposure, and can lend countercyclically — when the commercial banks contract, the development bank is expected to stay.

> The tension is the definition of the form: BDC is expected to be **commercially self-sustaining**, not a subsidy channel, while serving borrowers the market would decline. Its pricing and its loss experience are therefore the interesting numbers, not its volume: a development bank that loses money is a grant programme with a loan ledger; one that only picks winners is a commercial bank with a public charter.

## Reading it as a form

> The Crown corporation is what makes the mandate possible: it can borrow, lend, hold assets, employ on market terms and take equity — none of which a government department can do without a vote and a line item. The price is the arm's-length accountability that every statutory corporation carries.
