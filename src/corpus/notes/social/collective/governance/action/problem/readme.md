# Problem

> This note documents `Problem` as a social element: the intentional structure through which an agency apprehends a gap between conditions and a norm, formulates it, and takes it as the object of commitment. Problematization forms the problem; intention holds it; a goal specifies what closing it would look like.

> A **problem** is the normative gap between the current state of affairs and a desired state, as apprehended by an agent capable of acting on it. The policy-actionable specialization — a problem taken up as warranting a collective course under authority — is worked in [The policy problem](#sec-the-policy-problem); the domains it sits in are indexed in [The problem domains](#sec-the-problem-domains).

> The *art* of formulating the problem — how hard it is to say what the problem is — is worked in [Problematization](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/governance/action/problem/problematization.md).

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**Problem belongs to the `Problem / Problematization` social element type (Cognitive category, `Intention` branch of the agentic spine)** — the apprehended gap between conditions and a norm, and the process of forming it. It sits under `Interaction Unit -> Agency -> Intention`, alongside `Deliberation`, `Decision / Choice`, `Goal`, and `Objective / Target`.

It is distinct from `Goal` (the desired state taken as the intention's object, not the gap itself), from `Policy` (the authorized course answering the problem), and from [The Collective Action Problem](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/governance/action/problem/collective-action-problem.md) (the coordination failure that keeps recognized problems unanswered).

### What is this social instance?

> A problem has three moments, all held inside one intention: conditions are apprehended (problematization), the gap between conditions and a norm is formulated (the problem proper), and the gap is taken as a commitment's object (intention toward a goal). Without an agency that apprehends, there are conditions but no problem; without a norm, there is dissatisfaction but no gap; without a commitment, there is grievance or analysis but no intention.

> Problems are individual or joint: one unit may apprehend a gap alone (`Individual Agency`), or several units may jointly commit to a shared formulation (`Joint Agency`) — a bargaining pair naming the wage gap together, co-signing ministers declaring a deficit. The joint formulation is irreducibly plural: it cannot be recovered from the parties' separate apprehensions.

## The policy problem

> A **policy problem** is a problem taken up as warranting a policy course: it names what the authorized doing shall head toward, and decomposes into mandate, instrument, goal, target, agency, rule, and review.

> Problematization formulates the problem; [Policy](/view.html?p=notes/social/dynamic/action/guidance/policy.md) answers it with a course; the [problem domains](#sec-the-problem-domains) scope in which domain of social reality it sits. See [Problematization](/view.html?p=notes/epistemica/problematization.md) for the process, and [The Collective Action Problem](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/governance/action/problem/collective-action-problem.md) for the coordination failure that keeps recognized problems unanswered.

### What is a policy problem?

> A policy problem is a condition claimed to require collective doing under authority: it states a gap (what is versus what ought to be), attributes it to alterable arrangements rather than fate, and addresses an agency mandated to close it. Without the mandate there is grievance or analysis, not yet a policy problem.

### How do we know we have a policy problem?

> Four tests, all must hold: (1) a gap is stated between conditions and a norm; (2) the gap is attributed to arrangements agents can alter; (3) an agency exists, or can be constituted, with mandate over those arrangements; (4) instruments exist, or can be built, that plausibly close the gap. Failing (2) leaves fate; failing (3) leaves advocacy; failing (4) leaves research.

### When does a problem become policy-actionable?

> Through problematization to agenda to mandate: conditions are apprehended, the gap is formulated and evidenced, the formulation wins collective attention (agenda), an authority adopts it as its course (mandate), and instruments are chosen and tasked. Actionability is therefore not a property of conditions but of the chain linking them to a mandated agency with instruments.

## The problem domains

> What are the domains — or facets of social reality — that are the targets of reflection and evolution?

> How can improvement be achieved? Through which forms of agency can the direction of change be influenced?

> Every policy problem sits in a domain, at a scale, with a tractability and a mandate-holder: which domain of social reality it belongs to (the list below), how severe and wide it runs, whether instruments exist that can close it, and whose mandate binds the response. Scope all four before prescribing the course.

### Domain space (dimension)

1. **Production** – Core economic activity; interventions here increase material output and technological capability.
2. **Infrastructure** – Enables productive capacity and social welfare; investments in energy, transport, digital, and utilities are foundational.
3. **Human Capital** – Skills, education, and technological competence directly affect productivity and innovation.
4. **Monetary & Financial Ecosystems** – Ensure stability, liquidity, and the effective functioning of markets; critical for investment and entrepreneurship.
5. **Public Governance Ecosystem** – Legal, fiscal, and regulatory frameworks enable societal coordination, enforce rules, and ensure accountability.
6. **Research Ecosystem** – Drives innovation and technological progress; both fundamental and applied research are levers for long-term development.
7. **International Ecosystem** – External relations, trade, and collaboration expand opportunities, knowledge flows, and markets.
8. **Urbanization & Spatial Planning** – Organizes population, production, and services efficiently, impacting productivity and quality of life.
9. **Joint Cognition** – Collective knowledge, coordination, and decision-making capabilities of society; underpins strategic alignment and innovation.
10. **Miscellaneous Social Domains** – Welfare, health, and cultural aspects ensure social stability, resilience, and human development.
11. **The State** — Used to disambiguate (State Finance vs. Social/Regional Finance).
12. **Labor Market** – Employment, wages, informality and skills; how the market that prices labour works.
13. **Market Policy** – Competition, consumer protection and market design; keeping markets contestable.
14. **Legal System** – Property, contract and insolvency; the legal order that makes investment enforceable.
15. **Computation Policy** – Connectivity, compute, data and e-government; the digital general-purpose infrastructure.

## Systemic problems

> The cross-cutting problems of the social system itself, which hold across domains:

- [The Collective Action Problem](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/governance/action/problem/collective-action-problem.md) — a group recognizes the possibility of another reality yet cannot coordinate cognition and action toward it.
- [The Agent–Social-Reality Hard Coupling Problem](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/governance/action/problem/agent-social-reality-hard-coupling-problem.md) — agents are tightly coupled to the current state of reality, so their perceptions and behaviours converge on stable attractors that limit transformative agency.
- [The Agent Imagination – «Action Pattern Stability» Problem](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/governance/action/problem/agent-social-reality-reflection-problem.md) — agents have an inherent difficulty imagining alternative futures, and action patterns persist even in their internal states.
