# Market Policy

> **Market policy** is the set of rules that keeps markets contestable: competition, entry, consumer protection, and the regulation of the prices and access of natural monopolies.

## Domain space

- **Competition / antitrust** — mergers, abuse of dominance, cartels; the rules that keep incumbents from closing the door.
- **Consumer protection** — information, safety and redress; the demand side of market quality.
- **Market design** — auctions, procurement markets, matching; the rules that make a market clear.
- **Price and access regulation** — the tariffs and access conditions of natural monopolies: networks, utilities, ports.
- **State aid** — the boundary between public support and distortion.
- **Entry and licensing** — who may open a business, and at what cost.

## The growth question

Growth is largely the entry and survival of better firms. Market policy decides whether a productive entrant can reach customers and inputs, or whether the incumbent's position is protected by licence, tariff or tacit collusion. The failure mode is not "too much market" but a market that is formally open and practically closed.
