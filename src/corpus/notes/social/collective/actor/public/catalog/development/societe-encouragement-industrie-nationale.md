# Société d'encouragement pour l'industrie nationale

> The **Société d'encouragement pour l'industrie nationale** (Paris, **1801**) is the French **national** promotion society — founded with Chaptal, the chemist who was Napoleon's minister of the interior, to encourage invention and industry through premiums, medals and publication. It is the missing rung between the old-regime civic society and the nineteenth-century sectoral society: a **voluntary association with a state's ear**, doing the work a ministry of industry would later take over.

## What it did

| Instrument | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Premiums and medals** | Prizes for inventions, processes and improvements — the RSA's model, applied nationally |
| **Commissions** | Expert committees that examined machines and processes and reported on them; the society as a technical court of judgement |
| **The *Bulletin*** | Its journal, published from the early nineteenth century, carrying the prize questions, the commission reports and the industrial intelligence of the day |
| **Testing and standards** | Comparative trials of machines, fuels and materials — the ancestor of the standards laboratory |
| **Network** | Industrialists, engineers and scientists in one body — the technical elite of French industry organised as a society |

## The *Bulletin* as a primary record

> The society's importance for the corpus is not only institutional but **documentary**: the *Bulletin de la Société d'Encouragement pour l'Industrie Nationale* is digitised in full by the **CNAM** (Conservatoire national des arts et métiers) under the identifier `BSPI`. It is the record of what the society actually did — which premiums were offered and won, which machines were tested and how they performed, which processes were judged worth diffusing — and therefore one of the best sources for the technical history of French industry in the nineteenth century.

> The archive link is in the references below. Read as evidence rather than as decoration, it converts the note from a description of a society into a way of checking what the society thought industry needed.

## In the sequence

> Against the older forms, the 1801 society is **post-revolutionary**: not a chartered corporation of the *ancien régime* with local privileges, but a national association created in the same decade as the state's own technical institutions (the Conservatoire, the École polytechnique). It is the moment the improvement society becomes **national and quasi-public** without becoming a ministry — the arrangement the twentieth-century development agency would inherit in bureaucratic form.
