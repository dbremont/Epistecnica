# Göttingen Academy of Sciences and Humanities

> The **Göttingen Academy of Sciences and Humanities** (Akademie der Wissenschaften zu Göttingen) is a learned society founded in 1751 by King George II of Great Britain, Elector of Hanover, as the Royal Society of Sciences in Göttingen. It is a public-law corporation seated in Göttingen, with elected members and a portfolio of long-term scholarly projects.

## Formulation

### What is this social instance?

> A learned academy of some four hundred members that combines election, publication, prizes, and advice with a distinctive portfolio of long-term research projects — scholarly editions, dictionaries, and catalogues — carried out with public funding. It cooperates closely with the University of Göttingen but is a separate corporation; it is not a university and not a state ministry.

### Is the type of this `social element instance` ambiguous?

Yes, in three readings:

- **Academy vs. university.** The Academy and the University of Göttingen are separate corporations that share a city and cooperate; the Academy does not teach degree programmes.
- **Learned society vs. research-performing institution.** It is both: elected membership confers standing, while its long-term projects make it a research performer.
- **Public-law corporation vs. state ministry.** It is chartered and funded by the public, but self-governing; it is not an administrative department.

The learned-academy reading is primary; the projects are parts of the compound, not a second root.

### Which facet does this element belong to, if applicable?

**Scientific** — research, publication, and the evaluation of knowledge, with a strong **Humanities** component in its editions and dictionaries. Secondary touches: **Normative** (scholarly standards and prizes) and **Political** (advice), neither of which is the dominant mechanism.

### What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this `social element instance`?

Two load-bearing caveats.

**It is not the university.** The Academy and the University of Göttingen share a city and cooperate, but they are separate corporations with different missions: the Academy elects and projects, the University teaches and employs. Reading one as the other misplaces the boundary.

**Public-law status is not state ownership of its science.** The academy is chartered and funded by the public, but its members and board govern its programme. The state funds and receives advice; it does not dictate findings.

**Continuity is institutional, not corporate.** The 1751 Royal Society of Sciences, the nineteenth-century academy, and the present corporation are a lineage; the present body is the heir, not the same corporation.
