# French Academy of Sciences

> The **French Academy of Sciences** (Académie des sciences) is the scientific academy of the Institut de France, founded in Paris in 1666 under Louis XIV and Colbert. Suppressed in 1793, it was re-established in 1795 within the Institut national and has been one of the Institut de France’s five academies since 1803.

## Formulation

### What is this social instance?

> A collegial learned society of some 150 titular members, with foreign associates and correspondents, that recognizes scientific excellence, publishes the *Comptes Rendus*, awards prizes, and issues reports and advice on scientific questions. It is not a research institute and not a university: its work is election, publication, prize-giving, and expert judgment. It is distinct from the Académie française, the language academy, and from the other academies of the Institut de France.

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**The French Academy of Sciences belongs to the `Collective (→ Kind) Scientific Collective` social element type — a collegial learned society constituted of elected members, a bureau and perpetual secretary, statutes, and the publication, prize, and advisory activities through which it organizes scientific judgment.**

It is a **publicly chartered learned society** within the public Institut de France: collegial in constitution, public in status and mission. Its layer is **Ontic** — a corporation with members, premises, meetings, and a publication series. Its facet is **Scientific**: the dominant causal mechanism is the evaluation and articulation of warranted knowledge.

Readable secondarily as an `Interaction Unit` — a body that elects, publishes, awards, and advises under one name — and as an `Institution`: the statutes and the Institut affiliation as stabilized rules. Those readings are prose here, not second roots; the tree below is typed once.

### Is the type of this `social element instance` ambiguous?

Yes, in four readings:

- **Académie des sciences vs. Académie française.** The first is the scientific academy; the second is the language academy. They are sister academies of the Institut de France, not the same body.
- **Learned society vs. research institute.** The Académie des sciences does not run laboratories; its institutes and committees deliberate, publish, and advise.
- **The 1666 academy vs. the 1795 Institut.** The academy was suppressed in 1793 and re-established in 1795 as part of the Institut national; continuity is institutional, not corporate.
- **National academy vs. public authority.** It advises the state and certifies excellence, but it holds no sovereign power and no delegated administrative mandate.

The learned-society reading is primary; the Institut affiliation is a constitutional frame, not a second type.

### Which facet does this element belong to, if applicable?

**Scientific** — the evaluation and articulation of research. Secondary touches: **Normative** (prizes, standards, and scientific reports) and **Political** (advice to government), neither of which is the dominant mechanism.

### What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this `social element instance`?

Two load-bearing caveats.

**A collegial learned society, not a research performer.** The Académie des sciences elects, publishes, awards, and advises; it does not run laboratories or employ researchers. Its authority is the authority of recognized scientific standing, not of an institute.

**Continuity is institutional, not corporate.** The academy was suppressed in 1793 and re-established in 1795 within the Institut national; the present body is the heir of that lineage, not an unbroken corporation. Its 1666 founding date and its 1795 re-founding must be kept distinct.

**It is not the Académie française and not the Institut de France.** The first is the language academy; the second is the umbrella that houses all five academies. Confusing the three is the most common misreading of the name.
