# Russian Academy of Sciences

> The **Russian Academy of Sciences** (Российская академия наук, RAN) is Russia’s national academy and largest research organization, founded in St. Petersburg in 1724 by Peter the Great as the Imperial Academy of Sciences. It became the USSR Academy of Sciences in 1925, took its present name in 1991, and is seated in Moscow.

## Formulation

### What is this social instance?

> A state academy that combines elected membership with a network of research institutes: it elects full, corresponding, and foreign members, coordinates research across its institutes, publishes, awards prizes, and provides expert advice to the state. It is a public/state actor — publicly chartered, funded, and mandated — but self-governing in its membership and scientific councils. The 2013 reform transferred many institutes to a separate federal agency while the Academy retained coordination and expertise.

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**The Russian Academy of Sciences belongs to the `Collective (→ Kind) Scientific Collective` social element type — a state academy constituted of elected members, a presidium and general assembly, statutes, research institutes, and the publication and advisory activities through which it organizes inquiry.**

It is a **public/state** actor: a national academy with a public mandate and state funding, not a ministry. Its layer is **Ontic** — a corporation with members, institutes, premises, and a publication programme. Its facet is **Scientific**: the dominant causal mechanism is the production, coordination, and evaluation of warranted knowledge.

Readable secondarily as an `Interaction Unit` — a body that elects, coordinates, publishes, and advises under one name — and as an `Institution`: the statutes and state status as stabilized rules. Those readings are prose here, not second roots; the tree below is typed once.

### Is the type of this `social element instance` ambiguous?

Yes, in four readings:

- **State academy vs. ministry.** It is publicly chartered and funded, but self-governing in its membership and scientific councils; it is not an administrative department.
- **Russian Academy of Sciences vs. Russian Academy of Natural Sciences.** The latter is a separate non-state academy; the names are easily confused, but the bodies are distinct.
- **Imperial → USSR → Russian Academy.** The 1724 Imperial Academy, the 1925 USSR Academy, and the 1991 Russian Academy are a lineage, not one unbroken corporation.
- **Academy vs. university.** The Academy coordinates research institutes; it is not a teaching university, even where its institutes train researchers.

The state-academy reading is primary; the institutes are parts of the compound, not a second root.

### Which facet does this element belong to, if applicable?

**Scientific** — research, coordination, and the evaluation of knowledge. Secondary touches: **Normative** (expertise and prizes) and **Political** (advice to the state), neither of which is the dominant mechanism.

### What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this `social element instance`?

Two load-bearing caveats.

**A state academy is not a ministry.** The Academy is publicly chartered and funded, but its members and scientific councils govern its programme. The state funds and receives advice; it does not dictate findings. Reading the Academy as an administrative department mistakes the legal form for the epistemic relation.

**The 1925/1991 renamings and the 2013 reform make continuity institutional, not corporate.** The Imperial Academy, the USSR Academy, and the Russian Academy are a lineage; the present body is the heir, not the same corporation. The transfer of institutes to a separate federal agency changed the Academy’s portfolio without erasing its coordinating role.

**It is not the Russian Academy of Natural Sciences.** The similarly named non-state academy is a different body; the two must not be conflated.
