# Amigos del País

> The **Sociedades Económicas de Amigos del País** were the Spanish and Spanish-American network of chartered improvement societies — the Basque original in **1765**, the Madrid society in **1775**, and dozens of imitations across Spain and its empire. They are the [promotion agency](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/actor/public/catalog/development/promotion-agency.md) form in its **network** variant: the same statutes, instruments and membership profile travelling across a whole empire.

## The form

| Dimension | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Legal form** | A chartered *sociedad económica* — a corporation of members under royal protection, not a state body |
| **Membership** | Local notables: clergy, officials, landowners, merchants, the educated professions |
| **Instruments** | Prize questions, reports, schools (especially technical and agricultural), model farms, roads and irrigation advocacy, the periodical press |
| **Function** | "Useful knowledge" for economic improvement — the Enlightenment's applied arm |

## Why the network matters

> The societies were an **explicitly imitated institution**: the Basque statutes were circulated and copied, and the copies are recognisable as copies. That makes the network the cleanest case in the corpus of an **institutional export** — a form designed in one place and reproduced across an empire with its instruments intact, which is the same mechanism by which the modern development agency spread after 1945.

> The colonial instance is already in the corpus: the [Real Sociedad Económica de Amantes de la Patria de Guatemala](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/actor/private/research/gtm/real-sociedad-economica-de-amantes-de-la-patria.md) (1794). Read together, the Spanish and Guatemalan notes are the metropole and the colony of one institutional form.

> The societies declined with the *ancien régime*, and their functions passed to the state — schools, statistical offices, ministries of development — which is the standard fate of the form: it exists where the state's capacity does not, and disappears when the state takes over.
