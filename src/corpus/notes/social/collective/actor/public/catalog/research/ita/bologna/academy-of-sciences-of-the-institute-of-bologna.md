# Academy of Sciences of the Institute of Bologna

> The **Academy of Sciences of the Institute of Bologna** (*Accademia delle Scienze dell'Istituto di Bologna*) is a learned academy in Bologna, descended from the *Accademia degli Inquieti* founded in 1690 and constituted as the scientific class of Luigi Ferdinando Marsili's *Istituto delle Scienze* between 1711 and 1714. It is housed in Palazzo Poggi, the building Marsili obtained for the Institute, and is today associated with the University of Bologna.

> Its peculiarity among European academies is that it was **an academy built around a laboratory**. The Institute gave it an observatory, a physics cabinet, a chemistry laboratory, anatomy rooms and natural-history collections — an academy that not only elected members and published, but performed research in rooms it owned.

## Formulation

### What is this social instance?

> A learned academy constituted of elected members, a presidency and secretariat, statutes approved in 1711, and a building — Palazzo Poggi — holding the rooms, instruments and collections through which it organised inquiry. It elects members, publishes its *Commentarii* and proceedings, awards recognition, teaches through a set of professorial chairs, and advises.
>
> It is not a university: the University of Bologna (*Archiginnasio*) is a separate and older body, and the Institute was founded partly in reaction to its decline. It is not a state ministry: it was chartered under papal patronage and funded by the Senate of Bologna, but governed by its own constitutions. And it is no longer an institute: the *Istituto delle Scienze* was abolished in 1814 and never restored, while the academy survived.

It is a **publicly chartered learned society**: papal in patronage, civic in funding, self-governing in constitution, and — unusually for its century — research-performing in practice. Its layer is **Ontic** — a corporation with members, premises, instruments and a publication series. ### Is the type of this `social element instance` ambiguous?

Yes, in four readings, and each one changes what the note is about.

- **Academy vs. Institute.** The *Istituto delle Scienze* was the institution; the *Accademia delle Scienze* was its scientific class. The Institute was abolished in 1814 and its materials distributed among university laboratories; the academy was suspended in 1804 and reopened in 1829. What exists today is the academy that outlived the body it was created to serve.
- **Learned society vs. research institute.** Most academies of the period elected members and published. This one also ran an observatory, a physics cabinet, a chemistry laboratory, an anatomy theatre and a natural-history museum, with salaried professors holding chairs. It is a genuine hybrid, and calling it only a learned society would lose the reason it mattered.
- **Three founding dates.** 1690 is the *Accademia degli Inquieti*, founded at the house of the sixteen-year-old Eustachio Manfredi as a private mathematical club. 1711 is the approval of the Institute's constitution and the annexation of the Inquieti to it. 1714 is the opening of the Institute in Palazzo Poggi. Reference works disagree because all three are defensible; the note states what each dates rather than choosing.
- **Papal vs. civic.** Clement XI sponsored the enterprise and pressured the Senate to support it; the Senate bought and funded Palazzo Poggi after resisting the expense; Marsili financed much of it himself and, in 1728, abandoned the project in disgust at that same Senate. Patronage, funding and governance sat with three different actors.

The learned-society reading is primary; the Institute and its rooms are parts of the compound, not a second root.

### Which facet does this element belong to, if applicable?

**Scientific** — research, publication and the evaluation of knowledge. Secondary touches: **Normative** (membership as recognition by election, prizes, standards of evidence) and **Political** (advice to the Papal States, and the negotiation with the Senate that constituted the Institute at all). Neither secondary facet is the dominant mechanism.

## History

| Year | Event | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1690 | *Accademia degli Inquieti* founded at Eustachio Manfredi's house | The academy's ancestor: a private mathematical club of students reacting against a stagnant Studium |
| 1694 | The Inquieti move to Jacopo Sandri's house | Anatomy and medicine join mathematics in the programme |
| 1704 | President and secretary appointed | Informal club becomes a body with offices |
| 1705 | The Inquieti move to Marsili's palazzo | The future founder becomes the host |
| 1711 | Constitution of the Institute approved, 12 December; the Inquieti annexed | The academy takes its present name and purpose |
| 1712 | Marsili donates his museum; the Senate buys Palazzo Poggi | The collection and the building that make the Institute possible |
| 1714 | The Institute opens; five professorial chairs | The academy becomes a teaching and research institution |
| 1723–1728 | Conflict between Marsili and the Senate | The founder's terms are unmet; he withdraws in 1728 and dies in 1730 |
| 1731 | First volume of the *Commentarii* | The academy begins publishing its own proceedings |
| 1732 | Laura Bassi receives her degree and joins the Institute | One of the first women in Europe to hold a university post, presenting annual papers |
| 1728 | Bartolomeo Beccari isolates gluten from wheat | The Institute's chemistry produces a result still taught today |
| 1741–1758 | Benedict XIV's reforms | Instruments from London and the Netherlands, the library, the physics cabinet, the obstetrics school |
| 1742 | Ercole Lelli commissioned to make anatomical waxes | The anatomy rooms become a European attraction |
| 1745 | *Gabinetto di Fisica* opened | Physics teaching and experiment in a purpose-built room |
| 1758 | School of obstetrics established | The Institute trains practitioners, not only natural philosophers |
| 1791 | Galvani publishes *De viribus electricitatis* | The academy's scientific peak, at the end of the century |
| 1802–1803 | The Accademia Clementina is dissolved | The science academy is left alone in Palazzo Poggi |
| 1804 | The academy is suspended | Napoleonic reorganisation interrupts the institution |
| 1814 | The Institute of Sciences is abolished | Its materials are dispersed; only the academy continues |
| 1829 | The academy reopens | Restoration under papal authority |
| 1883 | "Royal" added to the name | Absorbed into the nomenclature of the Kingdom of Italy |
| 1945 | "Royal" dropped | The post-war republic's naming |
| Today | Academy in Palazzo Poggi, associated with the University of Bologna | The Palazzo Poggi Museum occupies the upper floor and holds the Institute's instruments |
