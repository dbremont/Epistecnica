# Netherlands Court of Audit

> The **Netherlands Court of Audit** (*Algemene Rekenkamer*) is the Dutch **supreme audit institution**: a High Council of State, constitutionally independent of the government, which audits the central government's finances and the effectiveness of its policy and reports to the States General. It is the [audit](/view.html?p=notes/social/action/activity/audit.md) type in its strongest institutional form — tenured, with statutory access, and publishing to parliament rather than to the minister it examines.

## What it is

| Dimension | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Position** | A **High Council of State**, alongside the Council of State and the National Ombudsman — constitutionally independent of the government |
| **Mandate** | Audits the legality and regularity of central government expenditure, and the effectiveness and efficiency of policy; reports the state's accounts to the States General |
| **Powers** | Statutory access to government documents and records; the right to publish its findings independently |
| **Membership** | Appointed for a fixed tenure, on the model of judicial independence rather than ministerial appointment |
| **Output** | Audit reports, the annual accountability of the state's finances, and policy studies that the House of Representatives uses in its scrutiny |

## The two audits

> The institution runs the two audit types the corpus distinguishes, and keeps them apart. The **regularity audit** asks whether the money was spent as the law allows and whether the accounts are faithful — the classic audit function, and the one that produces the annual declaration. The **performance audit** asks whether the policy worked, and it is closer to evaluation research: it selects a programme, reconstructs its objectives, gathers evidence on outcomes, and reports a judgement about effectiveness.

> Keeping them apart is the design point. A government can pass the first and fail the second completely — every euro lawful and the policy useless — and an institution that reported only one would certify the wrong thing. The Dutch arrangement reports both, to a recipient that can act on either.

## The independence parameters

> Read against the [audit note's](/view.html?p=notes/social/action/activity/audit.md) four elements, the Dutch case is instructive because it hard-wires all of them: the **tenure** removes the auditor from ministerial removal; the **statutory access** makes the account testable; the **publication right** prevents suppression; and the **recipient** is the States General, which is the body the government answers to. It is the combination, not any single feature, that makes the institution an external check rather than an internal control.

## In the Dutch state

> The [Dutch state](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/actor/public/polity/nld/state.md) note classifies it twice — among the High Councils of State and among the statutory bodies — which is accurate: it is constitutionally a High Council and functionally an audit institution. The classification is the useful part of the case: the Netherlands puts its auditor at the same constitutional level as its legal adviser and its ombudsman, not inside the ministry whose spending it examines.
