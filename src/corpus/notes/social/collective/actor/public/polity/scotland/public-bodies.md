# Public Bodies of the Scottish Government

> The **public bodies of the Scottish Government** are the organisations outside its direct directorates that deliver services, regulate, advise or hold it to account. What separates them is not what they do but **how far they sit from ministers** — a distance fixed by legal form, and legible in three questions: who owns the body, who appoints its board, and who approves its budget.

## The classification

| Form | Separate legal personality | Staff | Funding route | Typical example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Directorate / executive agency** | No — part of the Scottish Administration | Civil servants | Budget from the Scottish Government | An agency delivering a ministerial portfolio |
| **Non-departmental public body (NDPB)** | Yes, usually | Own staff | Grant-in-aid from the sponsor | Scottish Enterprise; the environmental and funding bodies |
| **Public corporation** | Yes | Own staff | Commercial revenue | Scottish Water |
| **Commissioner / ombudsman body** | Yes | Own staff | Grant-in-aid | The Scottish Public Services Ombudsman |
| **Company owned by the Scottish Ministers** | Yes — a company | Own staff | Share capital, commercial revenue | [Scottish National Investment Bank](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/actor/public/catalog/finance/scottish-national-investment-bank.md) |

> The classification is **legal and constitutional, not functional**. Two bodies doing similar work can sit in different rows, and the row is what decides whether the minister can direct the body, whether its staff are civil servants, and how its money is voted.

## Why the rows matter

> The distance is bought for a reason in each case. An NDPB keeps a service at arm's length from the political cycle; a public corporation can borrow and charge; a commissioner can investigate the government itself, which requires independence from it. The price is always the same: scrutiny moves from instruction to disclosure — a framework document, a corporate plan, annual accounts, and an appearance before a committee.

## The development bank in this landscape

> The Scottish National Investment Bank is a **company** wholly owned by the ministers, not an NDPB. That choice is the argument: a company can take equity, co-invest with private capital and hold an investment board, where a grant-funded body cannot. It is the same trade a [Statutory Corporation](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/actor/public/statutory-corporation.md) makes — powers of a firm, purpose of the state — with the company form chosen to signal commercial intent.
