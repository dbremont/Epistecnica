# Habsburg Monarchy

> The **Habsburg Monarchy** — also called the **Habsburg Empire**, the **Habsburg Realm**, or the **Danube Monarchy** — is the collection of empires, kingdoms, duchies, counties, and other polities ruled by the House of Habsburg, from the dynastic union of 1526 to the dissolution of Austria-Hungary in 1918, ruled from Vienna. From 1804 it was referred to as the Austrian Empire, and from 1867 onward as Austria-Hungary. It is a `Polity` read as a composite monarchy: one ruling house, one army and one foreign policy, over crown lands that kept their own laws, diets, and currencies.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**The Habsburg Monarchy belongs to the `Polity` social element type (Kind of `Political Collective`, Kind of `Collective`, `Social Compound`, Structural category), read as `Composite Structure` → `Empire`** — a ruling center exercising asymmetric authority over multiple peoples, territories, and subordinate political units. Its layer is **Ontic** where it exists extra-mentally (crown lands administered, the court and the army ruling) and **Synontic** where it exists through shared recognition (dynastic loyalty, the crown lands' corporate identities held under one house). Its facet is political: the dominant mechanism is dynastic authority exercised over crown lands within one composite jurisdiction.

Readable secondarily as a `Personal Union` (the crown lands held by one ruler, connected across the Hungarian half of 1867–1918 by little more than a shared monarch, a joint foreign ministry, and a joint army) and as an `Institution` (where the monarchy just is its dynasty and its court chambers). Those readings are prose, not second roots; the tree below is typed once.

It is distinct from the `Holy Roman Empire` — the Habsburgs held its imperial title from 1438 to 1740 and again from 1745 to 1806, but the title and the empire it named are not the monarchy, which was ruled from Vienna and outlived the empire's dissolution in 1806 by more than a century. It is distinct from `State` (a composite monarchy with separate diets and laws is not a single supreme authority within its jurisdiction), from `Nation` (no one people under one authority, but a dynasty over many), and from `Government` (the ministries and the court change, the dynasty persists). It is also distinct from its own late forms: the Austrian Empire of 1804 and the dual Austria-Hungary of 1867 are restructurings of this polity, not separate polities.

### What is this social instance?

> The monarchy is the dynastic union the Habsburgs built across Central Europe: the Hereditary Lands in the Austrian and Alpine core, the Lands of the Bohemian Crown, and the Kingdom of Hungary with Croatia and the Military Frontier — to which, over the centuries, they added Galicia (1772), Bukovina (1774), the Austrian Netherlands (1713–1795), Lombardy–Venetia (1814–1866), and, after 1878, the occupation of Bosnia and Herzegovina.
>
> Lineage: the dynastic union of 1526 (Ferdinand I elected king of Bohemia and of Hungary after the Battle of Mohács) → the early modern composite monarchy, in which each entity was governed according to its own particular customs and junior lines ruled portions of the Hereditary Lands as private apanages → the centralizing reforms of Maria Theresa and Joseph II in the mid to late eighteenth century, many abandoned after resistance → the neo-absolutist attempt after the revolutions of 1848 to make the monarchy a centralized bureaucratic state ruled from Vienna, abandoned after the defeats of 1859 and 1866 → the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867, under which Hungary became an equal sovereign with a personal union and a joint foreign and military policy → the annexation of Bosnia and Herzegovina (1908), governed by the joint Ministry of Finance and belonging to neither half → dissolution in 1918 under the ethnic independence movements after defeat in the First World War.
