# Flanders

> **Flanders** (*Vlaams Gewest*) is the northern region of Belgium: about 6.7 million people across 13,522 km², roughly 58% of the country's population and about 60% of its GDP. It is a federated unit of the Belgian federal state, and its institutional peculiarity is that its **Region and its Community were merged** — a single Flemish Parliament and a single Flemish Government exercise both the territorial competences of a region and the cultural and educational competences of a community.

> A region is not a country, and a profile of one cannot be a shortened country profile: Flanders has no currency, no army, no macroeconomic policy and no independent representation abroad, but it does hold most of what determines productivity — education, research, infrastructure, spatial planning, environment and employment policy.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**Flanders belongs to the `Polity` social element type — `Social Compound` `(→ Part)` `Collective` `(→ Kind)` `Political Collective` `(→ Kind)` `Polity` — and within it to the `Federated Unit` sub-kind: a constituent polity of a federation holding constitutionally guaranteed authority over its own territory.** Its layer is **Ontic** where it exists extra-mentally (territory administered, institutions ruling) and **Synontic** where it exists through shared recognition (a Flemish community that recognises itself as one, and that does not exactly coincide with the territory).

It is not a `State`: Flanders claims no sovereignty and no monopoly of supreme authority — that remains with the Belgian federal state, however contested the boundary. It is not a `Nation`: the Flemish nation extends to Brussels, where the Flemish Community exercises competences but the Flemish Region does not, so nation and region are deliberately non-coextensive. And it is not a `Government`: the Flemish Government is the incumbent administration, while Flanders the polity persists across coalitions.

Readable secondarily as an `Interaction Unit` — a bounded entity that negotiates with the federal state and the other regions — and as an `Institution`, where the merged Community–Region arrangement just is its stabilised governing rules. Those are prose readings, not second trees.

### What is this social instance?

> Flanders fixes *who rules whom, where, over what*: a **territory** (the five provinces of Antwerp, East Flanders, West Flanders, Flemish Brabant and Limburg), a **political community** (the Dutch-speaking population, plus the Flemish institutions in Brussels), a **governing apparatus** (parliament, government, administration), and a **bundle of competences** transferred by six successive state reforms between 1970 and 2014.
>
> The merger is the thing to understand first, because everything else follows from it. Belgium created two kinds of sub-state entity in 1980: **Regions**, which own territory-bound matters (economy, employment, infrastructure, environment, spatial planning), and **Communities**, which own person-bound matters (language, culture, education, welfare). Wallonia and the French Community kept them separate; Flanders merged its Region and its Community into one parliament and one government at once. The result is a polity that can run an industrial policy and a school system from the same cabinet — a coherence no other Belgian entity has.
>
> The asymmetry between nation and territory is the second thing. Brussels is the capital of Flanders and the seat of its parliament and government, but Brussels is a separate region; the Flemish Community operates there, the Flemish Region does not. This is not an administrative detail — it is the constitutional expression of the unresolved question of what Flanders is a polity *of*.

### What is the recursive instance decomposition of this social instance?

> Boundary: the polity as an institutional body — its territory, community, governing organs, administration and constitutional relations. The economic sectors it governs, the firms in them and the research institutions it funds are associated, not parts.
>
> Stopping rule: a row is terminal when it names a governing organ, a constitutional relation or a competence whose own decomposition lives elsewhere.
>
> Identity: Instance Tree Path is the stable identifier of each row; every path is unique. Level of organization: **macro** throughout — a federated unit of a state is a macro-scale polity. Limitation checklist for the segment: **Scale** (the same institution is macro for the region and meso for the federation), **Partial Observability** (competence boundaries are settled in constitutional text and in practice that diverges from it), **Non-Repeatability** (each state reform is a unique political settlement), **Uncertainty** (the direction of further devolution is contested by the governing parties themselves).

## Actor Space

> The actor space of a region is not the same as that of a state: it contains a legislature that legislates on a defined list of matters, an administration that has no macroeconomic instruments, firms that are mostly private and mostly foreign-owned, and research institutions that are formally independent but funded by the region.

## Production Space

> Flanders has a small, open, service-heavy economy with an unusually large chemical and pharmaceutical base for its size. Its productive structure is concentrated in a port complex, a pharma cluster, a machinery and mechatronics base, and a logistics and digital services sector — plus a declining textile industry that still matters regionally in the Kortrijk area.

## Human Training System

> Education is a **Community** competence, which in Flanders means the same parliament that sets industrial policy also runs the schools. That is the practical dividend of the 1980 merger: a region that can align curricula, apprenticeships and research funding with its own economic strategy.
