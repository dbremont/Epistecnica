# Standing Committee of Ways and Means

> The **Standing Committee of Ways and Means** (Swedish: *Bevillningsutskottet*, BevU) was the Swedish Riksdag's taxation committee from 1809 to 1970. Its business was the revenue side of the state: it prepared tax legislation, calculated what each tax would yield, and — once the state's needs had been fixed — proposed how taxation should be raised or lowered to meet them. Where a budget committee decides what the state will spend, a committee of ways and means decides what it will collect, and the second constrains the first.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**The Standing Committee of Ways and Means belongs to the `Public Organization` social element type (Collective / Organization branch), Kind of `Committee`** — a body of members drawn from the Riksdag, charged with preparing a defined slice of its business and reporting back to it.

It is not a `Legislature` — the Riksdag itself is the assembly that decides; this body prepares and recommends. It is not a `Ministry` or an `Agency` — the tax administration belonged to the state's executive machinery, while this committee sat inside the representative assembly and answered only to the chambers. It is not an `Ad Hoc Committee` either: it was a permanent committee of the Riksdag, one of five in 1905 and one of seven in 1923, holding its jurisdiction across sessions while its members rotated.

Readable secondarily as a **fiscal agency of the assembly** — the capacity in which the Riksdag formed its own estimate of what the country could bear, independent of the government's figures — and as the ancestor of the modern `Committee on Taxation`, which took its jurisdiction in 1970. Those readings are prose, not second roots.

### What is this social instance?

> The committee's origin is older than its name and narrower than its later reach. Its roots lie in the **Age of Liberty**, when a special taxation deputation sat in which the four estates were represented and votes were cast estate by estate; through the eighteenth century, temporary committees were appointed for particular taxes, spirits among them. At the **Riksdag of 1800** a committee explicitly called *bevillningsutskott* was appointed for the first time. Under the **Riksdag Act of 1810** it took permanent shape with twelve members from each estate, elected from different parts of the realm — an arrangement that held until the reform of 1866, when the Riksdag of the Estates gave way to a bicameral parliament and the committee came to be elected by the two chambers.
>
> Its competence was statutory and unusually precise. Under § 40 of the Riksdag Act the committee was to prepare the matters referred to it concerning changes to the rules for imposing taxes, **calculate the revenues from the various taxes**, and, once the state's financial needs had been determined, propose how taxation should be **increased or reduced in order to balance the state finances**; it could also propose, in taxation matters, measures it considered equitable and beneficial. Its revenue calculations formed the basis of the **Committee of Supply's** budget report — the fiscal loop in which the spending side could not report without the revenue side's numbers. Since 1809 it had extensive powers of **initiative**, bounded to taxation.
>
> By 1905 it was one of the Riksdag's five permanent committees: under § 37 it had 20 members, half elected by each chamber from among its own members, with alternate members appointed; it chose its own **chairman and vice-chairman**, and a **secretary from among persons who were not members of the Riksdag**. Its work ran in two divisions — the first dealing primarily with **customs and postal matters**, the second with **legislation on spirits, other special taxes, and the general tax** — some matters being taken in plenary session. In 1923 it was one of seven permanent committees, still 20 members, the divisions now split as customs, postal taxes and stamp-duty legislation on one side and **direct taxation** on the other. By 1951 its remit had followed the growth of public finance: it cooperated with the committees handling the state budget and proposed how balance could be reached through taxation, and in a report toward the end of each parliamentary session it calculated the coming fiscal year's taxes — **income and property rates** among them — along with local taxes and the laws and regulations on alcoholic beverages.
>
> Lineage: the taxation deputation of the Age of Liberty → the first committee so named at the Riksdag of 1800 → the estate-based committee of the 1810 Act, twelve from each estate (1809–1866) → the bicameral committee of 20, half per chamber, with its two divisions (1866–1970) → repeated proposals to divide it between two permanent committees, each refused → abolition in **1970** and replacement by the **Committee on Taxation**.
