# Committee

> A **Committee** is a body of members drawn from a larger assembly, charged with preparing matters in a domain and reporting to that assembly: the standing or select group in which the real drafting, evidence-taking, and amendment of a legislature's business happens, and which carries its recommendations back to the chamber that alone can decide. It holds delegated authority, not original authority — and that is exactly what makes it powerful, because the chamber mostly ratifies what its committees have settled.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**Committee belongs to the `Public Organization` social element type (Collective / Organization branch), Kind of `Committee`** — an organization wielding delegated public authority under law, whose specific warrant is a reference from a parent assembly and whose product is a recommendation back to it.

It is not a `Council` — appointed from outside to advise or coordinate, whereas a committee is drawn *from* its parent and holds a defined slice of the parent's business; not a `Commission` — constituted to investigate or regulate with its own standing powers; not a `Task Group` — which dissolves on delivery, whereas a committee's standing form persists across sessions and outlives its members; not a `Board` — which decides an organization's direction rather than preparing a matter for someone else's decision. [Board](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/actor/board.md) already carries this contrast from the other side, and this note gives the category it names its own decomposition.

Readable secondarily as an `Agency` of its parent — the structured capacity through which the chamber weighs a matter before committing to it — and, when it takes evidence, as a `Forum` in which the administration, industry, and the public are made to appear and account. Those readings are prose, not second roots.

The kind is not one thing: **standing**, **select**, **joint**, **conference**, and **ad hoc** committees differ in how they are warranted, how long they live, and whether their members come from one chamber or several — differences that change the body's behaviour without changing what it is.

### What is this social instance?

> The body at work. A standing order or a term of reference constitutes it and fixes its jurisdiction; membership is allocated by the parent — by election, by appointment, or by party proportion — and the chair, deputy, and rapporteur hold the offices that make it function; the referral power determines what comes before it and the initiative power, where it exists, lets it call for matters nobody sent it; hearings, evidence, markup, and deliberation convert an open question into a text; and the report carries that text back to the chamber, with minutes that make the work citable and dissent recordable.
>
> A committee's authority is *derivative and bounded*, and both halves matter. Derivative: it cannot bind anyone outside its reference, and its findings have whatever weight the parent gives them. Bounded: its jurisdiction is a domain — finance, foreign affairs, taxation, the constitution — and a matter outside that domain, however urgent, has to be referred elsewhere. Within the boundaries, though, it is the place where a chamber's collective ignorance is converted into something it can vote on, which is why the composition of committees and the allocation of chairs is itself a political settlement.
>
> Lineage: the ad hoc body convened for one matter and dissolved → the standing committee holding a domain across sessions → the joint and conference forms that reconcile two chambers → and the counter-current, the committee-centric legislature in which the chamber's plenary role shrinks to ratifying what the committees have agreed.
