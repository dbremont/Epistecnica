# Adrien-Marie Legendre

> **Adrien-Marie Legendre** (18 September 1752 – 10 January 1833) was a French mathematician whose work spans number theory, elliptic integrals, geometry, and celestial mechanics. He introduced the Legendre polynomials, the Legendre transformation, and the Legendre symbol, and he was one of the principal architects of the metric system.

## Life

Legendre was born in Paris and educated at the Collège Mazarin. He taught at the École Militaire and later at the École Normale, and in 1783 he was elected to the Académie des Sciences. He won the Berlin Academy prize in 1782 for his work on the trajectory of a projectile, and he served on the commission that established the metric system, including the measurement of the Paris meridian.

Legendre worked across the mathematical sciences: the attraction of ellipsoids, the figure of the Earth, the orbits of comets, the theory of numbers, and the elliptic integrals. He died in Paris in 1833.

## Number Theory

Legendre's *Essai sur la théorie des nombres* (1798) was the first systematic treatise on number theory after Euler. It contains the law of quadratic reciprocity, which Legendre stated and used extensively, though the first complete proof was given by Gauss in 1796. Legendre introduced the Legendre symbol \(\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)\) to express the reciprocity law, and he proved Euler's theorem on the totient function. His conjecture that there are infinitely many primes of the form \(n^2+1\) remains open.

## Elliptic Integrals and Special Functions

Legendre spent decades on elliptic integrals, classifying them into three kinds and reducing them to canonical forms. His *Traité des fonctions elliptiques* (1825–1832) is the summit of the classical theory; the functions that Jacobi and Abel later inverted were the objects of Legendre's integrals. His name is attached to the Legendre polynomials, the Legendre functions, the Legendre equation, the Legendre transformation, and the Legendre duplication formula.

## Geometry, Least Squares, and the Metric System

Legendre's *Éléments de géométrie* (1794) was the standard geometry textbook in France for a century, and it reorganized Euclidean geometry on a clearer logical plan. In 1805 he published the method of least squares in *Nouvelles méthodes pour la détermination des orbites des comètes*, a decade before Gauss's *Theoria motus*; the priority dispute with Gauss is one of the famous disagreements in the history of statistics.

His work on the figure of the Earth and on the Paris meridian was part of the measurement program that produced the metre. He also worked on the attraction of ellipsoids and on the theory of the Earth's rotation.

## Legacy

Legendre's name survives in the Legendre polynomials, the Legendre symbol, the Legendre transformation, the Legendre equation, and the Legendre duplication formula. His *Éléments de géométrie* taught Euclidean geometry to generations of students, and his number theory prepared the ground for Gauss's *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae*. He is a central figure in the French mathematical school that runs from Euler and Lagrange to Gauss and beyond.
