# José Miguel Gambra Gutiérrez

> **José Miguel Gambra Gutiérrez** (born 1950 in Pamplona) is a Spanish philosopher, professor emeritus of logic at the Universidad Complutense de Madrid, and a prominent figure in the traditionalist Catholic and Carlist milieu. He is known for his work on Aristotelian logic, analogy, and the theory of predicates, and for his traditionalist political philosophy, especially *La sociedad tradicional y sus enemigos* (2019). From 2010 to 2021 he was jefe delegado of the branch of the Comunión Tradicionalista loyal to Sixto Enrique de Borbón.

## Life

Gambra was born in Pamplona in 1950, the son of the Carlist philosopher Rafael Gambra Ciudad (1920–2004) and the teacher and novelist María del Carmen "Carmela" Gutiérrez (1921–1984). The family moved to Madrid in the mid-1950s. He studied philosophy at the Universidad Complutense de Madrid, where he completed his doctorate in 1977 with a thesis on the doctrine of analogy in the work of the Dominican theologian Santiago María Ramírez, written under the direction of Leopoldo Eulogio Palacios. The thesis was later revised and published as *La analogía en general* (2002).

Gambra began his teaching career in secondary education: in 1976 he was appointed professor of philosophy at the Instituto Nacional de Bachillerato de Porriño, in Galicia. He subsequently joined the Complutense, where he became professor titular in the 1980s and later catedrático de Lógica; he is now professor emeritus. His academic home has been the Department of Logic and Philosophy of Science, and his teaching and research have concentrated on the history of logic, Aristotle's Organon, the theory of analogy and predication, the philosophy of mathematics, and the theory of argumentation and fallacies. He has also taught at the Universidad Nacional de Educación a Distancia (UNED).

## Academic work

Gambra's philosophical formation was Thomist and Aristotelian. His teacher Leopoldo Eulogio Palacios introduced him to the scholastic tradition of logic, and his early research on Santiago María Ramírez's *De analogia* remained a lifelong point of reference. An earlier synthesis of Ramírez's doctrine is Alejandro del Cura's 1973 article "Sobre la analogía", a useful background to Gambra's dissertation and later book. His published work combines technical history of logic with systematic argument: he has written on Aristotle's theory of predication and categories, on the medieval logic of Walter Burley and Petrus Hispanus, on the analogy of being, on the nature of number, on Hilbert's philosophy of mathematics, and on the distinction between demonstrative and dialectical reasoning.

With Manuel Oriol Salgado he published *Lógica aristotélica* (2008) and *Ejercicios de lógica aristotélica* (2008), teaching texts that present the Aristotelian Organon in the Thomist tradition. He also translated Pierre Thibaud's study of Charles Sanders Peirce's logic (1982), edited a commented Spanish edition of Aristotle's *Categorías* (2016), and prepared a corrected and expanded edition of his father's *Curso elemental de filosofía* (2022). His articles have appeared in *Anuario Filosófico*, *Thémata*, *Tópicos*, *Philosophie Antique*, *Vivarium*, *Revista de Filosofía*, and *Verbo*.

## Traditionalist political thought

Gambra belongs to the Carlist and traditionalist Catholic current that rejects the liberal, parliamentary, and secular state in the name of a Catholic social order, the traditional monarchy, and the historic *fueros*. He was formed in the milieu of his father, Rafael Gambra, and has been active in the Comunión Tradicionalista: he was president of the Círculo Cultural Antonio Molle Lazo in Madrid, presented Archbishop Marcel Lefebvre at a Madrid event in 1980, and from 2010 to 2021 served as jefe delegado of the branch loyal to Sixto Enrique de Borbón (not the separate Comunión Tradicionalista Carlista). He resigned the latter office in 2021 but remained associated with the movement's political secretariat.

His principal political work, *La sociedad tradicional y sus enemigos* (2019), is an introduction to traditionalist political philosophy written from an explicitly confessional standpoint. Its title echoes Karl Popper's *The Open Society and Its Enemies*, but Gambra's target is the liberal-totalitarian alternative itself. The eight chapters move from Aristotle and the paradoxes of modern politics through the common good, the two swords, liberalism as "the root of evil," Catholic liberalism, fatherland and nationalism, corporations and totalitarianism, to the forms of government. Gambra argues that liberalism and totalitarianism are not opposites but share the same revolutionary philosophical premises, and that both reduce the human person to servitude; against them he sets the "traditional society" grounded in the Catholic tradition and the Carlist legacy. The book is a work of advocacy, not a neutral survey, and it has been reviewed chiefly in the traditionalist press.

## Reception

Gambra's logic manuals are teaching texts designed for philosophy students, and his studies on analogy, predication, and the history of logic appear in specialized journals. His political writing has a narrower audience: *La sociedad tradicional y sus enemigos* was reviewed in *Verbo* and *Aportes*, and *Escritos políticos* (2025) was reviewed in *Verbo*. Within Carlist and traditionalist circles he is treated as a master and a continuator of his father's work; outside them, his political philosophy occupies a marginal position in contemporary Spanish academic life. His technical reputation as a logician and the confessional character of his political writing belong to different registers.
