# François Pierre Guillaume Guizot

> **François Guizot** (Nîmes, 1787 — Val-Richer, 1874) was a historian who governed and a statesman who wrote history. His *Histoire générale de la civilisation en Europe* (1828) gave the nineteenth century its idea of civilisation as a **movement** rather than a state — "the development of the human mind and of society" — and his decade as the effective head of the July Monarchy (1840–47) made him the most visible defender of the property-based franchise that the revolution of 1848 destroyed.

> Guizot is the corpus's clearest case of a doctrine read through its author: liberal government as the rule of *capacity*, not of numbers. "The sovereignty of reason" against "the sovereignty of the people" is the formula for which he was carried out of office.

## Career

| Date | Position | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1812 | *Histoire des origines du gouvernement représentatif* | His first statement of representative government as a reasoned institution |
| 1826 | *Histoire de la révolution d'Angleterre* | Reads the English revolution as a contest between liberty and legitimacy — the frame he later applied to France |
| 1828 | *Histoire générale de la civilisation en Europe* | The work that made "civilisation" a historical concept |
| 1830 | Supports the July Revolution | The Doctrinaire group accepts Louis-Philippe as the legitimate outcome of 1830 |
| 1832–37 | **Minister of Public Instruction** | The **Guizot law** of 28 June 1833: every commune must maintain a primary school, every department a normal school — the founding statute of French public primary education |
| 1840–47 | **Minister of Foreign Affairs and de facto head of government** | The Entente with Britain; the *pays légal* consolidated around the propertied franchise |
| 1847 | *« Enrichissez-vous »* | The speech to the Chamber that fixed him as the defender of the property qualification and the target of the banquets campaign |
| Feb 1848 | Dismissed; the revolution | The Second Republic ends the July Monarchy; Guizot flees to England |
| 1849–74 | Retirement at Val-Richer | *Mémoires pour servir à l'histoire de mon temps* (1858–67); still politically active — he helped bring down Thiers in May 1873 |

## Ideas

**Doctrinaire liberalism.** Sovereignty belongs to *reason*, not to *number*. A government must be both **legitimate** and **capable**, and the franchise is the exercise of a capacity that only the propertied and educated possess. Hence the divide he is remembered for: the **`pays légal`** (the country that votes — some 250,000 men under the July Monarchy) against the **`pays réel`** (the country that does not). Tocqueville, on the other side, called the same arrangement a closed caste; 1848 settled the argument by force.

**Civilisation as process.** His histories read institutions as the sediment of ideas, and nations as moving at different distances from the frontier of mind and technique. It is the frame the corpus uses when it asks whether wealth is durable or circumstantial: the note *¿Fue Latinoamérica Siempre Pobre?* opens with exactly that distinction between a wealth that is built by learning and a wealth that is drawn from a resource.

**Legitimacy and revolution.** His account of the English revolution is the argument that a revolution is justified when it restores a violated legitimacy, not when it manufactures a new one — the Doctrinaire position that let him accept 1830 and reject 1848.

## Relations

| Person | Relation | What it produced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mme de Staël** | Patron and salon | Guizot entered public life through her circle as a young Protestant from Geneva |
| **Gibbon** | Read | The model for a history of civilisation written as a narrative of institutions |
| **Augustin Thierry** | Collaborator, then rival | The liberal historians of the Restoration divided between Guizot's institutional reading and Thierry's narrative of the conquered |
| **Adolphe Thiers** | Rival, then adversary | Opposed him in 1840 and replaced him in 1840; Guizot helped unseat Thiers in 1873 |
| **Alexis de Tocqueville** | Adversary of the doctrine | *Democracy in America* is the standing rebuttal of the *pays légal*: the capacity argument cannot survive universal suffrage |
| **Henry Thomas Buckle** | Reader | Carried Guizot's civilisation-as-process into English positivism |
| **John Stuart Mill** | Reader | Cited him in the liberal case for representative government he himself qualified |
