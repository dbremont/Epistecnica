# Ronald Fisher

> **Ronald Aylmer Fisher** (1890–1962) — statistician, geneticist and evolutionary biologist, and the single most consequential figure in the formation of modern statistical inference. He worked at Rothamsted Experimental Station (1919–1933), where the design of agricultural experiments gave him the problems that produced the analysis of variance; at University College London (1933–1943); at Cambridge (1943–1957); and finally at Adelaide.

> Fisher's achievement was to make statistics a **theory of estimation and inference** rather than a collection of curve-fitting recipes: likelihood, sufficiency, efficiency, information, the analysis of variance, the design of experiments, and the foundations of quantitative genetics all carry his mark. He is also a contested figure: his advocacy of eugenics and his role in the heredity-versus-environment disputes are part of the record, not an embarrassment to be edited out of it.

### What is this social instance?

> The statistician who turned the design of an experiment into part of the inference, and who insisted that an estimate is a statistic with a sampling distribution rather than a number to be reported. The institutional trail — Rothamsted, the Galton Laboratory, Cambridge, Adelaide — is the trail of the discipline's own formation.

### Works set

| Work | Year | What it established |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “The correlation between relatives on the supposition of Mendelian inheritance” (*Transactions of the Royal Society of Edinburgh*, 52, 399–433) | 1918 | Quantitative genetics: Mendelian inheritance reconciled with continuous variation |
| [“On the mathematical foundations of theoretical statistics”](/view.html?p=documents/papers/fisher-1922-mathematical-foundations-theoretical-statistics.md) (*Philosophical Transactions A*, 222, 309–368) | 1922 | Consistency, efficiency, sufficiency, and maximum likelihood — modern theoretical statistics |
| “On a distribution yielding the error functions of several well known statistics” | 1924 | The distribution that became the F distribution; the analysis of variance |
| *Statistical Methods for Research Workers* (Oliver & Boyd) | 1925 | The practical manual that carried the methods into every laboratory |
| “Theory of statistical estimation” (*Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society*, 22, 700–725) | 1925 | Efficiency and the information bound, stated for estimation |
| *The Genetical Theory of Natural Selection* (Clarendon Press) | 1930 | Natural selection formalised; the fundamental theorem; the evolution of sex and mimicry |
| *The Design of Experiments* (Oliver & Boyd) | 1935 | Randomisation, replication, blocking, factorial design — the experiment as an inferential object |
| “The use of multiple measurements in taxonomic problems” (*Annals of Eugenics*, 7, 179–188) | 1936 | Linear discriminant analysis |
| *Contributions to Mathematical Statistics* (Wiley) | 1950 | The collected papers, with Fisher's own retrospective framing |

> The 1922 paper is the hinge of the set, and the [Uusipaikka (2008)](/view.html?p=documents/papers/uusipaikka-2008-what-did-fisher-mean-estimate.md) reading is the corpus's guide to what Fisher meant by “estimate” in it: not a point value, but the construction of likelihood intervals from the likelihood function.
