# Alexis de Tocqueville

> **Alexis de Tocqueville** (1805–1859) was the French aristocrat who made democracy an object of observation rather than opinion. Sent to America in 1831 to report on its prisons, he came back with *Democracy in America* — the first sustained attempt to describe what happens to liberty, administration, and mores when equality of conditions becomes the generative fact of a society. He spent the rest of his life applying the lesson to France, as a deputy, a minister of the Second Republic, and finally as its historian.

## Formulation

### What is this social instance?

> This instance is the person Tocqueville as a participant in social reality: an individual whose inherited standing, his inquiry into another polity, his parliamentary practice, and his books together make him a single actor in the political life of nineteenth-century France — the observer who was also a participant, and whose observation was shaped by what he had seen the Revolution do to his own family.
>
> Born **29 July 1805** in Paris, died **16 April 1859** at Cannes, he was the third son of Hervé Clérel, Count of Tocqueville, an officer of Louis XVI's Constitutional Guard, and Louise Le Peletier de Rosanbo, and the great-grandson of **Malesherbes**, the statesman guillotined in 1793. His parents were themselves arrested and only escaped the same fate through Robespierre's fall. The family's restoration under the Bourbons gave his father a peerage and a prefecture; the son's problem was what an old nobility should do in a country where equality of conditions could no longer be undone.

### What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this `social element instance`?

> The verdicts first. **Conceptual identification**: adequate — the persona-concept locates him cleanly as a societal actor who held office without becoming a state actor. **Definitional clarity**: strained at one point, because "liberal aristocrat" names a position rather than a kind, and the tree resolves it by keeping class, office, and doctrine in separate parts. **Conceptual determinacy**: adequate, since each row names a distinct constituent. **Internal consistency**: strained where the actor is read as the author, because *Democracy in America* then appears twice — as an instrument of his action and as an epistemic element of the corpus. **Representational adequacy**: adequate for the actor, and deliberately silent on the content of the work, which belongs to the epistemic decomposition. **Purpose adequacy**: adequate — the note is meant to make him citable as an actor, and the rows do that.
>
> The disciplined cut is therefore the same one the corpus makes elsewhere: decompose the person here, refer the book to its own space, and let the polity he analysed be a polity. What the note must not do is resolve his contradictions into a thesis. He defended liberty in a treatise and approved a state of siege in June 1849; he wrote against majoritarian despotism and sought a "regularisation" of emergency powers; he was an abolitionist and a partisan of the colonisation of Algeria. Those are not inconsistencies of the concept but of the man, and a decomposition that smoothed them would misrepresent the actor it claims to identify.
