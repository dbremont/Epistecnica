# NASA Glenn Research Center

> **NASA Glenn Research Center** is NASA's Cleveland laboratory, founded in 1941 as the Aircraft Engine Research Laboratory and named for John Glenn in 1999. Its ceramics work is aerospace ceramics: ceramic matrix composites, environmental barrier coatings, and the high-temperature materials that turbine and hypersonic components need when a superalloy has run out of temperature.

## Ceramic focus

- **Ceramic matrix composites** — SiC/SiC and oxide/oxide systems for turbine and exhaust components.
- **Environmental barrier coatings** — the coating that keeps a composite from oxidising itself away.
- **Thermal barrier coatings** — ceramic layers over hot-section metal.
- **High-temperature ceramics** — materials for hypersonic and re-entry environments.
- **Processing and joining** — composite fabrication, infiltration, and the joins that decide whether a part survives.
- **Testing at temperature** — the facility that makes an aerospace ceramic credible.

## Place in the field

Glenn is the case where the requirement precedes the science: a material is asked to survive a turbine or a re-entry, and the ceramic is developed against that duty. The ceramic matrix composite programme is the reference example of a ceramic replacing a metal in a load-bearing, high-temperature application, and the coatings programme exists because the composite's weakness — oxidation — is chemical rather than mechanical. It is a mission laboratory, not a materials institute that happens to have aerospace projects.
