# Shanghai Institute of Ceramics (SICCAS)

> The **Shanghai Institute of Ceramics** is the ceramics institute of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. Its lineage begins with the Engineering Institute founded in 1928; the ceramic part became a separate institute in 1959 and took its present name in 1984. It is the largest dedicated ceramics research institute in China, and its portfolio runs from artificial crystals and transparent ceramics to biomaterials and the scientific study of ancient Chinese ceramics.

## Ceramic focus

- **High-performance ceramics** — fine microstructures, structural ceramics and composites.
- **Inorganic functional materials and devices** — electronic, optical and magnetic ceramics.
- **Energy materials** — materials for batteries, fuel cells and energy conversion.
- **Artificial crystals** — grown single crystals for optics, lasers and detectors.
- **Biomaterials** — bioceramics and tissue-engineering scaffolds.
- **Inorganic coatings and eco-materials.**
- **Characterisation and analysis** — the institute's shared instrument base.
- **Ancient and industrial ceramics** — archaeometry, glazes and the history of the craft.

## Place in the field

SICCAS is a CAS institute, which in the Chinese system means long-horizon public science with a heavy instrument base and a national mandate. Two things make it unusual among ceramics institutes: the artificial-crystal programme, which is a materials-growth capability rather than a ceramics one in the narrow sense, and the ancient-ceramics programme — the International Symposium on Ancient Ceramics has been run from the institute since 1982. The same building studies a Song glaze and a transparent spinel.
