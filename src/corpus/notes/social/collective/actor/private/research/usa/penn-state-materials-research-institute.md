# Penn State Materials Research Institute (MRI)

> The **Materials Research Institute** is Pennsylvania State University's interdisciplinary materials institute. Ceramic science at Penn State dates from 1923, and the university's ceramics programme became one of the largest in the United States; the MRI is where the fabrication and characterisation facilities sit across departments, and where the ceramics tradition is organised for outside users.

## Ceramic focus

- **Dielectrics and piezoelectrics** — the Center for Dielectrics and Piezoelectrics, an industry consortium, is the institute's best-known ceramics programme.
- **Structural and electronic ceramics** — processing, microstructure and properties.
- **Powder and colloidal processing** — the route from powder to dense body.
- **Characterisation** — electron microscopy and the shared materials facilities.
- **2D materials and thin films** — the non-ceramic side of a materials institute.
- **Education** — the ceramics degree tradition that supplies the field's staff.

## Place in the field

A university institute, not a mission laboratory: its product is graduates and papers, and its translation channel is the industry consortium, where firms pay a membership fee for early access to results and to the students who produced them. Penn State is one of the American universities that established ceramics as a degree subject, and the MRI is the administrative form that tradition takes when the equipment becomes too expensive for one department.
