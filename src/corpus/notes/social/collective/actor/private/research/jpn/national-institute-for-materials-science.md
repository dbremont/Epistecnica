# National Institute for Materials Science (NIMS)

> The **National Institute for Materials Science** is Japan's national materials institute, at Tsukuba, formed in 2001 by merging the National Research Institute for Metals and the National Institute for Research in Inorganic Materials. It is a public research institute under MEXT, and ceramics is one of its oldest and strongest lines — transparent ceramics, sintering science, and high-temperature and functional materials among them.

## Ceramic focus

- **Optical and transparent ceramics** — spinel, YAG, alumina and other polycrystalline transparent bodies.
- **Functional ceramics** — dielectric, piezoelectric, magnetic and superconducting materials.
- **High-temperature materials** — structural ceramics and coatings for extreme service.
- **Sintering science** — densification, grain growth and microstructure control.
- **Nanomaterials and thin films** — ceramic nanostructures and interfaces.
- **Characterisation** — electron microscopy and the national instrument base around it.

## Place in the field

NIMS sits upstream of JFCC and of industry: long-horizon, publicly funded, and equipped at a scale no single firm would carry. Its ceramics work is where a mechanism is established — why a body sinters, why a grain boundary conducts, why a transparent ceramic stays transparent — before anyone owns a product. It is a national institute, not a company laboratory and not a degree-granting department.
