# Institut de France

> The **Institut de France** is the umbrella body of the French academies — the *Académie française*, the *Académie des inscriptions et belles-lettres*, the *Académie des sciences*, the *Académie des beaux-arts* and the *Académie des sciences morales et politiques* — created in 1795 to replace the royal academies the Revolution had suppressed, and housed since 1805 in the [Collège des Quatre-Nations](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/actor/private/education/fra/college-des-quatre-nations.md) on the quai de Conti.

## What it is

| Dimension | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Legal form** | A public institution (établissement public) under the protection of the President of the Republic, with its own statutes |
| **Members** | Elected for life by the academy they join — the *immortels* of the Académie française are its forty seats |
| **Function** | Prizes and awards, learned publication, dictionary and language regulation, and the collective voice of the academies on questions put to them |
| **Relation to the state** | Consultative and honorific, not a funding council: it certifies and speaks, it does not allocate research money |

## The form to keep in view

> The Institut is the **academy as a certification body**, and it is worth distinguishing from the two institutions it is often confused with. It is not a university — it does not teach. It is not a research council — it does not fund. What it does is **confer standing**: election to a seat, a prize, the dictionary's ruling on usage. That is a real power in a learned culture, and it is the oldest power in the scientific institutional landscape: the seventeenth-century academy certified before the journal refereed, and before the state funded.

> The 1795 creation is also the moment the form was **nationalised**: the royal academies were abolished in 1793 as corporations of the old regime, and re-founded two years later as departments of a single Institut. The continuity is real — the same members, the same disciplines — but the constitutional position changed from privileged body to public institution, which is why the Institut is a useful case in the typology of [public organisations](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/actor/public/public-organization.md).
