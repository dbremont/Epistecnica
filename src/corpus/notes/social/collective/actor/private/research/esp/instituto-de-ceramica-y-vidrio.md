# Instituto de Cerámica y Vidrio (ICV-CSIC)

> The **Instituto de Cerámica y Vidrio** is the ceramics and glass institute of Spain's Consejo Superior de Investigaciones Científicas. It began in 1954 as the silicates section of another CSIC institute, was raised to an institute in June 1964, and has been on the Cantoblanco campus of the Universidad Autónoma de Madrid since 2002. It is a public research institute: project-funded science with its own processing, glass-melting and characterisation facilities.

## Ceramic focus

- **Ceramic engineering** — structural and high-temperature ceramics, phase equilibria and microstructure design.
- **Glass and glass-ceramics** — design, processing and characterisation, including sol-gel routes.
- **Bioceramics** — calcium phosphates, bioactive glasses and scaffolds.
- **Electroceramics** — ionic and electronic conductors, materials for electrochemical applications.
- **Colloidal processing** — suspension chemistry, shaping and sintering.
- **Archaeometry** — ceramic and glass heritage, a line that keeps the institute close to the pottery record.

## Place in the field

Spain's ceramic industry is large and tile-based, and its own industrial research is carried by technology centres in the tile districts; ICV is the public-science end of that system, upstream of the factory and inside the national research council. Four departments — Ceramics, Electroceramics, Surface Physics and Processes, and Glasses — give it a range that few ceramics institutes have: the same place that studies a suspension also studies a Roman glaze.
