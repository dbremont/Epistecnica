# University of Basel

> The **University of Basel** (German: *Universität Basel*) is a public research university in Basel, Switzerland, founded on 4 April 1460 — Switzerland's oldest university and among the oldest in the world. Where Bologna is the students' university and Paris the masters', Basel is the **city's**: endowed and paid by the town, chartered by a papal bull during the Council of Basel, and for five centuries governed as a municipal institution before it became a self-governing one.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**The University of Basel belongs to the `Collective (→ Kind)` `Education Actor (→ Kind)` `University` social element type — the chartered corporation of teachers and scholars that teaches, examines, and confers degrees.**

Its layer is **Ontic** — a treaty charter, faculties, professors, students, a library, and a city's accounts — and **Synontic** where it exists through shared recognition, the degree and the seal. Its facet is ideational-educational, with a strong civic and later cantonal face: the dominant mechanism through which it was funded and reformed was the government of the town that owned it.

Readable secondarily as a `State University` — after 1833 it is funded and disciplined by the canton, and after 2007 by two cantons in parity — and as a `Research Actor` in its modern form. Those readings are prose, not second roots.

It is distinct from a **Education Actor** bound to a court or a church: Basel's warrant came from a pope but its purse and its governing council came from the city, which is why its history reads as civic policy — including the moments the city nearly closed it.

### What is this social instance?

> Basel was founded in connection with the **Council of Basel**. During the years the Catholic clergy resided in the city a temporary university was established, between 1432 and 1448; in 1437 Demetrius was called to lecture Greek, and when Pope Eugene IV tried to move the council to Ferrara in the same year many of those involved stayed, negotiating on until 1439, when the council elected a counter-pope in Amadeus VIII, Duke of Savoy, as Felix V. The permanent university followed on 4 April 1460, decreed with four faculties — arts, medicine, theology, and jurisprudence — with the faculty of arts serving as the foundation for the other three until 1818.
>
> Its survival was a civic decision. From 1497 the Grand Council of Basel discussed whether the university should be closed, and only in 1501 — the year Basel joined the Old Swiss Confederacy — was it decided not to. Over the centuries Basel became an early centre of book printing and humanism, and the Basel University Library was founded around the same time as the university; it now holds over three million books and writings, the largest library in Switzerland.
>
> Lineage: the council's temporary university (1432–1448) → the municipal university of 1460, a Renaissance centre of learning → decline as the city turned commercial, enrollment of over a thousand around 1600 falling to sixty in 1785 with eighteen professors, mostly sons of the elite → the cantonal split of 1833, when the Federal Diet required the canton's assets, the library's books among them, to be divided two-thirds to the new half-canton of Basel-Landschaft, leaving the city to buy back its share while the university cut its courses and sent students on to German universities after about two years → the Faculty of Science (1937), Business and Economics (1996), and Psychology (2003) → independence from the cantonal government on 1 January 1996, and from 2007 sponsorship shared in parity with Basel-Landschaft.
