# KTH Royal Institute of Technology

> **KTH Royal Institute of Technology** (Swedish: *Kungliga Tekniska högskolan*, KTH) is Sweden's principal technical university: founded in Stockholm in 1827 as the *Teknologiska institutet*, renamed in 1877, and built to a purpose no guild and no city had proposed — to give an industrialising country the engineers its industry needed. Where Bologna was the students' university, Paris the masters', and Basel the city's, KTH is the **state's**: ordered by the crown, financed by appropriation, and conferring degrees under national law.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**KTH belongs to the `Collective (→ Kind)` `Education Actor (→ Kind)` `University` social element type** — the corporation of teachers and scholars chartered to teach, examine, and confer degrees.

Its layer is **Ontic** — a charter, five schools, professors and students, a campus on Valhallavägen, an appropriation — and **Synontic** where it exists through recognition: the *civilingenjör* title, the doctorate, the rank. Its facet is ideational-educational with a strong **industrial and state** face: the dominant mechanism through which it was founded, funded, and reformed was the government of the country that wanted engineers.

Readable secondarily as a `State University` — the Swedish state is its principal and its budget an appropriation — and as a `Research Actor` in its modern form. Those readings are prose, not second roots.

It is distinct from the **polytechnic as a local or municipal institution**: KTH was a national instrument from the outset, and when the municipal technical schools were folded into the university system after 1989 it was KTH that absorbed the students, not the other way round.

### What is this social instance?

> KTH's lineage runs back before its founding. Its earliest Swedish predecessor was the **Laboratorium Mechanicum**, a collection of mechanical models for teaching created in **1697** by **Christopher Polhem** — regarded as the father of mechanics in Sweden — as a school and research facility after his studies abroad; its models were used intermittently by various masters until the **School of Mechanics** (*Mekaniska skolan*) was founded in Stockholm in **1798**. In **1827** that school was transformed into the **Technological Institute** (*Teknologiska institutet*), following the establishment of polytechnical schools across Europe in the early nineteenth century, often on the model of the **École polytechnique**. The institute's establishment had been ordered by **King Charles XIV John on 18 May 1825**; its first lecture, in mathematics, was held on **6 June 1827**.
>
> The early institute was small and elementary: one professor of chemistry, one of physics, one class in mechanical engineering and one in chemical engineering, with teaching aimed more at craftsmanship than at engineering, and plagued by conflict between the faculty and its founder and head, **Gustaf Magnus Schwartz**, who was responsible for the artisanal focus. A government committee appointed in **1844** resolved the matter by removing Schwartz in **1845**; **Joachim Åkerman**, head of the School of Mining in Falun and a former professor of chemistry at the institute, took over and carried through a full reorganisation. In **1867** the regulations were overhauled again to state explicitly that the institute should provide **scientific training**; in **1869** the Falun School of Mining was moved to Stockholm and merged with it; in **1871** it took over the civil engineering course formerly given by the Higher Artillery College at Marieberg. In **1877** the name became *Kungliga Tekniska högskolan* — the change from *institut* to *högskola* — some courses were extended from three years to four, and **architecture was added** to the curriculum.
>
> Its degrees became a national currency. In **1915** the degree titles conferred by KTH received legal protection; by the late nineteenth century *civilingenjör* had spread from building and construction to most KTH-trained engineers, the mining engineers keeping *bergsingenjör*, and for a time the title was equivalent to "KTH graduate" — until **Chalmers** in Gothenburg became, in **1937**, the second Swedish engineering college allowed to confer it. Research came later than teaching: until the early twentieth century a KTH graduate going on to academic research had to earn a doctorate, usually in physics or chemistry, at a regular university, and only in **1927** was KTH granted the right to confer its own doctorates as *Teknologie doktor* — the first five created in **1929**. In **1917** the first buildings of the new campus on **Valhallavägen**, designed by **Erik Lallerstedt**, were completed and still form the main campus; the clock-tower bells followed in 1927. From **1984** the *civilingenjör* programmes at all Swedish universities were extended from four years to four and a half, and from **1989** the shorter technical programmes of the municipal polytechnical schools were gradually moved into the university system — two-year programmes from 1989, alternatively three-year from 1995 — which added campuses around Stockholm to KTH.
>
> Lineage: Polhem's *Laboratorium Mechanicum* (1697) → the School of Mechanics (1798) → the Technological Institute ordered by the crown and opened in 1827 as a craft school → the scientifically reformed college of 1867–1877, with mining and civil engineering absorbed and architecture added → the degree-granting university of 1927, with its own doctorates → the mass institution of 1989–1995, absorbing the municipal technical programmes → the consolidated university of 2018–2025, five schools on two campuses.
