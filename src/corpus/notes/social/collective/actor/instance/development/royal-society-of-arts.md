# Royal Society of Arts

> The **Royal Society of Arts** (RSA) was founded in **1754** as the *Society for the Encouragement of Arts, Manufactures and Commerce* — the London instance of the [promotion agency](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/actor/instance/development/promotion-agency.md) form, and the body that made the **premium** the standard instrument of British technical improvement.

## What it did

| Instrument | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Premiums** | Prizes for inventions, crops, dyes, machines — including the prize that drew attention to the cobalt-blue process and to mechanical improvements |
| **Exhibitions** | The society's exhibitions and its role in the movement that produced the Great Exhibition of 1851 |
| **Technical education** | The examinations it introduced in the 1850s, the ancestor of the technical-examination system |
| **Standards and testing** | Prizes for standard measures, and campaigns on quality |
| **Commemoration** | The blue plaques, its later and most visible activity |

## The difference from the RDS

> Both are chartered improvement societies with premiums, and the RSA is the more **urban and manufacturing** of the two: the Dublin Society began with husbandry, the London society with arts and manufactures, and the RSA's premium list is a catalogue of eighteenth-century industrial technique. Its examinations also made it the ancestor of the **technical-education** branch rather than only the grant branch.
