# Facet

> **Facet** is the analytical concept denoting a distinct dimension or space of social interaction: a coherent set of patterns, processes, or mechanisms, defined by its dominant causal mechanism, that can be analyzed independently while remaining interdependent with the others. Facets are analytical tools for organizing the representation of social reality, not reified entities; they scope inquiry, never owning nodes.

> A facet is defined by dominant causal mechanisms, not by location or actors. Assign a facet by asking which mechanism does the explanatory work here — market coordination, rule enforcement, meaning-making, technical transformation — not where the instance sits or who participates.

> This note is the shelf master. It owns the definition, the authoritative catalogue, the rules for assigning a facet, and the contract a per-facet note follows. The notes that develop a single row live in [`facet/`](/explorer.html?dir=notes/social/meta/facet), one per facet.

## Is there a cultural facet?

> No. Culture is not a single domain of social life and therefore does not form a facet of its own; it permeates all facets. Every facet is, in effect, constituted by cultural elements — shared symbols, meanings, norms, and habitual certainties — making culture a common component across the entirety of social reality rather than one corner of it.

## Why facets and not "systems"?

> The traditional **notion** of a system — a cohesive whole with fixed boundaries — is a poor organizer for social reality. More specialized concepts, such as **social regions**, **ecosystems**, or other **relational frameworks**, capture what matters instead: the multiplicity of interacting structures, emergent dynamics, fluid and porous boundaries, and complex interdependencies of the social sphere. Facets cut across these ensembles as analytical dimensions; no facet draws a boundary around a society.

## What a facet note contains

A note on this shelf is read in five parts, and each answers a question the catalogue cannot:

1. **The mechanism, in one sentence.** The row's mechanism restated so that it can be applied, not merely quoted — *what does the explanatory work here?*
2. **Its boundaries.** The adjacent facets it is most easily confused with, and the difference stated by mechanism rather than by subject matter.
3. **What it explains.** The kind of instance the reading is illuminating for, and the kind it is not.
4. **Where its elements live.** The corpus paths that carry the facet's elements, each keeping its own element type.
5. **QA, and references.** The questions the reading raises, and the notes and literature it rests on.

A facet note carries **no decomposition table**. A facet is a lens: it owns no nodes, so there is no instance tree for it to decompose. The `Instance Tree Path` tables belong to elements, and an element read under a facet keeps the table it already has.

## How a facet is assigned

- **Ask what does the explanatory work.** Not *who participates* and not *in which sector the instance sits*: a hospital, a bank and a farm each carry a technical facet, and none of them *is* the technical facet.
- **Keep the element's own type.** The instance is still an actor, an activity or a compound; the facet only scopes the inquiry. Typing is not reassigned by reading.
- **Expect more than one reading.** The same configuration answers to several mechanisms at once — a port city as labour market, jurisdiction and hiring culture — and each reading should be internally coherent.
- **Take a reading only if it explains.** A reading that explains nothing another facet does not is a redescription, and the mechanism test exists to reject it.

## What a facet note is not

- **Not a field.** A field is an arena with positions and resources — a locus; a facet is a dimension — a lens.
- **Not a system.** No boundary is drawn around a society, and no whole is claimed.
- **Not a sector.** Facets cross-cut industries, organisations and institutions.
- **Not a decomposition.** No branch, no type segments, no instance paths.

## Adding a facet

1. Add the row to the catalogue above, with the mechanism named in one line. The catalogue is the authority; a note without a row is an orphan.
2. Write the note on the [`facet/`](/explorer.html?dir=notes/social/meta/facet) shelf, tagged `[note, facet, <facet>, social-ontology]`, following the five parts above.
3. Rebuild the notes index so the shelf and the search snapshot see it.

## Facets and layers

> Facets and layers are two orthogonal axes. Layers (Ontic, Synontic, Noetic, Multi) state in which stratum an element exists — extra-mentally, through shared interpretation, as a descriptive instrument, or across strata; facets state by which dominant causal mechanism it is scoped. Every social element is read against both: an element's layer is part of its type, while its facet scopes the inquiry. See "Why do we need layers and facets?" in [Philosophia Socialium et Operis](/view.html?p=notes/philosophia-socialium-et-operis.md).

## Facet vs. field

> Do not confuse a facet with the `Social Field` social element type. A **facet** is an analytical dimension that scopes inquiry and owns nothing. A **field** is a structured arena of social relations, competition, and influence — an instance with positions, resources, and logics of its own — decomposable as a social element. Facets are lenses; fields are loci.

## QA

### Why do we need the idea of a facet?

> Because one configuration of social reality answers to many mechanisms at once, and naming none of them invites mono-causal explanation. Facets keep inquiry scoped — economic coordination is explained economically, normative binding normatively — without carving reality into separate containers or reifying each dimension into a substance of its own.

### Why integrate the idea of a facet into the instance decomposition?

> Because every associative move presupposes a parsing of who relates how *under which mechanism*. Typing each decomposition row from its grouping segments (per the well-formedness rule) and reading the whole tree against one dominant facet keeps the decomposition from mixing causal grammars mid-branch: the same port city decomposes as labor market under the economic facet, as jurisdiction under the legal one, and as hiring culture under the symbolic one — three readings, each internally coherent.

### Can a facet note exist before its elements?

> Yes. The [Research Facet](/view.html?p=notes/social/meta/facet/research.md) was written while its elements were still being filed, and it stated where they would live. The order matters for a different reason: a facet note is a claim about what the corpus will hold, and it should be written when the claim can be checked against something.
