# Cybernetic Facet

> The **cybernetic facet** is the dimension of social reality whose dominant causal mechanism is *control and delegation through designed systems*: units that measure a state, select an action and act on it, and the human–machine ensembles through which that agency is exercised.

> It answers the question *where does the cybernetic interaction of social reality go?* Into this facet, and not into a technical facet of its own: facets are distinguished by dominant causal mechanism, and "cybernetic" names the mechanism of control and delegation, not the material of the machine.

## The mechanism, not the sector

> A facet is assigned by asking which mechanism does the explanatory work here — market coordination, rule enforcement, meaning-making, technical transformation, control and delegation — not where the instance sits or who participates.

The cybernetic facet cross-cuts sectors: a bank with a trading algorithm, a hospital with a triage model, a farm with a climate controller and a state with a benefits engine all carry it, and none of them *is* the cybernetic facet. What makes a relation cybernetic is that control and delegation explain it — the loop, the reference, the delegated decision, and the accountability for it.

This is why the facet cannot be read off an org chart or an industry code. The same control system is technical by its implementation, cybernetic by its delegation, political by its authorization and normative by its liability.

## Boundaries: technical, cybernetic, cognitive, political, normative

| Facet | Dominant mechanism | What it explains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Technical** | Transformation of nature and society through material and knowledge systems | The machine's physics, the control technique, the implementation |
| **Cybernetic** | Control and delegation through designed systems | The loop as an actor, delegated agency, the human–machine ensemble |
| **Cognitive** | Perception, belief, expectation and interpretation | How the operators or the model interpret their situation |
| **Political** | Power, authority and collective decision | Who authorizes the delegation, and with what mandate |
| **Normative** | Rules, obligations and accountability | Who answers for the act, and under which rule |

The facets are adjacent, not identical. A thermostat is a technical object and a cybernetic unit at once; a predictive model in a ministry is technical in its training, cybernetic in its delegation and political in its authorization. The same instance can be read under several facets; the facet scopes the inquiry, it does not partition the instance.

## What the facet covers

- **The cybernetic actor** — the [Cybernetic Interaction Unit](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/cybernetic-interaction-unit.md): software agents, robots, control systems, machine-intelligence systems.
- **Delegated action** — the autonomy gradient, oversight and intervention, the difference between a tool and a principal.
- **The ensemble** — humans and machines acting as one configuration: human-in-the-loop, algorithmic management, mixed agency.
- **Accountability** — liability and answerability along the delegation chain.
- **Governance** — the policy that bounds the delegation: [Computation Policy](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/action/problem/computation/computation-policy.md).
- **The technique** — the [Control System](/view.html?p=notes/technica/cybernetics/control-system.md) and the [Active Inference System](/view.html?p=notes/technica/cybernetics/ais.md) that realize the loop.

## What it explains, and what it does not

The reading is illuminating for configurations where a designed system selects and acts and someone remains answerable for it: an automated trading desk, a clinical triage model, a content-ranking system, a factory controller. It explains the *delegation* — who set the reference, who can stop the unit, who bears the consequence.

It explains nothing about the machine's aesthetics, its physics or its training loss as such; those are the artistic, technical and scientific readings. It also does not decide whether the unit is conscious: the facet is about control and accountability, not about minds.

## QA

### Is the cybernetic facet just the technical facet?

No. The technical facet explains how a material or knowledge system transforms its object; the cybernetic facet explains who delegated the decision to it, how it selects, and who answers for the result. A hammer has a technical facet and no cybernetic one; a trading algorithm has both.

### When is a machine an actor?

When it is recognized as one through delegation: a principal sets its reference, can stop it, and answers for its acts. A unit with no answerable principal is an accident waiting to be attributed, not an actor.

### Does delegation change what an organization is?

It changes where the decision sits, not the type of the organization. The firm is still a firm; what changes is the locus of agency within it, and the monitoring and liability that follow.
