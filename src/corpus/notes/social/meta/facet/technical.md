# Technical Facet

> The **technical facet** — the catalog's **Technical** facet — is the dimension of social reality whose dominant causal mechanism is the *transformation of nature and society* through material and knowledge systems: the practices, techniques, objects and systems by which a collective acts on its world, and the institutions, agents and competences that carry them.

> It answers the question *where does the technical interaction of social reality go?* Into this facet, and not into a facet of its own: facets are distinguished by dominant causal mechanism, and "technical" names that mechanism exactly as the [Facet](/view.html?p=notes/social/meta/facet.md) catalog's Technical row already states. The vocabulary is collected in [Meta Tecnica](/view.html?p=notes/technica/meta/tecnica.md) and defined in the Glossarium.

## The mechanism, not the sector

> A facet is assigned by asking which mechanism does the explanatory work here — market coordination, rule enforcement, meaning-making, technical transformation — not where the instance sits or who participates.

The technical facet cross-cuts sectors: a hospital, a bank and a farm all have a technical facet, and none of them *is* the technical facet. What makes a relation technical is that the transformation of reality through a material or knowledge system is what explains it — the load path, the process, the technique, the instrument.

This is why the facet cannot be read off an org chart or an industry code. Two firms in the same sector may have different dominant mechanisms, and the same firm may be technical in its production, economic in its pricing and legal in its compliance.

## Boundaries: technical, scientific, innovation

| Facet | Dominant mechanism | What it explains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Technical** | Transformation of nature and society through material and knowledge systems | How a collective acts on its world: practices, techniques, objects, systems |
| **Scientific** | Production and certification of warranted knowledge | Research, experiment, peer evaluation, formalized inquiry |
| **Innovation** | Invention, applied knowledge, technological development, problem-solving | How novelties are conceived and brought into use |

The three are adjacent, not identical. A research laboratory is Scientific by its dominant mechanism even though its instruments are technical elements; a new product is Innovation by its mechanism even though it is also a technical object. The same instance can be read under several facets; the facet scopes the inquiry, it does not partition the instance.

## What the facet covers

The technical elements themselves, and the social forms that carry them:

- **Elements** — practices, techniques, technical objects, technical systems, knowledge, standards, interfaces.
- **Carriers** — technical institutions, agents, labor, competence, education and apprenticeship.
- **Dynamics** — technological trajectories, regimes, path dependence, lock-in, momentum, transitions.
- **Constitution** — sociotechnical systems, technical mediation, technical codes, actor-networks, sociotechnical imaginaries.
- **Authority** — technical expertise, technical authority, technocracy, technical rationality, technology assessment.
- **Work and structure** — technical division of labor, invisible work, deskilling, infrastructure, boundary objects.
- **Political economy** — techno-economic paradigms, dual use, appropriate technology, technological catch-up.

> Each is defined in the Glossarium; the full list is the [Meta Tecnica terminology](/view.html?p=notes/technica/meta/tecnica.md#sec-terminology).

> The facet is a lens; its elements are typed by their own element types and simply read under this mechanism.

- **Technical elements** — the [technica corpus](/view.html?p=notes/technica/atlas.html): practices, techniques, objects and systems decomposed in the technica grammar, and the [technical-object taxonomy](/view.html?p=notes/technica/technical-object-taxonomy.html).
- **Social forms** — [Research](/view.html?p=notes/social/dynamic/knowledge/research-system.md) and the [Knowledge System](/view.html?p=notes/social/dynamic/knowledge/knowledge-system.md) inventory — the roles, units and mechanisms by which a society produces, diffuses and holds technical knowledge.

## QA

### Is technique an external "impact" on society?

> No. The technical facet is not the study of technology's effects on a society that exists before it; it is the study of social reality as partly constituted by technique. Sociotechnical systems, technical mediation and technical codes all describe the same point from different angles: the technical and the social are analytically separable aspects of one configuration, not two substances that meet.

### Does every social relation have a technical reading?

> No. The mechanism test is a test: a relation is technical only when technical transformation does the explanatory work. A market exchange, a legal ruling and a religious rite all involve technical elements — instruments, records, buildings — but their dominant mechanism is economic, legal or spiritual. Reading them technically is possible; it is illuminating only when it explains something the other facets do not.

### Is the technical facet the same as the economic facet?

> No, though they overlap heavily in production. The economic facet explains allocation and exchange; the technical facet explains transformation. A factory is both, but the question *how does this material become that product?* is technical, while *who gets the product, and at what price?* is economic.
