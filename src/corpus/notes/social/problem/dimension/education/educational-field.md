# Educational Field

> **Education**: A formal or informal process of acquiring knowledge, cognitive skills, and cultural understanding to enable personal and societal development.

> **Training**: A structured process of developing specific practical skills or competencies to perform particular tasks or jobs effectively.

## Formulation

- Technical Training Ecosystem (TTE): Development of practical and technical competencies.
- Science & Engineering Preparation Ecosystem (SEPE): Acquisition of foundational scientific and engineering knowledge.
- General Formation Ecosystem (GFE): Development of cognitive, cultural, social, and ethical competencies for broad personal and societal formation.

## Theoretical Pedagogues

> What a formation ecosystem *is for* is not settled by its structure. The pedagogues below each answered that question differently — autonomy, *Bildung*, ordered instruction, cultivation — and the answers are still the ones the field argues with. Each row states a theory of formation, not a biography; the works are in the references.

| Thinker | Formation theory |
| --- | --- |
| **[Immanuel Kant](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/actor/private/persona/immanuel-kant.md)** | **Autonomy.** Education trains the capacity to use one's own understanding — *sapere aude* — so the pupil can act from reasons he has examined rather than from authority; it proceeds through discipline, cultivation, civilization and moralisation (*Über Pädagogik*, 1803). |
| **Wilhelm von Humboldt** | ***Bildung*.** The formation of all human powers into a harmonious whole, pursued for its own sake rather than for a role; the research university (Berlin, 1810) is the institution that gives the idea a place — teaching and inquiry joined in one body. |
| **Johann Friedrich Herbart** | **Systematic formation.** Pedagogy as a science derived from its own aim and grounded in psychology and ethics: instruction ordered in steps — clearness, association, system, method — and character formed through interest rather than coercion (*Allgemeine Pädagogik*, 1806). |
| **[Confucius](/view.html?p=notes/social/synontic/ideational/confucius-system.md)** | **Cultivation (*xué*).** Intellectual, moral and practical capacity formed together: study joined to practice and to ritual propriety (*lǐ*), so that learning makes a person fit to govern rather than merely informed (*Analects*). |
| **Jan Amos Comenius** | **Universal didactics.** Every child, in the vernacular, by stages matched to age, things before words and the senses before definitions; *Didactica Magna* (1657) is the first system of teaching as method rather than as content. |
| **[Jean-Jacques Rousseau](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/actor/private/persona/jean-jacques-rousseau.md)** | **Natural education.** The child's own nature sets the curriculum; "negative education" removes obstruction instead of imposing instruction, and learning comes from the consequences of action rather than from the master's precepts (*Émile*, 1762). |
| **Johann Heinrich Pestalozzi** | **Head, heart and hand.** Thinking, feeling and doing developed together, beginning from the child's own observation and advancing by graded elemental steps (*How Gertrude Teaches Her Children*, 1801). |
| **Friedrich Froebel** | **Self-activity and play.** The child forms himself through activity, and play is the child's mode of work; the kindergarten (1837) is the environment built for it (*The Education of Man*, 1826). |
| **John Dewey** | **Education as experience.** Growth through the reconstruction of experience by inquiry; learning by doing, school as a form of social life, democracy as both the method and the aim (*Democracy and Education*, 1916). |
| **Maria Montessori** | **Prepared environment.** Self-directed activity with didactic material, the teacher as observer rather than lecturer, and freedom inside an environment designed for the child's developmental stages (*The Montessori Method*, 1912). |
| **Lev Vygotsky** | **Social formation.** Mind is formed from the outside in, through tools and signs, language above all; instruction works in the **zone of proximal development** — what the learner does with help today he does alone tomorrow (*Mind in Society*, 1978). |
| **Paulo Freire** | **Critical pedagogy.** Education as dialogue rather than deposit; *conscientização* — the learner's critical awareness of his own situation — makes formation a practice of freedom (*Pedagogy of the Oppressed*, 1970). |
