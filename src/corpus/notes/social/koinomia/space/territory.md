# Territory

> The bounded domain over which a polity claims authority: the land, waters, and airspace it governs, together with the people and resources they carry.

A territory is not a region and not a jurisdiction. A [Region](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/space/region.md) is a delimitation concept — an area of denser internal interaction — and its boundary is drawn by the observer; a territory is *held*, and its boundary is claimed. Jurisdiction is the legal competence a polity exercises, and it need not coincide with the territory: it may reach beyond it (over nationals abroad, over ships under the flag) or stop short of it (over autonomous sub-units). The [Polity](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/polity/polity.md) is the political collective that holds authority; the [State](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/polity/polity.md) is the sovereign form that claims supremacy within a territory. The territory is what that claim is made over.

## What a territory carries

- **Ground and waters** — the physical substrate: land, rivers, lakes, territorial sea, and the airspace above them.
- **Boundary** — the line or zone separating one territory from another; a border, a frontier, or a shared-sovereignty arrangement such as Coto Mixto.
- **People** — the population resident within it, which a polity rules but does not own.
- **Resources** — the natural and built stock the ground carries: minerals, fisheries, forests, infrastructure.
- **Jurisdiction** — the competences the polity may exercise there, which federations divide between levels.

## Territory, region and scale

The shelf reads the spatial frame at three scales. The [World](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/space/world.md) is the planetary container; a territory is the bounded domain of a polity within it; a region is a delimitation of that domain by interaction density, and may cut across territories (a continental bloc, a cross-border metropolitan area) or sit inside one (the regions of a state). A [City](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/collective/city.md) is a region read as an inhabited, governed settlement.

## Territory and the commons

Koinomia governs shared resources, and most of them are territorial: fisheries, forests, water basins, grazing land, infrastructure. The commons question — who may use a resource, on what terms, and who bears the cost of maintaining it — is asked of a resource located somewhere. Territory is that *somewhere* read as a whole.
