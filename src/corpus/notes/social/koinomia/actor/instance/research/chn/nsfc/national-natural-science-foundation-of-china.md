# National Natural Science Foundation of China

> The **National Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC)** is the state agency that funds competitive basic research in China: proposals are submitted through registered host institutions, judged by expert review and panel, and awarded through a published schema of program types and discipline codes. Its review principles, in its own statement, are reliance on experts, collective decision, merit-based selection, fairness and appropriateness.

> What makes it worth a note is the **schema**: NSFC does not fund science through missions or programmes with targets. It funds it through **disciplines** (nine codes), **project types** (general, key, major, exploratory, joint, regional) and a **talent ladder** that funds people separately from projects. That structure — and not the money — is what determines what Chinese basic research looks like.

## The funding schema

### Discipline codes

| **Code** | **Department** |
| --- | --- |
| **A** | Mathematical and physical sciences |
| **B** | Chemical sciences |
| **C** | Life sciences |
| **D** | Earth sciences |
| **E** | Engineering and material sciences |
| **F** | Information sciences |
| **G** | Management sciences |
| **H** | Health sciences |
| **T** | Interdisciplinary sciences |

A proposal enters through a code, and the code's panel is what judges it. The allocation is therefore **bottom-up by discipline**: a field grows because its panel funds it, not because a ministry decided the field mattered.

### Project types

| **Type** | **What it funds** | **Function in the schema** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **General Program** | A single investigator's project over a normal grant term | The bedrock: the volume instrument, and the one most applicants actually compete for |
| **Key Program** | A larger, focused project within a discipline | Concentration above the general level |
| **Major Program** | Multi-project programmes around a defined scientific problem | Coordination across teams |
| **Major Research Plans** | Thematic multi-year initiatives in a strategic area | The nearest thing to a mission line |
| **Original Exploratory Programs** | High-risk ideas outside the consensus | The schema's admission that peer review suppresses the unfamiliar |
| **Major Non-Consensual Projects** | Projects the panel expects to be contested | The same admission, institutionalised at the top |
| **Joint Funds** | Co-funded programmes with provinces, state-owned enterprises and industry | Regional and sectoral alignment purchased from outside the budget |
| **Fund for Less Developed Regions** | Projects in under-resourced regions | Geographic redistribution inside a merit system |
| **Emergency Management Programs** | Rapid-response funding | Crisis instrument |
| **International programmes** | RFIS I/II/III, ICFCRT, the Science Fund for Global Challenges and Sustainability, NSFC–CERN and CJPL cooperation | Opening the system outward |

Joint Funds are the schema's quiet political economy: as of 2023, 29 provincial-level regions and 12 state-owned enterprises participated, alongside ten industry-sector funds — a way to extend the funding base without extending the central budget, and to attach regional and corporate priorities to a merit-reviewed scheme.

### The talent ladder

| **Tier** | **Former name** | **What it is** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Young Scientists Fund (C)** | 青年科学基金 | Entry tier: first independent grant, age-bounded |
| **Young Scientists Fund (B)** | 优秀青年科学基金 (优青) | Mid tier: recognition of early excellence |
| **Young Scientists Fund (A)** | 国家杰出青年科学基金 (杰青) | Top tier: the national marker of a leading investigator, now with a **selective continuation fund** |
| **Innovative Research Groups** | 创新研究群体 | Team-level funding above the individual ladder |
| **Basic Science Centers** | 基础科学中心 | Institutional-scale concentration |

The 2026 guide renames the tiers (A/B/C) and says why: to address "the misuse of talent labels". The same document adds a continuation mechanism for tier A — a long-term stable support line for the scientists it has already identified. Two reforms in opposite directions, and both are real: the naming is being deflated while the funding is being concentrated.

## The odds

The schema's real constraint is arithmetic, and NSFC publishes enough to see it:

- **2023, foundation-wide:** roughly 318,000 proposals from 2,404 host institutions; about 52,500 awards; about 31.9 billion yuan — a success rate around one in six.
- **Chemical sciences, 2025:** 16,999 General Program proposals for an average direct cost of ¥500,000; the Young Scientists Fund (C) drew 13,900 proposals and funded 2,182 — **15.70%**.

A one-in-six window does two things to a research system: it selects for proposal-writing capacity as much as for science, and it makes each award a career event rather than a working budget.

## The review

- **Two stages:** expert review of the written proposal, then a panel that ranks and decides. The panel is the point at which discipline politics enters.
- **Reviewer assessment:** since 2020 NSFC assesses reviewers' performance, an instrument aimed at the quality and consistency of judgement rather than its outcomes.
- **Integrity machinery:** the guide describes a rectification of "string-pulling" (*la guanxi*) and a multi-tier oversight system over funding — an acknowledgement that a competitive system this large generates its own distortion.
- **The escape hatches:** Original Exploratory and Major Non-Consensual programmes exist because the consensus that makes peer review reliable is also what makes it conservative. NSFC's stated aim in 2026 is to strengthen the identification of "non-consensual innovative projects" inside the ordinary programmes — that is, to make the exception routine.

## What the schema does

Four consequences follow from funding by discipline and by ladder rather than by mission:

- **Fields grow where panels are.** A discipline's budget is a function of its panel's composition and its applicants' quality, not of a national target. This is decentralisation in a centralised state, and it explains both the breadth of Chinese output and the difficulty of directing it.
- **People and projects are funded separately.** The talent ladder makes a career out of awards; the program ladder makes a project out of a proposal. The two interact: a tier-A award changes what the next General Program application can promise.
- **The label economy is intrinsic.** Any ladder that marks people creates labels, and labels get used for hiring, promotion and pay. Hence the renaming — a reform aimed not at the funding but at the social meaning of the funding.
- **The mission line is thin.** Major Research Plans are the place where a state priority can enter, and they are a small share of the whole. When the national five-year plan sets directions (see [China Five-Year Plan](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/action/instance/china-five-year-plan.md)), NSFC's schema is not the instrument that carries them.
