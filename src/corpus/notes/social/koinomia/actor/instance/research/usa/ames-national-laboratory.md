# Ames National Laboratory

> **Ames National Laboratory** is a U.S. Department of Energy national laboratory for materials, chemistry and critical materials, operated by Iowa State University on its campus in Ames, Iowa. It is a government-owned, contractor-operated laboratory: the Department owns the site and funds the work, the University runs it, and the two have been paired since the laboratory's founding in 1947.

> Not to be confused with [Ames Research Center](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/actor/instance/research/usa/ames-research-center.md), the NASA center in California that shares the name.

## Actor type

**Ames National Laboratory belongs to the `Research Actor` social element type, of the `National Laboratory` kind** — a large-facility public establishment hosting mission-directed science. Its social element type is `Collective / Organization`; its layer is ontic, since the buildings, instruments, staff and contract exist extra-mentally; its facet is research, since what the actor produces is warranted knowledge on a federal mission.

Readable secondarily as a `Public Organization` — the Department of Energy holds the laboratory and answers for it — but only partially, and the institutional form below is precisely why. The laboratory is not a government body: its staff are university employees working under a federal contract, its director answers to the University as operator and to the Department as sponsor, and the public authority attaches to the sponsor rather than to the operator. The secondary reading tracks the sponsorship, not the actor.

It is not a `Government Research Agency` — that is the Department of Energy's Office of Science, which sponsors it; not a `University Research Unit` — the campus is the site, not the owner; and not an `Enterprise Research Lab` — it does not research for a firm's capability. It is a research actor whose distinctiveness lies in its *form* rather than its function: many bodies produce materials science, and only this arrangement produces it on a university campus under federal ownership.

## Institutional Form of This Research Organization

> **Government-owned, contractor-operated (GOCO).** The federal government owns the laboratory — site, buildings, instruments — and funds its programme; a contractor operates it. Ames is held in this form, and the laboratory describes itself as unique among the Department of Energy's seventeen national laboratories in being physically located on the campus of a major research university.

What the form fixes, part by part:

- **Ownership.** The site and facilities are federal property held for the Department of Energy. The government's presence on the site is the Ames Site Office, at 305 TASF, 2408 Pammel Drive, Ames, Iowa.
- **Operation.** Iowa State University operates the laboratory for the Department under contract No. DE-AC02-07CH11358 — a management-and-operating contract, the instrument the Department uses to run its national laboratories. The University employs the laboratory's staff and administers its appointments.
- **Programme.** The Department sets the mission and funds the work; the contractor executes it. The laboratory's published figures are about 450 employees and an annual budget of about $65 million.
- **Boundary.** Because the operator is a university, the laboratory reaches undergraduate and graduate student talent that a standalone federal facility does not, and many of its scientists and administrators also hold faculty positions at the University. That is the form's distinctive advantage, and also the reason the actor is not a `University Research Unit`.

> The form is not a detail of the entry. A laboratory is a `National Laboratory` by function and a `Government-Owned Contractor-Operated Laboratory` by institutional form — and the second is what fixes who owns the ground under the instruments, who employs the people standing on it, and who answers for the money. Both readings are registered in the [Research Actor](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/research.md) type, which carries `Institutional Form` as a kind branch under `National Laboratory`, beside its government-operated counterpart.

## Research programme

The laboratory's science is organized into four divisions: **Chemical & Biological Sciences**, **Materials Sciences & Engineering**, **Simulation, Modeling & Decision Science**, and **Critical Materials**. Four named programmes sit across them: the Center for the Advancement of Topological Semimetals (CATS), the Critical Materials Innovation Hub (CMI), the Institute for Cooperative Upcycling of Plastics (iCOUP), and the Machine Learning Accelerated Materials Discovery Center.

Two facilities serve the wider field. The **Materials Preparation Center** purifies, prepares and characterizes metals, alloys and single crystals for research and industry. The **Sensitive Instrument Facility** houses electron microscopes in a vibration- and static-free environment for the highest attainable resolution.

The through-line is materials: the discovery, synthesis, analysis and application of new materials, novel chemistries and analytical tools — with critical materials as the mission area in which the laboratory's rare-earth inheritance is put to work.

## History

The laboratory descends from the **Ames Project**, set up in 1942 by Iowa State College chemistry professor Frank H. Spedding to bring chemistry into the Manhattan Project alongside its physics programme. Harley A. Wilhelm led the process work that made uranium metal pure enough to cast large ingots and cheap enough to matter: the Ames Project supplied one-third of the uranium for the first self-sustaining chain reaction at Stagg Field on 2 December 1942, and produced more than two million pounds of uranium before industry took the process over in 1945. The project received the Army–Navy E Flag for production excellence on 12 October 1945.

The Atomic Energy Commission formally established Ames Laboratory at Iowa State University in 1947 out of that work. Spedding was its first director, serving until 1968; Spedding Hall and Wilhelm Hall carry the two names. The laboratory's own count of Nobel laureates is one — Dan Shechtman, Chemistry, 2011.

## Place in the field

The rare-earth chemistry that built the laboratory never left it. Spedding was an expert in the chemistry of rare earths before the war, the wartime process was at bottom a separation problem, and the modern critical-materials mission is that problem at national scale: which elements a supply chain can be made to depend on, and how to substitute, recycle or separate around them. The Materials Preparation Center is the institutional form of the competence — a service for pure metals and single crystals rather than a laboratory's private stockroom.

Its position is unusual for a second reason. Ames is a federal laboratory sitting inside a university, which makes it at once a Department of Energy research establishment and a standing bridge between the Department and a land-grant university's teaching and research mission. That is what the GOCO form buys in this instance, and the reason the laboratory's history and the University's are difficult to write separately.
