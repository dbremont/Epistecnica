# Polish Academy of Sciences

> The **Polish Academy of Sciences** (Polska Akademia Nauk, PAN) is Poland's state academy: a corporation of scholars and a network of some seventy research institutes, founded in 1952 on the Soviet academy model and — unusually among the post-socialist academies — **institutionally reproduced** through the transition of 1989 rather than dismantled.

## What it is

| Dimension | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Legal form** | A state scientific institution, governed by its own statute and general assembly, with institutes as its research-performing units |
| **Founded** | 1952, absorbing the earlier Polish academies of learning into the socialist state-academy model |
| **Scope** | Mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, engineering, the humanities — the institutes span the whole research spectrum |
| **Funding** | State budget, with competitive project funding layered on after 2010 |
| **Role** | The basic-research pillar beside the universities, and the employer of a large share of the country's research personnel |

## The reproduction case

> [Heinecke (2016)](/view.html?p=documents/papers/heinecke-2016-polish-public-science-system.md) makes PAN the centre of the most interesting finding in the Polish science transition: while the economy was liberalised and the applied-research institutes were dismantled or displaced by private R&D, the academy **survived essentially intact** — same institutes, same disciplines, same internal organisation. The historical-institutionalist reading is that an institution with its own legal personality, its own property and its own internal labour market can absorb a political shock that destroys institutions which depend on a ministry's budget line.

> The consequence is visible in the comparison with the [Czech Academy of Sciences](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/actor/instance/research/cze/czech-academy-of-sciences.md): both are state academies founded on the same model in the same decade, and both were reproduced, which is the point — the academy form was robust across the bloc, while the *applied* research system was not.

## The reform context

> The reproduction did not mean stagnation. The 2010 reform package created the **National Science Centre** (NCN) for basic research and the **NCBR** for applied, introducing competitive funding beside the academy's institutional budget; the **parametric evaluation** by publication counts governed the institutes through the 2010s; and the 2017 **Constitution for Science** restructured evaluation and doctoral training. PAN's place in that system — an institutional pillar being progressively surrounded by project funding — is the current version of the tension every state academy carries: institutional money protects long-horizon work and makes redirection harder.
