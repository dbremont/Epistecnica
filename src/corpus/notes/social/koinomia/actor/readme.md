# Public Actor

> The **Public Actor** is the organization wielding delegated public authority under law — the government subtype of [Organization](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/organization.md): ministries, councils, delivery and research bodies, task groups, arm's-length agencies, state-owned enterprises, municipalities and public-private hybrids.

This is the shelf master. It defines the type, carries the decomposition, and indexes the notes filed below it.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**Public Organization belongs to the `Collective / Organization` social element type — a compound Interaction Unit with identity, membership, and rules, carrying its own agencies, roles, and practices.**

It is the government subtype of Organization: what individuates it is not its internal machinery (shared with every organization) but its constitutive relation to public authority — created or chartered under law, directed toward public purposes, and answerable through political oversight. Its layer is **Ontic**: ministries, agencies, and municipal bodies exist extra-mentally, with staff, premises, budgets, and address. Its facet is **political** — its dominant causal mechanism is authoritative rule-execution. Legal-instrument readings (statutes, charters) and economic readings (fees, commercial revenue) are prose here, not separate trees.

### What is this social instance?

> A public organization fixes *who exercises public authority and how*: the legal charter, the political principal to whom it answers, the agencies that form intention and select action, the internal governance stabilizing coordination, and the recurrent practices through which it serves, regulates, or trades. Canonical instances: ministry, council, implementation organization, policy research organization, task group, public agency, state-owned enterprise, municipal organization, nonprofit public-private organization.

## QA

### What Is a Characterization System for the `Public Organization Space`?

> `A Characterization System` for the `Public Organization Space` is a structured conceptual and methodological system for identifying, describing, distinguishing, classifying, and comparing the organizations that constitute the Public Organization Space.

> The resulting characterization should enable individual organizations to be described consistently, organizational types to be distinguished systematically, and the organization space to be analyzed as a structured domain.

> This note keeps only the Public Organization Space instantiation: the definition above and the five-dimension agency table. ### How do autonomous statutory, incorporated-administrative, executive-agency, Swedish-agency and crown-corporation forms differ?

> Along the five dimensions above: the **[Autonomous Statutory Public Agency](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/actor/type/statutory-corporation.md)** draws autonomy from its own statute and legal personality; the **[(Japan) Incorporated Administrative Agency](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/actor/type/incorporated-administrative-agency.md)** trades incorporation for bounded autonomy inside minister-set objectives; the **UK Executive Agency** trades a framework document for operational autonomy without separate legal personality; the **Swedish Government Agency** is constitutionally insulated from individual ministerial command while remaining under collective government direction; the **Crown Corporation** sits at the commercial pole — Crown-owned, board-governed, revenue-funded — bordering the Private Organization reading.

### Which are some mechanisms of organization leadership selection?

> Who selects the top leader is one of the dimensions on which the forms above differ, and it is the dimension that most visibly separates a ministry from an arm's-length agency, and both from a crown corporation or a research institute: the same autonomy that a statute or a framework document confers is a statement about who may appoint and remove the head.

| Leadership selection model | Who selects the top leader? | Typical mechanism | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct Political Appointment** | Minister / government | Political appointment | Government agencies |
| **Cabinet Appointment** | Cabinet / Council of Ministers | Formal government decision | Some European agencies |
| **Head-of-Government Appointment** | President / Prime Minister | Direct appointment | Some executive agencies |
| **Ministerial Appointment from Shortlist** | Minister | Expert search + ministerial decision | Many specialized agencies |
| **Board Appointment** | Board of Directors | Board selects CEO | State-owned corporations |
| **Board + Government Approval** | Board proposes; government approves | Two-stage appointment | Some public corporations |
| **Board Search Committee** | Search committee → Board | Competitive executive search | Research corporations |
| **Independent Search Committee** | Independent committee | Open international search → appointment | Advanced research institutions |
| **Competitive Public Recruitment** | Institution / board | Public vacancy + competitive selection | Some public institutions |
| **Academic/Scientific Peer Selection** | Scientific body / peers | Peer election or selection | Some research institutions |
| **Membership Election** | Members / association | Election by members | Fraunhofer-type associations |
| **Foundation Trustee Appointment** | Trustees | Trustees appoint executive | Foundation research institutes |
| **Shareholder Appointment** | Shareholder(s) | AGM/shareholder decision | State-owned companies |
| **Dual Appointment** | Government + board | Joint appointment authority | Hybrid public corporations |
| **Internal Succession** | Existing leadership/board | Promotion from within | Mature organizations |
| **Mixed Model** | Government + expert search + board | Multi-stage selection | Complex mission-oriented institutions |

> The table is the **mapping**, not the method: it says which selection model is available to which organization form, and what each model concedes about where the form's autonomy really sits. A statutory agency whose head the minister appoints is autonomous in its operations and not in its tenure; a research corporation that searches internationally and lets the board decide has bought distance at the price of a slower appointment.

> How an appointment should actually be run — composition types, the principles and methods of board-member selection, their integration into one selection system — is worked in [Board](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/board.md). What a form is, and who appoints its head, belongs to this note.

> Board composition, the principles and methods of board-member selection, their integration into a selection system, and the selection of executive leadership are worked in [Board](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/board.md) — the last because the executive is appointed by the board, and the appointment is therefore a board function.
