# Incorporated Administrative Agency

> The **incorporated administrative agency** (独立行政法人, *dokuritsu gyōsei hōjin*) is the Japanese public-body form introduced by the central-government reform of the late 1990s and operating from 2001: an organisation with **separate legal personality**, a **mid-term objective** set by the competent minister, a mid-term plan drawn up by the agency itself, and **ex-post evaluation** by the ministry at the end of the period. It is the compromise between a ministry — which cannot hire, contract or hold assets flexibly — and a company, which has owners and a profit test.

## The form

| Dimension | Incorporated administrative agency |
| --- | --- |
| **Creation** | A statute under the general law on independent administrative institutions; the ministry designates the body |
| **Legal personality** | Separate from the state |
| **Objective** | A **mid-term objective** (中期目標) of three to five years, set by the competent minister |
| **Plan** | A **mid-term plan** written by the agency to meet the objective and approved by the minister |
| **Funding** | Block or operating grant for the period, rather than a line-item budget — the agency allocates internally |
| **Governance** | A president and board appointed by the minister, with staff on the agency's own terms |
| **Evaluation** | Ex-post assessment against the objective by a committee of the ministry, feeding the next objective |

## Why the form

> The reform separated **policy from implementation**: the ministry keeps the objective and the evaluation, the agency keeps the operational decisions. The block grant is the enabling device — it is what lets an agency choose how to spend within a period instead of asking for each line, and it is what makes multi-year research or service programmes possible.

## The limits

> Three criticisms are standard, and they are the same three that any target-based autonomy attracts. The objective is set by the body that evaluates it, so the contract is not arm's length. A multi-year block grant softens the budget constraint — the agency is not allowed to fail, so it is not forced to economise. And senior staff still rotate in from the ministries, so the operational independence is partly formal: the form changes the budget and the hiring, less the culture.

## Instances

- **JAXA** — the space agency, an incorporated administrative agency since 2003
- **RIKEN** — the natural-sciences research institute, moved to the form in 2003
- **NIMS** — the materials-science institute, among the first cohort in 2001
