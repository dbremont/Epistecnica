# Investment

> **Investment** is the commitment of capital to an asset, project, or capability now in exchange for an expected future return — as opposed to consumption, which expends the capital.

> The state's investment is a different problem from the market's in one decisive respect: it has objectives that are not returns — mission, externality, capability, strategic autonomy — and **no single price to aggregate them**. The decision is therefore political-administrative, and its characteristic failures are political rather than computational: capture, visibility, and the inability to stop.

> This note is the decision and the mechanisms through which the state invests. The instrument space it chooses from is [Finance](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/action/problem/finance/finance.md); the market allocation it departs from is [Allocation](/view.html?p=notes/social/dynamic/finance/allocation.md); the claims it ends up holding are classified in the finance asset shelf — [Security](/view.html?p=notes/technica/production/industry/finance/asset/security.md), [Share](/view.html?p=notes/technica/production/industry/finance/asset/share.md), [Bond](/view.html?p=notes/technica/production/industry/finance/asset/bond.md), [Option](/view.html?p=notes/technica/production/industry/finance/asset/option.md).

## The allocation problem

| **Question** | **What has to be decided** | **Where it goes wrong** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **How much** | The fiscal envelope, and how much of it is committed rather than contingent | Contingent liabilities — guarantees, insurance, off-balance promises — are invisible until they are called |
| **To whom** | Sector, stage, firm size, region, or a named firm | The named firm is where allocation becomes patronage |
| **On what terms** | Loan, equity, grant, guarantee, tax expenditure; seniority; price | Terms chosen for the balance sheet rather than for the purpose |
| **For what** | The objective: capability, employment, exports, resilience, autonomy | Objectives multiplied until none constrains the decision |
| **For how long** | The horizon and the exit | No sunset, so the allocation becomes permanent by default |

## What an allocation is judged on

| **Criterion** | **The question it asks** | **The honest measure** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Market failure addressed** | Is there a reason private capital will not do this? | Evidence of the gap, not an assertion of it |
| **Additionality** | Would this have happened without the public money? | A counterfactual — the number almost nobody produces |
| **Leverage** | How much private capital does a unit of public capital move? | Private co-investment *net of what was already committed* |
| **Capability** | Does it leave the recipients able to do something they could not do before? | What the firm can now make, not what it received |
| **Autonomy** | Does it reduce a dependence that matters? | Substitutability of the input or technology in question |
| **Fiscal cost** | What is the expected loss, not the amount disbursed? | Provisioning and realised losses, including the contingent |

The criteria conflict, and pretending otherwise is where allocations acquire their alibis: a programme can maximise leverage by funding what was already bankable, or maximise capability by funding what will lose money.

## The four routes

| **Route** | **How capital reaches the firm** | **Typical forms** | **Characteristic failure** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct** | The state lends or takes equity itself | Development bank lending, sovereign equity stakes, project finance | **Soft budget constraint**: the borrower that cannot be allowed to fail because the state is the lender |
| **Intermediated** | The state capitalises funds that invest | [Mother fund](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/action/problem/finance/instrument/mother-fund.md) → [fund of funds](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/action/problem/finance/instrument/fund-of-funds.md) → firms | **Mission drift** down the chain: each layer optimises for the layer above |
| **Indirect** | The state forgoes revenue or absorbs risk | Tax credits, guarantees, subsidised insurance, accelerated depreciation | **Invisibility**: the cost never appears as spending, so it is never debated as spending |
| **Portfolio** | The state allocates as an owner | Sovereign wealth funds, holding companies | **Mandate displacement**: return logic quietly replaces the development purpose |

The routes are not substitutes. A state that lends directly bears the credit risk and the political pressure; one that intermediates buys distance at the cost of control; one that works through the tax system buys speed at the cost of visibility; one that allocates as a portfolio owner buys discipline at the cost of mission.

## The pathologies

- **Picking winners.** Not a failure to predict the winner but a failure to stop funding the loser: the political cost of exit exceeds the fiscal cost of continuation.
- **Capture.** The allocation becomes the constituency's income, and the constituency becomes the allocation's defender.
- **Crowding out.** Public capital displaces private capital that would have been there, at a lower price and with better monitoring.
- **Flagship cycles.** Allocation follows visibility: large, newsworthy projects are funded and maintenance, which is invisible, is not.
- **Measurement by disbursement.** The reported number is how much went out the door, which rises whenever money moves, whatever it achieves.

## Evaluation

Four instruments of discipline, in ascending order of how rarely they are used:

1. **Additionality testing** — a counterfactual estimate, even a crude one, of what private capital would have done. Without it, every claim of leverage is unfalsifiable.
2. **Cost per outcome** rather than capital deployed — per job, per unit of capacity created, per unit of dependence removed.
3. **Composition reporting** — where the portfolio actually sits against the mandate, which is the only measure that catches mission drift.
4. **Sunset clauses** — an allocation that must be renewed is an allocation that can be ended, and the possibility of ending it is most of the discipline available.
