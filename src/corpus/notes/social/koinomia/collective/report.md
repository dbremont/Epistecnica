# Report

> A **Report** is a social compound: an issued account of an inquiry, investigation, review or state of affairs, constituted by an issuing authority, authors, a text, a publication regime, an audience and a reception. Its content is an epistemic artifact; the report itself is the social object through which that content is made public, attributed and used.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**Report belongs to the `Social Compound` social element type, as a Kind of `Publication`** — a compound social object constituted by the people, authority, text and publication regime that make an account public.

Its layer is **Ontic** where the report exists as a document, a series, a publication record and an institutional object, and **Synontic** where its authority depends on who issued it, what mandate it carries, and how it is received. Its facet is social and ideational: the report is a social form for carrying and warranting an account.

It is not an **Epistemic Artifact** as its root: the evidence, model or argument inside the report is epistemic and is linked, not absorbed into the social tree. It is not a **Public Inquiry**: the inquiry is the actor that investigates; the report is what the inquiry issues. It is not a **White Paper**: a white paper is a kind of report, not the genus. It is not a **Statute** or a **Court Judgment**: those bind; a report recommends and informs unless a law adopts it.

Readable secondarily as an `Institution` where the report series and its publication rules are stabilized, and as an `Action Instrument` where a government uses a report to steer attention and decision. Those are prose readings, not second roots.

### What is this social instance?

> A report fixes *who speaks, with what authority, to whom, in what form*: the issuing authority that commissions or adopts it, the authors and secretariat that produce it, the evidence base it draws on, the text and recommendations it carries, the publication regime that makes it public, the audience it addresses, and the reception and uptake that decide what it becomes.

> The same text can be a report in one setting and an epistemic artifact in another. A technical paper is primarily an epistemic artifact; a commission report is primarily a social compound because its authority, addressee and institutional effect are part of what it is. The report can be official or unofficial, public or internal, serial or one-off.

> Boundary: one report kind — its issuing authority, authorship, evidence base, text, publication, audience, reception and uptake. Particular reports are instances and are not decomposed here; the content they carry is linked to epistemic space.
