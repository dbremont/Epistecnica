# Koinomia

> An institutional arrangement establishing the principles, rights, obligations, and coordination mechanisms through which multiple autonomous actors collectively govern shared resources, regulate their mutual interactions, and ensure equitable and reasonable participation in the commons.

The term is a corpus coinage from *koinos* (common) and *nomos* (law, management). It names the public arrangement through which a community governs what it holds in common, not a general theory of institutions.

## Philosophy of Commons

The commons is not a kind of good but a regime of access, use, and responsibility: a set of shared resources — land, water, fisheries, forests, knowledge, infrastructure, attention — held and governed under rules that distribute rights and duties among the people who depend on them. Its central question is not who owns the resource in the exclusive sense, but who may use it, on what terms, and who bears the costs of maintaining it.

The modern debate begins with Garrett Hardin's parable of the tragedy of the commons (1968), which treats an unmanaged common-pool resource as a race to exhaustion. Elinor Ostrom's work (1990) displaced that parable as a general law: communities frequently devise polycentric institutions — boundaries, graduated sanctions, conflict-resolution forums, nested rules — that sustain shared resources without either privatization or central command. The commons is therefore a political and normative arrangement, not a residual absence of property.

Koinomia names that arrangement in its public form: the principles, rights, obligations, and coordination mechanisms through which multiple autonomous actors govern shared resources and regulate their mutual interactions. A polity, a city-state, a tribe, or a historical micro-polity such as Coto Mixto (Couto Misto) are forms in which that arrangement becomes concrete. The commons is the shared-resource side of the same problem; it is not a separate shelf.

## Forms and Cases

- [Polity](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/polity/polity.md) — the political collective organized around governing authority over a political community, territory, or jurisdiction.
- **City-state** — a polity coinciding with a city and its hinterland; the City-State row of the polity decomposition and the city-state instances in the atlas.
- **Tribe** — a kin-based political community; the Tribe row of [Collective](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/collective/collective.md). A tribe is a koinomia when it governs shared resources and membership; it need not be a polity.
- **Coto Mixto** (*Couto Misto*) — a historical border micro-polity and shared-sovereignty arrangement between Galicia and Portugal, an example of a public order that does not fit the modern state model.
