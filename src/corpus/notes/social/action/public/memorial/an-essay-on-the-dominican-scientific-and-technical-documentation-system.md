# An Essay on the Problem of Scientific and Technical Documentation

In this document, we examine the concept of a national scientific and technical documentation system.


## What problem does this system solve?]

> The absence of an organized national capacity to collect, preserve, organize, retrieve, and circulate the scientific and technical documentation produced domestically and available internationally.

| Scope | Description |
|---|---|
| Scientific publications | Collection, organization, preservation, and dissemination of formal scientific publications. |
| Technical publications | Documentation of technical knowledge, engineering developments, and applied research. |
| Research reports | Documentation of research activities, investigations, and their results. |
| Theses and dissertations | Collection and preservation of academic research produced by higher education institutions. |
| Patents and inventions | Documentation of inventions, technological developments, and intellectual property. |
| Standards and specifications | Documentation of technical standards, specifications, and established procedures. |
| Scientific and technical datasets | Organization and preservation of data generated through scientific investigations and technical activities. |
| Scientific and technical databases | Integration and provision of access to specialized scientific and technical databases. |
| Research infrastructure documentation | Information about the country's scientific and technical infrastructure and its resources. |
| Research personnel documentation | Information about scientific and technical personnel and their research activities. |
| Research institutions documentation | Information about organizations engaged in scientific and technical activities. |
| Historical scientific and technical documentation | Preservation of accumulated scientific and technical knowledge and historical records. |
| International scientific and technical documentation | Acquisition and organization of relevant foreign scientific and technical literature. |

### Which are the guiding principles behind the incentive structure?

| Guiding Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| Contribution | Institutions and researchers should receive benefits and recognition for contributing scientific and technical documentation to the system. |
| Recognition | Scientific and technical outputs should be properly attributed, acknowledged, and made visible. |
| Reciprocity | Institutions that contribute information should also benefit from access to the collective national information resource. |
| Low Participation Costs | Submission, registration, and documentation procedures should impose minimal additional administrative and technical burdens. |
| Collective Benefit | Incentives should promote the preservation, accessibility, circulation, and reuse of national scientific and technical knowledge rather than institutional information monopolies. |
| Quality | Incentives should encourage documentary completeness, accuracy, consistency, and compliance with established information standards rather than merely maximizing submission volume. |
| Inclusiveness | The incentive structure should accommodate institutions with different levels of scientific, technical, financial, and documentary capacity. |
| Sustainability | Incentives should encourage long-term institutional commitments to documentation, preservation, and information sharing. |

## What incentive structure should guide the system?

> The incentive structure should encourage institutions and individuals to produce, submit, document, organize, preserve, share, and reuse scientific and technical information.

| Incentive | Description | Intended Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional Recognition | Recognition of institutions that systematically contribute scientific and technical documentation. | Encourage institutional participation and regular contributions. |
| Research Visibility | Increased visibility of researchers, research institutions, and their scientific and technical outputs. | Encourage researchers to document and disseminate their work. |
| Publication Recognition | Recognition of scientific and technical publications incorporated into the national system. | Encourage formal publication and documentation of research outputs. |
| Research Funding Conditions | Incorporation of documentation and information-deposit requirements into publicly funded research agreements. | Ensure publicly funded research produces accessible documentary outputs. |
| Institutional Performance Indicators | Inclusion of documentation contributions, repository maintenance, and information accessibility in institutional evaluations. | Encourage institutions to maintain active documentation practices. |
| Simplified Submission Procedures | Provision of standardized, low-cost mechanisms for submitting and registering documents. | Reduce the administrative burden associated with participation. |
| Intellectual Property Protection | Clear rules governing authorship, attribution, licensing, confidentiality, and intellectual property. | Encourage contributions without compromising legitimate ownership rights. |
| Access and Reuse | Promotion of open access and standardized reuse conditions wherever legally and technically possible. | Maximize the circulation and practical utilization of scientific and technical information. |
| Institutional Capacity Building | Technical assistance, training, and infrastructure support for participating institutions. | Reduce disparities in documentary and information-management capabilities. |
| Data and Documentation Standards | Adoption of common metadata, classification, bibliographic, and documentary standards. | Improve consistency, interoperability, and information quality. |
| International Integration | Recognition and support for contributions that improve the international discoverability of national scientific production. | Encourage participation in international scientific information networks. |
| Long-term Preservation | Institutional responsibilities and incentives for maintaining repositories and historical documentation. | Prevent the loss of accumulated scientific and technical knowledge. |

## How is a solution to be evaluated?

| Criterion | Description |
|---|---|
| Documentary Coverage | Extent to which the system collects and incorporates the different categories of scientific and technical documentation within its defined scope. |
| Institutional Coverage | Extent to which universities, research institutions, laboratories, government agencies, and other relevant organizations are represented. |
| Documentary Completeness | Extent to which collected documents contain the necessary information, metadata, and associated materials. |
| Information Accessibility | Ease with which users can locate, retrieve, consult, and access scientific and technical documents. |
| Information Discoverability | Capacity to identify relevant documents through search, indexing, classification, and bibliographic services. |
| Classification and Organization | Consistency and adequacy of the systems used to classify, catalogue, index, and organize documentation. |
| Information Quality | Accuracy, consistency, reliability, legibility, and integrity of the information maintained. |
| Processing Capacity | Capacity to collect, catalogue, abstract, classify, index, and process documentary materials. |
| Timeliness | Time elapsed between the production or publication of a document and its incorporation into the system. |
| Preservation | Capacity to maintain the long-term integrity, availability, and accessibility of scientific and technical documentation. |
| Interoperability | Capacity to exchange, integrate, and connect information with other national and international information systems. |
| International Coverage | Extent to which relevant foreign scientific and technical documentation is incorporated and made accessible. |
| User Coverage | Diversity of users and institutions that can effectively access and utilize the system. |
| Operational Efficiency | Relationship between the resources consumed and the volume and quality of documentary services provided. |
| Sustainability | Capacity to maintain the system's institutional, financial, technological, and operational functions over time. |
| National Scientific Visibility | Extent to which the system makes domestic scientific and technical production discoverable and accessible nationally and internationally. |

## How is the operation to be evaluated?

> The operational evaluation concerns how effectively the system performs its functions: collecting, receiving, processing, organizing, preserving, and disseminating scientific and technical documentation.

| Criterion | Description |
|---|---|
| Document Acquisition | Effectiveness in acquiring scientific and technical documentation from domestic and international sources. |
| Institutional Participation | Degree of participation and regularity of contributions from universities, research institutions, laboratories, and other participating organizations. |
| Submission Processing Time | Time required to receive, register, validate, and incorporate submitted documents. |
| Processing Throughput | Volume of documents processed and incorporated within a given period. |
| Processing Accuracy | Accuracy and consistency of bibliographic descriptions, abstracts, metadata, classifications, and indexing records. |
| Documentary Quality Control | Effectiveness in identifying incomplete, illegible, inconsistent, or improperly formatted submissions. |
| Classification Effectiveness | Consistency and adequacy of documentary classification, indexing, and subject categorization. |
| Information Retrieval Performance | Effectiveness in retrieving relevant documents in response to user queries. |
| Information Delivery | Effectiveness and speed in providing users with access to requested documentation. |
| System Availability | Availability and operational continuity of information platforms, databases, and repositories. |
| Preservation Effectiveness | Effectiveness of backup, archival, integrity verification, and digital preservation procedures. |
| Interoperability Performance | Effectiveness in exchanging and synchronizing information with external databases, repositories, and information systems. |
| Service Responsiveness | Time required to respond to user requests, resolve access problems, and address information-related inquiries. |
| Operational Cost | Financial, technological, and human resources required to perform the system's operational functions. |
| Error and Correction Rate | Frequency of errors in document registration, metadata, classification, indexing, and information retrieval, including their correction rates. |

## What is the history of such systems?

| Period | Event | Description |
|---|---|---|
| c. 2600 BCE | Ebla Royal Archives | Large-scale collection of administrative, diplomatic, economic, and literary clay tablets in the ancient city of Ebla (Syria). One of the earliest extensively documented institutional text collections. |
| c. 2500 BCE | Mesopotamian temple and palace collections | Sumerian cities developed institutional repositories of administrative records, lexical lists, mathematical texts, legal documents, and scholarly materials. |
| c. 2000 BCE | Nippur scholarly collections | Collections of mathematical, literary, lexical, and religious tablets were maintained and reproduced by scribal institutions in Mesopotamia. |
| c. 1800 BCE | Mari Royal Archives | The royal palace at Mari maintained extensive collections of diplomatic correspondence, administrative records, economic documents, and political reports. |
| c. 1600–1200 BCE | Hittite Royal Archives at Hattusa | The Hittite capital developed substantial palace and temple archives containing diplomatic treaties, legal texts, historical records, religious literature, and administrative documents. |
| c. 1400–1200 BCE | Ugarit Royal Archives | The city of Ugarit maintained palace and temple collections containing diplomatic correspondence, commercial records, multilingual texts, and literary works. |
| c. 700 BCE | Library of Ashurbanipal | The Assyrian king Ashurbanipal assembled an extensive collection of cuneiform tablets at Nineveh, including mathematics, astronomy, medicine, divination, literature, and administrative knowledge. |
| c. 300 BCE | Library of Alexandria | The Ptolemaic rulers established a major scholarly library associated with the Mouseion. It sought to collect, organize, copy, edit, and study literary and scientific works from across the known world. |
| c. 260 BCE | Library of Pergamon | The Attalid rulers established an important scholarly library in Pergamon, supporting the collection, reproduction, and study of Greek literary and scholarly works. |
| 221 BCE | Qin imperial documentary centralization | The Qin state unified administrative documentation and established centralized control over official texts, archives, and written standards. |
| 26 BCE | Han imperial bibliographic project | Emperor Cheng commissioned Liu Xiang to collect, collate, edit, and describe the texts held in imperial collections. This project contributed to one of China's earliest systematic bibliographic classification systems. |
| 39 BCE | First major public library in Rome | Asinius Pollio established Rome's first public library, creating an important precedent for public access to organized literary collections. |
| 4th century CE | Imperial Library of Constantinople | The Byzantine imperial capital developed institutions for preserving, copying, and transmitting Greek and Roman literary and scholarly works. |
| 5th century CE | Nalanda Mahavihara | The Buddhist monastic and educational complex in India developed into a major international centre of scholarship, with substantial manuscript collections and teaching activities. |
| 6th century CE | Monastery of Saint Catherine | The monastery in Sinai developed an important manuscript library preserving Greek, Syriac, Arabic, and other textual traditions. |
| 6th century CE | Cassiodorus' Vivarium | Cassiodorus established a monastic scholarly institution in southern Italy, organizing manuscript copying, preservation, and the transmission of classical and Christian texts. |
| 8th century CE | Tang imperial libraries and scholarly collections | The Tang dynasty expanded imperial textual collections, bibliographic organization, manuscript reproduction, and scholarly compilation. |
| 8th century CE | Shōsōin repository, Japan | The imperial repository at Nara preserved an extensive collection of documents, books, objects, and records, providing an important historical example of institutional preservation. |
| 8th–9th centuries | Carolingian scriptoria and libraries | Monasteries and cathedral institutions across the Carolingian Empire expanded manuscript copying, textual correction, preservation, and educational collections. |
| 8th–10th centuries | House of Wisdom (Bayt al-Hikma), Baghdad | The Abbasid court developed an important centre associated with the collection, translation, and study of Greek, Persian, and Indian scholarly works. |
| 10th century | Library of Córdoba | Under the Umayyad Caliphate of Córdoba, particularly during the reign of al-Hakam II, Andalusia developed important courtly book collections and scholarly networks. |
| 972 CE | Al-Azhar Mosque and scholarly institution | Established in Cairo under the Fatimids, Al-Azhar became an important centre of Islamic education and textual scholarship. |
| 1005 CE | Dar al-Ilm, Cairo | The Fatimid caliph al-Hakim established the House of Knowledge, an institution providing access to books, scholarly instruction, and intellectual activities. |
| 11th century | Nizamiyya educational institutions | The Seljuk vizier Nizam al-Mulk established a network of madrasas, including the Baghdad Nizamiyya (1067), supporting organized teaching, scholarly activity, and book collections. |
| 11th–12th centuries | European monastic and cathedral libraries | Monasteries and cathedral institutions expanded their collections, manuscript production, textual preservation, and scholarly organization. |
| 12th century | Toledo Translation Movement | Toledo became an important centre for translating Arabic scientific and philosophical works into Latin, facilitating the transmission of mathematics, astronomy, medicine, and philosophy into Western Europe. |
| 12th–13th centuries | European university libraries | The emergence of universities in Bologna, Paris, Oxford, and other cities created new institutional settings for organizing, reproducing, circulating, and consulting scholarly texts. |
| 1237–1248 | Tripitaka Koreana | The Goryeo dynasty sponsored the carving of an extensive collection of Buddhist canonical texts onto approximately 81,000 wooden printing blocks. |
| 14th century | Timbuktu scholarly collections | Timbuktu developed as an important centre of Islamic scholarship, manuscript production, teaching, and book collecting, supported by scholarly families and educational institutions. |
| 15th century | Renaissance humanist libraries | European rulers, scholars, and institutions expanded manuscript collections, philological research, classical-text recovery, and the systematic organization of books. |
| c. 1450 | Gutenberg printing revolution | Movable-type printing transformed the reproduction, circulation, preservation, and accessibility of written knowledge in Europe. |
| 1475 | Vatican Library | Pope Sixtus IV formally established the Vatican Library, consolidating an important institutional collection of manuscripts and printed works. |
| 1545 | Royal Library of France | Francis I's royal library, developed through earlier royal collections, became an increasingly important repository of manuscripts and printed works. |
| 16th century | Ottoman imperial libraries | The Ottoman court and educational institutions developed significant manuscript collections supporting religious scholarship, administration, history, medicine, astronomy, and other fields. |
| 16th century | Mughal imperial library | The Mughal court developed extensive manuscript collections and translation activities, particularly under Akbar, incorporating Persian, Arabic, Sanskrit, and other intellectual traditions. |
| 1665 | Emergence of scientific periodicals | The establishment of *Philosophical Transactions* of the Royal Society and *Journal des sçavans* created recurring channels for communicating scientific discoveries. |
| 1857 | Development of scientific abstracting | The Chemical Society of Paris began publishing scientific abstracts, reflecting the growing need to organize and summarize expanding scientific literature. |
| 1867 | Royal Society Catalogue of Scientific Papers | The Royal Society of London began publishing a systematic catalogue of scientific papers, establishing an important precedent for large-scale bibliographic indexing. |
| 1895 | International Catalogue of Scientific Literature | The Royal Society initiated an international project to coordinate the systematic indexing of scientific publications across countries. |
| 1901 | International Catalogue becomes operational | National bureaux contributed classified bibliographic records to a central international publication system, establishing an early model of international scientific information cooperation. |
| 1931 | International Institute for Documentation | The International Institute for Documentation emerged from the International Institute of Bibliography, advancing systematic documentation, classification, and information retrieval. |
| 1937 | American Documentation Institute | Established in the United States to advance documentation techniques, including microphotography, information storage, and retrieval. |
| 1939–1945 | World War II scientific information mobilization | Governments developed extensive mechanisms for collecting, processing, translating, and distributing technical intelligence and research documentation. |
| 1945 | US Publications Board | Established to collect, declassify, preserve, and disseminate scientific and technical information generated during the war, including captured German and Japanese technical documentation. |
| 1945 | Postwar scientific research institutionalization | The publication of Vannevar Bush's *Science, the Endless Frontier* helped establish the institutional basis for sustained federal support of scientific research and information dissemination in the United States. |
| 1946 | US Office of Technical Services | The Department of Commerce established OTS, consolidating technical information dissemination activities and succeeding the Publications Board's functions. |
| 1950 | US National Science Foundation | The NSF was established with responsibilities that included facilitating scientific information exchange and coordination. |
| 1952 | Soviet Institute of Scientific Information | The USSR established the Institute of Scientific Information under the Academy of Sciences, initiating the institutional development of VINITI. |
| 1952 | Indian National Scientific Documentation Centre | INSDOC was established to provide scientific documentation, abstracting, indexing, translation, and information services. |
| 1954 | Brazilian Institute of Bibliography and Documentation | IBBD was established to develop national bibliographic and scientific documentation capabilities. |
| 1955 | Establishment of VINITI | The Soviet Institute of Scientific Information was reorganized as the All-Union Institute for Scientific and Technical Information (VINITI), becoming a central institution for scientific abstracting and information processing. |
| 1957 | Japan Information Center of Science and Technology | JICST was established to organize and disseminate scientific and technical information in Japan. |
| 1957 | Canadian National Science Library | Canada established a national scientific library infrastructure to support access to scientific and technical documentation. |
| 1959 | German National Library of Science and Technology | TIB was established in Hannover as a national specialized library for scientific and technical literature. |
| 1962 | Korean Scientific and Technological Information Center | KORSTIC was established, marking the institutional development of South Korea's national scientific and technical information infrastructure. |
| 1965 | British Office for Scientific and Technical Information | The UK established OSTI to promote the coordination and development of scientific and technical information services. |
