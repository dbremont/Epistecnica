# Medical Scientist Training Program

> The **Medical Scientist Training Program** (MSTP) is the American dual-degree training program that funds a small number of students through both a clinical doctorate and a research doctorate — MD–PhD, and in a few cases DO–PhD or DVM–PhD — so that they leave able to move between the ward and the laboratory. It is not a degree but a **funding designation**: a medical school's MD–PhD program becomes an MSTP when the National Institutes of Health awards it a training grant, and loses the designation when the grant does not survive review.

> Its objective is a kind of person, not a product: the **physician-scientist** who can translate a laboratory finding into a treatment and a clinical problem into a research question. That is why the program is judged by careers rather than by papers, and why its funding model — a training grant held by an institution — makes the institution, not the student, the applicant.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**The Medical Scientist Training Program belongs to the `Program` social element type (Action category, `Action Organization` branch, Ontic layer), kind `Public Program` → `Training Program`.**

It is a program because it is a coordinated set of projects under one objective: a set of member MD–PhD programs, a training grant, a review cycle, and one workforce goal. It is a **public** program because a federal agency funds it toward a collective good, and a **training** program because its projects produce trained people rather than a service or a product.

It is distinct from the `Project` (a single student's training is an effort inside it), from the `Policy` (the NIH's course of action it executes), and from the `University` or medical school that hosts a member program (the actor; the MSTP is the funded arrangement around the actor's effort). Readable secondarily as a `Research Program` — the training is research training — and as an `Assistance Program` — it transfers stipend and tuition support. Those readings are prose, not second roots.

### What is this social instance?

> A federal training-grant program: the National Institute of General Medical Sciences designates institutions, each award funds a cohort of students through tuition, stipend and research support, a review cycle renews or ends the designation, and the member programs recruit, teach and graduate. As of 2026 there are 69 funded programs — 64 MD–PhD, 1 DO–PhD and 4 DVM–PhD — supporting more than five thousand students at all stages.

## History

> Dual-degree training existed before the federal program: Case Western Reserve began an MD–PhD program in **1956**, and other research-intensive schools followed. In **1964** the NIH created the MSTP designation to fund that training, with **Albert Einstein College of Medicine**, **Northwestern** and **NYU** as the first three programs.

> The program expanded in waves — Duke, Stanford, Chicago, Wisconsin, Columbia, Penn, Washington University and Yale by 1969; a cluster in the 1970s; a slower spread from the 1980s to the 2000s; and a new group of awards in the 2020s. In **2022** the NIH added the **LEAD MSTP**, aimed at institutions not historically represented among funded programs — historically Black colleges and universities, tribal colleges, and institutions in IDeA-eligible states — with the first award at the University of Nebraska Medical Center in 2024.

## Admissions and Funding

| Element | What it is | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Applicant pool** | Students with strong grades, MCAT scores and substantial research experience | The most competitive graduate medical training in the country |
| **Selection** | Institution-level admissions, with research interviews and often a multi-day process | The program selects for research aptitude, not only for clinical promise |
| **Funding** | Tuition, stipend, travel and supplies from the T32 | Removes the debt that would otherwise push graduates toward clinical practice |
| **Eligibility** | National Research Service Award rules — US nationals and certain registered non-citizens | Non-MSTP positions exist for others at the same institutions |
| **Duration** | Typically seven to eight years, with the PhD phase between clinical years | The opportunity cost is the reason the stipend matters |

> The economics are the design. An MD–PhD takes years longer than an MD and pays less than either a full clinical career or a full research career in the short run; the stipend and the tuition waiver are what make the longer path viable for a student who is not independently wealthy. The program is therefore an intervention in the **composition** of the research workforce, not only in its training.

## Outcomes

> A 2010 NIGMS study of graduates from the 1970s to the 2010s reported that 95 % entered a residency after graduation, and that MSTP graduates were roughly three times more likely to be successfully funded on NIH research grants than MD–PhD graduates who had not been in an MSTP. The finding is the program's own justification: the designation is associated with research persistence, and the association is what the renewal review weighs.

## Non-MSTP and Allied Programs

> The MSTP is a designation, not the whole of dual-degree training. As of 2026, 63 US institutions run MD–PhD programs without an MSTP award, sometimes with institutional funding, and 34 non-US institutions offer MD–PhD training. The **allied-institution** arrangement lets a member send its students to a partner research institute for the PhD phase — a way to buy research depth the home campus does not have.
