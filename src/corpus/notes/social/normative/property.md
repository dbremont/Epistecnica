# Property

> **Property** is the institution under which things are held: the arrangement of rights, titles and recognised claims through which an object becomes someone's rather than no one's, and through which it can be used, transferred, pledged, inherited and defended. It is what makes ownership a standing rather than a fact of control.

> The word carries two senses that must be kept apart. This note documents **property as ownership** — the institution that holds objects. The attribute sense, a characteristic predicated of a bearer, belongs to the `Social Property` shelf at [Property](/view.html?p=notes/social/property/property.md) and is a different concept that shares the name.

## Formulation

### What is this social instance?

> Property is the arrangement that turns a thing into a holding. It answers four questions at once: who may use the object and on what terms, who may exclude others from it, who may transfer it and to whom, and who bears the consequences when it is destroyed or taken. A society has property when those answers are settled in advance and can be relied on by people who are not the holder.

> Its history is a sequence of rearrangements rather than the career of a single institution. Roman *dominium* separated ownership from possession and gave both legal form; feudal tenure dissolved ownership into a ladder of obligations; the modern codes concentrated it again into a single absolute right; the corporation separated the shareholder's claim from the manager's control; and the register — land cadastre, share ledger, digital token — moved the institution's centre of gravity from the thing to the record of it, until the record itself began to execute the transfer. That last step is the one that needs care: a title instrument that runs its own rules is still an instrument, and what it holds is still determined by a regime that no ledger contains.

> Canonical instances: freehold title to land; shareholder equity in a company; cooperative membership in an enterprise; customary tenure in a commons; state ownership of a public utility; and the register entry that stands in for the object when the object cannot be handed over.

### What social element type does this instance belong to?

**Property belongs to `Social Compound`** — `Social Element (→ Kind)` `Ontic` `(→ Kind)` `Social Compound` — a compound constituted by several social elements and their organised relations: the property rights themselves, the regime that fixes who may hold them, the titles that record them, and the apparatus that enforces and recognises them.

It is **not a relation**: the relation is one of its components, documented at [Property Rights](/view.html?p=notes/social/normative/property-right.md). It is **not an attribute**: the attribute sense of the word is documented on the `Social Property` shelf. And it is **not an object**: the object is what the institution ranges over, documented at [Asset](/view.html?p=notes/social/synontic/asset.md).

Read as **Ontic** where it exists in courts, registers, fences and police; as **Synontic** where it exists in the mutual recognition that makes a title mean something. A register that nobody honours records nothing.

> The title system has now acquired a digital instrument, and the object domain a virtual member: an entry that is both the record of the holding and — where the platform enforces it — the only place the holding exists. The relation note decomposes that case at [Property Rights](/view.html?p=notes/social/normative/property-right.md) (its virtual object and tokenized representation); what the works cited below argue is the limited point that the token lowers the cost of reading a holding and does not by itself create the regime that makes the reading binding.

### Which facet does this element belong to, if applicable?

> Legal first: the institution is constituted by the rules that allocate, record and enforce holdings. Economic where it organises investment, credit and exchange; political where regimes are legislated, redistributed or expropriated; normative where its justification is contested. See [Facet](/view.html?p=notes/social/meta/facet.md).

### What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this social element instance?

> Evaluated with the concept-assessment criteria of [Philosophia Conceptuum et Operis](/view.html?p=notes/philosophia-conceptuum-et-operis.md), at its minimum set:

> **Verdict:** the institution is well identified and structurally coherent — rights, regimes, titles and enforcement are separable components and the decomposition keeps them apart — but the concept is under-determined at its boundaries, where it meets contract, regulation and mere control. Its comparative and historical reach is high; its precision as a single term is low, which is why the decomposition names a regime and a title before it says "property".

| **Category** | **Criteria** | **Property** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conceptual Specification | **Conceptual Identification** | Moderate–High (distinguished from the attribute sense, from the relation and from the object; confused in ordinary use with ownership as a title) |
| Conceptual Specification | **Definitional Clarity** | High (the institution is stated by what it settles — use, exclusion, transfer, liability) |
| Conceptual Specification | **Conceptual Determinacy** | Moderate (the boundary with contract and regulation is drawn by argument, and customary tenure stretches it) |
| Conceptual Specification | **Internal Consistency** | High (regimes, titles and enforcement presuppose one another rather than contradicting) |
| Representational Adequacy | **Representational Adequacy** | High (fits private, public, cooperative, common and mixed forms under one description) |
| Normative and Strategic Adequacy | **Purpose Adequacy** | High (adequate for comparing regimes and for allocation questions; not adequate as a term of art without naming the regime and the title) |

## Why property is not ownership of a thing

> The institution is a compound of relations among persons, and the object enters only as what those relations are about. That is why a register can stand in for the object when the object cannot be handed over, and why two regimes can hold the same field under arrangements that share nothing but the word.
