## Intermediaries and the lab-to-factory problem

> Technology transfer is not a single transaction. It is a chain of matching, adaptation and absorption: a result has to be found, its use has to be understood, the receiving organisation has to be able to adopt it, and the capability has to survive after the first project. Intermediaries exist because the two sides of that chain rarely meet on their own.

The **technology achievement supermarket** at the Shenzhen Institutes of Advanced Technology (SIAT) is a recent case. Its shelves carry cards for more than a thousand frontier results from SIAT's ten research divisions, sorted by track and linked by QR code to the technology's highlights, application scenarios and developer contact. The model is not a marketplace in the ordinary sense: it is a matching and translation intermediary between public research and industrial demand. See the [China note](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/polity/instance/chn/prc/china.md) for the case and the article.

The general problem is older than the supermarket. Joint ventures, licensing, contract research and public technology-transfer offices are all attempts to move a technical element across an organisational boundary when the receiver cannot yet absorb it. The literature below treats that movement as a production and capability-building process, not merely as a legal transaction.
