# Technology Acquisition

> Technology acquisition is not a purchase. It is a staged capability ladder: a country or firm identifies a gap, selects a foreign solution, obtains it, absorbs it, reproduces it, adapts it, improves it, and only then begins to develop technology of its own. Buying the equipment is one step; the capability is built across the whole sequence.

> The usual failure is to stop at acquisition. The licence is signed, the plant is imported, the equipment arrives — and the capability does not. What looks like a transfer is only a delivery.

## The ladder

| Stage | Question | What it produces | Characteristic failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Technology identification** | What do we lack, and what advanced solution exists elsewhere? | A map of gaps and candidate solutions | A wish list without an industrial priority |
| **2. Technology selection** | Which foreign technology fits our industrial requirements? | A justified choice among alternatives | Choosing the most advanced rather than the most usable |
| **3. Technology acquisition** | How do we obtain it — licence, purchase, recruitment, contract or other? | Access to the technology and its rights | Buying the artefact without the right to use or adapt it |
| **4. Technical absorption** | Can we understand and operate it? | Engineering capability and operating knowledge | Dependence on the seller's engineers |
| **5. Reproduction** | Can we reconstruct the equipment, process or components? | Local capability to reproduce the element | Copying without understanding the process |
| **6. Adaptation** | Can we fit it to domestic production conditions? | A version that works locally | Adaptation that quietly degrades the specification |
| **7. Improvement** | Can we raise performance, reliability, productivity and quality? | Incremental technical progress | Permanent followership behind the original |
| **8. Indigenous development** | Can we develop proprietary technological capability? | New technology of one's own | A capability that never leaves the licence |

## What the stages mean

**Identification** is not a shopping list. It begins from the industrial structure: which activities are constrained by a missing technique, which gaps are strategic, and which foreign solutions are close enough to be absorbed. A gap that cannot be staffed is not an acquisition opportunity.

**Selection** evaluates technologies against the domestic requirement, not against a global ranking. The relevant question is not "what is the most advanced?" but "what can be operated, maintained, adapted and improved here?"

**Acquisition** is the transaction: a licence, a purchase, a recruitment, a contract, a joint venture or an in-house development. It is the stage most easily mistaken for the whole. The market for technology — its licences, know-how, services and embedded capital goods — is treated in [Technology Transfer Market](/view.html?p=notes/social/coordinator/market/technology-transfer-market.md).

**Absorption** is the engineering work that turns access into competence: understanding the principle, operating the process, diagnosing failure, maintaining the equipment and training the people who will do it without the seller.

**Reproduction** is the test of whether the capability is real. Reconstructing a component or a process is a different act from operating the imported version; it exposes the parts of the technology that were never written down.

**Adaptation** is where the local conditions enter: raw materials, climate, scale, labour, infrastructure, regulation and the existing production system. The adapted version is not a lesser copy if it still meets the requirement.

**Improvement** turns the acquired technology into a moving target: better yield, reliability, productivity and quality. It is the first stage in which the adopter begins to set the direction rather than follow it.

**Indigenous development** is the end of dependence and the beginning of a different relation: a capability that can originate, not only receive. It is not a single project; it is the accumulation of people, laboratories, suppliers and failure experience.

## The ladder is not automatic

Each stage has its own condition. Acquisition without absorption is delivery; absorption without reproduction is dependence; reproduction without adaptation is copying; adaptation without improvement is catch-up that never closes; improvement without indigenous development is permanent followership.

The sequence is also not strictly linear. A firm may reproduce before it fully absorbs, adapt while improving, or return to selection when a technology proves unusable. The value of the ladder is diagnostic: it locates the stage at which the capability stopped.
