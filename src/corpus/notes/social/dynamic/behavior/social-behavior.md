# Social Behavior

> **Social behavior** is the pattern of action by interaction units when it is oriented to others and constituted in interaction — what actors do to, with, for, or against one another.

Behavior is a series of events and transformations over time ([Behavior](/view.html?p=notes/social/dynamic/behavior/behavior.md)). The social reading adds the other: an act is social when its meaning and its consequence depend on how others act, expect, or respond. It is not a single act ([Action](/view.html?p=notes/social/dynamic/action/action.md)), nor the rule that regulates it ([Norm](/view.html?p=notes/social/normative/norm.md)); it is the regularity those produce in the interaction between actors ([Interaction Unit](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/interaction-unit.md)).

## Forms of social behavior

| **Form** | **What it is** | **Canonical case** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cooperation** | Acting together toward a joint outcome | The cooperative outcome of a prisoner's dilemma |
| **Competition** | Acting to secure a scarce good against others | Market rivalry |
| **Conflict / aggression** | Acting to prevail over or harm another | Disputes, war, punishment |
| **Altruism / [prosociality](/view.html?p=notes/social/dynamic/behavior/prosocial-behavior.md)** | Costly action for another's benefit | Helping, donating, punishing free-riders |
| **Reciprocity** | Returning benefit for benefit, harm for harm | Gift exchange, retaliation |
| **Conformity** | Aligning judgment or conduct with the group | Asch's line experiments |
| **Obedience** | Complying with an authority's instruction | Milgram's obedience experiments |
| **Imitation** | Reproducing another's act | Tarde's laws of imitation, social learning |
| **Coordination** | Aligning actions without a common purpose | Schelling's meeting problem |
| **Collective action** | Joint action for a public good | Ostrom's common-pool resources |
| **[Herding](/view.html?p=notes/social/dynamic/behavior/herd-behavior.md) / cascades** | Following others' observed choices | Informational cascades, bank runs, fashions |
| **Social exchange** | Behavior as the trading of rewards and costs | Homans, Blau, Coleman |
| **Norm-following** | Acting as the norm requires, under sanction | Bicchieri, Elster |
| **Exit and voice** | Responding to decline by leaving or protesting | [Hirschman's framework](/view.html?p=notes/social/dynamic/behavior/exit-voice-loyalty.md) |
| **[Nepotism](/view.html?p=notes/social/dynamic/behavior/nepotism.md)** | Favoring kin and close ties | Family firms, patronage |
| **[Xenophobia](/view.html?p=notes/social/dynamic/behavior/xenophobia.md)** | Hostility to the out-group | [Ethnophaulism](/view.html?p=notes/social/dynamic/behavior/etnofaulismo.md) and group boundaries |

## Explanatory traditions

| **Tradition** | **Core claim** | **Key authors** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rational choice / exchange** | Behavior is the pursuit of rewards and the avoidance of costs under constraints | Homans (1958), Blau (1964), Coleman (1990) |
| **Symbolic interactionism** | Behavior is constituted in the meanings actors give to one another's acts | Mead (1934), Blumer (1969), Goffman (1959) |
| **Structural-functionalism** | Behavior is regulated by shared norms and the needs of the social system | Durkheim (1893), Parsons (1937), Merton (1949) |
| **Conflict** | Behavior is the pursuit of interests within unequal relations | Simmel (1908), Coser (1956) |
| **Social psychology** | Behavior is shaped by the presence, judgment and example of others | Asch (1951), Milgram (1963), Tajfel & Turner (1979), Bandura (1977) |
| **Behavioural economics** | Behavior departs systematically from the rational model | Kahneman & Tversky (1979), Gigerenzer (1999), Thaler & Sunstein (2008) |
| **Cultural evolution** | Behavior is transmitted and selected through social learning | Boyd & Richerson (1985), Henrich et al. (2010) |
| **Relational / network** | Behavior is embedded in the structure of ties | Granovetter (1973, 1985), Watts & Strogatz (1998), Barabási & Albert (1999) |
| **Practice / structuration** | Behavior reproduces the structures that produce it | Bourdieu (1972), Giddens (1984), Elias (1939) |

## Historical literature

- **Aristotle**, *Politics* — the human as a political animal, constituted by the *polis* rather than merely living in it.
- **Hobbes**, *Leviathan* (1651) — social order as the solution to the war of all against all.
- **Mandeville**, *The Fable of the Bees* (1714) — private vices, public benefits: the paradox of self-interested behavior producing collective good.
- **Hume**, *A Treatise of Human Nature* (1739–40) — sympathy as the mechanism of moral and social conduct.
- **Smith**, *The Theory of Moral Sentiments* (1759) — the impartial spectator and the social passions that precede the market.
- **Rousseau**, *Discourse on Inequality* (1755) — *amour-propre*, the comparison with others that makes social behavior a source of corruption.
- **Tarde**, *Les Lois de l'imitation* (1890) — imitation as the elementary social mechanism; society as repeated invention and imitation.
- **Le Bon**, *The Crowd* (1895) — the crowd as a distinct behavioral entity: suggestibility, contagion, deindividuation.
- **Durkheim**, *The Division of Labour in Society* (1893), *Suicide* (1897), *The Elementary Forms of Religious Life* (1912) — collective representations, social integration, and the social determination of individual acts.
- **Simmel**, *Soziologie* (1908) — the forms of sociation: conflict, exchange, secrecy, and the dyad and triad as elementary social structures.
- **Weber**, *Economy and Society* (1922) — the four types of social action: instrumental-rational, value-rational, affectual, and traditional.
- **Mauss**, *The Gift* (1925) — the triple obligation to give, receive and return; exchange as a total social fact.
- **Veblen**, *The Theory of the Leisure Class* (1899) — conspicuous consumption as social emulation.
- **Mead**, *Mind, Self and Society* (1934) — the self as internalized conversation of gestures; taking the role of the other.
- **Parsons**, *The Structure of Social Action* (1937) — action as normatively oriented, against utilitarian reduction.
- **Elias**, *The Civilizing Process* (1939) — the long-run transformation of behavior through the monopoly of violence and the courtly habitus.
- **Goffman**, *The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life* (1959) — social behavior as performance before an audience, front-stage and back-stage.
- **Bourdieu**, *Outline of a Theory of Practice* (1972) — habitus, the durable dispositions that generate behavior without explicit rules.
- **Giddens**, *The Constitution of Society* (1984) — structuration: behavior reproduces the structures that enable it.

## Empirical and theoretical papers

### Conformity, obedience and influence

- **Asch, S. E. (1951, 1956)** — group pressure produces conformity even against the evidence of the senses.
- **Milgram, S. (1963)** — ordinary people obey an authority's instruction to harm another.
- **Zajonc, R. B. (1965)** — social facilitation: the presence of others improves performance on simple tasks and impairs it on complex ones.
- **Cialdini, R. B. (1984)** — the influence principles: reciprocity, commitment, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity.

### Cooperation, reciprocity and collective action

- **Axelrod, R. (1984)** — tit-for-tat sustains cooperation among self-interested actors.
- **Ostrom, E. (1990)** — communities solve common-pool problems without privatization or the state.
- **Fehr, E., & Gächter, S. (2002)** — altruistic punishment: people pay to punish free-riders.
- **Nowak, M. A. (2006)** — five rules for the evolution of cooperation.
- **Gintis, H., Bowles, S., Boyd, R., & Fehr, E. (2005)** — moral sentiments and material interests.

### Herding, cascades and thresholds

- **Granovetter, M. (1978)** — threshold models of collective behavior.
- **Schelling, T. C. (1978)** — micromotives and macrobehavior; segregation from mild preferences.
- **Banerjee, A. V. (1992)** — a simple model of herd behavior.
- **Bikhchandani, S., Hirshleifer, D., & Welch, I. (1992)** — informational cascades and cultural change.

### Social structure and networks

- **Granovetter, M. (1973)** — the strength of weak ties.
- **Granovetter, M. (1985)** — economic action as embedded in social structure.
- **Watts, D. J., & Strogatz, S. H. (1998)** — small-world networks.
- **Barabási, A.-L., & Albert, R. (1999)** — scale-free networks.

### Judgment, norms and diffusion

- **Kahneman, D., & Tversky, A. (1979)** — prospect theory: behavior under risk departs from expected utility.
- **Elster, J. (1989)** — social norms as a distinct explanation from rationality.
- **Bicchieri, C. (2006)** — the grammar of social norms.
- **Rogers, E. M. (1962)** — the diffusion of innovations.
- **Thaler, R. H., & Sunstein, C. R. (2008)** — nudges and choice architecture.
- **Henrich, J., Heine, S. J., & Norenzayan, A. (2010)** — the WEIRD sample problem.

### Groups and pathology

- **Darley, J. M., & Latané, B. (1968)** — bystander intervention and the diffusion of responsibility.
- **Janis, I. L. (1972)** — groupthink.
- **Tajfel, H., & Turner, J. C. (1979)** — social identity and intergroup behavior.
- **Bandura, A. (1977)** — social learning through observation and modeling.
