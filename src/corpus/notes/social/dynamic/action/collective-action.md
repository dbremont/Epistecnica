# Collective Action

> **Collective Action** is the coordinated doing through which multiple agents pursue a shared goal or common interest — the action kind that arises where no single agent can secure the good alone, and whose central difficulty is that participation is costly while the benefit is shared.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**Collective Action belongs to the `Action` social element type (Action category)** — a coordinated kind of social action performed by more than one agent toward a joint result. Its layer is **Ontic**: the doing exists extra-mentally. It is the collective kind already listed in the [Social Action](/view.html?p=notes/social/dynamic/action/action.md) taxonomy.

Readable secondarily as an `Activity` (the situated bundle of doings) and as a `Social Compound` (the participating set and its arrangements); those readings are prose here, not a second root. It is distinct from the individual contribution (one agent's act), from collective behavior (emergent and uncoordinated), and from the collective actor (the organization that carries the action).

Its facet is not fixed: a case is political when the goal is a public good or a policy, economic when it is a cooperative or market action, symbolic when it is a cultural mobilization. The dominant mechanism of the case decides the facet; the type does not.

### What is this social instance?

> The coordinated doing by which a set of agents pools effort toward a goal none of them can secure alone: mutual aid, strikes, boycotts, petitions, lobbying, demonstrations, community provisioning, and the organized pursuit of public goods. What makes it collective is not the number of agents but the coordination: a shared goal, a rule of contribution, and an arrangement that makes contribution visible and defection costly. Its central failure is worked in [The Collective Action Problem](/view.html?p=notes/miscellanea/unclassified/collective-action-problem.md).

## QA

### How does collective action differ from collective behavior?

> Collective behavior is emergent and uncoordinated — a crowd, a fad, a panic. Collective action is organized: a shared goal, a contribution rule, and an arrangement that makes defection visible. The difference is the coordination arrangement, not the number of participants.

### Is every coordinated action collective action?

> No. A firm coordinating its employees is an organization acting; collective action names the case where the participants are the beneficiaries and the coordination is what secures the shared good. The boundary is the participating set, not the coordination alone.
