# Terms of Trade

> The **terms of trade** are the ratio at which a country exchanges its exports for imports: the price of what it sells abroad relative to the price of what it buys.

## What it measures

> The concept answers *how many imports a unit of exports can buy*. It is a relative-price measure, not a volume measure: the quantities traded can stay the same while the terms of trade move sharply.

## Measurement

- **Net barter terms of trade** (commodity terms of trade): the export price index divided by the import price index, usually multiplied by 100.
  \[
  \mathrm{NBTT} = \frac{P_x}{P_m} \times 100
  \]
- **Income terms of trade**: net barter terms multiplied by the export volume index — the import-purchasing power of exports.
- **Single-factorial terms of trade**: net barter terms adjusted for productivity in the export sector.
- **Double-factorial terms of trade**: adjusted for productivity in both export and import-competing sectors.

## Interpretation

> An **improvement** means a unit of exports buys more imports; a **deterioration** means it buys less. Commodity exporters experience large swings when the prices of their exports move against the prices of their imports. The Prebisch–Singer thesis argues that the terms of trade of primary-commodity exporters tend to deteriorate relative to manufactures over the long run — a claim that remains contested.

## Limitations

- The net barter measure ignores volume and income effects; a price gain can coincide with a volume collapse.
- The choice of price index and base period changes the result.
- Aggregate terms of trade say nothing about the distribution of the gains within a country.
- The Prebisch–Singer trend is sensitive to the period and the commodity basket chosen.
