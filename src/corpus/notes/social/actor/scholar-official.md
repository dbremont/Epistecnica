# Scholar-Official

> A **Scholar-Official** is the examination-recruited administrator of the Chinese imperial state: a man whose title to office was a degree in the canonical classics rather than birth, purchase, or arms, and whose standing joined learning, office and moral authority in one person. The type held the state for roughly a millennium, and its examination was the most consequential selection instrument any pre-modern polity ran.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**Scholar-Official belongs to the `Interaction Unit` social element type, as an individual actor** — the office-holder himself, not the body of office-holders. Its layer is **Ontic** where the office, the seal and the yamen exist extra-mentally, **Synontic** where the degree and the moral authority are worth only what a collective honours, and **Noetic** where the canon, the commentary and the letters live. Its facet is political–administrative first, ideational second.

> It is distinct from the [University](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/university.md) and the [Education Actor](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/education-actor.md), which are corporates that teach and certify, and from the **gentry** — the landholding families from which officials were drawn and to which they returned on retirement, and which the examinations never certified as such. The scholar-official is the *person*, not the examination, the family, or the bureau; those are his instruments, his base and his organization.

Readable secondarily as an aggregate: the corps of degree-holders read as a stratum ranked on canonical learning. That reading is what makes it the comparandum for the [Bildungsbürgertum](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/class/bildungsburgertum.md); it is prose here, and the stratum shelf carries the comparison.

### What is this social instance?

> The kind: the official whose career ran through the examination ladder — the county, provincial, metropolitan and palace examinations — to the *jinshi* degree and a post in the nine-grade hierarchy. The institution is conventionally dated from the Sui dynasty's examination decrees around 605, expanded under the Tang, made the dominant route to office under the Song, and formalized under the Ming and Qing in the curriculum of the Four Books and Five Classics with the prescribed commentary and the eight-legged essay (from the fifteenth century). It was abolished in 1905.

> Its archetype is the county magistrate, the state's only regular presence in a district of a hundred thousand: tax collector, judge, police chief, granary keeper and sacrificial officer in one office, supported by a staff of clerks and runners he brought with him and forbidden by the rule of avoidance to serve in his home province. Above him the prefect, the provincial administration, the six boards and the censorate; below him nothing but the village and the family. The system's logic was to make the man who governed the district someone whose qualification was *impersonal* — a text known to all competitors, examined by examiners who did not know him — so that the state need not depend on local magnates or hereditary warriors.

> Its base was the gentry household: landed, literate, and committed to the examination as a family project sustained across generations, with the degree-holder's office as the return on that investment. Its end came with the decree of 1905, when the canon ceased to be the state's syllabus and a millennium of recruitment by classical text gave way, within a decade, to the republic, the modern university, and the cadre.

### Is the type of this `social element instance` unambiguous?

> The actor reading is unambiguous: the individual office-holder is a definite thing with a documentary trail — a degree, a post, a seal, a dossier. The aggregate reading is the ambiguous one, because the corps of officials overlaps the gentry without being identical to it and shades into the lettered elite that held no office at all.

> The filing keeps the two apart: the note types the actor, the stratum reading is stated as prose, and the shelf carries the comparison with the [Bildungsbürgertum](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/class/bildungsburgertum.md). Nothing is decomposed twice.

### Which facet does this element belong to, if applicable?

> Political–administrative first: the state's presence in the locality, and the office as the unit of that presence. Ideational second: the canon is the qualification, and the moral claim is the legitimation. Economic only derivatively, through the salary, the customary fees and the family estate.

> Verdict: the concept passes determinacy, intensional adequacy, discriminative power, distinctiveness, leverage, operationalizability and measurement validity, and fails contextual robustness while partly failing structural coherence. The comparison is therefore not "the same thing in two places" but two techniques for making cultivation rank: one documentary, one performative.

## QA

### Is the scholar-official a stratum or an actor?

> Both readings are true of the same population, and the note takes the actor reading because the office-holder is the unit that acts, holds a seal, and appears in a register. The stratum reading — the degree-holding elite ranked on canonical learning — is the aggregate of that actor, and it is the reading that makes the type comparable to a European educated bourgeoisie. The [stratum](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/class/stratum.md) shelf carries the comparison; nothing here is decomposed twice.

### Did the examinations select an elite or produce one?

> The dispute is old and unresolved. The mobility reading, given its classic form by Ping-ti Ho, counts the share of degree-holders of humble origin and finds the ladder genuinely open, if narrow. The reproduction reading counts the cost of the years of study, the family's library and the patronage that carried a candidate and finds the examination a machine for legitimating a gentry that would have dominated anyway. Both are probably right: the examination selected a minority of commoners and justified the rule of the families that produced the majority.

### Why is it not simply a mandarin?

> *Mandarin* is the European name, from the Portuguese *mandar*, to command, applied by travellers to officials they saw from outside: it names the office and misses the letters. The Chinese terms distinguish what the traveller's word flattens — the *jinshi* degree-holder, the *guan* who holds office, the *shenshi* gentry family, the *wenren* lettered man — and those distinctions are the whole interest of the type. Using "mandarin" as a synonym imports the travel account's flatness with its word.

### What replaced it?

> The decree of 1905 abolished the examinations before it abolished the empire, and with them the canon as the state's syllabus. What followed — the modern university, the republican and then the party cadre, and, in the examination's long shadow, the *gaokao* — kept the selection function while changing the qualification from classical letters to technical and ideological competence. Whether the examination culture survived the institution is a live question; that the institution's legitimacy rested on impersonal, text-based selection is not.
