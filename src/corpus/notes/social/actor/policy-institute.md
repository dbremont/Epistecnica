# Policy Institute

> A **Policy Institute** is a research organization that produces policy-relevant knowledge and advice — analysis, evaluation, data, and recommendations — for public and private decision-makers. It is the policy-research form of the [Research Actor](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/research.md): the body that turns inquiry into a usable account of what a policy does, what it costs, and what should be done instead.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**Policy Institute belongs to the `Collective / Organization` social element type, as a Kind of `Research Actor`** — a bounded collective with identity, membership, and rules whose dominant work is the production of policy-relevant knowledge.

Its layer is **Ontic** where the institute exists extra-mentally — a board, a directorate, research programmes, staff, publications, an address, a budget — and **Synontic** where its standing depends on recognition: the authority of its analysis, its access to decision-makers, and its reputation for independence. Its facet is **research (scientific)** with a normative face: the institute is organized by the production of warranted knowledge, but the knowledge is selected and framed by the policy question it is meant to answer.

It is not a **University**: a university holds degree authority and makes teaching its core, while a policy institute may teach only through fellowships, training, or a university affiliation. It is not a **Firm**: it does not convert capability into exchangeable value as its dominant mechanism, even when it sells reports or contract research. It is not an **Advocacy Association**: advocacy may follow from the analysis, but the institute's claim is to research first. It is not a **Ministry** or **Government Agency**: it advises, evaluates, and publishes; it does not hold executive authority.

Readable secondarily as a **Public Organization** when it is publicly constituted — the `Policy Research Organization` kind — and as an `Institution` where its norms of independence, disclosure, and peer review are the stabilized rules it hosts. Those are prose readings, not second roots; the tree below is typed once.

### What is this social instance?

> A policy institute fixes *who produces policy knowledge and how*: the governing board that protects its mandate, the directorate that selects its programme, the researchers and fellows who carry the analysis, the funders who make the work possible, the publication and convening practices through which it reaches decision-makers, and the norms that keep the analysis from becoming mere advocacy.

> Its boundary is the organization, not the policy field. A single institute may work across fiscal policy, regulation, development, health, education, technology, or foreign affairs; what makes it one actor is the shared governance, staff, funding, and public claim to policy research. It may be independent, university-affiliated, government-affiliated, foundation-funded, or attached to a corporate or civil-society body.

> The policy institute is distinguished from the older **royal commission** or **committee of inquiry**, which is a temporary body convened to answer one question. The institute is continuous: it accumulates staff, data, methods, and reputation across policy cycles, and that continuity is what lets it advise before a question becomes a crisis.
