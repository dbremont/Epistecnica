# Alexander Suvorov

> Alexander Vasilyevich Suvorov (1730–1800) was the Russian general who never lost a battle — some sixty engagements across the Russo-Turkish wars, Poland and Italy — and who wrote the army's own manual of practice rather than a theory of war. He is the counter-case to the geometric school: the commander whose method was speed, bayonet and the soldier's initiative, expressed in *The Science of Victory* (*Наука побеждать*).

## The campaigns

| Campaign | What it established |
| --- | --- |
| **Rymnik (1789)** | The tactical formula: speed of concentration, attack in echelon, the bayonet over the volley |
| **Izmail (1790)** | The storming of a fortress considered impregnable — the assault as a deliberate, rehearsed operation |
| **Poland (1794)** | The campaign that ended the Kościuszko rising, and the reputation for ruthlessness that came with it |
| **Italy and Switzerland (1799)** | Trebbia, Novi, and the Alpine crossing — the campaign that showed the method worked against the French in the field |

> The **Swiss campaign** is the one to keep. Ordered to cross the Alps to join the Austrian army, he took his troops over the Gotthard and the Devil's Bridge in October, in snow, with no supply line, and brought them out — a march that cost him more men than any battle he fought. It is the clearest demonstration that his maxims were not a doctrine of the offensive for its own sake but a doctrine of **movement**, an army that arrives faster than the enemy expects.

## The Science of Victory

> *Наука побеждать* is not a treatise. It is a soldier's manual — written to be read aloud, in the soldiers' language, on the order of battle: the eye, speed, the bayonet, the attack. Its historical importance is that it is one of the few documents in which a commander states his method in the voice of the ranks rather than the staff. Read beside [Jomini](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/private/persona/antoine-henri-jomini.md), the two are the century's poles: a geometry of lines of operation on one side, and on the other the claim that the decisive instrument is the man with the bayonet and the will to use it.

> The caution is the one that always follows: Suvorov's method required Suvorov. The Russian army did not reproduce it, and the campaigns of 1805–1807 that followed his death are the evidence — the same army, without the commander, beaten by the same French he had beaten in Italy.
