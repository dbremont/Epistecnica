# Zheng He

> Zheng He (1371–1433) was the Ming eunuch-admiral who commanded the seven treasure fleets between 1405 and 1433. Born Ma He in Yunnan, a Muslim from a family that had served the Yuan, he was captured as a boy, castrated, and raised in the household of the future Yongle Emperor — and from that household he took the largest fleet the world had yet seen to Malacca, Calicut, Hormuz, the Arabian coast and the Swahili ports of East Africa.

## The voyages

> The fleets were a **state expedition**, not a commercial venture: tens of thousands of men, ships built in the Nanjing yards, and a purpose that was diplomatic and political — to enrol the maritime kingdoms in the Ming tribute system, to display the dynasty's reach, and to bring back envoys, goods and the occasional giraffe (Nanjing, 1415). The voyages reached the coast of Africa two generations before the Portuguese rounded it from the other side.

## Why it matters

> The treasure fleets are the great **counterfactual** of world history. In 1433 the voyages stopped: the Yongle emperor's successors cut the fleet, the Hai Jin edict restricted private overseas trade, and much of the shipyard and voyage record was destroyed by officials who saw the expeditions as a waste. Whether China *could* have become the maritime power that Europe became, and how much the withdrawal was a decision rather than a structural limit, is a debate as large as the voyages themselves.

> The comparison with the European case is the reason to keep the note: the same century that saw Zheng He's fleets also saw the Portuguese push down the African coast, and the difference in what happened next is a question about the state, not about seamanship.
