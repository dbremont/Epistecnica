# Shibusawa Eiichi

> **Shibusawa Eiichi** (1840–1931) was a Japanese industrialist, banker and Meiji-era institution builder, often called the “father of Japanese capitalism.” He treated business as a public service and argued that industrial development had to be carried by moral as well as technical competence.

## Contributions

- Promoted the joint-stock company as the organizational form of Meiji industrialization.
- Helped establish modern banking institutions, including the First National Bank.
- Took part in the founding and direction of enterprises across railways, paper, insurance, shipping and other sectors.
- Supported business education and the training of a managerial class.
- Articulated the idea of a **moral economy** (*rongo*): profit and public good were not opposites, and the legitimacy of the enterprise depended on both.
