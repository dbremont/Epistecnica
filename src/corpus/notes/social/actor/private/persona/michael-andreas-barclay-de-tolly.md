# Michael Andreas Barclay de Tolly

> Michael Andreas Barclay de Tolly (1761–1818) was the Russian minister of war and field marshal who devised the **retreat of 1812** — the strategy of trading space for the army and the country's depth — and was removed from command for doing it. He is the corpus's case of the strategist who is right before the strategy is popular.

## The decision

> In June 1812, with the Grande Armée across the Niemen, Barclay commanded the First Western Army and held the portfolio of war minister. He chose not to give battle: to withdraw, to join the Second Army, to deny the French the decisive engagement they needed, and to leave them a country they could not feed. Smolensk fell, the retreat continued, and the army and the court blamed him — a foreign-born Baltic German general, in command of a retreat, in a war of national survival.

> He was replaced by [Kutuzov](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/private/persona/mikhail-kutuzov.md) in August 1812, and the strategy he had chosen was the one Kutuzov then executed: Borodino, the abandonment of Moscow, the Tarutino flank. The fair reading, and the one the campaign history supports, is that **the reversal of fortune began with the decision to retreat**, not with the decision to fight.

## The return

> He did not disappear. He commanded the centre at Borodino, the Russian forces at Leipzig (1813), and the invasion of France in 1814, receiving the field marshal's baton — the recompense the campaign had denied him in 1812. The corpus's interest is not the rehabilitation but the structure: the same competence that made him right in June made him inconvenient in August, and the state resolved the tension by changing the commander rather than the strategy.

> It is the recurring problem of the defensive strategy in a political war: the general who loses ground is judged by the ground, not by the army he saves. Barclay is the case that shows how much of operational success depends on a political authority willing to absorb the appearance of defeat.
