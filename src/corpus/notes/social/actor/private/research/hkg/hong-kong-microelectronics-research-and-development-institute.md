# Hong Kong Microelectronics Research and Development Institute (MRDI)

> The **Hong Kong Microelectronics Research and Development Institute** is a government-established R&D institution created in 2024 to work on the core technologies of **third-generation semiconductors** and to carry their results from the laboratory into industry. Its own phrase for that second job is the "**1 to N**" — the translation stage that universities are not built for and companies will not fund alone.

> It is not a university laboratory with a budget. It is one of Hong Kong's **five key R&D institutions**, funded as a standing piece of the innovation system rather than as a project, and it has been given the operation of Hong Kong's first **national manufacturing innovation centre** — which says the government's diagnosis of the bottleneck was not discovery but the industrial uptake of it.

## Mandate

Three functions, stated in the institute's own terms:

- **Core technology R&D** in third-generation (wide-bandgap) semiconductors — the materials family of silicon carbide and gallium nitride, whose value is in power electronics and high-frequency devices rather than in logic.
- **The 1 to N.** Taking a result that already exists in a paper or a prototype and making it manufacturable, testable and adoptable — the stage where most publicly funded research in the field stops.
- **Convening** universities, research centres and industry, so that the institute is a hub rather than a campus.

The third is the one that distinguishes it from a laboratory and the one hardest to fund, because convening produces no papers and no patents of its own.

## Establishment and funding

- Announced in the **2023 Policy Address** as part of the government's innovation and technology programme.
- Established in **2024**, on a **HK$2,838.4 million** commitment under the **Innovation and Technology Fund**, approved by the Legislative Council Finance Committee on **17 May 2024** as paper **FCR(2024-25)6**.
- Reaffirmed in the **first Five-Year Plan (2026–2030)** and the 2026 Policy Address as one of the five key R&D institutions, with support for the accelerated establishment of the national manufacturing innovation centre it is to lead and operate.

The funding instrument is worth reading as a statement of intent. A single lump-sum commitment under the innovation fund, rather than a competitive grant programme, means the government is buying an institution's existence rather than a portfolio of projects — the same instrument any country uses when it decides a capability must be *held*, not rented.

## Place in the Hong Kong system

Hong Kong's applied R&D layer was built as a set of mission-specific institutes — applied science and technology, nano and advanced materials, textiles and apparel, logistics and supply chains — each tied to a sector. MRDI is the newest and the most narrowly focused of the five, and its sector is the one with the clearest geopolitical dimension: semiconductors are the field in which access to tools, design software and markets is itself a policy variable.

That is the context the note should record without editorialising. An institute created in 2024 in a city that imports its chips, funded to work on the materials family that power electronics depends on, is a bet about where a small economy can hold a position in a supply chain it does not control.

## Governance

> The organisation chart answers the question the rest of the note leaves open: how an institute that is supposed to convene universities, industry and its own laboratories is actually steered.

![MRDI organisation chart](../assets/note/social/actor/private/research/hkg/mrdi-organisation-chart.png)

Three things are worth reading off it, and none of them is decoration.

**The board is split three ways.** An **Audit Committee**, a **Finance and Administration Committee** and a **Technology Committee** sit under the Board of Directors — assurance, process and subject matter, separated rather than combined. That is the standard division in a public body that spends a large capital sum: the technology judgement is not allowed to audit itself, and the finance judgement is not allowed to set the research agenda.

**Audit reports up, not sideways.** The **Internal Audit Section** hangs off the board line, outside the executive divisions. In an organisation created on a HK$2.8 billion commitment, that placement is the control that makes the rest of the chart credible.

**The divisions give the mandate away.** Under the Chief Executive Officer sit five divisions — **Corporate Services**, **Process & Technology**, **Third Generation Semiconductor Packaging & Modules**, **Design & Application**, and **Quality Control & Reliability**. Four of the five are technical, and they are the four of a product organisation: process, packaging, design, reliability. There is no division called "research". The 1-to-N claim in the mandate is therefore not marketing — it is drawn into the structure, and it is the reason this is not another university laboratory.

The centre of the chart states the mandate in the institute's own words: *"Collaboration among the Government, industry, academia and research institutes", technology transfer, commercialisation, enhancement of the I&T ecosystem* — with **Universities**, **R&D Centres** and **Industry** drawn as the three partners it convenes.

## What it is not

- **Not a foundry.** The institute does not manufacture at volume; it develops the technologies and the processes that a foundry would adopt.
- **Not a funding agency.** FONDOCYT-like competitive funding in Hong Kong lives elsewhere; MRDI is a performer, not a grant-maker.
- **Not a pure research centre.** Its success measure is adoption, which is why the 1-to-N formulation is the informative part of its mandate rather than its marketing.
