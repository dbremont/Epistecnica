# Japan Fine Ceramics Center (JFCC)

> The **Japan Fine Ceramics Center** is a ceramics research institute in Nagoya, established in 1985 with money put up by several hundred Japanese companies — the industry itself financed the centre that would evaluate and develop the materials it meant to sell. Its founding is the Japanese fine-ceramics programme in institutional form: a national bet on advanced ceramics, paid for by the firms that would have to make them.

## Ceramic focus

- **Fine ceramics** — the Japanese term for advanced, non-traditional ceramics: electronic, structural, optical and thermal.
- **Nanostructure and electron microscopy** — the centre is known for its transmission-electron-microscopy work on grain boundaries and interfaces.
- **Functional materials** — dielectric, piezoelectric, magnetic and ion-conducting ceramics.
- **Battery and energy materials** — electrode and electrolyte ceramics.
- **Computational materials science** — simulation of sintering, microstructure and properties.
- **Evaluation and standardisation** — shared testing of powders, bodies and components.

## Place in the field

JFCC is the shared evaluation laboratory of the Japanese ceramics industry. A firm's own laboratory has an interest in its powder looking good; a centre funded by the whole industry does not, and that is the institutional point of the place. It is not a university and not a manufacturer: it develops and measures, and it lets a mid-sized ceramics firm use instruments it could not buy.
