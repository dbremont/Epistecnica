# Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL)

> **Oak Ridge National Laboratory** is a United States Department of Energy national laboratory in Oak Ridge, Tennessee, established in 1943 for the Manhattan Project and managed since 2000 by UT-Battelle. Its ceramics work is a mission programme rather than a materials department's interest: nuclear fuel and waste forms, refractory materials, and ceramic matrix composites for energy and defence.

## Ceramic focus

- **Nuclear ceramics** — fuel forms, inert matrices, and waste forms designed to hold fission products for geological time.
- **Ceramic matrix composites** — SiC/SiC and oxide/oxide systems for high-temperature service.
- **Refractory materials** — linings and structural ceramics for furnaces and process vessels.
- **Advanced processing** — powder synthesis, sintering, joining, and additive manufacturing of ceramics.
- **Characterisation** — neutron and X-ray scattering at the Spallation Neutron Source and the High Flux Isotope Reactor, electron microscopy.
- **Coatings** — environmental and thermal barrier coatings.

## Place in the field

ORNL is a mission laboratory: the ceramics agenda is set by what the energy and defence programmes need to survive, not by a product line. Its distinctive asset is measurement — the neutron sources make it a place where a ceramic's structure and residual stress are measured rather than inferred. It is not a university and not a regulator; it is where a material is qualified for a duty nobody else has to meet.
