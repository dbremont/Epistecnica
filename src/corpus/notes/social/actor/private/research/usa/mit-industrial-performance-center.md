# MIT Industrial Performance Center (IPC)

> The **MIT Industrial Performance Center** is an interdisciplinary research centre at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology studying industrial performance, manufacturing, productivity, technological change and the future of work. It was founded in 1992 and continues the MIT tradition of industry studies that produced the *Made in America* report in 1989.

The IPC brings together engineering, management, social science and policy researchers. Its work is applied and industry-facing: factory technology adoption, workforce development, manufacturing competitiveness, automation and the conditions under which new technologies scale.

## Research programme

The centre's research is organised around three main thrusts:

- **Work of the Future** — technology, work and society, including automation, AI adoption and the quality of jobs.
- **Manufacturing** — technology adoption, workforce development and productivity in factories, with a long lineage in MIT industry studies.
- **Progress Studies** — how science and innovation can make faster progress on large goals such as clean energy and health.

Its output is a mix of working papers, reports, datasets and policy-facing analysis. The centre is a research performer and convenor rather than a regulator or a standard-setter.

## Place in the US research system

The IPC sits inside MIT, not inside government. It is privately chartered, university-hosted and project-funded, which makes it a useful bridge between engineering research, firm-level practice and public policy. It is one of the US research actors that translate industrial performance into a research question rather than a lobbying position.
