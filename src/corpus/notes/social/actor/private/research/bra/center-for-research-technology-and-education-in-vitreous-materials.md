# Center for Research, Technology and Education in Vitreous Materials (CeRTEV)

> The **Center for Research, Technology and Education in Vitreous Materials** is a FAPESP Research, Innovation and Dissemination Center hosted by the Federal University of São Carlos, started in 2013 and directed by Edgar Dutra Zanotto. It is the Brazilian glass centre, and its subject is the vitreous state rather than ceramics in the narrow sense — glass, glass-ceramics, and the crystallisation that turns one into the other. FAPESP renders its name as the Center for Research, Teaching, and Innovation in Glass; the institute's own title is the one above.

## Ceramic focus

- **Physics of the vitreous state** — structure, relaxation, viscous flow and diffusion, under the centre's own framing of a glass "genome".
- **Crystallisation and nucleation** — the kinetics that decide whether a glass stays glass or becomes a glass-ceramic.
- **Glass-ceramics** — crystallisation controlled on purpose for strength, thermal-shock resistance and machinability.
- **Bioactive and functional glasses** — glasses for health, and glasses with optical or electrical function.
- **Processing and properties** — forming, tempering, chemical durability and mechanical strength.
- **Education and dissemination** — the São Carlos School on Glasses and Glass-Ceramics, courses and public material.

## Place in the field

CeRTEV is a CEPID, which is the Brazilian instrument for a long, mission-sized programme: FAPESP funds a centre for a decade or more and expects it to do science, innovation and dissemination rather than pick one. Three universities are in it — UFSCar hosts, USP and UNESP participate — which is why one centre can cover a subject from nucleation kinetics to a bioactive implant.

It is the odd one out among the ceramic institutes, and deliberately so. Glass is a ceramic in the materials-science sense: inorganic, non-metallic, made by cooling a melt rather than sintering a powder. Glass-ceramics sit between the two classes by construction, since they are glasses crystallised on purpose. The boundary that separates them is a processing boundary, and CeRTEV works on both sides of it — which is exactly why the centre belongs on this shelf rather than beside it. It is not a testing house and not a company: it is a university centre with a national mandate.
