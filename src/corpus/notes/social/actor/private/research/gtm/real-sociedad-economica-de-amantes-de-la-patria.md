# Real Sociedad Económica de Amantes de la Patria de Guatemala

> The **Real Sociedad Económica de Amantes de la Patria** (1794) was the Guatemalan member of the **Amigos del País** family — the Enlightenment-era societies founded across the Spanish empire to promote "useful knowledge": agriculture, manufactures, education and public works. It is the colonial case of a form that the corpus reads as the **pre-history of the development agency**: a private corporation of notables, chartered by the Crown, doing what a ministry of economy would later do.

## What it was

| Dimension | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Founded** | 1794, in Guatemala City, under royal authorisation |
| **Form** | A *sociedad económica* — a chartered corporation of members, not a government body |
| **Membership** | The colonial elite: officials, clergy, merchants, landowners, and the educated professions |
| **Method** | Prize questions on crops and manufactures, reports, schools, and the promotion of roads and trade — knowledge as a programme of improvement |
| **Press** | The periodical press of the captaincy, which the society used to circulate its reports and economic arguments |

## The form to keep in view

> The *Amigos del País* societies were a **network**, not a set of independent clubs: the Basque originals were imitated across Spain and its empire, and the imitation was explicit — the statutes, the prize-question format and the membership profile travelled together. Guatemala's society is therefore the colonial instance of an *institutional export*, which is the part of the Enlightenment that the corpus finds most legible as technique.

> Its instrument was publication plus prize: pose a question about a crop, a road or a manufacture, offer a reward, publish the best answer. That is a **research-funding mechanism before research funding existed**, and it shares the structure of the modern innovation agency — a small budget, an open call, and a public report — with none of the bureaucracy.

## The end

> The society declined with the colony. Its functions did not disappear; they were absorbed by the institutions of the republic — the university, the ministries, the statistical office — which is the standard fate of a chartered corporation whose mandate becomes a state function. The corpus records it for that reason: the form is a transitional one, and the transition is what the note documents.
