# Société Minière de Boké

> **Société Minière de Boké** (**SMB**) is the Guinean bauxite consortium that turned Guinea into China's principal source of aluminium ore in under a decade. Formed in 2014 by a consortium of investors — the Winning International Group (Singapore), Shandong Weiqiao (the parent complex of [China Hongqiao](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/private/firm/china-hongqiao-group.md)) and UMS International (Turkey), with the Guinean state holding a minority stake — it mines in the Boké region and ships from its own terminal on the Rio Nunez.

## What made it work

> The innovation was not the deposit; it was the **package**. SMB built the mine, the haul road and railway, and the river port as one project, financed by the offtake. Where the incumbent (CBG, the Compagnie des Bauxites de Guinée) had operated for decades under a bauxite-convention model, SMB arrived with an **infrastructure-for-resource** structure: the ore leaves on infrastructure that exists only because the ore leaves.

| Element | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Resource** | High-grade bauxite in the Boké region, mined by open pit |
| **Logistics** | A dedicated rail link and the **Dapilon** terminal on the Rio Nunez, sized for capesize loading |
| **Market** | Chinese alumina refineries, chiefly the Weiqiao/Hongqiao complex |
| **Leverage** | The same consortium won the **Simandou** iron-ore tender in 2019, extending the model from bauxite to iron |

## The reading

> SMB is the corpus's cleanest case of **infrastructure as the entry ticket** in a resource market. The barrier to entry in bauxite is not the geology — Guinea's deposits were known for a century — it is the port and the rail, and the firm that finances them sets the terms. That is why a consortium assembled in 2014 displaced established producers within five years, and why Guinea's export surge is a logistics story before it is a mining one.

> The exposure is the same structure read from the other side: a project whose economics depend on one buyer's refineries and one river terminal has very little to fall back on if either the price or the policy changes — and Guinea's successive revisions of its mining conventions are the standing reminder.
