# Kyoto University

> **Kyoto University** (京都大学) is Japan's second imperial university, founded in 1897, and the country's other pole of academic research beside Tokyo. It is the institution that gave its name to the **Kyoto school** of philosophy and that built its reputation on research institutes rather than on professional faculties — the model of the university as a federation of institutes.

## What it is

| Dimension | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Founded** | 1897, as Kyoto Imperial University — the second imperial university, after Tokyo (1886) |
| **Form** | A national university corporation since the 2004 corporatisation of the national universities |
| **Distinctive structure** | Institutes alongside faculties — chemistry, theoretical physics, mathematics (RIMS), the humanities — which is where much of the research happens |
| **Associations** | The **Kyoto school** of philosophy (Nishida Kitarō and his successors), and more than a dozen Nobel laureates among its affiliates |

## Why the case matters

> The Japanese research university is the third institutional form in the corpus's Japanese set, and the one that is neither a ministry nor an [incorporated administrative agency](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/actor/type/incorporated-administrative-agency.md): it is a university that acquired corporate status in 2004 and still answers to the state for its funding. The interesting design is the **institute model**: a research institute inside the university, with its own budget, staff and programme, hired for a field rather than for a faculty. It is a way of buying depth without buying a new university.

> The Kyoto school is the other half of the reputation, and it belongs to the history of ideas rather than to science policy: a Japanese philosophical movement that read Husserl, Heidegger and Nishida together and produced a body of work that is neither a Western import nor a traditionalist reaction. Its presence in a research-university note is the reminder that the institute model also works for the humanities.
