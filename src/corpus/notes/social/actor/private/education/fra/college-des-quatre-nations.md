# Collège des Quatre-Nations

> The **Collège des Quatre-Nations** was a Paris college founded on Mazarin's bequest in the 1660s for sixty students from the four provinces annexed to France by the treaties of Westphalia and the Pyrenees — Artois, Alsace, Pignerol, and Roussillon-Cerdagne. It was suppressed in the Revolution; its building, opposite the Louvre, has housed the [Institut de France](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/private/research/fra/institut-de-france.md) since 1805.

## The institution

| Dimension | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Founder** | Cardinal Mazarin, who left his fortune to endow a college for the "four nations" |
| **Design** | Le Vau's building on the quai de Conti, begun in the 1660s, opposite the Louvre |
| **Purpose** | To educate the elite of the newly annexed provinces into the French state — an institution of **assimilation** as much as of instruction |
| **End** | Suppressed during the Revolution; the building served as a prison before becoming the Institut's seat |

## The point of the case

> The college is the cleanest example in the corpus of a rule worth keeping: an **institution outlives its function**. The educational purpose disappeared with the provinces' integration and the Revolution, and the building survived into a different institution with a different mandate. The physical plant, the endowment and the location are durable; the mission is not.

> It also records a mechanism of state formation that is easy to miss in a history told through wars: annexation was followed by **endowment** — a college, a chair, a pension — to bind the new elite to the capital. The four nations were not only ceded by treaty; they were given a school in Paris.
