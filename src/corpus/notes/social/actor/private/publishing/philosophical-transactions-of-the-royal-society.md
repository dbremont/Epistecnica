# Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society

> The first scientific journal: Henry Oldenburg's periodical for the Royal Society, first number published in London in 1665 — the publishing organization of editors, board, contributors and readers through which priority-of-publication and the scientific paper were made into an institution.

## Formulation

### What is this social instance?

> A scholarly journal organization fixes *who decides what is told first, and in what form*: the editorial board and editor-in-chief who select and admit, the editorial office that runs the cycle, the contributors who submit, the readers and subscribers for whom circulation counts, and the publication regime that makes printing an announcement. *Philosophical Transactions* is the founding instance of that arrangement — Oldenburg editing, Fellows contributing, the Royal Society sponsoring, and a number appearing every few weeks where before there was correspondence and priority dispute.

### What type of social element does this instance belong to?

**Philosophical Transactions belongs to the `Collective / Organization` social element type (Ontic category), as an instance of the `Publishing Organization` kind and its `Scholarly Journal` sub-kind** — a compound Interaction Unit with identity (title and masthead), membership (board, editors, contributors, subscribers), rules (submission and refereeing), and a decision architecture (editor-in-chief and editorial board) through which it selects and publishes.

It is not an `Institution` as such: the priority-and-refereeing regime it operates is a *component* of the organization (and is stabilized by it), not the whole. It is not a `Social Action`: a single number is a bounded occurrence, while the serial run is the organization's standing output. And it is not a bare `Social Compound`: the plurality of contributors, readers, and publication rules is what the organization *sustains and reproduces*, not the entity that decides.

Readable secondarily as a `Social Compound` — the serial run and the publication regime as the compound of contributors, readers, and rules — and as an `Institution` — the priority-and-refereeing rules in force. Those readings are prose here, not second roots; the tree below is typed once.

### Is the type of this `social element instance` ambiguous?

Yes, in three readings — grown one tree per reading, never two types on one row:

- **`Collective / Organization` → `Publishing Organization` → `Scholarly Journal` (the tree below)** — the journal as the editorial organization that decides, selects, and publishes. Typed once here.
- **`Social Compound`** — the serial run plus the publication regime as the compound of contributors, readers, and rules; what the organization sustains and reproduces. Prose reading, not a second root.
- **`Institution`** — the priority-and-refereeing regime as stabilized rules; a component of the typed tree, not a second root.

The organization reading is primary because the other two presuppose an office that decides: a serial run does not referee itself, and a regime does not apply itself.

### Which facet does this element belong to, if applicable?

**Scientific** — knowledge production, research, peer evaluation, formalized inquiry. Secondary touch: **Normative** (the priority and refereeing rules it holds) and **Literature** (the serial as textual transmission), neither of which is the dominant mechanism.

### What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this `social element instance`?

Three load-bearing caveats.

**The organization and the compound are one entity, not two.** The masthead is the organization's public face; there is no second entity called "the journal" standing beside the editorial board and office. The serial run is the organization's output and the contributor–reader plurality is what it sustains, so a tree that lists "the journal organization" and "the journal compound" as separate nodes would duplicate one thing. The compound reading survives only as the organization's *milieu*, which is why it is prose here.

**The reference table has a gap here.** The Activity reading and the guide's own QA both hold that *every Activity decomposes into the granular actions it bundles*, yet the Permitted Link Forms table licenses no link from an `Activity` to the `Social Action`s it bundles — only `Social Compound (→ Part/Component/Association)` and `Interaction Unit (→ Participation) Activity`. The tree therefore stops at `Publication Cycle Activity` rather than inventing a permitted link; the gap is recorded for table extension, per the rule on unlicensed relationships.

**Do not anachronize the institution.** The 1665 number instituted priority of publication and serial regularity; formal peer refereeing arrived later. Naming the component "Priority-and-Refereeing" describes the modern organization — a reader must not attribute modern referee practice to Oldenburg's first numbers. Note also what the tree deliberately excludes: the printed issues and the papers are technical and epistemic artifacts handled by technique/production and Research Space respectively, reachable by association only — a social decomposition is about the coordination, not the artifacts that carry it.
