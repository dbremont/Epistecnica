# Royal Commission

> A **Royal Commission** is a public inquiry body appointed by the Crown — in practice by the government of the day — to investigate a matter of public concern, given for that purpose the powers to summon witnesses, compel documents and take evidence on oath, and dissolved once it has reported. It is an actor constituted for one question: it finds facts and recommends, it does not adjudicate, and its authority ends with the report it hands to the authority that appointed it.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**Royal Commission belongs to the `Public Organization` social element type (Collective / Organization branch), Kind of `Task Group`** — an organization wielding delegated public authority under law, constituted by an instrument for a bounded mandate and dissolving on delivery. Its layer is **Ontic**: commissioners, counsel, a secretariat, hearing rooms, exhibits and transcripts exist extra-mentally. Its facet is **political** — the dominant causal mechanism is authoritative inquiry on behalf of public power.

It is not a `Committee` — a committee is drawn *from* a parent assembly, holds a slice of that assembly's business, and reports back into a decision the assembly will take; a royal commission is appointed from outside any assembly and reports to the executive that appointed it. Not a `Court` — a court adjudicates a dispute between parties and its judgment binds; a commission inquires into a matter, its findings bind nobody, and where it needs coercion it must certify the refusal to a court. Not a standing `Regulator` — it holds no continuing jurisdiction over a sector, and the body ceases when the report is delivered. Not a `Department` — it holds no program, no budget line, and no continuing administration.

Readable secondarily as an `Agency` of the appointing authority — the structured capacity through which a government makes a matter knowable and answerable — and, because its hearings are usually public, as a `Forum` in which the state, firms and citizens are made to appear and account. Those readings are prose, not second roots.

The kind is not one thing: the **investigative** commission appointed for one matter, the **statutory** commission of inquiry whose powers come from a standing Act rather than from letters patent, and the **standing** commission given continuing authority over a sector differ in warrant, life and compulsion without changing what an inquiry is.

### What is this social instance?

> The body at work. An instrument — letters patent, an order in council, or a statute — appoints the commissioners and fixes the terms of reference: what is to be inquired into, what powers are conferred, and when the report is due. The terms are the whole constitution of the body, and the fight over their wording is the first political fight of any inquiry, because a term of reference can open a subject or close it.
>
> Membership is small and personal: a commissioner or a panel, counsel assisting who presents the evidence in the public interest rather than for a party, and a secretariat that runs the hearings, exhibits and transcripts. The powers are the ones the instrument confers — to summon, to compel documents, to examine on oath, to suppress evidence where publicity would defeat the inquiry or prejudice a trial — and they are borrowed powers: the commission cannot punish, so a refusal is certified to a court.
>
> The work is evidence gathering, public or closed hearings, the commission's own research into records and practice, and the commissioners' settlement of findings of fact. The product is a report: findings, and recommendations that bind only if the receiving government adopts them. That last step is where an inquiry's authority actually lands — a recommendation implemented becomes law or practice, a recommendation shelved becomes a document — and the gap between the two is the standing measure of what the form can achieve.
>
> Lineage: the ad hoc inquiry convened for one scandal → the royal commission as the Commonwealth form for matters too large or too compromised for a department to investigate itself → the statutory commission of inquiry that supplies the same powers from a standing Act → and the standing commission, where a sector rather than a single matter is given continuing oversight.
