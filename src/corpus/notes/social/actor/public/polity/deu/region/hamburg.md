# Hamburg

> The **Free and Hanseatic City of Hamburg** is Germany's second city and one of its three city-states: the Elbe port, the publishing and media capital, and — before 1919 — a civic republic governed constitutionally by its hereditary grand burghers, the [Hanseaten](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/collective/class/hanseaten.md).

## The city and its constitution

> Hamburg's public law is a thousand years of contracts. Its 1241 alliance with [Lübeck](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/polity/deu/region/lubeck.md) is treated as the founding moment of the [Hanseatic League](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/polity/hsa/hanseatic-league.md); its 1266 contract with Henry III of England let its merchants establish a *hanse* in London, the first recorded use of the word for the League's trading guild; and its 1270 *Ordeelbook*, written by the Senate's solicitor, was the first account of civil, criminal, and procedural law for a German city in the German language. The negotiated compromise of 1410 is counted as its first constitution, which is the right way to read the city: a republic whose constitution is a settlement between interests rather than a grant from above.

> It was a free imperial city of the Holy Roman Empire, a fully sovereign city-state before 1871, and one of the thirty-nine sovereign states of the German Confederation after the Congress of Vienna confirmed its independence in 1815. The 1860 constitution was semi-democratic — the Senate elected by adult taxpaying males, with separation of powers, disestablishment, and freedom of press, assembly, and association — and the city joined the German customs union only in 1888, the last of the German states along with Bremen. It became a federal state of the Empire in 1871 and kept its self-government through the Weimar Republic. Today it is a state and a municipality at once: a Senate and a Bürgerschaft, a mayor whose office is closer to a minister-president than to a city mayor, and seven boroughs subdivided into a hundred and four quarters.

## The Hanseatic republic

> The republic was not a democracy before 1919, and the sources say so plainly: it was an oligarchy, and the oligarchy was the class of hereditary grand burghers. Only they could be elected to the Senate; only they held full freedom of large-scale and foreign trade; and the nobility was excluded from property, from political life, and from residence inside the walls. The [grand burgher right](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/collective/class/grand-burgher.md) was the constitution in legal form, and the [Hanseaten](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/collective/class/hanseaten.md) were the families who held it.

> What replaced it was the revolution of 1918–19 and the Weimar Constitution, which abolished the hereditary right and made the Senate an elected government. The official name kept the word: *Freie und Hansestadt Hamburg* has been the city's style since 1815, and it records a constitutional history rather than a medieval membership.

## The port

> The city sits at the base of the Jutland peninsula where the Elbe estuary begins, at the confluence of the Alster and the Bille, and its fortune is the river. The Port of Hamburg is Germany's largest and Europe's third-largest, after Rotterdam and Antwerp; the warehouses of the Speicherstadt and the office quarter of the Kontorhausviertel were declared World Heritage Sites in 2015, and HafenCity is the largest inner-city development in Europe. The shipyards and the plant of Blohm + Voss and Airbus keep the industrial side, and the merchant banks keep the old one: Berenberg, founded in 1590, is described as the world's oldest merchant bank, and the city is the seat of Germany's oldest stock exchange.

## Fire, bombs, flood

> The city has been destroyed three times and rebuilt richer each time. The Great Fire of 1842 began on the night of 4 May and burned until the 8th, taking about a quarter of the inner city, three churches, and the town hall; the reconstruction took more than forty years and produced the ring of streets and the warehouse quarter that the port still uses. The Allied firebombing of 23 July 1943 created a firestorm that destroyed whole boroughs. The North Sea flood of 1962 raised the Elbe to its highest recorded level, inundated a fifth of the city, and killed more than three hundred people — the disaster that produced the modern flood defences.

## The modern city

> Hamburg is a media and publishing city — *Der Spiegel*, *Die Zeit*, Gruner + Jahr, the regional broadcaster NDR — as well as a research one, with the University of Hamburg, the DESY synchrotron laboratory, and the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea. Its rivers and canals are crossed by some 2,500 bridges, more than any other city in Europe, and it holds five of the world's twenty-nine churches taller than a hundred metres. Helmut Schmidt and Angela Merkel were born there; Olaf Scholz was its mayor before he became chancellor.
