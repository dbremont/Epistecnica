# Chengdu Aircraft Research and Design Institute

> The **Chengdu Aircraft Research and Design Institute** (CADI, the "611 Institute"), founded in **1970**, is the design bureau behind the Chengdu line of Chinese combat aircraft: the J-7 derivatives, the **J-10**, the JF-17/FC-1 with Pakistan, and the **J-20**. It is the institutional form in which China's military aircraft design knowledge sits — a design institute paired with, but separate from, the factory that builds its designs.

## The organisational form

| Element | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **The 所/厂 split** | A design institute (所) holds the design authority; the aircraft company (厂) holds production. The Soviet-derived separation is what the Chinese aviation industry inherited and kept |
| **The chief designer** | A named **chief designer** carries the technical authority across a programme — Song Wencong for the J-10 and the J-20's early definition — rather than a rotating committee |
| **Parent** | Within the AVIC structure, alongside the Shenyang (601) and Xi'an institutes |
| **Output** | Design authority, prototypes, and the tacit knowledge that does not transfer with drawings |

## The programmes, as a ladder

> The J-7 line was **licensed-derivative** work: the MiG-21 copied, then modified. The **J-10** (first flight 1998) was the break — a canard-delta configuration with a digital fly-by-wire system, which is a control-law problem as much as an aerodynamic one, and therefore evidence that the institute had acquired the *design* capability rather than the *manufacturing* capability. The **J-20** (first flight 2011) moved to the stealth generation, where the design problem becomes the integration of airframe, sensors and software.

> That progression is why the institute is in the corpus rather than the aircraft: the same organisation moved from copying to designing to integrating, and the interesting object is the **institution that accumulated the capability**, not any single airframe.

> It also contrasts with the mission-agency model: the [US research catalog](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/research/usa/national-aeronautics-and-space-administration.md) puts design authority in a government agency competing with private primes; the Chinese arrangement keeps design authority in an institute inside the industrial group, and the competition happens between cities and institutes rather than between firms.
