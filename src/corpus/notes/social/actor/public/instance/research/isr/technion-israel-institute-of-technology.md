# Technion — Israel Institute of Technology

> The **Technion** is Israel's oldest university and its engineering school: founded in **1912**, opened in **1924** in Haifa, and for decades the institution that supplied the technical workforce of the Yishuv and then of the state. Its importance in the corpus is not as a research institute but as **technical infrastructure** — the place a small polity trained the engineers it could not import.

## What it is

| Dimension | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Position** | A university of engineering and applied science — the technical university model, not the academy model |
| **Role in the system** | The supply of engineers and applied researchers for industry, defence and the semiconductor sector |
| **Recognition** | Three Nobel laureates in chemistry among its faculty — Hershko and Ciechanover (2004, ubiquitin) and Shechtman (2011, quasicrystals) |
| **Relation to industry** | Close: the Israeli technology sector's founding generation was trained here, and multinational R&D centres (Intel among the earliest) drew on it |

## Why it belongs in the corpus

> The [KNAW](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/research/nld/royal-netherlands-academy-of-arts-and-sciences.md) note poses the academy question — who certifies standing — and the Technion poses the other one: **who produces the people**. An economy with no engineering school can buy equipment and cannot buy the tacit knowledge of how to use it; the Technion is the case of a state that treated that constraint as binding and built the school before it built the industry.

> It also complicates the "startup nation" story, which usually credits culture or military service. The institutional fact is duller and more portable: a technical university, founded early, tied to industry, in a country with no natural resources. That is a policy that can be copied, and the corpus keeps it as the example.
