# Agency for Science, Technology and Research (A*STAR)

> The Agency for Science, Technology and Research (A*STAR) is a statutory board in Singapore that oversees the country's research and development efforts in science and technology. It plays a crucial role in fostering collaboration between academia, industry, and government agencies to drive innovation and economic growth.

## Profile

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Constitution | Statutory board established under Singapore's Agency for Science, Technology and Research Act 1990. A*STAR is a body corporate with perpetual succession, legal personality and the authority to own property, enter into agreements and conduct its statutory functions. |
| Mandate | Advance scientific discovery and technological innovation through mission-oriented research; develop scientific and engineering capabilities; nurture research talent; facilitate technology transfer and commercialisation; and strengthen Singapore's economic competitiveness and societal development. |
| Representative Principal | Government of Singapore, represented principally through the Ministry of Trade and Industry (MTI). |
| Authority Model (Governing Structure) | Board–executive model: Board of Directors provides institutional governance and strategic oversight; Chief Executive Officer leads executive management and implementation of institutional strategies. |
| Interorganizational Position | Singapore's principal public-sector R&D agency. Positioned between government, universities, research institutions and industry, with responsibilities spanning research execution, scientific capability development, industrial collaboration and technology commercialisation. |
| Supervisory Authority | Ministry of Trade and Industry (MTI), acting through ministerial oversight, statutory governance arrangements, board appointments and applicable government financial and accountability controls. |
| Executive Authority | Chief Executive Officer, appointed under the Public Sector (Governance) Act framework. Mr Beh Kian Teik assumed the position on 8 November 2024. |
| Leadership Tenure | Board members and Chairman serve ministerially determined appointment terms. Individual appointment durations are specified through government appointment announcements. CEO tenure is not established as a single fixed statutory term. |
| Operational Autonomy | Statutory corporate autonomy over institutional administration, research operations, personnel recruitment and resource management, subject to the Agency's enabling legislation, ministerial directions, public-sector governance requirements and government funding conditions. |
| Organizational Structure | Board of Directors, CEO, executive management and four principal organizational divisions: Biomedical Research Council (BMRC); Science and Engineering Research Council (SERC); Innovation & Enterprise (I&E); and Corporate Group. These oversee research institutes, national centres, research programmes and institutional support functions. |
| Functional Specialization | Multidisciplinary scientific research and technological development encompassing biomedical sciences, biotechnology, physical sciences, engineering, microelectronics, advanced manufacturing, materials science, information and communications technologies, computational sciences and sustainability technologies. |
| Operating Mode | Mission-oriented research, fundamental and applied research, experimental development, collaborative R&D, technology development, industry-oriented research, translational research, technology transfer and commercialisation. |
| Instruments of Action | Research institutes, national research centres, competitive research funding, collaborative R&D projects, industry partnerships, intellectual property licensing, technology transfer, research scientist secondment programmes, venture creation, startup development and research talent programmes. |
| Funding Regime | Predominantly government-funded through institutional appropriations and national Research, Innovation and Enterprise (RIE) programmes. Under RIE 2025 (FY2021/22–FY2025/26), A*STAR managed approximately S$8.24 billion in R&D funding initiatives. Additional resources derive from industry collaborations, contract research, licensing and other research-related activities. |
| Personnel Regime | Institutionally recruited scientific, engineering, technical, managerial and administrative personnel. A*STAR also develops research talent through scholarships, graduate programmes, fellowships and research training. Its CEO reports responsibility for an agency employing more than 4,100 research staff. |
| Accountability Line | Board of Directors for institutional governance; MTI for ministerial and statutory oversight; and relevant government financial and audit authorities for the administration and accountability of public resources. |
| Reporting Regime | Annual institutional reporting, financial statements, corporate governance disclosures, reporting on research and innovation programmes, and accountability reporting associated with government-funded R&D initiatives. |
| Performance Control | Institutional performance monitoring through research publications, scientific impact, intellectual property generation, licensing revenue, industry R&D projects, industry R&D expenditure, technology commercialisation, startup creation, industry secondments and research talent development. Government also assesses A*STAR's contribution to industrial development and economic outcomes. |
| Corrective Authority | Board of Directors exercises institutional governance and managerial oversight. MTI retains statutory supervisory authority, including the power to issue ministerial directions under the enabling legislation. Relevant government authorities exercise financial, audit and funding-compliance oversight. |
| Sanction Regime | Statutory governance requirements, public-sector financial and administrative controls, employment-related disciplinary procedures, internal investigation mechanisms and applicable consequences for financial or grant-compliance violations. Specific institutional disciplinary provisions require further documentary verification. |
| Intra-Agency Coordination Regime | Central executive management coordinates the Biomedical Research Council, Science and Engineering Research Council, Innovation & Enterprise and Corporate Group. Research institutes and national centres conduct specialized research, while cross-institutional programmes and industry partnerships facilitate multidisciplinary collaboration and technology translation. |
| Institutional Plasticity Model | Board-led institutional governance combined with government-directed strategic priorities, periodic national RIE planning cycles, reorganization of research capabilities, establishment of national research platforms, development of new scientific capabilities and expansion of industry-oriented research and commercialisation activities. |

## Operating Model

The A*STAR model typically involves several key components:

1. **Research Institutes**: A*STAR comprises several research institutes specializing in various fields such as biomedical sciences, physical sciences, engineering, and more. These institutes conduct cutting-edge research and development to address both fundamental scientific questions and practical challenges.
2. **Industry Collaboration**: A*STAR works closely with industry partners, including multinational corporations and small and medium enterprises, to translate research outcomes into practical applications and commercial products. Collaboration with industry helps to accelerate the adoption of new technologies and enhance Singapore's competitiveness on the global stage.
3. **Talent Development**: A*STAR invests in talent development by providing opportunities for researchers, scientists, and engineers to pursue advanced degrees, gain hands-on research experience, and develop their skills through training programs and fellowships. This focus on talent development ensures a pipeline of skilled professionals capable of driving innovation and sustaining Singapore's knowledge-based economy.
4. **Technology Transfer and Commercialization**: A*STAR facilitates the transfer of technology from research laboratories to the marketplace through licensing agreements, spin-off companies, and other commercialization strategies. By bridging the gap between research and industry, A*STAR helps to create economic value and stimulate entrepreneurship.
5. **International Collaboration**: A*STAR actively engages in collaborative research initiatives with leading institutions and organizations worldwide to leverage expertise, share resources, and tackle global challenges. International collaboration enhances Singapore's research capabilities and promotes knowledge exchange on a global scale.


## Industry Collaboration

The Agency for Science, Technology and Research (A*STAR) is a statutory board under the Ministry of Trade and Industry in Singapore, focusing on fostering scientific research and innovation to drive economic growth and societal advancement. A*STAR collaborates with various industries through several models aimed at facilitating technology transfer, knowledge exchange, and research commercialization. Here's an overview of some typical collaboration models:

1. **Research Partnerships**: A*STAR partners with industries on collaborative research projects. This model allows companies to leverage A*STAR's expertise, facilities, and resources to address specific technological challenges or develop new products/processes.
2. **Joint Laboratories**: A*STAR establishes joint laboratories with industry partners, where researchers from both sides work together on mutually beneficial research initiatives. These labs are often focused on a particular technology or industry sector.
3. **Technology Licensing**: A*STAR commercializes its research outcomes by licensing technologies to industry partners. Companies can access A*STAR's intellectual property (IP) portfolio to integrate innovative solutions into their products or services.
4. **Spin-off Companies**: A*STAR supports the formation of spin-off companies based on its research discoveries. These startups receive funding, mentorship, and access to A*STAR's R&D infrastructure to accelerate their growth and commercialization efforts.
5. **Collaborative Platforms**: A*STAR develops collaborative platforms or consortia that bring together multiple industry players, research institutions, and government agencies to address common challenges or explore emerging opportunities in specific technology domains.
6. **Funding Programs**: A*STAR administers funding programs to support collaborative research projects between academia and industry. These grants provide financial support for joint R&D activities, technology development, and innovation-driven projects.
7. **Industry Attachments and Secondments**: A*STAR facilitates industry attachments and secondments for its researchers, allowing them to gain practical experience, transfer knowledge, and build partnerships with industry stakeholders.
8. **Open Innovation Initiatives**: A*STAR promotes open innovation by engaging with external partners to co-create solutions, share resources, and collaborate on pre-competitive research projects in areas of strategic importance.

### Who controls the Board personnel?

> The **Minister for Trade and Industry (MTI)** controls Board composition by appointing the Chairman and other Board members, determining their appointment terms and conditions, and exercising the statutory authority to terminate their appointments.

> There is not a clear procedure for this - is opaque - and discretionary. They are not composition rules, etc.


### Who controls the executive personnel?

> The **Board of Directors** appoints the Chief Executive Officer subject to the Minister's approval, while A*STAR independently manages the appointment, remuneration and employment conditions of its remaining executive, research and administrative personnel.
