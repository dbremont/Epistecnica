# Promotion Agency

> A **promotion agency** is an organisation chartered to improve a country's or region's **technical-productive capability** before a ministry of industry existed: it awards premiums for inventions and crops, runs schools and model farms, organises exhibitions, writes standards, inspects quality, and publishes statistics. The modern development agency is its **bureaucratised descendant** — the same instruments, a different legal form.

## The form

| Function | Instrument | Instance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stimulate invention** | Premiums and prizes for useful improvements | Royal Society of Arts (1754); Royal Dublin Society (1731) |
| **Diffuse technique** | Model farms, drawing schools, technical classes | RDS drawing schools; the *Amigos del País* schools |
| **Set quality** | Standards, marks, regulation of process | Inspection des manufactures (1669) |
| **Circulate knowledge** | Transactions, journals, prize questions | *Société de l'industrie minérale* (1855); the economic societies' reports |
| **Promote trade** | Exhibitions, missions, prizes for exports | RSA and the exhibition line; later the trade agencies |
| **Measure** | Statistics and surveys of industry | The inspection and the economic societies alike |

## The line of descent

> The corpus files the modern bodies — [IDA Ireland](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/development/ida-ireland-industrial-development-authority.md), [Enterprise Ireland](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/development/enterprise-ireland.md), [Austrade](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/development/austrade.md) — as development agencies. This note records their **pre-history**, in four institutional forms:

| Period | Form | Function | Instance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1669** | **State inspection** | Enforce process and quality standards; gather statistics | [Inspection des manufactures](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/development/france-inspection-des-manufactures.md) |
| **1731–1775** | **Chartered improvement society** | Premiums, schools, model farms, exhibitions | [Royal Dublin Society](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/development/royal-dublin-society.md); [Royal Society of Arts](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/development/royal-society-of-arts.md); [Amigos del País](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/development/amigos-del-pais.md) |
| **1801** | **National encouragement society** | Premiums and medals, expert commissions, the *Bulletin* as clearing-house | [Société d'encouragement pour l'industrie nationale](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/development/societe-encouragement-industrie-nationale.md) |
| **1855** | **Industry technical society** | Congresses, standards, journals for a sector | [Société de l'industrie minérale](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/development/societe-industrie-minerale.md) |
| **20th century** | **Development agency** | Grants, FDI attraction, export promotion, cluster policy | IDA Ireland, Enterprise Ireland, Austrade |

> The constant is the **function** — make the productive system better — and the variable is the **form**: regulation, association, or agency. The Irish case is the cleanest single line: the Royal Dublin Society (1731) and IDA Ireland (1949) are the same country doing the same job with two centuries of institutional technology between them.

## Why the form is worth a type note

> Because it shows that **industrial policy is older than the industrial ministry**. The instruments now administered by agencies — a prize, a school, a standard, an exhibition, a statistic — were invented by chartered societies and inspectors, at a time when the state had neither the budget nor the bureaucracy to run them. What changed was not the idea of promoting production but its **carrier**.
