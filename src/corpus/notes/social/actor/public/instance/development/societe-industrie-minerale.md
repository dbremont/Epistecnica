# Société de l'industrie minérale

> The **Société de l'industrie minérale** (France, **1855**) is the **sectoral** turn of the [promotion agency](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/development/promotion-agency.md) form: not a national improvement society but an association of mining engineers and operators that made a *sector's* technique its object — congresses, standards, statistics and a technical journal.

## What it did

| Instrument | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Congresses** | Annual meetings where operators compared practice — the technical conference as a transfer mechanism |
| **Journal** | The *Revue de l'industrie minérale*, the sector's record of methods, accidents and costs |
| **Statistics** | Mine and metallurgy data collected and published by the industry itself |
| **Safety standards** | The technical response to mining accidents, in an era before a labour inspectorate |
| **Technical education** | Support for the mining schools and the engineering profession |

## Why the case is worth keeping

> It shows the form **specialising**. The eighteenth-century society addressed "useful arts" in general; the nineteenth-century industry society addresses one sector, with a membership defined by profession rather than by civic standing. That is the same shift that turns the general development agency of the twentieth century into the **sectoral agency** — a mining institute, an energy agency, a semiconductor authority.

> It is also the case where the **industry** rather than the state or the civic elite is the carrier: the society is the trade association and the learned society at once, which is why the technical standards of a sector often originate in such bodies.
