# Inspection des manufactures

> The **Inspection des manufactures** (France, **1669**) was Colbert's corps of state inspectors of manufactures — the **regulatory** form of the [promotion agency](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/development/promotion-agency.md), created to enforce quality and process standards across French industry rather than to reward improvement.

## What it did

| Instrument | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Règlements** | Detailed rules on the length, width, dye and finish of cloth and other manufactures |
| **Inspection** | Travelling inspectors with the power to seize non-conforming goods |
| **Marks** | Certification marks on compliant products — an early quality mark |
| **Statistics** | Reports on production, capacity and trade, the state's industrial intelligence |
| **Technical diffusion** | Inspection reports as a channel for best practice, and the inspectors as technical experts |

## Why the contrast matters

> The inspection is what the chartered societies are **not**: compulsory rather than voluntary, state rather than civic, regulating process rather than rewarding improvement. And it is the reason the [promotion agency](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/instance/development/promotion-agency.md) note distinguishes the two instruments — because the historical record shows the state tried regulation first, and the premium-based societies emerged where the state's enforcement capacity failed or where the political order preferred voluntary improvement.

> The corps was abolished in the Revolution (1791), and its functions returned in the nineteenth century in different form — the labour inspectorate, the standards bodies, the statistical offices. The corpus reads the sequence as the standard one: **inspection → association → agency**, three carriers of the same productive-improvement function.
