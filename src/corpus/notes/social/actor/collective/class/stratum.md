# Stratum

> A **Stratum** is a gradational layer of a social structure: a set of occupants ranked above and below one another on a scale of some valued good — cultivation, income, prestige, occupation, authority — rather than defined by their place *against* other positions in production. Strata rank; classes oppose.

## Typing

> The socialium guide licenses no `Stratum` kind, and this shelf does not invent one. What a stratum names is a **`Social Property`** — gradational position on a scale — carried by a `Social Compound` that occupies it. The notes filed here are therefore each typed once, in their own file, as the compound they are; "stratum" is the reading that makes them a shelf, not a second taxonomy.

> The shelf exists because the reading is load-bearing: a compound defined by *how much* of a good its members hold behaves differently from one defined by *which side* of a relation they stand on, and the corpus needs a place to say so without smuggling a kind into the guide.

## Strata and classes

> The two cuts cross and must not be merged. An occupation ladder is a stratum; a property relation is a class. The same population can be both — a professional is high on the cultivation scale and, in the Marxist reading, a seller of labor power — which is why the [Social Class](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/collective/class/social-class.md) note refuses to absorb strata into its kinds and instead links them by association.

> Where the scale is cultivation and the credential that certifies it, the stratum acquires a further property: its ranking can be *transmitted* through schooling without being *convertible* into property. That is the case the [Bildungsbürgertum](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/collective/class/bildungsburgertum.md) note works out.

> The other great case is the Chinese [Scholar-Official](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/scholar-official.md) — a stratum ranked on canonical learning and certified by state examination. It is not filed here: the note at the actor hub types the office-holder, the individual actor, and the stratum reading of the corps of degree-holders is carried as prose there. The shelf links it as the comparison, not the member, which keeps "stratum" a reading and not a kind.

## Scales

> A stratum is only as determinate as the scale it is drawn on, and the scale is part of the definition rather than an afterthought. The scales this corpus uses are cultivation (schooling, canon, credential), income and wealth, prestige (the standing a collective confers — see [Status](/view.html?p=notes/social/synontic/status.md)), occupation, authority, and civic right (the legal standing of municipal membership, where a charter rather than a market sets the rank). Naming a stratum without naming its scale leaves the gradation unmeasured, and a gradation nobody can order is not a stratum.
