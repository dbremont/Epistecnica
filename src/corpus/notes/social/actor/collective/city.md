# City

> A **city** is a human settlement characterized by defined boundaries, governance structures, infrastructure, and population.

> Read as a technical system, the **city** is the organized set of built works, networks, fleets, facilities and practices that together deliver shelter, water, power, mobility, communication and public services to people within one bounded territory. The two definitions are not rivals: the settlement is the social compound, and the technical system is the fabric that keeps it habitable.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**City belongs to the `Social Compound` social element type (Ontic branch)** — a compound constituted by social elements and their organized relations: the settled territory, the resident population, the built fabric and its technical networks, the institutions that stabilize conduct, and the relations binding the settlement to its region.

Its layer is **Ontic**: the ground, the buildings, the people, the pipes and the budgets exist extra-mentally. Its facet is **structural-spatial** — the dominant causal mechanism is co-residence within one bounded territory, which is what makes a city a unit of social life rather than an aggregate of unrelated actors.

It is not an `Interaction Unit` — a city does not decide as one mind. The [Public Organization](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/actor/public-organization.md) that governs it is an actor *inside* the compound; its decisions are one part of the city, never the whole. It is not a `Region` — a [Region](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/region.md) is a field of interaction density, while a city is a settlement with a built fabric and institutions, though every city is a region of a kind and regions are often named after one. It is not a `Polity` — that is the political community organized under a common authority; a city-state is both, and the compound/polity split is how the corpus keeps them apart. And it is not, strictly, a `Production Technical System`: that is the reading of its fabric, carried in the next section, not a second root.

Readable secondarily as a `Production Technical System` (the technical reading below) and as a `Technical Element Set` — the ensemble of heterogeneous technical members. Those readings are prose and a carried decomposition, not additional roots.

### What is this social instance?

> The settlement at work. A bounded territory holds a resident population and the daily life that population conducts there: households housed in a dwelling stock, organizations trading and employing, institutions — property, planning, policing, schooling — stabilizing conduct, public space where strangers meet, and the technical networks that carry water, power, goods, people and information. What individuates the city is not its size but the closure of that daily life: most of what a resident needs lies within the territory, and the boundary is where that reachability thins out.

> Its line of descent is a settlement history: the walled or river-crossing settlement whose boundary was a fortification → the industrial city whose boundary became a sanitary and administrative line → the metropolitan city whose built fabric spills across its administrative boundary while its daily life is organized by commuting → the conurbation, where several cities compose one housing and labour market. Each step moves the boundary away from the wall and toward the flow.

## The city as a technical system

> The same object under its second reading. What follows is the decomposition carried from the former `city-system` note: the city as the organized technical system that realizes urban habitability.

**Read as a technical system, the city belongs to the `Production Technical System` technical element type** — an organized set of production technical objects whose interaction realizes a technical capability. The capability realized is *urban habitability*: a settled population kept sheltered, watered, powered, mobile, connected, safe and served within a bounded territory.

Readable as a `Technical Element Set`: the city is also a coherent ensemble whose members belong to different technical element types — systems (water, power, transit), facilities (hospital, school, depot), objects (dwelling, drain), practices (waste collection, emergency response) — related through one common purpose. The two readings answer different questions and are kept apart: the *system* tree asks how the city's objects interact to realize the capability; a *set* tree would ask what the city contains.

It is **not** the city government (`Technical Agent`), urban planning (`Technical Practice`), or urban infrastructure (`Technical Element Set`): the government is one actor inside the system, planning is one practice, and infrastructure is a part, not the whole. It is **not** a good: the city is the producer, and the goods it yields (housing services, delivered water, mobility) are what gets exchanged.

### What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this `technical element instance`?

The concept under evaluation is **City**, defined above as *an organized technical system of a settled population: the built works, networks, fleets, facilities and practices that together deliver shelter, water, power, mobility, communication and public services within one bounded territory*. The evaluation applies the criteria of [Philosophia Conceptuum et Operis](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/csiglab/Naturgnosis/refs/heads/main/app/note/data/meta/philosophia-conceptuum-et-operis.md).

**Verdicts.** Conceptual Identification — **pass**: the concept is placed against the guide's technical-element types and separated from city government, urban planning and urban infrastructure. Definitional Clarity — **pass with qualification**: every defining term (*organized set*, *production technical object*, *bounded territory*) is a guide term or a measurable attribute, except *settled population*, which needs a working threshold. Conceptual Determinacy — **qualified**: the membership rule admits megacities and small towns alike and leaves the city/non-city boundary at the limits (suburbs, conurbations, city-states) to an explicit territorial rule rather than a population count. Internal Consistency — **pass**: the system reading and the set reading are declared as separate roots (see the guide's multi-root decomposition), so no row has to serve both. Representational Adequacy — **qualified**: the tree represents the city by its technical members, so it captures infrastructure and services well and administrative, cultural and fiscal life only where a technical carrier exists. Purpose Adequacy — **pass**: the purpose is an operative decomposition usable for planning, failure analysis and cross-domain comparison, and the tree is shaped for it.

## Planning, architecture and zoning

Three frames act on the same object at different scales, and the corpus files each in the urbanism scope.

**Urbanism** — the practice of the settlement's form and function: density, land-use mix, street network, public space, and growth or shrinkage. It is the scale at which the city is steered as a whole, and the scale at which the trade-offs between the other two frames are settled.

**Architecture** — the building scale the city aggregates: type, structure, envelope, style. A city's fabric is the sum of decisions taken building by building, and architectural style is what makes that sum legible as a period or a place. The city is not a building, but it is nothing except buildings and the ground between them.

**Zoning and planning law** — the legal instrument binding the other two. Zoning fixes use districts, height, density, setbacks, heritage protection and the permit that admits a project; the plan hierarchy runs from the comprehensive plan to the detailed plan to the building permit. The modern instrument has three anchors: *Village of Euclid, Ohio v. Ambler Realty Co.* (1926), which upheld municipal use zoning in the United States and gave "Euclidean zoning" its name; the *Town and Country Planning Act 1947*, which established the comprehensive planning system in the United Kingdom with development plans and development control; and Sweden's *Plan- och bygglagen* (2010:900), under which the municipality adopts comprehensive and detailed plans and Boverket is the national board for housing, building and planning.

> Zoning is where the settlement and its technical system meet. The plan decides which network must be extended where, and the network decides which sites are buildable — so the two steer each other, and what carries the decision is law rather than engineering.
