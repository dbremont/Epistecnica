# Transceiver

> A **transceiver** is a device that transmits and receives in one unit: a transmitter and a receiver sharing the antenna, the line, or the port that carries the signal in both directions.

The name is functional, not material — what makes a device a transceiver is the duplex role, not the medium it drives. Two different goods are sold under it, and the catalogue keeps both.

| Use | What it is | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Radio | A station that shares one antenna between transmit and receive, duplexed in time or separated in frequency | Handheld radio, base-station radio unit, radar front end, satellite modem |
| Data networking | A pluggable module that drives a cable from a port and takes the signal back off it | [Optical Transceiver](/view.html?p=notes/social/good/telecom/optical-transceiver.md) on fibre, copper SFP on twisted pair |

## Where the value sits

The radio good is sold as equipment defined by a band and a standard: the duplex method, the modulation, the transmit power, and the licence to radiate. The networking good is sold as a swappable part, where the value is interoperability — with the host port, with the far-end module, and with the standard the link is specified against.
