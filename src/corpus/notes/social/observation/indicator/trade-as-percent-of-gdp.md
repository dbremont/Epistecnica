# Trade as % of GDP

> **Trade as % of GDP** — trade openness — is the sum of exports and imports of goods and services divided by gross domestic product, expressed as a percentage.

## What it measures

> The indicator answers *how large are cross-border flows relative to the size of the economy*. It is a measure of openness and integration, not of welfare or of policy liberalisation.

## Measurement

- Numerator: exports plus imports of goods and services (gross flows), from the national accounts or the balance of payments.
- Denominator: GDP at current prices.
- The ratio is sensitive to the price of tradables, the exchange rate and the size of the economy.

## Interpretation

> Small economies tend to show high ratios because their domestic market is small relative to their trade. Large economies can be highly integrated with low ratios. A rise is consistent with deeper integration, but not necessarily with better outcomes.

## Limitations

- Gross flows double-count value added across global value chains.
- A high ratio can reflect re-exports, processing trade or a single dominant commodity.
- GDP and trade are measured at current prices; price swings move the ratio.
- Openness is not the same as trade policy or trade barriers.
