# Labor Force Participation Rate

> The **labour force participation rate** is the share of the working-age population that is in the labour force — employed or unemployed and actively seeking work.

## What it measures

> The rate answers *how many people are offering their labour*, regardless of whether they find work. It is the bridge between the employment rate and the unemployment rate: participation can fall because people retire, study, care for dependants, or become discouraged and stop searching.

## Measurement

- **Labour force** = employed + unemployed, using the same definitions as the two component indicators.
- **Working-age population** is conventionally 15+ or 15–64.
- Data come from labour-force surveys; administrative series may cover only registered workers.

## Interpretation

> Falling participation can make the unemployment rate look better while the employment rate falls. Rising participation can raise unemployment even as hiring improves. The three labour indicators must be read together.

## Limitations

- Age structure, retirement age, education and care responsibilities move the rate independently of the labour market.
- Discouraged workers and informal work are poorly captured in some systems.
- Definitions of the working-age population differ.
