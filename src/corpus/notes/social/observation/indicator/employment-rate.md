# Employment Rate

> The **employment rate** is the share of the working-age population that is employed: the number of employed persons divided by the population of working age.

## What it measures

> The rate answers *how much of the population that could work is actually working*. Unlike the unemployment rate, its denominator is the whole working-age population, not the labour force, so it falls when people leave the labour force as well as when they lose jobs.

## Measurement

- **Employed** usually means persons who worked for pay or profit for at least one hour in the reference week, including part-time and some unpaid family work.
- **Working age** is conventionally 15+ or 15–64, depending on the statistical office.
- Data come from labour-force surveys and, where coverage is weaker, from administrative records.

## Interpretation

> A high employment rate is consistent with a tight labour market, but it also reflects demography, participation and the definition of employment. It is best read beside the unemployment rate and the participation rate, not alone.

## Limitations

- Part-time and underemployed workers count as employed.
- Informal and subsistence work may be poorly captured.
- Age structure and retirement patterns move the rate independently of the labour market.
- Definitions of employment differ across countries.
