# Trade Balance

> The **trade balance** is the difference between a country's exports and imports of goods and services: exports minus imports, also called net exports.

## What it measures

> The balance answers *whether a country sells more abroad than it buys*. A surplus is positive net exports; a deficit is negative. It is a component of aggregate demand (`NX` in `AD = C + I + G + NX`) and of the current account.

## Measurement

- **Goods balance** (merchandise trade) and **services balance** are reported separately; the **goods and services balance** combines them.
- The **bilateral** balance with one partner differs from the **overall** balance; the overall position is what enters the national accounts.
- Re-exports, processing trade and rules of origin distort bilateral comparisons.

## Interpretation

> A surplus or deficit is not inherently good or bad. The balance is tied to saving and investment (`NX = S − I`) and to capital flows; a deficit can finance investment or consumption, and a surplus can reflect weak domestic demand.

## Limitations

- Bilateral balances are not a measure of fairness or of the gains from trade.
- Gross flows double-count value added across value chains.
- Re-exports and processing trade inflate the recorded balance.
- Currency valuation and timing affect the comparison.
