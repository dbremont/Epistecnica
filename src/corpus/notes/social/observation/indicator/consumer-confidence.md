# Consumer Confidence

> **Consumer confidence** is the reported optimism of households about their own financial situation and the state of the economy, collected by household surveys and used as a leading signal of consumption.

## What it measures

> Confidence is a *sentiment*, not a behaviour: it records what households say about current conditions and expectations, not what they spend. It is read as a leading indicator because consumption is the largest component of aggregate demand and because households act on expectations, not only on current income.

## Measurement

- **Household surveys** ask about the household's finances, the general economy, employment prospects, and major purchases.
- The **Conference Board Consumer Confidence Survey** (US) reports a Present Situation Index and an Expectations Index.
- The **University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers** report the Index of Consumer Sentiment.
- The **OECD** publishes a harmonised consumer confidence indicator for member economies.

## Interpretation

> Higher confidence is consistent with stronger consumption and with the expansion phase of the cycle; falling confidence often precedes a slowdown. The *change* in confidence usually carries more information than its level.

## Limitations

- Stated sentiment and actual spending can diverge.
- Survey design, question wording and sample composition differ across countries, so levels are not directly comparable.
- Responses are sensitive to events, media coverage and political context.
- Confidence is a symptom as much as a cause: it can fall because income, employment or prices have already changed.
