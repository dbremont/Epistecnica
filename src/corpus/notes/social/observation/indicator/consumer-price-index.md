# Consumer Price Index (CPI)

> The **Consumer Price Index** measures the change in the price of a fixed basket of goods and services purchased by households. Its percentage change is the standard headline measure of inflation.

## What it measures

> The CPI compares the cost of the same basket at different dates. It answers *how much more (or less) does the typical household basket cost now than in the base period*, not *how much does a particular household spend*.

## Construction

- A **basket** of goods and services is fixed from household expenditure surveys and weighted by its share of consumption.
- Prices are collected monthly across outlets and regions.
- The index is usually a **Laspeyres-type** index: base-period quantities, current prices.
- **Headline CPI** includes all items; **core CPI** excludes food and energy, which are volatile.
- The **base period** is set to 100; the index is reported as a level and as a year-on-year or month-on-month percentage change.

## Interpretation

> A rising CPI is inflation; a falling CPI is disinflation, not necessarily deflation (deflation is a fall in the price level). The CPI is also used to deflate nominal series into real terms and to index wages, pensions and contracts.

## Limitations

- **Substitution bias**: households shift away from goods whose prices rise; a fixed basket does not.
- **Quality change**: a better product at a higher price is not the same good.
- **Outlet and basket relevance**: new products, e-commerce and changing consumption patterns date the basket.
- It is a *price index*, not a full cost-of-living index, and it differs from the PCE deflator in coverage and weights.
