# Unemployment Rate

> The **unemployment rate** is the share of the labour force that is unemployed: the number of unemployed persons divided by the labour force (employed plus unemployed).

## What it measures

> The rate answers *how much of the people who want to work and are looking cannot find work*. Its denominator excludes people outside the labour force — those not working and not seeking work — which is why it can fall while the employment situation worsens.

## Measurement

- The ILO definition of **unemployed**: without work, currently available for work, and actively seeking work during the reference period.
- The **labour force** is the sum of employed and unemployed persons.
- National definitions differ on active search, availability and registration; harmonised series (OECD, ILO) adjust for comparability.

## Interpretation

> A rise signals a loosening labour market; a fall can reflect hiring *or* discouraged workers leaving the labour force. Read with the participation rate and the employment rate.

## Limitations

- Excludes discouraged workers and the underemployed.
- Does not capture hours, informality or job quality.
- Duration and composition matter more than the headline rate.
- Cross-country comparisons require harmonised definitions.
