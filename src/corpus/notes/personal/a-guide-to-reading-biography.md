# A Guide to Reading Biography


> A **biography** is a detailed account of a person's life, written by someone else, covering their experiences, achievements, and impact.
> 

> **Biography** is a very definite region  bounded on the north by history, on the south by fiction, on the east by  obituary, and on the west by tedium. - Guedalla
> 

> The **art of reading biographies** involves understanding the historical context, analyzing structure and themes, actively engaging with key events and sources, critically evaluating biases, annotating insights, and using logical or computational models to extract deeper knowledge.
>

## Index

## Context

- Read a short summary (e.g., Wikipedia) to get context.
- Understand the person’s era, major accomplishments, and significance.

## Structure

- Look at the table of contents to see how the biography is organized.
- Check for themes: chronological order, key achievements, personal struggles, etc.

## **Read Actively**

- Highlight key events, decisions, and turning points.
- Note recurring themes (e.g., resilience, innovation, leadership).
- Look for primary sources (quotes, letters, diary entries) for authenticity.

## **Analyze Bias & Perspective**

- Consider the author’s perspective—are they objective, critical, or admiring?
- Cross-check with other sources for a fuller picture.

## eBook Reading Strategy

- annotate the book itself, and flag the annotations with post-its; or
- transfer quotations into a Word document - simply page number and quotation (sometimes with a third for 'thoughts').

## Warnings

- Assuming the biography is 100% accurate and unbiased.
- Overlooking that the author may have personal opinions or agendas.
- Consuming the biography like a story without critically engaging.
- Judging the subject solely by today’s values without understanding their era.
- Believing the biography tells the whole story without considering others involved.
