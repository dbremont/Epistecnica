## Locked Method

> John Locke, the 17th-century English philosopher, developed a method for organizing commonplace books that aimed to efficiently categorize and retrieve information. Commonplace books were a way for scholars, students, and thinkers to compile knowledge, quotations, observations, and ideas from various sources. Locke’s system was innovative for its time, providing a structured approach to information management.
> 

### Key Aspects of Locke’s Method:

1. **Alphabetical Indexing**:
    - Locke introduced a method based on an alphabetical index. Each page in the commonplace book was assigned to a specific letter of the alphabet and divided into several subsections according to the first two letters of a keyword.
    - For example, if you wanted to record something about "love," you might place it under "Lo" in the book. This way, if you later wanted to find information about "love," you could easily locate it under that section.
2. **Categorization by Headings**:
    - Locke’s system encouraged users to assign headings or topics to the information they recorded. These headings were usually one or two words that captured the essence of the entry.
    - The headings were then indexed in the alphabetical system, making it easier to cross-reference and find related entries.
3. **Use of Margins**:
    - Locke recommended writing the main content of the entry on the right side of the page and leaving the left margin for noting down the headings or key ideas.
    - This marginalia served as a quick reference, allowing the reader to scan the pages for relevant topics without reading the entire entry.
4. **Flexibility and Adaptation**:
    - Locke’s method was designed to be adaptable. Users could expand the index as needed, adding new topics or refining existing ones.
    - This flexibility made it suitable for a wide range of subjects and types of content, from literary excerpts to personal reflections.
