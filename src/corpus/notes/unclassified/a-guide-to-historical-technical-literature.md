# Historical Technical Library: Technology and Engineering, 1600–1900

> The library is organized as a **multidisciplinary collection** rather than a mechanical-engineering one. Its objective is to reconstruct the **technical knowledge, engineering methods, productive processes, machinery and technological capabilities** available during the early modern and industrial periods.

Three historical periods organize it:

1. **1600–1750:** Mechanical arts, practical inventions, hydraulics, mining and early industrial techniques.
2. **1750–1850:** Industrial engineering, applied mechanics, chemical processes, metallurgy and agricultural improvement.
3. **1850–1900:** Engineering sciences, industrial chemistry, electrical technology, instrumentation and systematic manufacturing.

What follows is a curated collection of **primary technical literature** — not modern histories of technology.

## 1. Mechanical arts, machinery and machine design

| Author | Work | Year | Technical domain |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Fausto Veranzio | *Machinae Novae* | 1615–16 | Mechanical inventions |
| Georg A. Böckler | *Theatrum Machinarum Novum* | 1661 | Machinery and mechanisms |
| Bernard Forest de Bélidor | *Architecture Hydraulique* | 1737–53 | Hydraulic machinery |
| Denis Diderot & Jean le Rond d'Alembert | *Encyclopédie: Recueil de planches sur les sciences, les arts libéraux et les arts mécaniques* | 1762–72 | Mechanical arts and manufacturing |
| Charles Babbage | *On the Economy of Machinery and Manufactures* | 1832 | Manufacturing systems and production organization |
| William J. M. Rankine | *A Manual of Machinery and Millwork* | 1869 | Machine construction |
| Franz Reuleaux | *The Kinematics of Machinery* | 1875 | Mechanism theory and machine design |

**Why these matter:** they allow the evolution from individual mechanical inventions to the systematic representation of machines, mechanisms, production operations and machine design to be studied directly. The *Encyclopédie* is the hinge: its technical plates document workshops, tools, production equipment and manufacturing operations.

## 2. Industrial chemistry and chemical engineering

| Author | Work | Year | Technical domain |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Humphry Davy | *Elements of Chemical Philosophy* | 1812 | Chemical science |
| Andrew Ure | *A Dictionary of Arts, Manufactures, and Mines* | 1839 | Industrial technology |
| Justus von Liebig | *Chemistry in Its Application to Agriculture and Physiology* | 1840/42 | Applied chemistry |
| James Sheridan Muspratt | *Chemistry, Theoretical, Practical, and Analytical, as Applied and Relating to the Arts and Manufactures* | 1860 | Industrial chemistry |
| Rudolph Wagner | *A Handbook of Chemical Technology* | 1868/72 | Chemical manufacturing |
| Thomas Edward Thorpe | *A Dictionary of Applied Chemistry* | 1891–94 | Applied chemical knowledge |

**The strongest recommendations:** Ure for the technological composition of an industrial economy; Muspratt for the chemical processes behind industrial products; Wagner for a more systematic handbook of chemical technologies. Muspratt's two volumes document chemical substances, manufacturing applications, and processes such as bleaching, dyeing, glassmaking, soapmaking, paper production and metallurgy; the Science History Institute provides digitized originals of both volumes.

## 3. Metallurgy, mining and mineral processing

| Author | Work | Year | Technical domain |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Georgius Agricola | *De Re Metallica* | 1556 | Mining and metallurgy |
| Lazarus Ercker | *Treatise on Ores and Assaying* | 16th century | Ore analysis and metallurgy |
| John Percy | *Metallurgy: The Art of Extracting Metals from Their Ores* | 1861 | Extractive metallurgy |
| John Percy | *Metallurgy: Iron and Steel* | 1864 | Iron and steel production |
| Hilary Bauerman | *A Treatise on the Metallurgy of Iron* | 1868 | Iron metallurgy |
| Isaac Lowthian Bell | *The Chemical Phenomena of Iron Smelting* | 1872 | Blast-furnace chemistry |

Agricola and Ercker predate the stated period but stay as foundational references: they document the practical knowledge from which later mining and metallurgical engineering developed. For the nineteenth century, **John Percy's metallurgy series is indispensable** — an unusually detailed treatment of materials, furnaces, ores, extraction and metal production.

## 4. Agricultural technology and agricultural sciences

| Author | Work | Year | Technical domain |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Jethro Tull | *The Horse-Hoeing Husbandry* | 1731 | Agricultural machinery and cultivation |
| Humphry Davy | *Elements of Agricultural Chemistry* | 1813 | Agricultural chemistry |
| Justus von Liebig | *Chemistry in Its Application to Agriculture and Physiology* | 1840 | Plant nutrition and agricultural chemistry |
| James F. W. Johnston | *Elements of Agricultural Chemistry and Geology* | 1853 | Soil and agricultural chemistry |
| Samuel W. Johnson | *How Crops Grow* | 1868 | Plant physiology and crop science |
| Samuel W. Johnson | *How Crops Feed* | 1870 | Plant nutrition |

These works show the development of agriculture from practical cultivation techniques toward experimentally grounded agricultural science — Davy for the chemical composition of soils, plants and agricultural materials; Liebig for the chemical foundations of plant nutrition; Johnston for geology, soil composition and agricultural production; Johnson for plant composition, growth and nutrient acquisition.

| Thomas Tredgold | *A Practical Essay on the Strength of Cast Iron and Other Metals* | 1822 | Structural materials |
| Thomas Tredgold | *Elementary Principles of Carpentry* | 1820 | Structural construction |

Tredgold matters because his cast-iron tract shows engineering moving from empirical construction practice toward experimentally established material properties and quantitative structural design — the reference for the early nineteenth century's iron structures.

|---|---|---:|---|

| Alessandro Volta | *On the Electricity Excited by the Mere Contact of Conducting Substances* | 1800 | Electrical phenomena |
| Michael Faraday | *Experimental Researches in Electricity* | 1839–55 | Electromagnetism |

The experimental foundations and the later engineering literature are distinct literatures: Priestley and Faraday carry the scientific knowledge; Prescott, Lockwood and Slingo & Brooker carry the actual technological systems.

This category deserves further expansion into distinct technological branches — optical instruments; precision mechanical instruments; electrical measuring instruments; surveying and geodetic instruments; thermometry and pressure measurement; acoustic instruments; seismological and geophysical instrumentation. These are separate families, not subdivisions of general physics.

## 13. Distinguishing technology from engineering science

Four separate bibliographic categories organize the collection:

| Category | Object of study | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Technical arts | Practical techniques and operations | Diderot's *Encyclopédie* |
| Technology | Machines, apparatus, processes and production systems | Ure's *Dictionary* |
| Engineering | Design and construction of technical systems | Rankine's manuals |
| Engineering science | Scientific principles underlying technical systems | Reuleaux, Carnot and Faraday |

The distinction matters because a book about the physical principles of a steam engine is not the same kind of technical document as a book explaining how to manufacture, install, operate and maintain one. The strongest starting pair: **Ure's 1839 dictionary and Diderot's *Encyclopédie*** — together an unusually rich beginning for reconstructing the technological capabilities and productive knowledge of historical societies.
