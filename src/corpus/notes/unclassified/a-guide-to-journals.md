# A Guide to Scientific Journals

> A catalog of the **venues** rather than the works: the journals, proceedings and transactions in which the scientific literature was actually printed. Its companion guides list what was known ([Historical Scientific Literature Review](/view.html?p=notes/unclassified/a-guide-to-historical-scientific-literature.md)) and what was made ([Historical Technical Literature](/view.html?p=notes/unclassified/a-guide-to-historical-technical-literature.md)); this one lists where either came to be published.

> A journal is not a container but an **institution with four functions**: it *registers* a claim with a date, it *certifies* it by review, it *disseminates* it, and it *archives* it. The four came apart historically — the academies did registration and archiving for a century before refereeing existed — and they are coming apart again, which is why the last section is about preprints.

> The columns are `Journal | Founded | Body | What it did`. **Founded** is the first year of the title or of its direct predecessor; **Body** is the academy, society or publisher; **What it did** names what the venue is known for, not what it contains.

## Logia Academiarum

> The proceedings of the learned bodies. Before the specialised journal existed, the academy's volume *was* the literature: it printed the note, dated it, and archived it. The weekly and monthly proceedings are the direct descendants.

| Journal | Founded | Body | What it did |
|---|---:|---|---|
| [Philosophical Transactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_Transactions_of_the_Royal_Society) | 1665 | Royal Society of London | The first journal devoted to science: registration by dated letter, and the model every later journal copied |
| Journal des sçavans | 1665 | Paris | The first learned journal of any kind; reviews and extracts rather than original claims |
| Acta Eruditorum | 1682 | Leipzig | Leibniz's Latin review of the European republic of letters; where the calculus was announced |
| Mémoires de l'Académie Royale des Sciences | 1699 | Paris Academy | The academy volume as the literature: dated, sealed, archived |
| Commentarii Academiae Scientiarum Petropolitanae | 1728 | St Petersburg Academy | Euler's first printing venue, and the Russian academy's window on Europe |
| Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society | 1838 | American Philosophical Society | The American academy's transactions, older than any US specialised journal |
| [Comptes rendus de l'Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comptes_rendus_de_l%27Acad%C3%A9mie_des_Sciences) | 1835 | Académie des sciences, Paris | The weekly note: Cauchy printed several hundred there, and Lebesgue announced the generalised integral in 1901 |
| Atti della Accademia Nazionale dei Lincei | 1876 | Accademia dei Lincei, Rome | The Italian academy's proceedings, carrying the nineteenth-century mathematical school |
| Sitzungsberichte der Preußischen Akademie der Wissenschaften | 1882 | Berlin Academy | Planck's and Einstein's early notes, and the German academy's weekly record |
| Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences | 1914 | US National Academy | The American academy volume; short papers, member-communicated |
| Doklady Akademii Nauk | 1933 | USSR Academy of Sciences | The Soviet academy's rapid-note format, and the venue of Landau, Kolmogorov and Sakharov |

## Logia Matheseos

> Mathematics acquired specialised journals a century before the other sciences, because its results needed no instrument and no laboratory — only a printer.

| Journal | Founded | Body | What it did |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik | 1826 | Crelle, Berlin | The first journal devoted to mathematics alone; the model of the editor-founder |
| Journal de mathématiques pures et appliquées | 1836 | Liouville, Paris | The French counterpart; Cauchy, Sturm and Liouville himself |
| Annali di Matematica Pura ed Applicata | 1858 | Brioschi and Cremona, Milan | The Italian school's journal, and the vector-analysis tradition |
| Mathematische Annalen | 1868 | Teubner, Leipzig | The German flagship: Klein, Hilbert, Noether |
| American Journal of Mathematics | 1878 | Johns Hopkins | The first American mathematical journal, founded by Sylvester |
| Acta Mathematica | 1882 | Mittag-Leffler, Stockholm | The international journal: open to every school, and an early rigorous referee system |
| Annals of Mathematics | 1884 | Princeton | Where the twentieth century's central results were printed |
| Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society | 1891 | AMS | Short announcements and the book-review record of the field |
| Transactions of the American Mathematical Society | 1900 | AMS | Full-length papers; the split between announcement and article made permanent |
| Mathematische Zeitschrift | 1918 | Springer, Berlin | Founded as the rival of the *Annalen*; where much of the Göttingen output landed after 1933 |
| Duke Mathematical Journal | 1935 | Duke University | The American generalist journal of the mid-century |
| Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics | 1948 | Courant Institute | The applied-analysis bridge: PDEs read as mathematics |
| Publications mathématiques de l'IHÉS | 1959 | Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques | Long papers of the French school: Grothendieck, Deligne |
| Inventiones Mathematicae | 1966 | Springer | The post-war generalist flagship; where the modern research article took its present length |
| Journal of Functional Analysis | 1967 | Academic Press | The operator-theory journal, and the home of the spectral literature |
| Journal of the American Mathematical Society | 1988 | AMS | The society's flagship, founded to compete at the top of the citation table |
| Annals of Statistics | 1973 | Institute of Mathematical Statistics | The statistics side, split from the *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* (1930) |

## Logia Physicae

> Physics is the science whose journals are most entwined with its discoveries: the *Annalen* printed Einstein because it was the German physics journal, not because it was prestigious.

| Journal | Founded | Body | What it did |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Philosophical Magazine | 1798 | Taylor & Francis, London | The British physics journal of the nineteenth century; Faraday, Maxwell, Joule |
| Annalen der Physik | 1799 | Halle, later Leipzig | The German physics journal: Planck's quantum paper and Einstein's 1905 papers |
| Annales de chimie et de physique | 1789 | Paris | The French physical-chemistry journal of the revolutionary era |
| Il Nuovo Cimento | 1844 | Italian Physical Society | The Italian physics journal, and the early quantum literature |
| Physical Review | 1893 | American Physical Society | The American physics journal; the twentieth century's centre of gravity |
| Zeitschrift für Physik | 1920 | Springer, Berlin | The German journal of the quantum revolution — and the one that lost it after 1933 |
| Reviews of Modern Physics | 1929 | American Physical Society | The review article as a genre: a field stated once, for everyone |
| Physical Review Letters | 1958 | American Physical Society | The four-page letter, and the speed race that came with it |

## Logia Caeli

> Astronomy's journals are the oldest continuously published scientific periodicals after the *Transactions*, because the data — positions, magnitudes, dates — had to be printed to be usable by the next observatory.

| Journal | Founded | Body | What it did |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Astronomische Nachrichten | 1821 | Altona, later Kiel | The international clearing house of positional astronomy |
| Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society | 1827 | RAS, London | The British astronomical record, and the venue of the 1915 general-relativity papers |
| Astronomical Journal | 1849 | Gould, then AAS | The American positional and dynamical journal |
| Publications of the Astronomical Society of the Pacific | 1889 | ASP | Instrumentation, observatory practice and review |
| The Astrophysical Journal | 1895 | University of Chicago | Astrophysics as a discipline of its own: spectra, magnitudes, stellar structure |
| Icarus | 1962 | Academic Press | Planetary science, founded as the subject became one |
| Astronomy & Astrophysics | 1969 | European Southern Observatory and national societies | The European merger journal, and the model of the consortium journal |

## Logia Chemiae

> Chemistry's journals track the shift from the academy memoir to the rapid communication: the *Berichte* was founded precisely so that results would not wait for a volume.

| Journal | Founded | Body | What it did |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Annales de chimie | 1789 | Paris | Lavoisier's journal, and the printing of the new nomenclature |
| Annalen der Chemie (Liebigs Annalen) | 1832 | Liebig, Giessen | The organic-chemistry journal of the German school |
| Journal für praktische Chemie | 1828 | Erdmann, Leipzig | Practical and analytical chemistry, and an early abstracts section |
| Berichte der Deutschen Chemischen Gesellschaft | 1868 | German Chemical Society | The rapid-communication journal: short papers, weekly, and the model for the letter |
| Journal of the American Chemical Society | 1879 | American Chemical Society | The American chemical record, and the society-owned journal |
| Zeitschrift für physikalische Chemie | 1887 | Ostwald and van 't Hoff, Leipzig | Physical chemistry founded as a journal before it was a department |

## Logia Terrae

> The earth sciences had societies before they had journals, and their journals were built around the regional survey — the map sheet and the stratigraphic column, printed with the paper.

| Journal | Founded | Body | What it did |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Transactions of the Geological Society of London | 1811 | Geological Society | The first geological journal; the stratigraphic memoir with its plates |
| American Journal of Science | 1818 | Silliman, Yale | "Silliman's Journal": the American earth sciences for a century |
| Quarterly Journal of the Geological Society | 1845 | Geological Society, London | The consolidated British record; Darwin's and Lyell's papers |
| Zeitschrift der Deutschen Geologischen Gesellschaft | 1848 | German Geological Society | The German mapping tradition and its regional monographs |
| Bulletin of the Geological Society of America | 1890 | GSA | The American bulletin, built around the regional synthesis |
| The Journal of Geology | 1893 | University of Chicago | Geology read as a physical science, not only a descriptive one |

## Logia Vitae

> The life sciences came late to the specialised journal and then dominated the landscape: physiology, then genetics, then molecular biology each founded a journal when the field acquired a method.

| Journal | Founded | Body | What it did |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Pflügers Archiv | 1868 | Bonn | The first physiology journal: the method paper and the tracing |
| Journal of Physiology | 1878 | Physiological Society, London | The British physiological record, and the founding of the society-owned journal |
| Journal of Experimental Medicine | 1896 | Rockefeller Institute | Experimental medicine as a laboratory science |
| Journal of Biological Chemistry | 1905 | American Society for Biochemistry | The biochemistry journal, and the rise of the methods paper |
| Genetics | 1916 | Genetics Society of America | Genetics as a discipline with its own literature |
| Journal of Cell Biology | 1955 | Rockefeller Institute | Electron microscopy and the cell as an object of its own |

## Logia Medicinae

> Medicine's journals are the oldest mass-circulation scientific press, and the first to be read by practitioners rather than by the academy.

| Journal | Founded | Body | What it did |
|---|---:|---|---|
| The New England Journal of Medicine | 1812 | Massachusetts Medical Society | The longest-running medical journal in English |
| The Lancet | 1823 | Thomas Wakley, London | The independent medical weekly: reports, campaigns and the clinical trial |
| The BMJ | 1840 | British Medical Association | The general medical journal, and an early evidence-based-medicine push |
| JAMA | 1883 | American Medical Association | The American medical weekly, and the modern trial-report format |
| Annals of Internal Medicine | 1927 | American College of Physicians | The internal-medicine review journal |

## Logia Mentis

> Psychology founded its journals as it left philosophy: *Mind* was a philosophy journal that made room for the new science, and the rest followed within twenty years.

| Journal | Founded | Body | What it did |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Mind | 1876 | Alexander Bain, Oxford | Philosophy's journal, and the first home of the new psychology |
| American Journal of Psychology | 1887 | G. Stanley Hall, Johns Hopkins | The first English-language psychology journal |
| Psychological Review | 1894 | American Psychological Association | Theory and the review article in psychology |
| Journal of Experimental Psychology | 1916 | APA | The laboratory paper as the discipline's unit |
| Cognitive Science | 1977 | Cognitive Science Society | The interdisciplinary journal, founded with the field |
| Behavioral and Brain Sciences | 1978 | Cambridge University Press | The target article with open peer commentary — a genre invented for a field |

## Logia Machinarum

> The technical journals are society journals before they are academic ones: the profession certifies itself through its proceedings.

| Journal | Founded | Body | What it did |
|---|---:|---|---|

| Proceedings of the Institution of Civil Engineers | 1837 | ICE, London | The engineering memoir: the work described, the calculation shown |
| Proceedings of the IRE | 1913 | Institute of Radio Engineers, later IEEE | Electronics and radio, and the ancestor of the IEEE Transactions |
| IEEE Transactions on Information Theory | 1953 | IRE/IEEE | Shannon's field given its own journal |

| Communications of the ACM | 1958 | ACM | The profession's magazine, and the bridge from research to practice |
| SIAM Journal on Computing | 1972 | SIAM | Algorithms and complexity as a field with a journal of its own |

## Logia Oeconomiae

> Economics acquired its journals with its mathematisation, and its prestige ordering has been tied to them more tightly than in any other science.

| Journal | Founded | Body | What it did |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Journal of the Statistical Society of London | 1838 | Statistical Society, London | The statistical record, and the ancestor of the *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society* |
| The Quarterly Journal of Economics | 1886 | Harvard | The first English-language economics journal |
| Journal of Political Economy | 1892 | University of Chicago | The Chicago journal, and the case-study tradition |
| American Economic Review | 1911 | American Economic Association | The association's flagship and its annual proceedings |
| Econometrica | 1933 | Econometric Society | Econometrics founded as a journal; where the formal apparatus was standardised |
| The Review of Economic Studies | 1933 | Oxford | The young economists' journal, and the theory paper as its unit |

## Logia Electronica

> The last section is not history: the four functions of a journal — registration, certification, dissemination, archiving — are being unbundled, and the preprint server takes the first and third while the journal keeps the second.

| Venue | Founded | Body | What it did |
|---|---:|---|---|
| arXiv | 1991 | Los Alamos, later Cornell | The preprint server: registration and dissemination without certification, and the model for every field since |
| PLOS ONE | 2006 | Public Library of Science | Review for soundness rather than importance; the megajournal |
| eLife | 2012 | HHMI, Max Planck, Wellcome | The journal as an overlay on preprints, and the attempt to replace the acceptance decision |
| bioRxiv | 2013 | Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory | The life-sciences preprint server, a decade after physics |
| SciPost | 2016 | SciPost Foundation | Community-run, publicly funded, with open refereeing |
| Journal of Open Source Software | 2016 | Open Source Software Foundation | Software as a citable research output |
| Zenodo | 2013 | CERN | A repository rather than a journal: the archive function, detached from the venue |
