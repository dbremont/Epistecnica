# Secure Multi-Party Computation


> Secure Multiparty Computation is a cryptographic technique that enables multiple parties to jointly compute a function over confidential inputs, ensuring privacy and maintaining data integrity.

## Decision Making

Secure Multi-Party Computation (SMPC) is a cryptographic technique that allows multiple parties to collaboratively compute a function on their individual private inputs while keeping those inputs confidential. In the context of decision-making, SMPC enables parties to make joint decisions without revealing their individual preferences or inputs. Here’s how SMPC can enhance secure decision-making:

1. **Privacy-Preserving Decision-Making**: SMPC ensures that individual parties can contribute their inputs for decision-making without disclosing the specifics of their inputs to each other. This is particularly useful when sensitive or confidential information is involved.
2. **Joint Analysis**: SMPC enables parties to jointly analyze and compute functions over their private inputs, such as aggregating preferences, calculating weighted averages, or determining consensus, while preventing any party from learning the individual values of others.
3. **Trust Minimization**: By using SMPC, parties can make decisions without having to fully trust each other. They rely on the cryptographic protocols to ensure that no party gains an advantage by learning more than they should.
4. **Fairness and Equity**: SMPC can help ensure fairness and equity in decision-making processes by preventing any party from manipulating or biasing the outcome based on their private inputs.
5. **Complex Decision Models**: SMPC allows for the computation of complex decision models involving multiple variables and constraints while maintaining data privacy.
6. **Real-World Applications**: SMPC can be applied in scenarios like budget allocation, resource sharing, negotiation, and other collaborative decision-making processes where privacy and confidentiality are paramount.
