# Cow Production

> **Cow production** is the branch of animal production that raises cattle for what they yield: milk from the dairy herd, meat from the beef herd, and — where the same animals are kept for both — a dual-purpose system that trades some of each. It is the largest animal-production enterprise by value and land, and the one whose biology sets the longest clock: a cow is a year in gestation, two years to first calving, and a decade in the herd.

> The two specialised notes are the outputs — [Beef Cattle Production](/view.html?p=notes/technica/production/industry/agriculture/animal/beef-cattle-production.md) and [Dairy Production](/view.html?p=notes/technica/production/industry/dairy-production.md). This note is the animal they share: the herd, its reproduction, its feeding and the systems it is kept in.

**Guiding Questions:**

- What is the production (economic) activity of **cow production**?
- When is a herd beef, dairy or dual-purpose, and what does the choice cost?
- What fixes the number of calves a cow can produce in a lifetime?
- Where does the feed come from, and what does the rumen let the animal do that a pig or a chicken cannot?

## Herd Structure

> A cattle enterprise is a population with an age structure, and the structure is the production plan: only the cows reproduce, so the herd's output per year is the number of calves weaned and the milk those cows give.

| Class | Role | Typical share of a breeding herd |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Breeding cows** | The reproducing core; one calf per year is the biological target | About half to two-thirds of the herd |
| **Replacement heifers** | Future cows, raised from within or bought in | 15–20 %, set by the culling rate |
| **Bulls** | Sires; natural service or artificial insemination | A few per hundred cows, or none with AI |
| **Calves** | The output until weaning | One per cow per year |
| **Stocker and finishing animals** | Grown to slaughter weight, on grass or in a feedlot | The beef output |

> **Replacement rate is the hidden variable.** A cow lasts several parities, so the herd renews at 15–25 % a year; cull cows are themselves a beef output, and the replacement heifer is a cost carried for two years before she earns anything. The dairy herd and the beef herd differ mainly in what the cow is selected for: milk or weaning weight.

## Reproduction

| Event | Typical timing | What it fixes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Puberty and first breeding** | 12–15 months (heifers), 15–18 months (bulls) | Age at first calving, around 24 months |
| **Gestation** | About 283 days | One calf per year requires rebreeding within ~85 days of calving |
| **Calving interval** | 12 months target; 13–14 common | The annual output of the herd |
| **Weaning** | 6–8 months | The calf's entry into growing or finishing |
| **Culling** | After several parities, or on failure to conceive | The replacement demand |

> The calving interval is the production bottleneck. A cow that calves every 13 months instead of 12 loses a twelfth of her lifetime output, and the loss compounds over a herd. That is why nutrition around calving, bull fertility and heat detection are managed as production variables rather than as husbandry details.

## Feeding

> Cattle are ruminants: the rumen's microbial population digests cellulose that no monogastric animal can use, which is why cattle can live on grass, straw and by-products and why they compete with humans for grain only in the finishing phase.

| Feed | What it supplies | Where it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pasture and forage** | The cheapest energy and protein; the basis of the herd | Grazing season, or conserved as hay and silage |
| **Silage and hay** | Stored forage for the winter or the dry season | The conservation bridge between seasons |
| **Concentrates** | Energy and protein beyond what forage provides | Dairy cows in milk; finishing cattle; late pregnancy |
| **By-products** | Distillers' grains, beet pulp, oilseed meals | Cheap nutrient recovery from the food chain |
| **Minerals and water** | The limiting factors that are easiest to overlook | Continuous |

> The feed conversion of a cow is poorer than a pig's or a chicken's, and that is not a defect: the cow converts material humans cannot eat — grass, crop residues, by-products — into material they can. The argument about cattle and food security turns on whether the animal is eating grain or grass, not on the species.

## Outputs and Value Chains

| Output | From | Chain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Milk** | The dairy herd | Farm → chilling → processing (pasteurisation, cheese, powder, butter) → retail |
| **Meat** | Beef herd and cull dairy cows | Farm → finishing → slaughter → primal cuts → retail and food service |
| **Hides** | Slaughter by-product | Tannery → leather goods |
| **Manure** | The herd | Compost or slurry → soil nutrient and organic matter |
| **Calves and breeding stock** | The herd's reproduction | Sale to other farms, or herd replacement |

## Production Systems

| System | Feed base | Where it fits | What it optimises |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Extensive ranching** | Native or sown pasture, large land area | Dry grasslands, rangelands | Land and labour cost per animal |
| **Pasture-based dairy** | Grazed pasture with supplementary feed | Temperate regions with reliable rainfall | Milk from forage |
| **Intensive dairy** | Conserved forage plus high concentrate | Housed herds, high-yield breeds | Milk per cow |
| **Feedlot finishing** | Grain and by-product ration | The last months before slaughter | Growth rate and carcass grade |
| **Mixed crop–livestock** | Crop residues and pasture in rotation | Smallholder and integrated systems | Nutrient cycling and risk spreading |
| **Dual-purpose** | Pasture plus the sale of milk and calves | Subtropical regions, e.g. the Brangus country | Both outputs from one herd |

> The systems are distinguished by what the cow eats, not by the breed alone — but the breed is matched to the system: a high-yield Holstein needs the feed and housing of an intensive dairy, while a Brahman-influenced composite such as [Brangus](/view.html?p=notes/nature/life/organism/animal/brangus.md) is built for heat, parasites and pasture.
