# Mutual Fund

> A **mutual fund** is a pooled investment vehicle that issues shares or units to investors and uses the proceeds to buy a portfolio of assets, valued at the fund's **net asset value** (NAV).

## The forms

| Form | Redemption | Pricing | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Open-end fund** | At NAV, daily | NAV once a day | The US mutual fund, the European UCITS |
| **Closed-end fund** | Traded on an exchange | Market price, at a discount or premium to NAV | A listed investment trust |
| **Unit trust** | At NAV, per the trust deed | NAV | The UK unit trust |
| **[ETF](etf.md)** | Intraday on an exchange | Market price, arbitraged to NAV | The index ETF |
| **Money-market fund** | Daily, at a stable NAV | NAV | A liquidity vehicle |

## Costs and management

- **Expense ratio** — the annual charge deducted from the fund.
- **Load** — a sales charge, front or back, on entry or exit.
- **Active vs passive** — a manager selecting holdings, or a rule tracking an index.

The fund is a **collective** vehicle: the investor owns units in the pool, not the underlying assets directly, and the manager owns the decisions. That separation is the fund's advantage — diversification, scale, administration — and its agency problem: fees, mandate drift, and closet indexing.

## Fund vs direct holding

A fund buys diversification and administration at the cost of a fee and a loss of control; a direct holding gives control and tax treatment at the cost of concentration. The choice is a trade of scale against specificity.
