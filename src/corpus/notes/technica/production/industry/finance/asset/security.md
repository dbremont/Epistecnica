# Security

> A **security** is a tradable financial claim — a unit of equity, debt, or a hybrid of the two — that can be bought and sold in a market and is the object of securities regulation.

## The families

| Family | Claim | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Equity** | Ownership in the issuer | [Share](share.md) |
| **Debt** | A fixed claim to interest and principal | [Bond](bond.md) |
| **Hybrid** | A debt claim convertible or preferred into equity | [Convertible bond](convertible-bond.md) |
| **Securitised** | A claim on a pooled set of assets | Mortgage-backed and asset-backed securities |

[Derivatives](derivative.md) are usually treated separately: their value is derived from a security rather than being a claim on the issuer.

## What makes a security a security

- **Tradability** — the claim is embodied in an instrument that can be transferred.
- **Standardisation** — the terms are uniform enough for a market to price.
- **Regulation** — issuance and trading fall under securities law, with disclosure and conduct rules.

## Securitisation

A pool of illiquid claims — mortgages, auto loans, credit-card receivables — is sold to a special-purpose vehicle, which issues securities backed by the pool's cash flows. The pool is tranched by seniority, so the same collateral supports claims of different risk. The mechanism turns loans into tradable instruments, and it is also how a local credit risk becomes a systemic one when the tranching hides the correlation.
