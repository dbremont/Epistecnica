# Technique

> **Technique** is the meta note on technique itself: what a technique is as an object of study, why the branch that studies it cannot take the name *technology*, and the forms of change a technique can undergo.

> The naming problem is real. The branch that studies technique should be called *technology*, but the word has been taken by the technical objects themselves — so the corpus keeps *technica* for the branch and reserves *technology* for the systematic study of technique in the canonical guide. The metonymy is not an error of usage; it is the reason the vocabulary needs a note, and the readings are collected in [Technology](/view.html?p=notes/technica/meta/tecnica.md).

## Technique as an object of study

> Five questions pare the object down: whether a technique is ontic, epistemic or relational; how it exists when nobody is using it; how techniques co-evolve; and in which forms it can participate in the change of reality.

- Is a technique primarily ontic, epistemic, or relational?
- How does a technique exist when not in use?
- How do techniques co-evolve?
- In what types of dynamics or forms of change in reality can a 'technique'—regarded as a potential first-class syntonic object—participate or be engaged?
- Which forms can techniques take?

The first two are answered in [Meta Tecnica](/view.html?p=notes/technica/meta/tecnica.md): technique is a procedure held in a practice, reproducible without being an act. The third belongs to the evolution scope. The last two are answered by the table below, which is this note's own contribution.

## Terminology

The vocabulary of technique is defined in the Glossarium. This note lists the terms that have an entry, then keeps the ones that do not.

| Term | Where it is defined |
| --- | --- |
| **Technique** | [Glossarium](/glossarium/?t=technique) — a procedure for a class of technical operations, held in the practice → task → general technique → operative technique → constitutive technique → technical act chain; not a practice, not an act — the procedure itself. |
| **Technical Change** | [Glossarium](/glossarium/?t=technical-change) — a difference between two technical states or configurations. |
| **Technical Evolution** | [Glossarium](/glossarium/?t=technical-evolution) — historical change in technical artifacts, processes, knowledge and capabilities over time. |
| **Technique Efficiency** | [Glossarium](/glossarium/?t=technical-efficiency) — evaluation of a technique by performance metrics, resource usage and effectiveness at achieving its goal. |
| **Technique Diffusion** | [Glossarium](/glossarium/?t=technical-diffusion) — the spread and adoption of techniques across individuals, organizations or societies. |
| **Technical Reverse Engineering** | [Glossarium](/glossarium/?t=technical-reverse-engineering) — the analysis of an existing technical system to identify its components, techniques, structures and principles, so that it can be replicated, improved or integrated. |

### Terms without an entry

- **Technique Space** — the set of all possible techniques available within a domain, visualized as a landscape of options or capabilities. Read as a natural element, it is [Technique Space](/view.html?p=notes/nature/technique-space.md).
- **Technique Interdependence** — the way techniques rely on, complement, or constrain each other within the technique space.
- **Technique Innovation Potential** — the capacity of a given technique or technique space to generate new, more effective methods.
- **Production Space** — the set of all feasible configurations, processes and resources through which techniques can be applied to generate outputs; the operational or systemic landscape in which techniques are enacted, including both physical and organizational dimensions.
- **Technical Replication** — the reproduction of an existing technical system, product or process by applying the same techniques, methods and configurations identified in the original; **faithful reproduction** rather than modification or innovation.

## Forms of technical change

> The table reads a technique as a first-class element and asks in which dynamics it can participate. The seven families are the coarse grammar — generative, transformational, evolutionary, diffusion, structural/systemic, degenerative, reflexive — and the rows are the moves.

| **Dynamic Type**        | **Dynamic**                   | **Description**                                                                  | **Instance(s)**                                                        |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Generative**          | Creation (Origination)        | Emergence of a novel structured action-pattern not previously instantiated.      | Invention of CRISPR protocol; Development of double-entry bookkeeping. |
| **Generative**          | Articulation (Formalization)  | Conversion of tacit know-how into explicit, codified, communicable form.         | Craft practice written into a technical manual; SOP documentation.     |
| **Generative**          | Instantiation (Execution)     | Concrete enactment of a technique in a specific situation.                       | Performing a surgical procedure; Running a deployment pipeline.        |
| **Transformational**    | Adaptation                    | Modification to fit new environmental, institutional, or technical constraints.  | Irrigation technique adapted to desert agriculture.                    |
| **Transformational**    | Optimization                  | Parameter refinement to improve efficiency, speed, or reliability.               | Compiler optimization passes; Lean process refinement.                 |
| **Transformational**    | Recombination                 | Integration of elements from multiple techniques into a composite configuration. | Hybrid ML models; Combining agile and DevOps practices.                |
| **Transformational**    | Abstraction                   | Extraction of higher-order generalizable pattern from specific instances.        | Generalizing “divide-and-conquer” from specific algorithms.            |
| **Transformational**    | Specialization                | Narrowing a general technique to a specific domain or problem class.             | General ML → Radiology image classification technique.                 |
| **Evolutionary**        | Variation                     | Generation of multiple competing versions within a population.                   | Competing software development methodologies.                          |
| **Evolutionary**        | Selection                     | Environmental filtering based on performance, cost, legitimacy, or power.        | Market elimination of inefficient manufacturing techniques.            |
| **Evolutionary**        | Drift                         | Change due to stochastic or non-performance-based influences.                    | Administrative practices changing due to bureaucratic turnover.        |
| **Evolutionary**        | Co-evolution                  | Reciprocal adaptation between technique and surrounding systems.                 | Accounting techniques evolving with regulatory regimes.                |
| **Diffusion**           | Transmission                  | Direct transfer between agents (learning, apprenticeship).                       | Surgical training; Code review knowledge transfer.                     |
| **Diffusion**           | Diffusion (Population Spread) | Network-based propagation across institutions or societies.                      | Global adoption of Lean manufacturing.                                 |
| **Diffusion**           | Translation                   | Modification during cross-context transfer (semantic or institutional shift).    | Western management practices adapted in East Asia.                     |
| **Structural/Systemic** | Embedding                     | Integration into a larger technical or organizational architecture.              | Encryption algorithms inside network stacks.                           |
| **Structural/Systemic** | Modularization                | Encapsulation into reusable, bounded component.                                  | API-wrapped algorithm; Reusable design pattern.                        |
| **Structural/Systemic** | Standardization               | Formal constraint into canonical form governed by norms or standards.            | ISO procedures; IEEE protocol standards.                               |
| **Structural/Systemic** | Infrastructure Coupling       | Dependence on enabling material or symbolic infrastructure.                      | Cloud-native deployment techniques reliant on Kubernetes.              |
| **Degenerative**        | Obsolescence                  | Displacement by superior alternatives.                                           | Film photography techniques replaced by digital imaging.               |
| **Degenerative**        | Atrophy                       | Skill decay due to non-use.                                                      | Manual navigation skills declining due to GPS reliance.                |
| **Degenerative**        | Fossilization                 | Persistence in symbolic form without active functional relevance.                | Ritualized but non-functional bureaucratic procedures.                 |
| **Reflexive / Meta**    | Meta-technical Reflection     | Techniques used to design or improve other techniques.                           | Systems engineering methodologies; Design thinking frameworks.         |
| **Reflexive / Meta**    | Theorization                  | Formal abstraction of technique into general explanatory framework.              | Game-theoretic modeling of negotiation strategies.                     |
| **Reflexive / Meta**    | Automation                    | Encoding of a technique into machine-executable form.                            | CI/CD pipelines automating release processes.                          |
