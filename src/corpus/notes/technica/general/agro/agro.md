# Agro

> **Agro** is the technical domain that turns land, water, nutrients, organisms and labour into a harvested biological yield. It is the domain of the cultivated field and the managed herd — not the science that explains them, and not the industry that sells what they produce, but the organized work that makes a plant or an animal yield something on purpose.

> This note is the entry point to the shelf, not a second decomposition. The plant-level practice is decomposed in [Plant Cultivation](/view.html?p=notes/technica/general/plant-cultivation-technical-domain-set.md), the protection apparatus in [Plant Health](/view.html?p=notes/technica/general/plant-health-technical-set.md), the breeding programme in [Plant Genetic Improvement](/view.html?p=notes/technica/general/plant-genetic-improvement.md), and the sector that organises them in [Agriculture](/view.html?p=notes/technica/production/industry/agriculture/readme.md). What this note adds is the map and the reading order.

## Formulation

### What technical element type does this technical instance belong to?

**Agro belongs to the `Technical Element Set` technical element type — a coherent ensemble of technical elements held together by one bounded field: the cultivated production of biological yield.**

It is a set and not a single practice because cultivation, protection, breeding, irrigation, fertilization, harvesting and the input industries are different technical element types that co-operate inside one domain. It is not a `Production Technical System` in the narrow sense — that type belongs to a particular organized system (a farm, a plant, a line), and agro is the family of systems and practices, not one of them.

The boundaries are worth stating. **Agricultural science** is the epistemic discipline that explains soils, plants, pathogens and populations; it lives in [Agricultural Science](/view.html?p=notes/miscellanea/discipline/agricultural-science.md). **Agriculture** as an industry and a market is the sector master in the production shelf. **The agricultural product space** is the goods that leave the farm. Agro is the technical work in between — the practices a grower or a herd manager actually runs.

### What is this technical instance?

> The domain of the field and the herd: preparing land, selecting and propagating material, supplying water and nutrients, protecting the standing crop, harvesting and handling the product, and breeding the next generation — with the input industries (fertilizer, agrochemicals, seed, machinery) and the measures (yield, efficiency, quality) that judge the result. Its scope runs from a garden bed to an industrial monoculture, and from a herd of cattle to a pond of shrimp.

## Reading Order

> Start with the **sector** if the question is what agriculture is as an industry; start with **cultivation** if the question is how a crop is raised; start with **crop productivity** if the question is why one field out-yields another. The **inputs** (fertilizer, protection chemistry) explain the interventions; the **protection** and **breeding** sets explain how the crop is kept standing and improved; the **goods** space explains what is sold.

> The domain's two independent axes are worth keeping apart. The **biological** axis runs through genetics, physiology and pathology — what the organism can do. The **management** axis runs through water, nutrients, protection, timing and labour — what the grower does. Yield is the product of the two, which is why a better variety and a better fertilizer are not substitutes.

## What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this technical element instance?

The concept under evaluation is **Agro**, defined above as *the technical domain that turns land, water, nutrients, organisms and labour into a harvested biological yield*. The evaluation applies the criteria of [Philosophia Conceptuum et Operis](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/csiglab/Naturgnosis/refs/heads/main/app/note/data/meta/philosophia-conceptuum-et-operis.md).

**Verdicts.** Conceptual Identification — **pass**: the domain is separated from the science that explains it, the sector that organises it, the goods that leave it and the individual practices that compose it. Definitional Clarity — **pass**: "cultivated biological yield" states the operation and the output without importing a particular technology. Conceptual Determinacy — **pass with qualification**: the boundary against forestry, aquaculture and horticulture is conventional — all are cultivation of organisms for yield — and the note admits the family rather than policing it. Internal Consistency — **pass**: the note routes to the decompositions instead of duplicating them, so no branch carries two owners. Representational Adequacy — **qualified**: complete as a map; the depth is deliberately in the linked notes, and that is a choice, not a gap. Purpose Adequacy — **pass**: shaped for a reader entering the shelf — every row is a note and an ownership statement.

| **I. 1600–1800** | Improvement manuals, forest and husbandry systems, population arithmetic | Husbandry becomes a programme of deliberate improvement |
| **II. 1800–1900** | Experimental stations, soil and plant chemistry, inheritance, fermentation, blight, soil genesis | Agriculture acquires experimental science and the field trial |
| **III. 1900–1950** | Genetics, quantitative inheritance, breeding plans, soil microbiology, epidemiology, experimental design | Breeding and experiment become quantitative disciplines |
| **IV. 1950–2000** | Economics of technical change, farm structure, quantitative genetics, epidemiology, the Green Revolution | Productivity, economics and the global food problem meet |
| **V. 2000–2026** | Intensification with limits, food systems, organic and sustainable redesign, planetary boundaries, models and assessments | The environmental and systems frame replaces the yield-only frame |

> The line that runs through the selection: each period's central artefact is a **method as much as a result** — the trial, the hybrid, the selection index, the epidemic model, the response function, the systems model, the assessment. The agricultural literature is unusually explicit about this, which is why it is a good case for reading science as technique.
