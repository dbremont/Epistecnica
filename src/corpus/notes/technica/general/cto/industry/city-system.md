# City

> A **city** is the organized technical system of a settled population: the built works, networks, fleets, facilities and practices that together deliver shelter, water, power, mobility, communication and public services to people within one bounded territory. It is not a building, an infrastructure, or a service — it is the system those things compose.

## Formulation

### What technical element type does this technical instance belong to?

**City belongs to the `Production Technical System` technical element type — an organized set of production technical objects whose interaction realizes a technical capability.** The capability realized is *urban habitability*: a settled population kept sheltered, watered, powered, mobile, connected, safe and served within a bounded territory.

Readable as a `Technical Element Set`: the city is also a coherent ensemble whose members belong to different technical element types — systems (water, power, transit), facilities (hospital, school, depot), objects (dwelling, drain), practices (waste collection, emergency response) — related through one common purpose. The two readings answer different questions and are kept apart: the *system* tree asks how the city's objects interact to realize the capability; a *set* tree would ask what the city contains.

It is **not** a city government (`Technical Agent`), urban planning (`Technical Practice`), or urban infrastructure (`Technical Element Set`): the government is one actor inside the system, planning is one practice, and infrastructure is a part, not the whole. It is **not** a good: the city is the producer, and the goods it yields (housing services, delivered water, mobility) are what gets exchanged.

### What is this technical instance?

> A bounded urban settlement whose built fabric, utility networks, mobility fleets, civic facilities and operating practices are coordinated to sustain daily life: potable water reaching taps, power reaching loads, goods reaching shelves, people reaching work, waste and effluent leaving, and health, education and emergency services responding within minutes.

The catalog of technologies and infrastructures across urban systems — planning, housing, water, energy, waste, mobility, health, digital, education, environment, governance — was compiled as a working list in the urbanism scope and is integrated here as the decomposition below; its 2026-11-12 version is the row-for-row source of the tree.

### What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this `technical element instance`?

The concept under evaluation is **City**, defined above as *an organized technical system of a settled population: the built works, networks, fleets, facilities and practices that together deliver shelter, water, power, mobility, communication and public services within one bounded territory*. The evaluation applies the criteria of [Philosophia Conceptuum et Operis](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/csiglab/Naturgnosis/refs/heads/main/app/note/data/meta/philosophia-conceptuum-et-operis.md).

**Verdicts.** Conceptual Identification — **pass**: the concept is placed against the guide's technical-element types and separated from city government, urban planning and urban infrastructure. Definitional Clarity — **pass with qualification**: every defining term (*organized set*, *production technical object*, *bounded territory*) is a guide term or a measurable attribute, except *settled population*, which needs a working threshold. Conceptual Determinacy — **qualified**: the membership rule admits megacities and small towns alike and leaves the city/non-city boundary at the limits (suburbs, conurbations, city-states) to an explicit territorial rule rather than a population count. Internal Consistency — **pass**: the system reading and the set reading are declared as separate roots (see the guide's multi-root decomposition), so no row has to serve both. Representational Adequacy — **qualified**: the tree represents the city by its technical members, so it captures infrastructure and services well and administrative, cultural and fiscal life only where a technical carrier exists. Purpose Adequacy — **pass**: the purpose is an operative decomposition usable for planning, failure analysis and cross-domain comparison, and the tree is shaped for it.

| Criterion | Verdict | Diagnostic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conceptual Identification | pass | Distinguished from the government, the planning practice and the infrastructure set; typed as `Production Technical System`, readable as `Technical Element Set`. |
| Definitional Clarity | pass (qualified) | *Settled population* is the only term without a guide definition; it is operationalised by the bounded-territory rule under Determinacy. |
| Conceptual Determinacy | qualified | Membership is fixed by the bounded-territory plus service-delivery rule; the suburb (a member of a surrounding system) and the conurbation (a system of cities) are borderline and resolved by that rule, not by population size. |
| Internal Consistency | pass | No path mixes the system and set readings; every non-root row extends its parent by one licensed link form. |
| Representational Adequacy | qualified | Complete for infrastructure and services, partial for administration, culture and finance — included only where they have a technical carrier. |
| Purpose Adequacy | pass | Shaped for operative decomposition: each member system can be drilled into its own note without re-typing the city. |
