# Distribution

> A **distribution** is a continuous linear functional on a space of test functions: $T \in \mathcal{D}'(\Omega)$ sends each $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ to a number $\langle T, \varphi\rangle$, continuity being taken with respect to the topology of the test space. It is not an irregular function; it is the *pairing* that replaces point evaluation, so that objects with no pointwise value — the Dirac delta, the derivative of a step — become exact.

> The construct exists because the derivative is not closed on functions. A limit of differentiable functions need not be differentiable, and classical analysis keeps losing objects it needs: a charge concentrated at a point, an impulse, the Green's function of a differential operator, the Fourier transform of a constant. Schwartz's move was to stop asking what an object assigns to a *point* and ask what it assigns to a *probe* — a smooth compactly supported function — and then to move every operation to the probe side by adjunction.

> Two consequences organise the theory. Differentiation becomes unconditional, since $(\partial^\alpha T)(\varphi) = (-1)^{\lvert\alpha\rvert} T(\partial^\alpha \varphi)$ asks nothing of $T$ — every distribution is infinitely differentiable. Multiplication becomes impossible in general, since no associative product extends the product of continuous functions, so $\delta^2$ has no meaning. Smoothness is free; algebra is the price, and the [impossibility theorem](/view.html?p=notes/epistemica/epistemic/construction/mathematical/schwartz-impossibility-of-multiplication.md) is what the price buys.

## Formulation

### What is this epistemic instance?

> Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ be open. The **test-function space** $\mathcal{D}(\Omega)$ is $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ — smooth functions with compact support in $\Omega$ — carrying the topology generated by the seminorms
>
> $$p_{K,m}(\varphi) = \sup_{x \in K} \sup_{\lvert\alpha\rvert \le m} \lvert \partial^\alpha \varphi(x) \rvert$$
>
> for compact $K \subset \Omega$ and multi-indices $\alpha$. A **distribution** on $\Omega$ is a linear map $T : \mathcal{D}(\Omega) \to \mathbb{C}$ that is continuous in this topology, equivalently a map for which every compact $K$ admits constants $C$ and $m$ with $\lvert \langle T, \varphi\rangle\rvert \le C\, p_{K,m}(\varphi)$ for all $\varphi$ supported in $K$. The distributions form the dual space $\mathcal{D}'(\Omega)$.

> What the theory defines is the **pairing** $\langle T, \varphi\rangle$ and nothing else: there is no $T(x)$, and a claim about the "value of $T$ at a point" is not a hard question but a malformed one. Two embeddings make the construct continuous with what it generalises. A locally integrable function $f$ is represented by the *regular distribution* $T_f(\varphi) = \int_\Omega f\varphi\,dx$, the map $f \mapsto T_f$ being injective, linear, and continuous with dense image in $\mathcal{D}'$ — which is why the identification is written as a convention and not as an equality. A Radon measure $\mu$ is represented by $\varphi \mapsto \int_\Omega \varphi\,d\mu$, so every measure of the theory of measure is a distribution, and the inclusion is proper: $\delta'$ is a distribution and no measure.

> The standard instances fix the vocabulary. The **Dirac delta** is $\langle \delta, \varphi\rangle = \varphi(0)$; the **Heaviside distribution** is the regular distribution of the step, whose distributional derivative is $\delta$; the **Cauchy principal value** is $\langle \mathrm{pv}\,\tfrac{1}{x}, \varphi\rangle = \lim_{\varepsilon \downarrow 0}\int_{\lvert x\rvert > \varepsilon} \varphi(x)/x\,dx$, which is a distribution although $1/x$ is not integrable; the **finite part** is the family of Hadamard regularisations. Each of these is a functional that no function represents.

> The operations are the theory's real content, and each is stated on the probe side. Differentiation is $(\partial^\alpha T)(\varphi) = (-1)^{\lvert\alpha\rvert} T(\partial^\alpha\varphi)$; multiplication by $a \in C^\infty(\Omega)$ is $(aT)(\varphi) = T(a\varphi)$; translation and dilation are the transpose of the inverse action on $\varphi$; convolution with $\psi \in C_c^\infty$ is $(T * \psi)(x) = T(\tau_x \tilde\psi)$, smoothing $T$ into a function, and $T * S$ is defined when one factor has compact support; the Fourier transform is defined on the tempered distributions (see [Tempered Distribution](/view.html?p=notes/epistemica/mathematical/mapping/distribution/tempered-distribution.md)); restriction to an open subset makes the assignment a sheaf, which is what gives *support* and *singular support* their meaning.

> The structure theorem says what a distribution is made of: every distribution is locally a finite sum of distributional derivatives of continuous functions. So the apparent generality is bounded — a distribution is a derivative of something continuous, taken finitely many times, locally.

### What epistemic element type does this epistemic instance belong to?

> Primary type: **`Epistemic Representation`** — the **distribution scheme**: the pairing, the probe system it is taken against, and the rules that license rewriting one distribution into another. The subject of the note is the formal instrument that makes singular objects expressible, not any physical segment they describe.

> `readable as` an **`Epistemic Construction`** when attention falls on the distribution as the object of Schwartz's theory — a mathematical object built, taught, and extended alongside the measure and the integral. `readable as` an **`Epistemic Tool`** when attention falls on it operationally: an instrument that takes a probe and returns a number, as in impulse-response calculus, charge-density modelling, or the Green's function of an operator. `readable as` an **`Epistemic Act`** is *refused*: an act has no constitutive parts, so the test space, the axioms, and the transformation rules decomposed below would have no home under it; the acts that apply the scheme are the computation and the modelling act, never the distribution.

> The formal order is primary; the segments a distribution is used to describe — a charge density, an impulse, a field — are read in the **Ontic Order** when the scheme is applied to them. Which segment admits which distribution is a question for the naturalis grammar, not this one.

### Is the type of this `epistemic element instance` ambiguous?

> Yes, in three cuts the decomposition keeps apart.

> **Mathematical distribution vs probability distribution.** The two share a word and not an element. A probability distribution assigns chances to events — a measure on an event algebra. A mathematical distribution is a continuous linear functional on test functions. They meet where finite Radon measures embed in $\mathcal{D}'$, and that meeting is exactly what makes the confusion plausible; but the probability law is the measure, and the functional that represents it is a different object in a different theory. Reading a probability distribution as a distribution in $\mathcal{D}'$ is a legitimate move that loses the normalisation (total mass one) and gains nothing.

> **Generalized function vs distribution.** Genus and species. *Generalized function* is the umbrella term for the objects that extend the function concept — Schwartz distributions, hyperfunctions, ultradistributions, Colombeau algebras. A distribution is the Schwartz species: the one whose test space is $C_c^\infty$ with its seminorm topology. Not every generalized function is a distribution, and calling a Colombeau algebra a distribution is a mis-filing rather than a shorthand.

> **Distribution vs function, and vs measure.** Both are proper inclusions and neither is onto. $C^0 \subset L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ embeds in $\mathcal{D}'$, but the embedding is not surjective: $\delta$ is not represented by any function. Radon measures embed in $\mathcal{D}'$, but again not onto: $\delta'$ is a distribution no measure represents, since measures assign mass to sets and $\delta'$ assigns none. So "distribution" is not a synonym for "function" (it generalises it) and not a synonym for "measure" (it also generalises it, and beyond).

### What Is a critical conceptual evaluation of this `epistemic element instance`?

> **Verdict.** The construct is as determinate as the theory it belongs to: a functional, linear and continuous on a named topology, with every operation stated as a rule on the probe. Its determinacy is bought by abstraction — the object loses points, and with them the ordinary algebra of functions — and the price is exact rather than incidental: differentiation is total *because* multiplication is not. What the definition cannot tell a user is which distribution represents a given physical density; that is a modelling choice, made outside the scheme.

| Criterion | Verdict | Diagnostic Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conceptual Identification | Strong | Distinguished from a function (no point evaluation, and no function represents $\delta$), from a measure (measures embed, but $\delta'$ does not come from one), from a probability distribution (an event algebra, not a test space), and from a generalized function (the genus); the differentia — continuous linear functional on $C_c^\infty$ — is stated outright. |
| Definitional Clarity | Strong | Linearity plus the seminorm bound, on a topology that is itself specified; no circularity, and the one open parameter — the order $m$ — is explicit rather than hidden. |
| Conceptual Determinacy | Strong | Boundary rule: a functional is a distribution iff it is linear and continuous for the test-space topology. Borderline cases are decided by exhibiting the failing seminorm, not by taste: $\mathrm{pv}(1/x)$ passes, an arbitrary non-continuous functional fails, a Colombeau element is not a distribution because its test object is not a probe. |
| Internal Consistency | Strong | The unconditional derivative and the refused product are two faces of one decision: operations are defined by transposition, and transposition needs a probe on which the operation is classical. Nothing in the scheme promises a product it cannot deliver. |
| Representational Adequacy | Strong | Pairing, test space, axioms, conventions, rules, readings, kinds, and predicates occupy separate branches, so a kind is never confused with a rule and a reading is never confused with a definition. |
| Purpose Adequacy | Strong | The purpose — make singular objects exact and differentiation total — is met exactly, and the scheme never claims an algebra it does not have; the impossibility is stated as a theorem about the scheme, not as a gap in it. |

## QA

### Why can every distribution be differentiated, while distributions cannot be multiplied in general?

> Because differentiation is defined by *transposition*, and transposition needs nothing from the object being differentiated. The derivative of a probe is again a probe — smooth, compactly supported, same support — so $(\partial^\alpha T)(\varphi) = (-1)^{\lvert\alpha\rvert}T(\partial^\alpha\varphi)$ is a definition that always applies. Iterating it, every distribution is $C^\infty$; the classical obstruction, that a limit of differentiable functions need not be differentiable, simply does not arise.

> Multiplication has no such transposition. A product of two functionals would have to be tested against a probe, and the natural candidate $\langle TS,\varphi\rangle = \langle T,\varphi\rangle\langle S,\varphi\rangle$ is not even linear in $\varphi$. Every attempt to repair it runs into the identity $x\,\delta = 0$: associativity would force $(x\delta)\delta = x(\delta^2)$, so $\delta^2$ would have to be supported at the origin, hence a finite combination of $\delta$ and its derivatives; applying the product rule to $x\delta^2$ a second time then contradicts the same identity, whatever coefficients are chosen. Schwartz's theorem is the statement that no associative product extending the continuous product survives this obstruction — see [Schwartz's Impossibility of Multiplication](/view.html?p=notes/epistemica/epistemic/construction/mathematical/schwartz-impossibility-of-multiplication.md).

> Products do exist where the singularities do not collide: when the singular supports of $T$ and $S$ are disjoint, the product is defined pointwise, and Hörmander's wavefront-set criterion extends this to pairs whose singularities never add to a zero covector. What fails is a *global* algebra — one product for every pair.

### Is the Dirac delta a function, a measure, or a distribution?

> All three readings are used, and they are not the same element. The delta is a **Radon measure**: the Dirac measure $\delta_0$, mass one at the origin, zero on every set not containing it. Every Radon measure is a distribution, so it is also a **distribution**: the evaluation functional $\langle\delta,\varphi\rangle = \varphi(0)$. It is *not* a **function**: no function vanishes almost everywhere and still integrates to one, and the algebra of functions forbids the identities the delta is asked to satisfy.

> The physicist's $\delta(x)$ is a notation, not a pointwise object — the symbol writes the functional or the measure in the shape of a density, and the formula $\int \delta(x)\varphi(x)\,dx = \varphi(0)$ is the pairing written as an integral. Where the measure and the distribution readings part company is the derivative: $\delta'$ is a distribution and no measure, and products such as $\delta^2$ have no meaning in either reading. See [Dirac Delta](/view.html?p=notes/epistemica/mathematical/mapping/distribution/dirac-delta-function.md).

### Is a mathematical distribution the same thing as a probability distribution?

> No. They share a word and nothing else structural. A probability distribution assigns chances to events: a measure on an event algebra, normalised to total mass one, and its questions are about the likelihood of sets. A mathematical distribution assigns a number to a **probe**: a continuous linear functional on $C_c^\infty$, and its questions are about whether a singular object can be differentiated, convolved, or transformed.

> There is exactly one bridge, and it explains the confusion: a finite Radon measure is a distribution, so a probability measure on $\mathbb{R}^n$ that is Radon — every probability measure on a Polish space is — represents a distribution. But the embedding forgets the normalisation, which is the whole of the probabilistic content, and it says nothing about independence, conditioning, or convergence in distribution. Two theories, one loanword.
