Trigonometric Playground

live noteplayground#geometry#trigonometry

On the unit circle every trigonometric function is a ratio of two of three numbers: the horizontal coordinate x, the vertical coordinate y, and the radius r — which is 1 by definition. Measure x and y once, and every function follows. Every number on this page is computed in the browser from the angle you set.

The lab

Drag the point around the circle, type an angle (degrees or radians, pi/6 works), or pick a quick angle. Hold Shift while dragging to snap to 15° steps. The blue bar is x, the orange bar is y, and the dashed grey segment is the radius r = 1.

What the page computes

Each function is one ratio of the three numbers the circle gives you. With r = 1 the radius drops out of the arithmetic and the values are just the coordinates or their reciprocals.

FunctionRatioOn the unit circle
sin θy / ry
cos θx / rx
tan θy / xy / x
csc θr / y1 / y
sec θr / x1 / x
cot θx / yx / y
haversin θ(r − x) / (2r)(1 − x) / 2
versin θ(r − x) / r1 − x
coversin θ(r − y) / r1 − y
exsec θ(r − x) / x1/x − 1

A quotient is undefined exactly when its denominator is zero — tan and sec at 90°, cot and csc at 0° — which is the geometric fact that those ratios have no value there, not a numerical accident. The page marks those cells in red.

Out of scope. Hyperbolic functions (sinh, cosh, tanh) are built from eˣ, not from x and y on the circle; inverse functions (arcsin, arccos, arctan) go the other direction — they recover the angle from a ratio.
