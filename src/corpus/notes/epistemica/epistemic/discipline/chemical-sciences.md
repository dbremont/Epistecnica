# Chemical Sciences

> In this set of notes, Chemical Sciences is treated as a **discipline family** — an `Epistemic Practice` whose members are the practices that inquire into substance — composition, structure, transformation, and synthesis.

> **Chemical Sciences** is the family of practices that treat the world as substance: what a sample is made of, how it transforms, and how to make it.

> Its members differ in intent rather than in segment — analysis asks what is there, synthesis asks how to make it, mechanism asks by which path it forms, and application asks what it is good for — while all of them share the bench, the glassware, and the assay as their common infrastructure. That shared instrument and shared standard are what make the family one practice.

> Physical chemistry, biochemistry, and materials chemistry sit on the family's edges and ask a chemical question — which substance, in what amount, by which transformation — with another family's formalism. They are carried here, and the borrowing is recorded in their descriptions rather than by a second parent.

This note is the family-level decomposition; each member discipline is a practice in its own right and can be promoted to its own note without rewriting this table. The schema it instantiates is owned by the canonical [Philosophia Artium Epistemicarum et Operis](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/csiglab/Naturgnosis/refs/heads/main/app/note/data/meta/philosophia-artium-epistemicarum-et-operis.md).

## Formulation

### What is this epistemic instance?

> Chemical Sciences is the organized inquiry into substance: it determines composition and structure, explains and predicts transformation, and synthesizes new matter. Its product is a characterized substance together with the route that made it; its warrant is the reproducible synthesis and the reproducible assay.

### What epistemic element type does this epistemic instance belong to?

> `Epistemic Practice` — the family is a doing: a set of practices carried by agents, applying methods and acts, producing constructions, and judged by its own standard. It is not an `Epistemic Element Set` read as a catalogue, and not an `Epistemic Framework` read as a lens; both are secondary readings, recorded below.

### Is the type of this `epistemic element instance` ambiguous?

> No at the root: Chemical Sciences is one organized doing, so `Epistemic Practice` is its primary type. Secondary readings stay prose: `readable as` an `Epistemic Element Set` when the family is taken as a catalogue of disciplines rather than as a doing, and `readable as` an `Epistemic Framework` when its shared methods are read as one lens over the target. Neither is a second primary type, and no row below carries two types.

> The boundary is drawn by segment and warrant, not by vocabulary. Physics measures the same matter and asks what law governs it; biology studies the organism that the chemistry happens to compose; materials science applies a substance without characterizing its route.

### What is the recursive instance decomposition of this epistemic instance?

> **Boundary.** This table decomposes one epistemic instance — the family as an organized doing — and names its member disciplines as `Member` sub-practices. The scaffolding the members share (orientation, access, representation, evaluation, infrastructure) is typed once at the family root, because the same instruments, standards, and notations serve every member; repeating it per discipline would restate one element once per discipline. Sub-fields are grown only where a discipline genuinely divides, and each remains a `Member` practice, never a second type.

> **Stopping rule.** A row is terminal when it names a discipline or a sub-field. No discipline is decomposed into its own theories, and the segment it addresses is named here but decomposed only in the naturalis grammar.

> **Identity.** Instance Tree Path holds instances only; every path is unique, and no type segment terminates a branch. A discipline that also belongs to a neighbouring family — physical chemistry, biochemistry, biophysics, mathematical physics — is carried by the family whose question it asks, and the overlap is recorded in its description rather than by giving the row two parents.

> **Typing.** The root path declares the role once: `Epistemic Element` (→ Kind) `Epistemic Element Set` (→ Kind) `Epistemic Practice` (→ Instance) Chemical Sciences. Members hang by `Member`, not by `Part`: the family is an aggregate of practices, not a whole with components.

### What Is a critical conceptual evaluation of this `epistemic element instance`?

> **Verdict.** The family is determinate where it is analytic and looser where it is synthetic: composition and yield are measurable, while "which transformation is the essential one" is settled by the mechanism the community accepts. Its strength is the shared bench — one standard of purity and one record of assay serve every member — and its limit is that a synthesis is warranted only for the route actually run.

| Criterion | Verdict | Diagnostic Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conceptual Identification | Strong | Distinguished from physics (substance rather than matter as such) and from biology (molecule rather than organism); the intent list separates analysis from synthesis. |
| Definitional Clarity | Moderate–Strong | "Composition, structure, transformation, synthesis" states the cut; "reproducible synthesis and assay" states the warrant. |
| Conceptual Determinacy | Moderate | Boundary rule: a claim is chemical iff it concerns which substance, in what amount, by which transformation. Borderline cases — physical chemistry, materials chemistry, biochemistry — are placed by the question the practice asks. |
| Internal Consistency | Strong | Analysis, synthesis, and mechanism are members, not competing definitions; the bench is shared infrastructure, not a second type. |
| Representational Adequacy | Moderate–Strong | The structural formula and the assay record cover both the made substance and the measured one; mechanism is carried as a discipline rather than as a row type. |
| Purpose Adequacy | Strong | Making, identifying, and explaining substances are all served; the note does not claim prediction beyond what the assay shows. |
