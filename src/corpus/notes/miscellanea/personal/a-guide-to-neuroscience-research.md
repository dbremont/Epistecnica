# A Guide to Neuroscience Research


> **Neuroscience** is the scientific study of the nervous system, including the brain, spinal cord, and peripheral nervous system, as well as the interactions between these systems and behavior. The field of neuroscience encompasses a wide range of scientific disciplines, including biology, chemistry, psychology, physics, and mathematics.
> 

> **Neuroscientists** use a variety of techniques and approaches to study the nervous system, including brain imaging techniques like fMRI and PET, electrophysiology techniques like EEG and single-unit recording, and genetic and molecular techniques like optogenetics and CRISPR. They study the structure, function, development, and plasticity of neurons and neural circuits, as well as the mechanisms underlying behavior, cognition, and emotion.
> 

> **Neuroscience** is an interdisciplinary field, with researchers from diverse backgrounds working together to understand the complex workings of the nervous system. Advances in neuroscience have led to new treatments for neurological and psychiatric disorders, and have also shed light on broader questions about the nature of consciousness, self-awareness, and the relationship between brain and behavior.
> 

## Phenomena

Neuroscientists make models of various aspects of the brain and neural systems better to understand their structure, function, and behavior. Here are some things neuroscientists make models of:

1. **Neurons**: Models of individual neurons simulate the electrical and biochemical processes occurring within a single neuron, including action potentials, synaptic transmission, and ion channel dynamics.
2. **Neural Networks**: Models of neural networks simulate the interactions between multiple neurons and synapses within a network. These models can represent specific brain regions or circuits and are used to study information processing, learning, and memory formation.
3. **Brain Regions**: Models of brain regions aim to simulate the activity and connectivity patterns within specific brain regions, such as the cortex, hippocampus, or basal ganglia. These models help elucidate the functions of different brain regions and their contributions to cognition and behavior.
4. **Cortical Columns**: Cortical column models simulate the organization and activity patterns within cortical columns, the basic functional units of the cerebral cortex. These models study sensory processing, perception, and cortical dynamics.
5. **Brain Circuits**: Brain circuit models represent the connectivity and activity patterns within larger-scale neural circuits spanning multiple brain regions. These models help elucidate how information is processed and integrated across different brain areas.
6. **Synaptic Plasticity**: Models of synaptic plasticity describe the mechanisms underlying the strengthening or weakening of synapses over time in response to neuronal activity. These models help explain learning and memory processes at the cellular and network levels.
7. **Brain Development**: Brain development models simulate the growth, organization, and connectivity patterns during embryonic and postnatal brain development. These models help elucidate the mechanisms underlying neural morphogenesis and circuit formation.
8. **Neurological and Psychiatric Disorders**: Models of neurological and psychiatric disorders simulate the neural and behavioral abnormalities associated with conditions such as Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, schizophrenia, and depression. These models aid in understanding disease mechanisms and developing therapeutic interventions.
9. **Neural Coding and Information Processing**: Models of neural coding and information processing aim to elucidate how sensory stimuli are encoded by neural activity and processed to generate perception, cognition, and behavior. These models help uncover the principles governing neural computation and communication.
10. **Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs)**: Models of brain-computer interfaces simulate the interaction between neural signals and external devices, enabling direct communication between the brain and computers or prosthetic devices. These models aid in developing assistive technologies and neural prosthetics for individuals with disabilities.

These are just a few examples of the many things neuroscientists make models of to advance our understanding of the brain and neural systems. Models play a crucial role in integrating experimental data, testing hypotheses, and predicting the behavior of complex biological systems.

## Fields

- **Behavioral Neuroscience**: This field focuses on the relationship between the brain and behavior and how different aspects of behavior are controlled by neural systems. Researchers in this field study learning and memory, motivation, emotion, and social behavior.
- **Cellular Neuroscience**: This field focuses on the structure and function of neurons and glial cells, including their morphology, biochemistry, and signaling mechanisms—researchers in this field study neural development, synaptic plasticity, and neurotransmitter release.
- **Cognitive Neuroscience**: This field focuses on the neural mechanisms underlying cognitive processes, such as perception, attention, language, memory, and decision-making. Researchers in this field use various techniques, including brain imaging and electrophysiology, to study how different brain regions work together to support these processes.
- **Computational Neuroscience** focuses on developing mathematical and computational models to understand neural function and behavior. Researchers in this field use techniques such as neural network modeling and machine learning to study the properties of neural circuits and the relationship between the brain and behavior.
- **Developmental Neuroscience** focuses on the development of the nervous system from early embryonic stages to adulthood. Researchers in this field study neurogenesis, axon guidance, and synapse formation.
- **Molecular Neuroscience**: This field focuses on the molecular mechanisms underlying neural function and behavior—researchers in this field study gene expression, protein synthesis, and epigenetic modifications.
- **Systems Neuroscience**: This field focuses on the organization and function of neural circuits, and how they give rise to behavior and cognition. Researchers in this field study sensory processing, motor control, and neural coding.
