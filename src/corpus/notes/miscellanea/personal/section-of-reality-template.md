### **🌌** Signature

> A comprehensive list of aspects to cover in order to understand a topic.
> 

> **Note:** Every exploration of a topic should begin by analyzing the terms linguistically, while keeping in mind that language is only a medium of representation. What we want to uncover is never the language itself, but what it stands for.
> 

> 
> 
> 
> Language is inherently complex—because it is a serialization (text, voice, etc.), it almost never provides a fully faithful reflection of internal mental structures. Therefore, in our inquiry, we must focus on the representation target, carefully disambiguate the language, and approach it critically and evaluatively.
> 

> Never assume the meaning of a term; if it is relevant, examine what it denotes in its specific context.
>

### 🧠 Mental Representation

> Let’s construct a clear ontic–epistemic map of the topic.
> 

> (The Most General Question) (Affirmation Space) what type of affirmations can be done about the this piece of reality and it's manifestations?
> 

> What are the kinds of mental representation that can be held about the topic?
> 

## Input Layer

> See [Ontology](https://app.notion.com/p/Ontology-138eea37a34f43ed87c16d1818629723?pvs=21).
> 

Section of Reality:

- What is the **precise bounded segment of reality** being modeled?
- What are the **spatiotemporal limits** of the domain?
- What entities, processes, and interactions are **included vs excluded**?
- What are the elements of dynamics (Change, Evolution,  Processes, Interaction, Phenomena, Event, etc.) in the section of reality?
- What is the **granularity of resolution** (micro / meso / macro)?
- What are the **primary ontic categories** (objects, processes, big picture process (life cycle, etc), structures, relations)?
- What constitutes a **state of the system** at time *t*?
- What are the **boundary conditions** separating system from environment?
- What are the **causal closure assumptions**, if any?
- Which are the concepts; terms; effective abstractions?
- Which are the properties that can be attributed to  the study subject?
- What is the affirmation type space that applies to the piece of reality?

Epistemic Domain:

- What class of system is this (physical, biological, social, computational, hybrid)?
- What are the **dominant causal regimes** (deterministic, stochastic, chaotic, adaptive)?
- What are the **relevant state variables vs latent variables**?
- What variables are **observed vs inferred vs unobservable in principle**?

Observation Interface:

- What is the **sensorial or measurement boundary** of access to reality?
- What instruments mediate observation (human, machine, hybrid)?
- What is the **sampling mechanism** (continuous, discrete, event-based)?
- What distortions are introduced by the interface (bias, noise, filtering, delay)?
- What is the **transduction pipeline** from physical signal → data artifact?
- What is the failure mode of measurement (saturation, aliasing, dropout)?

Observation Encoding:

- How is raw signal mapped into a **formal representational structure**?
- How to capture the state of affairs?
- What discretization scheme is used (quantization levels, binning, tokenization)?
- What compression or transformation is applied (feature extraction, embedding)?
- What is preserved vs lost during encoding?
- What schema defines validity of encoded observations?

Encoding Substrate

- What physical or computational medium stores observations?
- What are the persistence, mutability, and integrity properties of storage?
- What is the latency between event occurrence and encoding availability?

## Generative Layer

> …
> 

Epistemic Representation Form:

- What formal structure encodes knowledge (graphs, equations, tensors, programs)?
- What primitives define representational expressiveness?
- What operations are syntactically valid in the representation system?
- What constraints restrict admissible forms?

Concrete Epistemic Artifact

- What is the **fully instantiated object representing a claim about reality**?
- What are its parameters, variables, and fixed structures?
- What domain claims does it encode (prediction, explanation, classification)?
- How is its validity evaluated against observed data?
- What is its error structure or residual mapping?

Epistemic Operators:

- What transformation rules map one artifact into another?
- What inference mechanisms are available (deduction, induction, abduction)?
- What optimization procedures adjust artifacts to fit data?
- What simulation procedures generate synthetic observations?
- What validation procedures test artifact consistency?

Epistemic Framework:

- What global reasoning system structures inference (Bayesian, causal, logical)?
- What constitutes a valid explanation within the framework?
- What notions of causality, probability, or mechanism are assumed?
- How are contradictions handled or resolved?

Epistemic Principles:

- What rules constrain model formation (parsimony, falsifiability, invariance)?
- What tradeoffs are prioritized (accuracy vs simplicity vs interpretability)?
- What constitutes epistemic legitimacy?

(Epistemic Priors / Inductive Bias):

- What assumptions constrain hypothesis space before data is observed?
- What structural regularities are assumed (smoothness, sparsity, locality)?
- What hypotheses are considered a priori implausible?

Epistemic Strategy:

- What is the sequencing logic of inquiry (explore → hypothesize → test → refine)?
- What allocation rule governs computational/experimental resources?
- What is the exploration vs exploitation balance?
- What decomposition strategy is used (hierarchical, modular, reductionist)?

Epistemic Process:

- What is the stepwise pipeline from observation → model → revision?
- How are artifacts iteratively updated over time?
- What triggers model revision (error thresholds, anomaly detection)?

Epistemic Constraints:

- What limits identifiability of the system?
- What is the noise structure in observations?
- What computational complexity bounds inference?
- What variables are fundamentally unobservable or underdetermined?

Epistemic Feedback:

- What signals indicate mismatch between model and reality?
- How is prediction error computed and propagated?
- What mechanisms allow correction or reinforcement of artifacts?

Epistemic Standard (Validation)

- What criteria determine correctness (statistical fit, logical consistency, empirical adequacy)?
- How is robustness defined under perturbation?
- What constitutes acceptable error or uncertainty?

Epistemic Act

- What are the atomic operations (observe, compare, infer, intervene)?
- What is the minimal unit of epistemic transformation?
- How is attention allocated during epistemic processing?

## Output Layer

Domain Epistemic Artifact Set (DESA):

- What is the **complete set of instantiated epistemic objects for this domain**?
- What artifacts exist simultaneously (models, datasets, equations, simulations)?
    - Which kinds of theories exists?
    - Which kinds of models exists?
    - Which kinds of theories exists?
- How are artifacts interrelated (dependency graph, causal graph, hierarchy)?
- Which artifacts are primary vs derived vs auxiliary?
- What is the consistency structure across the set?
- What defines completeness of the DESA?

Practical Enabler:

- What transformations map DESA back to actionable predictions or interventions?
- How are artifacts used for control, explanation, or forecasting?
- What real-world interventions are enabled by the DESA?
