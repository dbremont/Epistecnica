# Toxicology

> In this set of notes, Toxicology is treated as an **epistemic discipline** — an `Epistemic Practice` that owns one object: the harmful interaction between a substance and a living system.

> **Toxicology** is the discipline that makes harm from substances knowable and predictable: it asks what a substance does to a living system, at what dose, by which mechanism, under which exposure, and with what warrant the answer is asserted.

> It borrows the identity of the substance from chemistry, the mechanism from biology and physiology, the dose–response formalism from statistics, and the intervention from medicine, and it returns something none of them returns: a warranted statement that this dose of this substance will injure this kind of organism, and how confident that statement may be. The chemistry establishes what the molecule is; toxicology establishes what its presence means for a living system.

> The discipline's boundary is drawn by the question, not by the vocabulary. Pharmacology asks what a substance does that may be therapeutically useful, medicine acts on the organism, chemistry asks what a sample is made of, and epidemiology measures the association between exposure and outcome in people; toxicology asks whether the interaction between substance and living system is injurious, at which dose, and by which path — and it asks it of any substance, not only of poisons.

This note is the discipline-level decomposition. It follows the schema owned by the canonical [Philosophia Artium Epistemicarum et Operis](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/csiglab/Naturgnosis/refs/heads/main/app/note/data/meta/philosophia-artium-epistemicarum-et-operis.md), and the substance it addresses is decomposed in the naturalis grammar — see [Toxin](/view.html?p=notes/nature/life/molecular/toxin.md) for the worked case.

## Formulation

### What is this epistemic instance?

> Toxicology is the organized inquiry into the injurious interaction between substance and living system: whether a substance can injure, at what dose, by which mechanism, under which exposure, and with what probability. Its product is a hazard characterization and a dose–response account that together support a risk statement; its warrant is the reproducible experiment, the replicated observation, and the declared model on which extrapolation rests.

### What epistemic element type does this epistemic instance belong to?

> `Epistemic Practice` — the discipline is a doing: agents applying methods and acts to a segment, producing constructions, and judged by its own standards. It is not an `Epistemic Element Set` read as a catalogue of facts about poisons, and not an `Epistemic Framework` read as one lens over biology; both are secondary readings, recorded below.

### Is the type of this `epistemic element instance` ambiguous?

> No at the root: Toxicology is one organized doing with one question, so `Epistemic Practice` is its primary type. Secondary readings stay prose: `readable as` an `Epistemic Element Set` when the discipline is taken as the corpus of toxicity data rather than as a practice, and `readable as` an `Epistemic Framework` when its dose–response and mode-of-action apparatus is read as a lens over any substance.

> Two boundaries are genuinely contestable and are drawn here explicitly. **Against pharmacology:** the same experiment serves both, but the question differs — pharmacology asks whether an effect can be made useful and at which dose it becomes harmful, toxicology asks whether the effect is harm and at which dose it appears. **Against risk management:** toxicology warrants the statement that a dose carries a given probability of injury; the decision to accept that probability belongs to regulation and politics, not to the discipline.

### What is the recursive instance decomposition of this epistemic instance?

> **Boundary.** This table decomposes one epistemic instance — the discipline as an organized doing — across the scaffolding it shares with every epistemic practice (orientation, segment, observable, access, representation, evaluation, guidance, method, construction, infrastructure, agency) and names its member practices by `Member`. Members are practices in their own right and can be promoted to their own notes without rewriting this table.

> **Stopping rule.** A row is terminal when it names a discipline, a sub-field, a method, a standard, a record form, an instrument or an institution. No member is decomposed into its own theories.

> **Identity.** `Instance Tree Path` holds instances only; every path is unique, and no type segment terminates a branch. A member that belongs to a neighbouring family — ecotoxicology, which shares its segment with ecology; genetic toxicology, which shares it with genetics — is carried here by the question it asks, and the overlap is recorded in its description rather than by a second parent.

> **Level of organization.** The discipline's segment spans molecule (target site), cell (lesion), organism (dose and route), population (exposure cohort) and ecosystem (contamination) — which is why the member practices are filed by the level they cut.

### What Is a critical conceptual evaluation of this `epistemic element instance`?

> **Verdict.** The discipline is sharply bounded where it is analytic — a claim is toxicological when it concerns injury to a living system as a function of dose — and loose where it is predictive, because every prediction crosses a species, a dose range or a mixture. Its strength is the dose–response form, one notation that serves hazard, mechanism and regulation alike; its limit is that the reference value is derived by convention (uncertainty factors) at least as much as by measurement.

| Criterion | Verdict | Diagnostic Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conceptual Identification | Strong | Separated from pharmacology (harm versus usefulness), from medicine (warranting versus intervening) and from chemistry (injury versus composition); the segment is named as the interaction, not the substance. |
| Definitional Clarity | Strong | "Injurious interaction between substance and living system" fixes the object; "reproducible experiment and declared model" fixes the warrant. |
| Conceptual Determinacy | Moderate–Strong | Boundary rule: a claim is toxicological iff it relates a dose or exposure to injury in a living system. Contested cases — pharmacology, ecotoxicology, epidemiology — are placed by the question asked, not by the method used. |
| Internal Consistency | Strong | The member practices are filed by level or by endpoint, never by client; the four-step method appears once as method, not as four competing definitions. |
| Representational Adequacy | Moderate–Strong | The dose–response curve and the reference value carry the central claim; uncertainty is carried in the model and its factors rather than in the curve alone, which is where the residual weakness sits. |
| Purpose Adequacy | Moderate | Hazard identification and protection are well served; risk characterization is served only as far as the exposure model is, and the discipline cannot by itself settle the acceptability of a risk. |

## QA

### Is toxicology a branch of chemistry or of biology?

> Neither, and the attempt to file it under one is what makes its boundaries look confused. Chemistry supplies the substance and the analytical means to identify it; biology supplies the organism and the mechanism. What toxicology adds is the relation between the two at a dose — and that relation has its own experimental form (the dose–response series), its own reference values, and its own failure modes, none of which either parent owns. The same molecule is a chemical object and a toxicological object, and the second description is not derivable from the first.

### Is risk assessment part of toxicology?

> Only the part that warrants the statement. Toxicology establishes the hazard, the dose–response relation and the mechanism, and it estimates the risk with its uncertainty; the *decision* to accept a risk — the setting of a limit, the tolerance of a residue, the withdrawal of a product — is a normative act carried by regulation. The discipline is answerable for the estimate and not for the choice, which is why the decomposition separates the risk estimate (a construction) from the acceptable daily intake (a standard).

### Why is toxinology a member, and not a separate discipline?

> Because it asks the same question with a different substance class. Toxinology studies the toxins — venoms, microbial and plant toxins — as substances that injure, and it does so with the same demand: identity, dose, mechanism, reference value. What it adds is a substance-side expertise (the natural history, the isolation, the structure–activity reading) that the other members do not need, which is exactly what a member practice is for. It is also the member that connects the discipline to the natural corpus, where the toxin kind is decomposed.

### What does the note do with "the dose makes the poison"?

> It treats it as the discipline's founding principle rather than as a slogan. The principle is what makes a toxicological claim falsifiable: no substance is injurious as such, only at a dose, by a route, for a duration. Its consequence is the reference-value apparatus — a no-observed-adverse-effect level, a benchmark dose, an acceptable daily intake — and its cost is that the apparatus must extrapolate from what was tested to what is protected, which is where the discipline's uncertainty lives.
