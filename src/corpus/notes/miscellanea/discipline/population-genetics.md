# Population Genetics

> **Population Genetics** is the study of genetic variation within and between populations: how allele and genotype frequencies are generated, maintained and changed by mutation, selection, genetic drift, migration and recombination.

## Scope

Population genetics connects Mendelian inheritance to evolution. It asks how variation is distributed across populations, how it changes over time, and what forces explain the patterns — from a single locus to genome-wide data.

## Core processes

| Process | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Mutation** | Introduces new alleles. |
| **Selection** | Changes allele frequencies through differential fitness. |
| **Genetic drift** | Changes frequencies through finite-population sampling. |
| **Gene flow** | Moves alleles between populations. |
| **Recombination** | Reshuffles variants across loci. |
| **Population structure** | Creates differentiation through subdivision and isolation. |

## Methods

Allele-frequency models, Hardy–Weinberg analysis, linkage-disequilibrium analysis, coalescent models, genome-wide association and selection scans, and simulation.
