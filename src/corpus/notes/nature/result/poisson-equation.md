# Poisson Equation

> The **Poisson equation** is the elliptic law of a potential generated by a source: the Laplacian of the field equals — up to a coupling constant and a sign — the density that produces it. Written once as $\nabla^{2}u = f$, it is read as the electrostatic potential of a charge distribution, the Newtonian gravitational potential of a mass distribution, the steady temperature of a conducting body with internal heating, the hydraulic head of a seepage flow, the torsion function of a twisted bar, and the stream function of a two-dimensional vortex field. Read per [Philosophia Artium Epistemicarum et Operis](/view.html?p=notes/philosophia-artium-epistemicarum-et-operis.md).

> It is the corpus's cleanest case of an equation that was never about one field. **Poisson wrote it for gravitation** (1813), electrostatics adopted it as its own, and the other four readings arrived later, each without changing a symbol. What changes between readings is the interpretation of $u$, of $f$, and of the constant that couples them — never the form.

## Formulation

### What is this epistemic instance?

**The Poisson equation**, in its general form

\[
\nabla^{2}u(\mathbf{x}) = f(\mathbf{x}),
\qquad \mathbf{x}\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},
\]

with the field $u$ fixed on $\partial\Omega$ (Dirichlet), or its normal derivative fixed there (Neumann), and its two canonical physical readings

\[
\nabla^{2}\varphi = -\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_{0}}
\qquad\text{(electrostatics)},\qquad
\nabla^{2}\Phi = 4\pi G\rho
\qquad\text{(Newtonian gravity)}.
\]

What is **in**: the elliptic operator and its principal symbol; the field as unknown; the source density as inhomogeneity; the coupling constant, which is a convention as much as a constant; the boundary data that select one solution; the fundamental solution and the Green's function built from it; superposition, which follows from linearity; the uniqueness theory; and the screened extension that adds a length scale and turns the equation into the Debye–Hückel and Yukawa form.

What is **out**: the phenomena the six readings describe — the electrostatic field, the gravitational field, the temperature distribution, the seepage, the twisted bar, the vorticity field — each of which is a segment of reality or a natural element typed elsewhere; the **Laplace equation** $\nabla^{2}u = 0$, which is the homogeneous case and a separate construction rather than a part of this one; the operators as mathematical objects; and the numerical solvers, which are techniques.

### What epistemic element type does this epistemic instance belong to?

**`Epistemic Construction`**, kind **`Model`** — a construction enabling prediction or explanation of a segment. It is a *result*: a law of the field obtained from Gauss's law plus the definition of the potential, not an observation and not the theory that carries the constructs. It is **not** a `Theory`: the field and the potential are supplied by the theory that owns them ([electrostatics](/view.html?p=notes/nature/electrostatic-discharge-esd.md), Newtonian gravitation), and the equation states only how the field is sourced. It is **not** a `Description`: it asserts the field at every point of a region, including where nothing has been measured.

Its standing is `Validated` inside the static, linear, continuum regime — where the field is measured to obey it — and it is silent outside it: no retardation, no radiation, no nonlinear medium, no quantum source.

### Is the type of this `epistemic element instance` ambiguous?

**Yes, in three ways, and the third is the reason this note exists.**

First, the name covers the general form and its homogeneous twin. $\nabla^{2}u = f$ and $\nabla^{2}u = 0$ are different models: the first is sourced, the second is not, and the second's solution set is a harmonic-function theory rather than a Green's-function theory. The Laplace equation is cited, not merged.

Second, the six readings are not variants of one physics. Electrostatic and gravitational potentials differ in the sign of the source term and in the constant; thermal conduction has a conductivity inside the divergence; Darcy's law has a permeability; the stream function is a mathematical device rather than a potential. The readings are *instances of one form*, and they are separate models because their sources, coefficients and boundary conditions are different.

Third, and decisively: **the equation's content is a form, and the form does not say which field it is about.** That is not an ambiguity to be resolved but the equation's epistemic character, and it is why the note must be filed once, at the form, with the readings as its instances. Filing it inside electrostatics would make gravitation look like an analogy; filing it inside gravitation would make electrostatics look like one. Both are wrong: the same derivation produces both.

The attribution needs the same care as Boltzmann's. **Poisson** (1813) wrote the equation for gravitational attraction; **Green** (1828) supplied the function that solves it; **Debye and Hückel** (1923) added screening for electrolytes, and **Yukawa** (1935) the same form for the nuclear potential. The note attributes the equation to Poisson and the readings to their fields.

### What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this epistemic instance?

The concept under evaluation is **the Poisson equation**, precised as *the elliptic law whose Laplacian is fixed by a source density, with boundary data attached*. Its verdicts: conceptually identified against the six fields that read it and against the Laplace equation it degenerates to, defined precisely enough that each reading can be computed from a Green's function, internally consistent — superposition, uniqueness and the mean-value property are consequences of ellipticity rather than added — determinate only once a reading, a coefficient and boundary data are bound, representationally adequate to exactly the degree that the reading is specified, and adequate for its purpose in each field while being, on its own, a form with no physics in it. The gap is deliberate: **the equation cannot tell you which field it is about, and that is the property that made it the most reused equation in physics.**

| Criterion | Verdict | Diagnostic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conceptual Identification | Strong | Separated from the six fields (each a segment or a natural element typed there), from the Laplace equation (the homogeneous case), from the operators as mathematical objects, and from the solvers. The name's other carriers — the [Poisson distribution](/view.html?p=notes/epistemica/observational/distribution/poisson-distribution.md), the Poisson process, Poisson regression — are not variants of this. |
| Definitional Clarity | Strong | The operator, the source, the coupling constant, the boundary data, the fundamental solution and the screened extension are all stated. What is left to convention — the sign, and whether the constant sits inside the source — is named as convention rather than absorbed. |
| Conceptual Determinacy | Conditional | Bound to one reading with data, the equation determines a unique field. Unbound, it determines nothing, and the same string denotes six different models. The determinacy is inherited from the interpretation, not from the form. |
| Internal Consistency | Strong | Linearity gives superposition; ellipticity gives the maximum principle and uniqueness; the Green's function is the inverse of the operator and the superposition integral is its statement. Nothing in the six readings contradicts another, because they share all of it. |
| Representational Adequacy | High per reading, empty as a form | Electrostatic and gravitational fields are predicted to measurement accuracy; conduction and seepage are predicted from material coefficients. The notation represents the constraint and none of the physics, so it neither misrepresents a field nor represents one. |
| Purpose Adequacy | Adequate in every static linear reading, inadequate wherever the source evolves | For "what field does this source produce", it is the right model and no alternative improves on it. For "how does the field change and how does it radiate", it is the wrong equation, and the wave equation is not a correction to it. |
