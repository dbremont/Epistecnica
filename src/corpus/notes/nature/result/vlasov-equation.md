# Vlasov Equation

> The **Vlasov equation** is the collisionless kinetic equation: the phase-space density of a many-body system is transported by the mean field the system itself produces, with no collision term at all. It is the [Boltzmann equation](/view.html?p=notes/nature/result/boltzmann-equation.md) with its right-hand side deleted, and it governs the two regimes where long-range forces make collisions irrelevant — plasmas and self-gravitating stellar systems. Read per [Philosophia Artium Epistemicarum et Operis](/view.html?p=notes/philosophia-artium-epistemicarum-et-operis.md).

> Its epistemic interest is that **one deletion makes one equation serve two fields whose forces point the other way**: repulsive and charge-screened for a plasma, attractive and unscreened for a galaxy. What is shared is not the physics but the structure — a mean field determined by the very distribution it transports, which is the closure that makes the equation nonlinear and self-consistent.

## Formulation

### What is this epistemic instance?

**The Vlasov equation** for a distribution $f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v},t)$ under a force $\mathbf{F}$ generated by $f$ itself:

\[
\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}
\;+\;\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}f
\;+\;\frac{\mathbf{F}}{m}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f
\;=\;0,
\qquad
\mathbf{F} = q\left(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}\right),
\]

with the fields determined self-consistently from the moments of $f$,

\[
\rho_{q} = q\!\int f\,d^{3}v,
\qquad
\mathbf{J} = q\!\int \mathbf{v}f\,d^{3}v,
\]

through Maxwell's equations (the **Vlasov–Maxwell** system) or, electrostatically, through the [Poisson equation](/view.html?p=notes/nature/result/poisson-equation.md) (the **Vlasov–Poisson** system). In the gravitational reading the force is $-\nabla\Phi$ with $\nabla^{2}\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$ and $\rho = m\!\int f\,d^{3}v$: the collisionless Boltzmann equation of stellar dynamics.

What is **in**: the distribution function as the unknown; the free-streaming term; the mean-field acceleration term; the self-consistency that makes the field a functional of the distribution; the collisionless assumption and the small parameter that licenses it; the dispersion relations the linearised equation yields; Landau damping and the phase mixing that produces it; and the equilibrium distributions — Maxwellian for the plasma, any function of the integrals of motion for the galaxy.

What is **out**: the collision term, which is what Boltzmann adds and what this equation deliberately lacks; the collisional transport that resistivity and viscosity of a plasma actually require; the [Jeans equations](/view.html?p=notes/nature/result/jeans-equations.md), which are its moments and a separate model; the [Liouville equation](/view.html?p=notes/nature/result/liouville-equation.md), of which it is a mean-field reduction; and the particle-in-cell and N-body practices that solve it numerically.

### What epistemic element type does this epistemic instance belong to?

**`Epistemic Construction`**, kind **`Model`** — a construction enabling prediction or explanation of the segment. It is a *result*: a mean-field closure of the exact $N$-body problem, not a fundamental law, and not the theory that supplies the distribution function. It is **not** the [kinetic theory](/view.html?p=notes/epistemica/singular/kinetic-theory.md) that interprets $f$, and it is **not** a `Description` of any particular plasma or galaxy.

Its standing is `Validated` inside the collisionless, weakly coupled regime — where the dispersion relations it predicts are measured, including the damping that has no collisional explanation — and it is silent outside it: collisional transport, strongly coupled plasmas, and the long-time behaviour of gravitational systems each require more than this closure provides.

### Is the type of this `epistemic element instance` ambiguous?

**Yes: the name is one equation with two force laws and two fields, and the corpus must not collapse them.**

First, the **plasma reading** and the **stellar reading** are the same representational system with different sourceless fields: Maxwell for the plasma, Poisson for the galaxy. They share the form, the mean-field closure and the linearisation; they differ in sign, in screening (a plasma screens a charge over a Debye length; gravity screens nothing), in equilibrium (a Maxwellian is available to a plasma, and a self-gravitating system has no stable homogeneous equilibrium), and in the sign of the heat capacity. The corpus holds both and files them as one model because the equation is one; the readings are its instances, not two notes.

Second, the name attaches to **Vlasov** for plasmas (1938) and to **Jeans** for stellar systems (1915) — the same equation, discovered twice, in two literatures, and named differently in each. The attribution is dual, and neither name owns the form.

Third, the equation is **the collisionless limit of Boltzmann** and therefore belongs to a family whose members are distinguished by a closure, not by a subject. The [Liouville equation](/view.html?p=notes/nature/result/liouville-equation.md) is exact and unusable; Boltzmann closes with molecular chaos; this one closes by deleting the collision term. The three are one hierarchy, and the corpus's filing must show the hierarchy rather than three unrelated laws.

### What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this epistemic instance?

The concept under evaluation is **the Vlasov equation**, precised as *the collisionless transport equation for a many-body distribution, closed by the mean field the distribution itself generates*. Its verdicts: conceptually identified against Boltzmann (the collision term) and against the exact Liouville equation (the mean-field reduction), defined precisely enough to give dispersion relations and damping rates, internally consistent — the self-consistency is the closure and every consequence follows from it — determinate inside the collisionless weakly coupled regime, representationally adequate there and empty where collisions carry the transport, and adequate for its purpose while being the wrong equation wherever a plasma's resistivity is the question. The gap is the Jeans swindle in the gravitational reading: **one of the two instances rests on an equilibrium that the field equation forbids.**

| Criterion | Verdict | Diagnostic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conceptual Identification | Strong | Held apart from Boltzmann (which has the collision term this one deletes), from Liouville (exact, no mean field), from the Jeans equations (its moments), from kinetic theory (the interpretation of $f$), and from the particle-in-cell and N-body practices. |
| Definitional Clarity | Strong | The streaming term, the acceleration term, the self-consistent field, the collisionless parameter and the equilibrium distributions are all stated. What varies between readings — the field equation — is named rather than left implicit. |
| Conceptual Determinacy | Conditional | Given the field equation and the equilibrium, the linearised response is determinate and measurable. The nonlinear regime is determinate in principle and unresolvable in practice, because filamentation has no cutoff. |
| Internal Consistency | Strong, with one declared fiction | Transport, self-consistency and the dispersion relation cohere; the fine-grained entropy is constant while the coarse-grained one grows, and the difference is stated rather than smoothed. The Jeans swindle is the one inconsistency, and the equation inherits it from the equilibrium it is linearised about. |
| Representational Adequacy | High in the collisionless regime, empty outside | Plasma-wave dispersion, Landau damping and Debye shielding are predicted to measurement accuracy. Collisional transport is not misrepresented; it is absent, which is why the equation cannot be blamed for it. |
| Purpose Adequacy | Adequate for collective behaviour, inadequate for transport | For "how does this collisionless system respond collectively", it is the right model. For "what is the resistivity of this plasma", it is silent by construction, and the honest answer invokes collisions the equation was built to ignore. |
