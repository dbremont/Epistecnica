# Glucose

> **Glucose** is a six-carbon monosaccharide, C₆H₁₂O₆, carrying an aldehyde group at one end and one hydroxyl on each remaining carbon. It is the monomer from which starch, glycogen and cellulose are built, and the sugar that most cells burn. The name was coined in 1838 for the sugar obtained alike from honey, grapes, starch and cellulose — the first hint that one compound underpins three very different materials.

## Formulation

### What natural element type does this glucose instance belong to?

**Glucose, read as a substance, belongs to `Ontic Element (→ Kind) Substance (→ Kind) Substance / Matter` — a stuff-kind individuated by composition.**

Readable secondarily as a `Building Block`: at the level of the polymers it composes, glucose is the repeating unit — and that is how the starch, glycogen and cellulose notes meet it. Readable again as an `Entity` when a single molecule is counted or tracked. Those readings are prose here, not second roots.

### What is this natural instance?

> Glucose exists in two forms that are always in equilibrium in solution, and the distinction is not cosmetic. In the **open chain** it is an aldose — an aldehyde at C1 and hydroxyls down the rest of the spine. In solution it overwhelmingly closes into a six-membered pyranose ring, and the closure turns C1 into an **anomeric carbon**: the hydroxyl on it can point down (**α**) or up (**β**), and the two interconvert through the open form.
>
> That single geometry at C1 is the fork the whole carbohydrate world turns on. Linking glucose α-1,4 gives chains that fold into **starch** and **glycogen** — digestible stores. Linking it β-1,4 gives a straight, hydrogen-bonded ribbon that no human enzyme cleaves — **cellulose**, the structural material of the plant world. Same monomer, same bond chemistry, one stereocentre inverted.
>
> Glucose is also the hub of the metabolic map: glycolysis splits it, the pentose phosphate pathway harvests reducing power from it, glycogen and starch store it, and the blood carries it at a concentration the body defends within narrow limits. The note [Blood Glucose Level](/view.html?p=notes/nature/human/blood-glucose-level.md) takes the regulatory side; this note is the molecule.
>
> Two structural facts discipline the literature. Because the open chain is an aldehyde and the ring is a hemiacetal, glucose is a **reducing sugar** — the property the classical colour tests detect, and the reason its measurement has a chemistry of its own. And because it is chiral with four asymmetric carbons, its configuration had to be proved rather than assumed: Fischer established the family's stereochemistry, and Haworth fixed the ring.

The polymers it builds, the cells that consume it and the enzymes that handle it are associated, not parts.
