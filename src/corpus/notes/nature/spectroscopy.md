# Spectroscopy

> **Spectroscopy** is the family of techniques that measure a quantity as a function of **energy, frequency or wavelength** — the response of matter to radiation, or the radiation matter emits, resolved into its components. The family's power is that the spectrum is a fingerprint: the positions of the lines identify the species, their intensities its quantity, and their widths and shifts the state it is in.

| Member | What is varied | What is learned | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Infrared** | Photon frequency in the infrared | Molecular vibrations, functional groups | [Infrared Spectroscopy](/view.html?p=notes/nature/infrared-spectroscopy.md) |
| **Nuclear magnetic resonance** | Radio frequency in a magnetic field | Molecular structure, through nuclear spin environments | [NMR Spectroscopy](/corpus/notes/epistemica/observation/organon.html) |
| **Angle-resolved photoemission** | Photon energy and emission angle | Electronic band structure, $E(\mathbf{k})$ | [ARPES](/view.html?p=notes/nature/angle-resolved-photoemission-spectroscopy-arpes.md) |
| **UV-Vis** | Photon energy in the visible and ultraviolet | Electronic transitions, concentration | See the catalogue |

> The family divides by **what the spectrum is of**. In absorption spectroscopy the sample removes photons from a beam; in emission it supplies them; in scattering it redistributes them. The three are not interchangeable — an absorption measurement needs a source and a path length, an emission measurement needs an excitation and a dark background, a scattering measurement needs a geometry — and the choice is dictated by whether the interesting quantity is a concentration, an excited state, or a structure.

> The common limit is the same as microscopy's, inverted. Microscopy trades resolution for context; spectroscopy trades **specificity for ensemble**. A spectrum is an average over the illuminated volume, so it sees the dominant species and misses the rare one unless the technique is tuned to it — which is why the analytical chemistry in the catalogue is arranged by purpose: composition, structure, trace.
