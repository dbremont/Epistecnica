# Phenomena

> A **phenomenon** is a regularity predicated over a state-trajectory: a repeatable pattern that a sequence of states exhibits, named as one thing because the pattern holds across systems, not because the systems are alike. It is what a *process* produces and what a *property* cannot state — the shape events take, rather than the events themselves or the features they carry.

> This note is the directory index of the phenomena shelf under `nature/phenomena/`. Each member is a phenomenon in that precise sense: interference is not a wave and not a property of a medium, it is the pattern two waves produce together; the Doppler effect is not a frequency and not a motion, it is the relation between the two that a moving observer sees. The shelf collects the general ones — the patterns that recur across sound, light, matter and engineering — not the particulars that instantiate them.

## Formulation

### What natural element type does this natural instance belong to?

`Phenomenon` at **meso** level of organization (Ontic; Dynamical), per the Tabular view — a *higher-order property of a state-trajectory*: a pattern or regularity predicated over a sequence of states.

The boundary matters, because the same event can be read three ways and only one of them is this shelf:

- **Not a `Process`.** A process is a bounded causal sequence in one system — this pulse travelling, this vessel boiling. A phenomenon is the pattern that many such sequences share, and it is why the same name serves a sound, a light wave and a water ripple.
- **Not a `Property`.** Frequency, amplitude and wavelength are properties carried by a state. Interference is not carried by either wave alone: it exists only in their superposition, which is exactly what a phenomenon adds to a property.
- **Not a `Constraint`.** A constraint bounds what may happen; a phenomenon is what does happen, regularly, when nothing forbids it. Resonance is not a law that says responses must be large — it is the pattern that they are, at a frequency.

The guide's `Phenomenon` also admits `(→ Predication) State` and `(→ Predication) Trajectory`, which is the structural statement of the same point: the phenomenon is predicated *of* a trajectory, not located inside it.

### What is this natural instance?

> Two sound waves meeting and cancelling at one point; a passing siren dropping in pitch; a wave bending around a doorway it cannot pass through; a glass breaking at one particular note; a prism spreading white light; the sky polarised at ninety degrees from the sun; water climbing a narrow tube; a pump impeller eaten away by bubbles; a quartz crystal humming at a voltage.

Each is a pattern that any sufficiently similar system will exhibit, and each is *named* rather than derived because the name is what lets the same reasoning travel between sound, light and matter.

**Limitations.** *Scale* — a pattern at the scale of a wave is one thing; at the scale of the medium's particles it is a different node, and the same name then denotes two. *Nonlinearity* — most of these phenomena are described linearly and depart from the description at large amplitude (shock formation, harmonic generation). *Partial Observability* — a phenomenon is inferred from a series of measurements, and the pattern is only as good as the sampling. *Many-Body coupling* — in a real medium several of these occur at once and cannot be attributed separately without an experiment designed for it.

## How the shelf is cut

| Family | Members | What they share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Propagation | Interference, Diffraction, Dispersion, Polarisation, Doppler effect | A wave meeting something — a boundary, a medium, another wave, a moving frame |
| Resonance | Resonance | A system with a natural frequency and a drive near it |
| Surface and phase | Capillary action, Cavitation | A liquid interface under a pressure or surface-tension gradient |
| Coupling | Piezoelectric effect | A crystal converting strain to charge and back |

Two neighbours are deliberately **not** on this shelf, and both are visible from here: [Oscillation](/view.html?p=notes/nature/wave/oscillation.md) and [Vibration](/view.html?p=notes/nature/wave/vibration.md) are the temporal patterns a wave *carries*, and they are decomposed on the wave shelf where their relation to propagation is the subject; [Pitch](/view.html?p=notes/nature/pitch.md) is the perceptual property of a frequency, not the pattern of its shifting.
