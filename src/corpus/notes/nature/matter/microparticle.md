# Microparticle

> A **microparticle** is a particle of matter with dimensions between **0.1 and 100 micrometers** — above the [nanoparticle](/view.html?p=notes/nature/matter/nanoparticle.md) regime and below the macroscopic — large enough that **bulk behavior dominates** its interior chemistry, small enough that surface still matters to its transport and reaction.

> The IUPAC definition runs from 1×10⁻⁷ m to 1×10⁻⁴ m, with the lower boundary against nano-sizing explicitly left under debate. The class is a **size class, not a composition**: its members sell commercially as ceramics, glass, polymers and metals; their daily-life cases are pollen, sand, dust, flour and powdered sugar. The study of fine particles is **micromeritics** — the same 1948 word DallaValle gave the technology of fine particles; and it is at this scale that surface-to-mass still does industrial and biological work: metals finely divided enough can combust in air, and the body's platelets shed their own microparticles into the bloodstream.

## Formulation

### What natural element type does this natural instance belong to?

`Substance / Matter` at meso level of organization (Constituent category), per the [Tabular view](/view.html?p=notes/philosophia-naturalis-et-operis.md) — a size class above nanoparticle and below bulk matter.

### What is this natural instance?

> A matter particle between 100 nanometers and 100 micrometers in its largest dimension: the regime where the interior behaves bulk-like, but the surface-to-volume ratio remains large enough to set transport, settling and reactivity — the regime of powders, aerosols and dispersions. Note the boundary conventions: the biological literature's "microparticle" (platelet-derived microvesicles, typically ≥100 nm) sits at the class's very bottom, and the IUPAC micro/nano lower limit is expressly unsettled.
