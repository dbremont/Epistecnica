# Fermionic Condensate

> A **fermionic condensate** is a state of matter analogous to the [Bose–Einstein condensate](/view.html?p=notes/nature/matter/state/bose-einstein-condensate.md) but composed of **paired fermions** — fermions bound into pairs that behave as composite bosons and then occupy one macroscopic quantum state together, forming a superfluid.

> Fermions cannot share a quantum state the way bosons can, so the condensate has to be built differently: fermions **pair up**, and the pairs — behaving as bosons — condense. The pairing is the same mechanism that underlies superconductivity in metals, where two electrons form a Cooper pair; the fermionic condensate realizes it with atoms instead — ultracold lithium-6 gases are the record's case. The state is thus the bridge between superconductors and BEC: fermions paired under quantum degeneracy.

Read per [Matter](/view.html?p=notes/nature/matter/matter.md)'s state table: the substratum is paired fermions under quantum degeneracy; the identity of the state is the pairing, not the atom.
