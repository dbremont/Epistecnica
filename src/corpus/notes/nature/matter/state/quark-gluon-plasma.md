# Quark–Gluon Plasma

> A **quark–gluon plasma (QGP)** is a state of matter in which **quarks and gluons — the fundamental constituents confined inside hadrons — are set free from their confinement** into one interacting plasma. It forms at extreme temperature and density, in conditions at the early universe (the first microseconds after the Big Bang) and in heavy-ion collisions.

> In ordinary matter quarks are permanently locked into protons and neutrons by the strong force — confinement. Drive energy or density high enough and the hadrons themselves dissolve: the quarks and gluons form a plasma that interacts as a fluid rather than as individual particles. The state is studied in heavy-ion colliders where it lives for only a brief interval before confining back into hadrons as it cools.

Read per [Matter](/view.html?p=notes/nature/matter/matter.md)'s state table: the substratum is the fundamental quarks and gluons themselves, not atoms. It is the one state in the table whose particles are not composite.
