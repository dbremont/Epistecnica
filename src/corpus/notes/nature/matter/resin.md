# Resin

> A **resin** is a plant exudate: a hydrophobic mixture of terpenes, resin acids and phenolics, secreted into ducts and canals, released when the tissue is wounded, and hardened as its volatile fraction leaves. It is the tree's seal, not its food — the substance is there to close a breach and to make the breach hostile to whatever would enter through it.

> A natural element read per [How to decompose any natural instance?](/view.html?p=notes/philosophia-naturalis-et-operis.md).

## Formulation

### What natural element type does this natural instance belong to?

`Substance / Matter` at meso level of organization (Ontic; Existential), specialized from `Substance` — a stuff-kind individuated by composition and phase behaviour, not by being a particular body.

**Boundary.** The word carries two unrelated referents, and only one of them is a natural element. The **plant exudate** is the subject of this note: a mixture whose composition is set by the species that secreted it. The **synthetic resin** — epoxy, polyester, phenolic — is a technical substance, manufactured and specified rather than exuded, and belongs to the technica reading of materials. A lump of amber is a body and can be read as a `Natural Object`; the resin is the stuff it is made of, which is why this note decomposes the substance and not the lump. The polysaccharide exudates (gum arabic, tragacanth) are a different family again: they are water-soluble, and they harden by drying rather than by resin-acid chemistry.

### What is this natural instance?

> A pine trunk bleeding where the bark was cut, a blister under the bark of a spruce, a lump of amber on a Baltic beach. The fresh material is a **oleoresin** — mobile and sticky, carrying the essential oils that make it smell of turpentine; leave it in air and the volatiles leave, and what remains is a brittle, translucent solid whose colour runs from pale yellow to deep brown.

Chemically it is a mixture rather than a compound: monoterpenes and sesquiterpenes (the volatile fraction), diterpene **resin acids** such as abietic and pimaric acid (the solid fraction), and a smaller share of phenolics and neutral compounds. That mixture explains the material behaviour. It has no crystal lattice and therefore no melting point; it passes through a **glass transition** instead, softening over a range and flowing without ever becoming a liquid of fixed viscosity.

**Levels.** Micro for the composition, structure and molecular properties; meso for the states, ducts, wound surfaces and bounds of the material as it is met; macro for the fossil state, where geological time is what produced it.

**Limitation checklist** — **Complexity** (a mixture whose proportions vary with species, season and tissue, so no single composition describes it); **Scale** (a duct, a lump and an amber deposit are the same substance at different extents); **Partial Observability** (the composition of a fresh exudate is never fully measured before it changes); **Non-Repeatability** (no two trees yield the same mixture, and a fossil is a sample of one moment).

**Secondary readings** (kept as prose, never merged into the tree): readable as **technique** — tapping, distillation into [rosin and turpentine](/view.html?p=notes/social/good/agriculture/resin.md), varnish making; readable as **production** — the naval-stores trade and its three supply routes; readable as **social** — the supply chain of a forest product collected by hand; readable as **epistemic** — the colour and acid-value grading as a measurement construction laid over a mixture.
