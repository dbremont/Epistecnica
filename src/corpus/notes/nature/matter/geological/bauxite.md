# Bauxite

> **Bauxite** is a residual sedimentary rock unusually rich in hydrated aluminium oxides — gibbsite, boehmite and diaspore — with iron oxides, kaolinite, quartz and titanium minerals as its usual companions. It is the world's principal source of aluminium: the rock is digested in caustic soda to make alumina, and the alumina is reduced to metal.

> Named after Les Baux-de-Provence, where Berthier described it in 1821, bauxite is not a mineral but a rock name — and a commercial one. A laterite becomes bauxite when its aluminium concentration and its extractability make it worth mining, which is a statement about the rock, the process and the market at once.

## Formulation

### What natural element type does this bauxite instance belong to?

**Bauxite belongs to `Ontic Element (→ Kind) Substance` — a material body individuated by its composition, and specifically a rock: an aggregate of aluminium hydroxide minerals with iron oxide and clay gangue.**

The boundary against `Mineral` is the first one. Gibbsite is the mineral; bauxite is the rock that carries it. No single lattice defines bauxite, and the same name covers rocks whose dominant hydroxide differs — gibbsitic in the tropics, boehmitic in the Mediterranean and the Caribbean, diasporic in China and Greece. The boundary against `Ore` is the second: bauxite ore is bauxite read under a cut-off, classified by available alumina, reactive silica and the cost of refining. The rock is the root here; the economic reading belongs to [Ore](/view.html?p=notes/nature/matter/geological/ore.md).

Readable secondarily as a `Substance / Matter` at the province scale — a bauxitic weathering blanket as one stuff-kind. Not a second root.

The choice matters because bauxite is a rock name that has become an industrial specification. What the geologist describes as a lateritic weathering profile, the refiner describes by two numbers, and the two descriptions are of the same body.

### What is this natural instance?

> A red, pink, grey or white earthy rock, pisolitic or oolitic in texture, soft to hard depending on the hydroxide: gibbsite (Al(OH)₃) in tropical deposits, boehmite (γ-AlO(OH)) in European and Caribbean ones, diaspore (α-AlO(OH)) in Chinese and Greek ones. Iron oxides give the colour and the iron content; kaolinite and quartz are the main diluents; anatase, rutile and zircon are the trace heavies.
>
> Its decisive properties are not the total aluminium but the **available alumina** — the fraction the Bayer digest can dissolve — and the **reactive silica**, which dissolves too and consumes caustic soda in an unrecoverable side reaction. A rock with high alumina and high reactive silica can be less valuable than a leaner, cleaner one.

## Composition and Grade

| Component | Typical range | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Available alumina** | 30–60 % | The recoverable Al₂O₃; the primary price driver. |
| **Total Al₂O₃** | 40–60 % | Includes alumina locked in clay, which the digest does not recover. |
| **Reactive silica** | 1–10 % | Consumes caustic in the digest; the penalty that can make a rich rock uneconomic. |
| **Fe₂O₃** | 2–30 % | Colours the rock and reports to the red mud. |
| **TiO₂** | 1–5 % | Affects mud settling and can be recovered as a by-product. |
| **Moisture** | 5–30 % | Transported at a discount; drying is an energy cost. |

> The grade is not the total aluminium. A rock is bought on *available alumina* and penalised on *reactive silica*, and the two are measured by a caustic digest, not by a chemical analysis of the whole rock. That is why a lean gibbsitic bauxite can outrank a rich diasporic one.

## Genesis

| Path | Mechanism | Where it is seen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lateritic weathering** | High rainfall and temperature leach silica and alkalis, concentrating iron and aluminium oxides in the profile | Guinea, Brazil, Australia, Indonesia, India |
| **Karst residual** | Limestone dissolves, its insoluble residue accumulates and hardens in the depressions | France, Greece, Hungary, Jamaica |
| **Sedimentary reworking** | A pre-existing bauxite is eroded, transported and redeposited | Some European and Asian deposits |
| **Hydrothermal alteration** | Aluminous rocks altered by hot fluids, locally producing boehmite or diaspore | Minor deposits associated with igneous activity |

> The first two are the same process on different substrates: weathering removes what is soluble and leaves what is not. Bauxite is what remains when the parent rock contains enough aluminium and the climate is wet enough for long enough; laterite is the same process at lower intensity, and the boundary between the two is drawn by grade, not by a change in mechanism.

## Uses

| Use | What the rock becomes | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Aluminium metal** | Alumina by the Bayer process, then aluminium by Hall–Héroult | Available alumina and reactive silica set the refinery economics |
| **Refractories** | Calcined and fused alumina, brown fused alumina | Iron and titania lower the refractory grade |
| **Abrasives** | Calcined bauxite, corundum and emery | Hardness and purity |
| **Proppants** | Sintered bauxite beads for hydraulic fracturing | Roundness, strength and density |
| **Cement and slag** | Aluminous cement and synthetic slag | Iron and silica tolerated |
| **Chemicals** | Aluminium sulfate, polyaluminium chloride, zeolites | Purity and iron content |

> Aluminium production dominates every other use, and it dominates the rock's economics: the Bayer process is designed around the bauxite it receives, so a refinery is tied to a residue chemistry as much as to a mine.

## Residue

> The Bayer process dissolves the alumina and leaves everything else as **red mud** — iron oxides, silica, titania, caustic and trace metals, at roughly one to two tonnes per tonne of alumina. It is alkaline, fine and voluminous, and its storage is the environmental constraint the industry is judged by. Bauxite's natural composition is therefore not only an input specification; it is a waste specification, fixed before the rock is mined.

## What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this natural element instance?

The concept under evaluation is **Bauxite**, defined above as *the residual rock rich in hydrated aluminium oxides, with available alumina and reactive silica as its decisive attributes*. The evaluation applies the criteria of [Philosophia Conceptuum et Operis](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/csiglab/Naturgnosis/refs/heads/main/app/note/data/meta/philosophia-conceptuum-et-operis.md).

**Verdicts.** Conceptual Identification — **pass**: the rock is separated from the minerals that dominate it (gibbsite, boehmite, diaspore), from laterite (the same process at lower intensity) and from the ore and alumina it becomes. Definitional Clarity — **pass**: the definition names the mineral assemblage, the gangue and the two measured attributes that decide use. Conceptual Determinacy — **pass with qualification**: the bauxite/laterite and bauxite/ore boundaries are drawn by grade and by process economics, not by a natural discontinuity; the note states the convention instead of treating the number as given. Internal Consistency — **pass**: minerals, gangue, attributes, deposit classes and the bound are distinct branches; no row mixes the rock with the mine or the refinery. Representational Adequacy — **qualified**: complete for the rock and its specification; the mine, the refinery and the residue management are linked, not grown. Purpose Adequacy — **pass**: shaped for resource description and refinery feed selection — every leaf is a constituent, a measured property or a deposit class.
