# Geological Matter

> **Geological matter** is the matter of the solid Earth and its surface: minerals, rocks, sediments, soils and the materials extracted from them. It is the part of matter individuated not by what it is made of but by **how it came to be** — by genesis, transport, deposition and alteration.

> The same atoms appear in a mineral, in the rock that contains it, in the sediment that rock weathers into, and in the soil that sediment becomes. What distinguishes these is not composition but process.

## Which are the types of geological matter?

> A **geological matter type** is individuated by its formative process, not by its chemical composition. Two rocks with the same elements in different proportions are different rocks; the same rock at a different depth, pressure or temperature is a different rock again.

> These rows form a **hierarchy**, not a list. Mineral → Rock → Sediment → Soil descend by aggregation and reworking, and the table flattens them only for reading. A sample can answer to several rows at once: a grain of quartz is a mineral, part of a sandstone rock, itself part of a sediment body before lithification.

| Geological Matter Type | Structure | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mineral** | crystal lattice of a definite composition | A naturally occurring inorganic solid with an ordered atomic structure. The unit of geological matter: identity is the lattice, so one chemical formula in two different lattices is two different minerals. |
| **Mineraloid** | amorphous, no lattice | A natural solid without crystalline order — opal, obsidian, tektite. A mineral's chemical equal and its structural opposite. |
| **Rock** | coherent aggregate of minerals | One or more minerals, mineraloids or organic materials held together. Individuated by **genesis**: igneous (cooled melt), sedimentary (lithified sediment), metamorphic (recrystallised under heat and pressure). |
| **Sediment** | loose particulate | Grains, fragments or precipitates moved and deposited by water, wind, ice or gravity, sorted by size — gravel, sand, silt, clay. What a sedimentary rock was before lithification. |
| **Regolith** | weathered mantle over bedrock | The broken, loose layer produced by weathering — soil, saprolite, colluvium. The covering, not the rock beneath it. |
| **Soil** | mineral grains, organic matter and pore space | Regolith reorganised by biology and hydrology into a layered profile. The only row whose identity includes living material. |
| **Ore** | host rock or sediment carrying a concentration | Material whose mineral concentration can be recovered at a profit. The only row individuated by **price**: the rock does not change when the price falls, it simply stops being ore. |
| **Coal** | organic-rich sedimentary rock | Lithified plant remains. A rock by genesis, an organic material by composition and a fuel by use — three readings of one material. |
| **Alluvium, colluvium, loess** | sediment named by its transport | Moved by flowing water, by gravity, by wind respectively. The name records the agent, not the substance. |

Three points are easy to miss in this table and worth stating.

**Sediment is rock at an earlier stage.** Lithification is the whole difference between a sand bank and a sandstone. The row names a stage in a process, not a separate kind of substance — which is why the two sit next to each other rather than in separate branches.

**Ore is the one economic category.** Every other row here is individuated by a natural process; ore is individuated by recovery. A body of rock is or is not ore according to its grade, the mineral hosting the metal, the available technology and the market price. Calling it a geological matter type is a convenience, and the note says so rather than hiding it.

**Magma is excluded.** Molten rock is a *state* of rock, not a type of it — the same rule that keeps states out of the table of matter. Lava, magma, and the glass that quenches from them are rock in different states of the same process.

> **State is not type.** Just as [Matter](/view.html?p=notes/nature/matter/matter.md) separates the types of matter from its states of aggregation, this table separates the geological types from the states in which they occur.
