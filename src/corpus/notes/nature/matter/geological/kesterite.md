# Kesterite

> **Kesterite** is the mineral Cu₂ZnSnS₄ and the name of the structure type it defines — a body-centred tetragonal crystal in which sulphur is close-packed and copper, zinc and tin occupy ordered cation sites. Its interest is double: it is a mineral of the sulphide family, and it is the **earth-abundant photovoltaic absorber** around which the CZTS research programme was built.

## The mineral

| Dimension | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Composition** | Cu₂ZnSnS₄ — the sulphide end-member of the Cu₂ZnSn(S,Se)₄ family |
| **Structure** | Tetragonal, space group I‑4; sulphur in cubic close packing, the cations ordered on the sites that distinguish kesterite from stannite |
| **Level of organization** | Meso — a crystalline solid, a constituent of the material system |
| **Sibling phases** | Stannite (the disordered cation arrangement), and the selenide Cu₂ZnSnSe₄ |

## The material

> The photovoltaic argument is a substitution argument: CIGS (Cu(In,Ga)Se₂) is an excellent absorber whose elements are scarce and expensive, and kesterite replaces indium and gallium with **zinc and tin**, which are neither. The absorber keeps the properties that matter — a direct band gap around 1.5 eV for the sulphide, tunable downward by selenium, and an absorption coefficient above 10⁴ cm⁻¹, so that a micron of film absorbs the useful spectrum.

> The difficulty is the defect chemistry, and it is what the efficiency record measures. The elements are close in size and the compound is tolerant of substitution, so growth produces **secondary phases** (Cu₂SnS₃, ZnS, Cu–Sn alloys) and a high density of intrinsic defects; the consequence is a **voltage deficit** — an open-circuit voltage well below what the band gap allows — that has kept kesterite below the competing thin-film absorbers. The material is abundant, non-toxic and cheap; it is not yet efficient.

> The corpus's reading is that kesterite is the clean test of the "earth-abundant" thesis: it isolates the question of whether a material with the right optical properties and the wrong defect physics can be engineered into a device. The answer so far is "not by composition alone" — the work is in the growth and in the interfaces.
