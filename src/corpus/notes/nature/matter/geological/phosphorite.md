# Phosphorite

> **Phosphorite** — **phosphate rock** in its economic reading — is a sedimentary body composed largely of calcium phosphate minerals, carbonate-fluorapatite above all, with calcite, chert, clay and organic matter as gangue. It is the natural reservoir from which industrial phosphorus is taken: the rock is ground, digested with acid and rebuilt into phosphoric acid and fertilizer.

> An instance read per [How to decompose any natural instance?](/view.html?p=notes/philosophia-naturalis-et-operis.md).

## Formulation

### What natural element type does this phosphorite instance belong to?

**Phosphorite belongs to `Ontic Element (→ Kind) Substance` — a material body individuated by its composition, and specifically a rock: an aggregate of apatite and gangue, not a single lattice.**

The boundary that matters is the one against `Mineral`. Apatite is the mineral; phosphorite is the rock that carries it, and its grade is a property of the aggregate — the same apatite at 8 % or 32 % P₂O₅ is the same mineral in a different rock. The boundary against `Ore` is the other side: phosphate rock is phosphorite read under a cut-off, classified by what can be recovered at a price. The rock is the root here; the economic reading belongs to the [Ore](/view.html?p=notes/nature/matter/geological/ore.md) note, which owns grade, recovery and cut-off.

Readable secondarily as a `Substance / Matter` at the province scale — the phosphogenic province as one stuff-kind. That reading is not a second root.

The choice is not cosmetic. A mineral is individuated by its lattice; a rock by its genesis and its aggregate composition. Phosphorite is named chemically because one mineral dominates it, but it is individuated sedimentologically — by the upwelling, the replacement and the reworking that concentrated the phosphate.

### What is this natural instance?

> A bed, nodule or concretion of carbonate-fluorapatite (francolite) with carbonate, silica and clay: P₂O₅ from a few percent to more than 35 %, fluorine in the lattice, and cadmium, uranium and thorium carried as trace substitutes for calcium. Its textures record how it formed — peloids and coated grains in a high-energy shelf, laminated cryptalgal crusts, nodules and hardgrounds, or the massive replacement of a limestone. Its deposits are the world's phosphate provinces: the Moroccan and Western Saharan basins, Florida and North Carolina, Yunnan and Guizhou, the Kola Peninsula, Senegal and Togo, Jordan and Syria.
>
> The decisive property is not grade but *reactivity* — how readily the apatite dissolves in acid — because that is what decides whether the rock can be processed into phosphoric acid economically. The second decisive property is contamination: cadmium above the regulatory limit makes a high-grade rock unusable in fertilizer regardless of its P₂O₅.

The mine, the beneficiation plant and the fertilizer market are associated systems, not parts of the instance — though grade and contamination are defined against them.
>

## Composition and Grade

| Component | Typical range | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P₂O₅** | 5–35 % (run-of-mine 15–30 %) | The grade that fixes the price and the process flow. |
| **CaO** | 30–50 % | The cation of the apatite and of the carbonate gangue. |
| **F** | 2–4 % | Lattice fluorine; released as fluoride in acid digestion and recovered as a by-product. |
| **CO₂** | 1–6 % | Carbonate substitution in francolite and free carbonate gangue; raises acid consumption. |
| **SiO₂** | 2–20 % | Chert and quartz; abrasive and acid-insoluble. |
| **Cd** | < 1 to > 100 mg/kg | The regulatory gate for fertilizer use; varies by province and by mineralogy. |
| **U, Th** | tens to hundreds of mg/kg | Natural radioactivity; a constraint on processing and on by-product use. |

> Grade is a property of the aggregate and it is not stable in the ground: weathering enriches the surface, beneficiation raises the mill feed, and blending at the port changes the number again. A stated grade is a statement about a sample, a location and a process.

## Genesis

| Path | Mechanism | Where it is seen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Marine upwelling** | Cold nutrient-rich water fuels high productivity; phosphate is released from organic matter and precipitates as apatite in the sediment | The Moroccan and Western Saharan basins, Florida, Yunnan |
| **Carbonate replacement** | Phosphate-bearing pore water replaces limestone and dolomite, preserving the bedding | Shelf carbonates throughout the phosphogenic provinces |
| **Guano derivation** | Bird and bat guano leaches and reprecipitates calcium phosphate on karst and islands | Pacific islands, Caribbean, Peru |
| **Igneous association** | Apatite concentrates in carbonatite and alkaline complexes | Kola Peninsula, Brazil, South Africa |
| **Residual weathering** | Carbonate is removed and phosphate enriched in the weathering profile | Residual caps over carbonate phosphorite |

> The first two are the same event seen at two moments: upwelling supplies the phosphorus, and the sediment it lands in becomes the host that replacement converts to rock. That is why the largest deposits are sedimentary, marine and stratigraphically thin — a bed a few metres thick over hundreds of kilometres.

## Uses

| Use | What the rock becomes | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fertilizer** | Phosphoric acid by the wet process, then ammonium phosphate and superphosphate | Cadmium limit; carbonate and silica consume acid |
| **Phosphoric acid** | Digested with sulfuric acid (wet) or reduced in an electric furnace (thermal) | Gypsum by-product and fluoride recovery |
| **Animal feed phosphate** | Purified calcium phosphate — DCP, MCP | Heavy-metal and fluoride limits tighter than fertilizer |
| **Industrial phosphates** | Detergents, food additives, flame retardants, lithium iron phosphate | Purity and food-grade certification |
| **Elemental phosphorus** | Electric-furnace reduction, then conversion to phosphoric acid and organophosphorus chemistry | Energy-intensive; increasingly displaced by the wet process |

## Contaminants and Regulation

> Phosphorite carries its impurities in the apatite lattice, not beside it: cadmium substitutes for calcium, uranium and thorium follow the same site, and fluorine is a constituent of the mineral. Beneficiation removes gangue but not the lattice substitutes, which is why a clean, high-grade rock can still be unusable.

> Regulation therefore acts on the contaminant, not on the grade. Cadmium limits for phosphate fertilizers differ by jurisdiction and are tightening; natural radioactivity is regulated at the processing site and in the gypsum and phosphogypsum by-products; fluoride is recovered or scrubbed. The rock's natural composition is the constraint, and the fertilizer industry's process choices are largely responses to it.

## What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this natural element instance?

The concept under evaluation is **Phosphorite**, defined above as *a sedimentary body composed largely of calcium phosphate minerals, with its grade, texture, contamination and reactivity*. The evaluation applies the criteria of [Philosophia Conceptuum et Operis](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/csiglab/Naturgnosis/refs/heads/main/app/note/data/meta/philosophia-conceptuum-et-operis.md).

**Verdicts.** Conceptual Identification — **pass**: the rock is separated from the mineral that dominates it (apatite), from the ore reading of the same body (phosphate rock), and from the fertilizer and acid it becomes. Definitional Clarity — **pass**: the definition names the composition, the gangue and the four attributes that decide use, all measurable. Conceptual Determinacy — **pass with qualification**: the boundary between phosphorite and phosphate-bearing limestone is drawn by P₂O₅ content, which is a convention rather than a natural discontinuity; the note states the convention rather than treating the number as given. Internal Consistency — **pass**: minerals, gangue, attributes, deposit classes and the bound are distinct branches, and no row mixes the rock with the mine or the market. Representational Adequacy — **qualified**: complete for the rock and its decisive properties; the mining, beneficiation and fertilizer systems are linked, not grown. Purpose Adequacy — **pass**: shaped for resource description and process selection — every leaf is a constituent, a measured property or a deposit class.
