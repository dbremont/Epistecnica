# Wax

> A **wax** is a lipophilic solid that is malleable near ambient temperature: long aliphatic chains — wax esters of fatty acids with long-chain alcohols in the biological kinds, homologous alkanes in the petroleum and synthetic kinds — melting typically above about 40 °C into low-viscosity liquids. It is insoluble in water and soluble in nonpolar solvents such as hexane, benzene and chloroform. The behaviour that identifies it is the pair: a plastic solid at room temperature, a thin melt just above it.

> The word names a **class**, not a compound. Beeswax, carnauba and paraffin share no formula and no origin; what they share is a property regime. That is why the note types wax by the guide's composition-and-phase criterion while insisting that the *phase* half carries the identity.

## Formulation

### What natural element type does this natural instance belong to?

`Substance / Matter` at meso level of organization — the guide's stuff-kind *individuated by composition and phase behaviour*, specialized from `Substance`. Composition supplies the long-chain esters or alkanes; phase behaviour supplies the malleable solid and the thin melt. Wax is an **instance** of that kind, and the named waxes below are its kinds.

It is **not a `Natural Object`**: a wax sample is not a body whose parts are held by a load path, and its coherence is chemical, not structural. It is **not a `Compound`** in the strict sense: the biological waxes are esters, but a commercial wax is a mixture of esters, acids, alcohols, ketones, aldehydes and hydrocarbons, and its identity survives a change of proportions. It is **not a `Property`**: malleability and the melting range are attributes wax carries, and they are recorded below as such.

Two neighbouring kinds must be kept out. A **fat or oil** is a triglyceride — glycerol esterified with three fatty acids — whereas a wax ester bonds a fatty acid to a long-chain alcohol, not to glycerol; the melting behaviour follows from that difference, fats softening across a range, waxes melting sharply into thin liquids. A **resin** is a terpenoid or phenolic exudate: hard and brittle rather than plastic, soluble in the same solvents, and not a wax. Japan wax is the standing false friend — a vegetable triglyceride that carries the name and not the chemistry.

### What is this natural instance?

> Waxes are produced by plants and animals and occur in petroleum. Plant waxes sit in and on the cuticle, where they control evaporation, wettability and hydration — mixtures of alkanes, alkyl esters, fatty acids, primary and secondary alcohols, diols, ketones and aldehydes. Animal waxes are typically wax esters: **beeswax**, whose major component is myricyl palmitate (an ester of triacontanol and palmitic acid, melting 62–65 °C), **spermaceti** with cetyl palmitate, **lanolin** from wool with sterol esters, plus the insect secretions Chinese wax and shellac wax.

> The plant kinds that matter commercially are **carnauba**, the hard wax of the Brazilian palm *Copernicia prunifera* built on myricyl cerotate, and beside it candelilla, ouricury, rice bran, soy and jojoba — the last a liquid wax ester rather than a solid. **Montan wax** is fossil: extracted from lignite and brown coal, very hard because of its concentration of saturated fatty acids and alcohols, dark and odorous until purified. **Ozocerite** occurs in lignite beds; ceresin and the peat waxes belong to the same mineral family.

> The petroleum waxes are hydrocarbons, not esters: **paraffin** is a mixture of saturated straight- and branched-chain alkanes with naphthenes and substituted aromatics — hentriacontane, C₃₁H₆₄, is a typical member — refined by vacuum distillation and produced in millions of tonnes a year. **Microcrystalline wax** carries a higher share of branched and naphthenic hydrocarbons and a much finer crystal structure, which is what makes it tougher and more flexible than paraffin. Synthetic waxes are mostly **polyethylene waxes**, made by direct polymerisation of ethylene, by thermal degradation of high-molecular-weight resin, or by recovery of the low-molecular-weight stream from resin production; the three routes give different viscosity, density and melt point, and the recovered stream must be stripped of volatile oligomers and catalyst residues before use.

> One property deserves a note of its own: **branching governs hardness**. The degree of branching in a paraffin and the ester-to-hydrocarbon ratio in a plant wax decide whether the solid is friable, plastic or tough — which is why the same name covers a candle wax and a floor polish.

**Limitations.** *Uncertainty* — composition varies with species and, for plant waxes, with geographic location, so a named wax is a range and not a formula. *Scale* — the same name denotes a molecule, a sample and a deposit-grade material; the rows above record the level each instance is read at. *Partial Observability* — cuticular wax is a surface film a few molecules thick, and sampling it disturbs the very barrier being measured. *Irreducibility* — hardness and melt point are not read off a single constituent; they follow from the mixture, which is why the class survives a change of proportions.

## Technical uses

> The uses below are the **technical reading** of the substance, recorded here because they are what the material is for, but not decomposed here: the practices that consume wax are technical elements, and this note stays on the natural kind. All of them rest on the same two properties — a solid that holds a shape and a melt that flows thin.

| Use family | What the wax does | Instances |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Illumination | Burns as the fuel, the solid holding its own shape round a wick | Paraffin, beeswax and tallow candles; soy wax from hydrogenated soybean oil |
| Sacrificial pattern | Holds the shape, then is melted or burnt out to leave a cavity | Lost-wax casting of gold and silver since antiquity; the wax model is removed, the mould is filled |
| Barrier coating | Seals a porous surface against water and staining | Waxed paper and impregnated card; cheese coating; chocolate-covered sweets; leather and fabric waterproofing; Manila hemp rope; food-safe coatings on wooden boards |
| Release and slip | Low-friction solid that parts two surfaces | Mould release agents in mould making; polyethylene wax dispersions in inks to reduce friction; slip agents on furniture; beeswax on wood-on-wood drawer slides |
| Polish and finish | Fills and levels a surface, then buffs to sheen | Shoe, wood, floor, furniture and automotive polishes; carnauba as the hard plant wax of choice |
| Plastics and paint additive | Confers matting, wear resistance and corrosion resistance; carries colourant | Polyethylene and polypropylene waxes in colourant formulations for plastics; matting agents in paints |
| Writing and closure | Takes an impression or a mark, and seals a document shut | Wax tablets as writing surfaces; sealing wax closing important documents in the Middle Ages; carbon paper with carbon black suspended in montan wax; crayons, china markers and coloured pencils; encaustic painting |
| Cosmetics | Blends with fats and pigments into a shape-holding stick or paste | Lipstick and mascara; beeswax and lanolin as the common bases |
| Sport and surface | Changes friction between a board and snow, water or concrete | Ski and snowboard wax; surfboard and skateboard wax |
| Modelling and decoration | Casts and holds fine detail, takes colour | Figurines, dolls and puppet heads; wax sculptures; Easter-egg decoration by wax resist; lava lamps |
| Simulation | Stands in for lead at low energy | Wax bullets |

> Two of these rows carry the whole history of the material. **Lost-wax casting** is the case where wax is valuable precisely because it is destroyed — the pattern is a *sacrificial* object, and the technique's tolerance depends on the wax melting out cleanly rather than on the wax surviving. **Sealing wax** is the case where the material's weakness becomes the point: a seal that can be broken and cannot be reclosed without trace is exactly what a closed document needs, which is why the impression in wax carried authentication before the signature did.
