# Nanoparticle

> A **nanoparticle** is a particle of matter with all dimensions in the **nanometric range — roughly 1 to 100 nanometers** — small enough that its behavior is governed by **surface**: the fraction of its atoms sitting at the boundary becomes large, and quantum confinement can subordinate the bulk properties the same material shows at macroscopic size.

> Size alone is the identity, not composition. A nanoparticle may be a [molecule](/view.html?p=notes/nature/matter/molecule.md) or macromolecule, an atomic cluster (metal nanoparticles of a few to a few thousand atoms), a crystalline fragment of a compound, or a soot particle — which is why it is filed in [Matter](/view.html?p=notes/nature/matter/matter.md)'s table as a **size-class row, cut across every composition row**. What individuates the class is the regime: below roughly 100 nm the surface becomes a significant fraction of the particle's total energy and quantum confinement of the carriers begins, so the object's chemistry, optics and thermodynamics drift away from the bulk values a chemist's table assumes — the gold that stops being gold-colored and the semiconductor crystal whose band gap shifts with size.

## Formulation

### What natural element type does this natural instance belong to?

`Substance / Matter` at meso level of organization (Constituent category), per the [Tabular view](/view.html?p=notes/philosophia-naturalis-et-operis.md) — a size class, read secondarily as a `Phenomenon` where its surface-dominated behavior is the point.

### What is this natural instance?

> A matter particle of 1–100 nm in every dimension, whose identity criterion is the **size-and-surface regime itself**: a surface-area-to-volume ratio enormous compared with bulk, a non-negligible share of atoms at the surface, and — where the dimension is small enough — quantum confinement of the carriers. The lower bound distinguishes from [molecules](/view.html?p=notes/nature/matter/molecule.md) and atomic clusters proper (identity by connectivity); the upper bound distinguishes from [microparticles](/view.html?p=notes/nature/matter/microparticle.md) and bulk matter (identity by regime). The boundaries are conventions, and the nano/micro edge is still under debate.

### What is the recursive instance decomposition of this natural instance?

> Boundary: the size class as a category, not one particle instance; the class is individuated, its members typed by composition under the class.

| Instance Tree Path | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `Substance / Matter` → **Nanoparticle** | Bound root: the 1–100 nm size class of matter, identity by surface regime. |

Limitation checklist: **Scale** (the regime sits between the molecular and the bulk, owned by neither identity criterion) and **Partial Observability** (several members of the class are characterized indirectly) apply.
