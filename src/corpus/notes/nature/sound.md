# Sound

> **Sound** is a mechanical disturbance propagating through an elastic medium as a **longitudinal pressure wave** — alternating regions of compression and rarefaction travelling away from a vibrating source — heard when it reaches an ear.

> It is not a substance but a **state of motion of a medium**: strike a bell and the metal pushes the air, the air pushes its neighbour, and the pattern travels while the material itself stays roughly in place. That is why sound needs a medium and does not cross a vacuum, and why its speed is a property of the medium rather than of the sound — roughly 343 m/s in air at 20 °C, about 1 480 m/s in water, some 5 000 m/s in steel.

## Properties

| Term | Definition | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Frequency (f)** | Number of oscillations per second — what the ear hears as pitch | Hertz (Hz) |
| **Wavelength (λ)** | Distance between two consecutive compressions or rarefactions, λ = v/f | Meter (m) |
| **Amplitude (A)** | Maximum displacement of the medium from equilibrium — what the ear hears as loudness | Meter or pascal (pressure amplitude) |
| **Speed (v)** | How fast the disturbance propagates, set by the medium's density and elasticity | Meter per second (m/s) |
| **Phase** | Position within a cycle (0°–360°, or radians) — what governs interference | Radian |
| **Period (T)** | Duration of one cycle, T = 1/f | Second (s) |

## Representation

> A pure tone is a **sine wave**: $y(t) = A \sin(2\pi f t + \phi)$, with $A$ the amplitude, $f$ the frequency, and $\phi$ the phase.

> A complex sound is a **sum of partials** — the Fourier series $y(t) = \sum_n A_n \sin(2\pi f_n t + \phi_n)$ — which is why a note and a violin playing it have the same pitch and different timbre.

## Propagation

- Sound requires a **medium**: an elastic solid, liquid, or gas. It does not propagate in vacuum.
- The medium's density, elasticity and temperature set the speed and the attenuation.
- In open air a source radiates **spherically**, and the intensity falls as the square of the distance: amplitude is lost as the front spreads.
- At boundaries the wave is **reflected, refracted, diffracted and absorbed** according to the acoustic impedance mismatch between the media.
- Standing waves form where reflection closes a path — the resonances of a string, a pipe, or a room.

## Perception

> The ear converts the mechanical wave into neural signal, and the auditory system reads frequency as **pitch**, pressure amplitude as **loudness**, and the partial structure as **timbre**. The perceptual reading is a distinct natural note: [Psychoacoustics of Perception](/view.html?p=notes/nature/human/psychoacoustics-perception.md), with the organ in [Human Auditory System](/view.html?p=notes/nature/human/human-auditory-system.md).

## Sound in different media

| Medium | Behaviour | Order of speed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Air | Longitudinal waves; speed rises with temperature and humidity | ~343 m/s at 20 °C |
| Water | Longitudinal waves, low attenuation — the medium of sonar and whale song | ~1 480 m/s |
| Solids | Longitudinal and transverse waves; the elastic moduli set the speed | ~5 000 m/s in steel |
