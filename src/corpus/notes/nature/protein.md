> What makes a biomolecule a protein?
>

> Which other non-protein bio structure composed of “ polymers of amino acids” linked  or not by peptide bonds? …

> What **factors** contribute to the complexity of determining whether a biological structure is a protein or not?

| **Criterion** | **Description** | **Method** | **Example/Indicator** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Structural Composition** | Proteins are polymers of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. | Chemical analysis or sequencing | Presence of amino and carboxyl groups |
| **Chemical Tests** | Specific reactions indicate the presence of proteins. | Biuret Test, Ninhydrin Test, Xanthoproteic Test | Violet (Biuret), Yellow (Xanthoproteic) |
| **UV Absorption** | Proteins absorb UV light, especially at 280 nm due to aromatic amino acids. | UV-Vis Spectroscopy | Strong absorbance at 280 nm |
| **Infrared Spectroscopy** | Detects amide bonds characteristic of proteins. | FTIR Analysis | Amide I and Amide II bands |
| **Electrophoresis** | Separation of proteins by molecular weight or charge. | SDS-PAGE, Native PAGE | Protein bands visible in gel |
| **Chromatography** | Separation based on size, charge, or affinity. | Size-Exclusion, Affinity Chromatography | Distinct peaks indicating proteins |
| **Nitrogen Content** | Proteins contain nitrogen due to amino acids, unlike most carbohydrates or lipids. | Kjeldahl Method | High nitrogen content detected |
| **Mass Spectrometry** | Identifies protein fragments and amino acid sequences. | MALDI-TOF or LC-MS/MS | Protein-specific peptide patterns |
| **Biological Function** | Proteins exhibit specific biological roles such as enzymatic activity, signaling, or structural support. | Functional assays | Observed activity like catalysis or transport |
| **Immunodetection** | Antibodies confirm protein identity. | Western Blotting or ELISA | Specific binding with antibodies |

## Localization

> **Proteins** are found in various locations within the cell, including the cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, and cell membrane, as well as in the extracellular space and extracellular matrix.
>

- **Cytoplasm:** The gel-like substance where many metabolic processes and protein functions occur. Contains enzymes, structural proteins, and other components involved in cell maintenance.
- **Nucleus:** Contains the cell's DNA and is the site of transcription (RNA synthesis). Proteins like transcription factors and histones are involved in gene regulation and DNA packaging.
- **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)**
    - **Rough ER**: Has ribosomes attached for protein synthesis.
    - **Smooth ER**: Involved in lipid synthesis and detoxification. Proteins are folded and processed here.
- **Golgi Apparatus:** Modifies, sorts, and packages proteins received from the ER. It is involved in protein glycosylation and preparing proteins for secretion or targeting to other organelles.
- **Mitochondria:** Powerhouse of the cell, responsible for ATP production. Proteins involved in oxidative phosphorylation and the Krebs cycle are found here.
- **Ribosomes:** Tiny structures that synthesize proteins by translating mRNA. Found both in the cytoplasm and attached to the rough ER.
- **Cell Membrane:** Contains integral membrane proteins (like receptors, ion channels, and transporters) that regulate substance entry and cell signaling.
- **Lysosomes:** Contain enzymes for breaking down waste, damaged organelles, and foreign particles. Proteins here include hydrolases for degradation.
- **Peroxisomes:** Organelles involved in the breakdown of fatty acids and detoxification. Contain enzymes like catalase to break down hydrogen peroxide.
- **Cytoskeleton:** Network of protein filaments (actin, microtubules, and intermediate filaments) that provide structural support, aid in movement, and facilitate intracellular transport.
- **Secretory Vesicles:** Transport proteins and other molecules to the cell membrane for secretion or delivery to other organelles.
- **Extracellular Matrix (ECM):** A complex network outside the cell, composed of proteins like collagen and fibronectin, which provides structural support to tissues and helps in cell communication.
- **Endosomes:** Membrane-bound vesicles that sort and transport materials taken in by endocytosis. Proteins here are involved in sorting and recycling internalized molecules.
