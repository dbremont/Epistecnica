# Active Ingredient

> An **active ingredient** is the component of a formulated product that produces the intended effect. In a medicine it is the **active pharmaceutical ingredient (API)**: the substance that binds a biological target and changes what that target does, as distinct from the excipients that carry, stabilize, preserve or deliver it.

## What the term covers

| Term | What it names | Relation to the active ingredient |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Active ingredient / active substance** | The component responsible for the intended effect | The general term |
| **Active pharmaceutical ingredient (API)** | The manufactured substance used in a medicine | The pharmaceutical case |
| **Drug substance** | The API before formulation | The input to the drug product |
| **Drug product** | The formulated, packaged, authorised article | Contains the API plus excipients |
| **Excipient** | Inactive ingredient used for formulation, stability, preservation or delivery | Everything the API is not |
| **Active moiety** | The part of the molecule that carries the pharmacological activity | May be the API itself or a metabolite |
| **Prodrug** | An inactive precursor converted to the active moiety in the body | A delivery form of the active principle |

The distinction is functional, not chemical. The same molecule can be an active ingredient in one product and an intermediate, a fine chemical or a contaminant in another. What makes it active is its role in the formulated product and its effect on the target organism.

## Active ingredient and the product

An active ingredient is rarely delivered alone. A tablet contains the API together with fillers, binders, disintegrants, lubricants and a coating; a liquid contains solvents, buffers, preservatives and flavourings; an injectable contains water, salts and a pH buffer. The excipients are not "inactive" in a trivial sense: they determine whether the API is stable, soluble, absorbable and safe in the intended route.

The same API can therefore become different products — a tablet, an inhaler, a cream, an injection — with different bioavailability and different authorisation. The active ingredient is the part that acts; the product is the thing that is manufactured, tested and prescribed.

## Classes of active ingredient

| Class | What it is | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Small-molecule API** | A low-molecular-weight synthetic or natural compound | Aspirin, metformin, atorvastatin |
| **Biological API** | A protein, antibody, nucleic acid or other substance made in a living system | Insulin, trastuzumab, mRNA |
| **Natural-product API** | A substance isolated from a living organism | Morphine, paclitaxel, artemisinin |
| **Semi-synthetic API** | A natural product chemically modified to improve properties | Semi-synthetic penicillins, artemether |
| **Prodrug** | An inactive precursor converted in the body | Enalapril, codeine |
| **Radiopharmaceutical API** | A radionuclide or labelled molecule | FDG, lutetium-177 dotatate |

## Psychoactive active ingredients

A **psychoactive active ingredient** is a substance that crosses the blood–brain barrier and alters mood, perception, cognition, arousal or behaviour. The class is defined by action on the central nervous system, not by chemical family.

Examples include:

- **Caffeine** — adenosine receptor antagonist.
- **Nicotine** — nicotinic acetylcholine receptor agonist.
- **Ethanol** — central nervous system depressant with complex receptor effects.
- **Opioids** — mu-opioid receptor agonists.
- **Cannabinoids** — including **tetrahydrocannabinol (THC)**, the principal psychoactive constituent of cannabis.
- **Antidepressants and antipsychotics** — monoamine and dopamine-system acting medicines.
- **Psychedelics** — serotonergic compounds such as psilocybin and LSD.

**Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC)** is the main psychoactive cannabinoid in cannabis. It acts at cannabinoid receptors and is the active ingredient read separately from the plant material, the cannabis product and the non-psychoactive cannabinoid cannabidiol (CBD). The distinction matters legally, pharmacologically and industrially: the plant is a raw material, the extract is a mixture, and THC is one active substance within it.

## Specification and regulation

An active ingredient is defined by a specification before it is defined by a brand. The specification fixes:

- **Identity** — the substance is what it claims to be.
- **Assay** — the content of active substance against the label claim.
- **Purity and impurities** — residual solvents, heavy metals, related substances, microbial limits.
- **Potency** — the biological or chemical activity per unit.
- **Stability** — how the substance changes under heat, light, moisture and time.
- **Physical form** — polymorph, particle size, solubility and hygroscopicity.

Pharmaceutical APIs are made under **good manufacturing practice (GMP)**, released against a pharmacopoeial monograph or an in-house specification, and documented in a batch record. The active ingredient is also the point at which chemical quality, pharmacological action and regulatory status meet: a substance can be chemically identical to a drug and still not be a licensed active ingredient if it is not made, tested and authorised as one.
