# Morphogenesis

> **Morphogenesis** is the generation of biological form: how a mass of cells becomes a body with axes, organs, digits and a shape. It is the process where **genotype meets mechanics** — the genes specify which cells do what, the physics decides what the tissue can become — and neither alone predicts the outcome.

## The two explanatory programmes

| Programme | Claim | Canonical statement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Positional information** | Cells acquire a position on a coordinate system built by a **morphogen gradient**, and read their fate from the local concentration | Wolpert's French-flag model; the *bicoid* gradient in *Drosophila* |
| **Self-organisation** | Pattern arises from local interactions without a pre-existing map — reaction and diffusion of two species suffice | Turing (1952), *The Chemical Basis of Morphogenesis*; later mechanochemical models |

> The honest state of the debate is that both operate, and the disputes are about **which dominates where**. Limb-bud patterning, digit spacing and hair-follicle spacing are the standard Turing cases; the anterior–posterior axis of the fly and the vertebrate neural tube are the standard positional cases. The productive modern position treats the gradient as setting the competence and the reaction–diffusion as choosing the pattern within it.

## The cell behaviours

> A pattern is only a specification; the shape comes from what cells do with it.

| Behaviour | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Proliferation** | Differential growth rates bend and fold tissue |
| **Apoptosis** | Removes the webbing between digits, sculpts the lumen, deletes unneeded structures |
| **Apical constriction** | Contraction at the apical surface bends an epithelium into a tube or a groove |
| **Convergent extension** | Cells intercalate, so a sheet narrows and elongates — the body axis |
| **Migration** | Neural crest, primordial germ cells, branching vessels |
| **Adhesion change** | Sorting into tissues; cadherin switching is how boundaries stay sharp |

## The mechanics

> The part that is not genetics: **differential growth and tension**. Tissues are materials, and the same genetic pattern gives different shapes if the matrix stiffness, the pressure or the boundary conditions change. This is why the field now measures forces (traction microscopy, laser ablation) alongside expression, and why the "genetic programme" metaphor understates the role of physical constraints — a sheet can buckle because it grows faster than its neighbours, with no extra instruction.

## The canonical instances

- **Gastrulation** — the blastula becomes a layered embryo, by invagination, involution and migration
- **Somite formation** — periodic blocks of mesoderm, one of the best-supported clock-and-wavefront (not Turing) cases
- **Limb-bud patterning** — the apical ectodermal ridge, the zone of polarising activity, and the digit Turing model
- **Neural tube** — convergent extension plus apical constriction, and the failure modes that produce spina bifida
- **Leaf venation and phyllotaxis** — auxin transport as pattern formation
- **Regeneration** — the hydra and the planarian re-establish the pattern from a fragment, the strongest evidence that the mechanism is local
