# KCNQ1

> **KCNQ1** is the gene encoding **Kv7.1**, a voltage-gated potassium channel. With the accessory subunit KCNE1 (minK) it forms the **slow delayed rectifier current** $I_{Ks}$ — the current that repolarises the cardiac action potential when the heart rate is high — and it is the textbook case of one channel gene whose mutation produces different diseases in different tissues.

## The channel

| Property | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Gene / protein** | KCNQ1 (chromosome 11p15.5) → Kv7.1, a six-transmembrane voltage-gated K⁺ channel |
| **Complex** | Kv7.1 + **KCNE1** = $I_{Ks}$, slow-activating, non-inactivating, enhanced by sympathetic stimulation |
| **Function in the heart** | Repolarisation, especially at short cycle lengths — the reserve that shortens the action potential when rate rises |
| **Other tissues** | Inner ear (K⁺ secretion into the endolymph by the stria vascularis), kidney, intestine, thyroid |

## The phenotypes

| Mutation | Disease | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Loss of function, dominant** | **Long QT syndrome type 1** (Romano–Ward) | Reduced $I_{Ks}$ prolongs the action potential; triggered typically by exercise or emotional stress, the settings where $I_{Ks}$ matters most |
| **Loss of function, recessive** | **Jervell and Lange-Nielsen syndrome** | Prolonged QT **plus congenital deafness** — the same current is needed to secrete endolymph, so the ear and the heart fail together |
| **Gain of function** | Familial atrial fibrillation | Shortened action potential and repolarisation dispersion in the atria |

> The case is the corpus's cleanest illustration of the **lesion-versus-phenotype** distinction: the mutation is a defect in one channel, and the phenotype is the set of tissues that depend on that current. "The gene for long QT syndrome" is shorthand; the correct reading is that the heart and the inner ear share a transport requirement, and the gene is where the requirement is written.

## Pharmacology

> Kv7.1 is a drug target and an anti-target. Activators are pursued as anti-arrhythmics (shortening the action potential where it is dangerously long); blockers are a liability, because a drug that reduces $I_{Ks}$ adds to the repolarisation reserve problem in a patient who already has a long QT. The broader lesson — that a channel's physiological role is rate- and tissue-dependent, so its pharmacology cannot be read off a single expression system — is why cardiac safety testing looks at repolarisation as a system rather than at one current.
