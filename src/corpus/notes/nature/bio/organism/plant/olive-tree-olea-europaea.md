# Olive Tree (Olea europaea)

> An evergreen tree of the Mediterranean basin, grown for a drupe whose flesh carries up to a fifth of its weight in oil. Its leaf is small, leathery and silver underneath; its wood is dense and slow; its root system reaches further than its canopy — the whole constitution is a fit to a summer that gives no water and a winter that gives little warmth.

> A natural element read per [How to decompose any natural instance?](/view.html?p=notes/philosophia-naturalis-et-operis.md).

## Formulation

### What natural element type does this natural instance belong to?

`Organism` at **meso** level of organization (Ontic; Existential), specialized from `Substantive` — a self-maintaining organization, here one tree. Its organs and the processes they perform sit at micro, the cultivated grove and the terraced landscape it builds sit at macro; those are separate nodes and are not merged here.

It is **readable as** a `Natural System` when the grove — trees, soil, pollinators, pruner — is taken as the unit, and as a `Constitutive Technical Object` when the orchard is read as production. Both readings are prose, not second roots.

The taxonomic position is stated once: **Olea europaea** L., family **Oleaceae**, order Lamiales. The cultivated tree is subsp. *europaea*; the wild **oleaster** (var. *sylvestris*) is the same species, thorny and small-fruited, and is what cultivated trees are grafted onto. The distinction matters for the natural reading: most orchard trees are two organisms joined, not one.

### What is this natural instance?

**Olive (Olea europaea)**: an evergreen sclerophyll 8–15 m in cultivation, usually held lower by pruning, with opposite lanceolate leaves 3–8 cm long, green above and silver-white beneath from a dense scale layer that reflects light and slows transpiration. The fruit is a **drupe** — a thin skin, an oily fleshy mesocarp, and a hard stone — green turning violet-black as it ripens.

Cultivation is roughly six thousand years old and began in the Levant; the tree followed the Phoenicians, Greeks and Romans across the Mediterranean, and every producing region holds a set of local cultivars selected for fruit size, oil yield, rooting and ripening date. The tree is long-lived — individual trees in the eastern Mediterranean are counted in millennia, and a felled tree regrows from its crown — so the same individual can outlast the orchard that was planted around it.

**Levels.** The rows sit at three levels and each instance row states its own: **micro** for the organs and processes of a single tree, the leaf, the flower, the drupe, the photosynthetic and uptake machinery; **meso** for the tree as a body — its canopy, its states and its bounds; **macro** for the climate and the cultivated landscape it occupies. The tree is not a grove, and the grove is not a climate: the same word at two levels would be two nodes.

**Limitation checklist** — the limitations the segment exhibits, recorded as properties of the segment rather than as failures of method: **Complexity** (yield coupled to previous crop, winter chill, water and pruning at once); **Nonlinearity** (the alternate-bearing cycle means this year's crop is a function of last year's); **Scale** (one tree and one landscape are the same species read at two levels); **Partial Observability** (the root system and the stored reserves are not seen, and the orchard's age is read from the trunk only); **Non-Repeatability** (no two bearing cycles repeat, and a millennium-old tree's history is not reconstructible from the living tree).

**Secondary readings** (kept as prose, never merged into the tree): readable as **technique** — grafting, pruning, harvest and pressing, all decomposing under the technicarum grammar; readable as **production** — the orchard as a perennial asset whose output is a good, [olive oil](/view.html?p=notes/social/good/agriculture/olive-oil.md), and whose economics are dominated by the alternate year; readable as **social** — the terraced landscape, the smallholder structure of the sector, and the protected-origin schemes that price a place; readable as **epistemic** — the cultivar taxonomy and the sensory panel as the instruments that decide what an oil is.
