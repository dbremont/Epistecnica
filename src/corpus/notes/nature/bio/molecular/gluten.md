# Gluten

> A **gluten** is the viscoelastic protein network that remains when the starch and soluble matter of a cereal flour are washed away with water — in wheat some 75% protein on a dry weight basis, the rest starch and lipids. It is the network that lets wheat dough hold gas, and therefore the substance behind leavened bread.

## Formulation

### What natural element type does this gluten instance belong to?

**Gluten, read as a substance, belongs to `Ontic Element (→ Kind) Substance (→ Kind) Substance / Matter` — a stuff-kind individuated by composition.**

Readable secondarily as a `Natural System`: the network is not a single molecule but an entangled and disulfide-bonded assembly of many proteins, with a recognizable internal structure and emergent mechanical behaviour. Readable again as a **mixture**: the substance is a blend of two functional protein families rather than one chemical species. Those readings are prose here, not second roots.

### What is this natural instance?

> Gluten is not one protein. The proteins it comprises are the cereal **storage proteins** deposited in the starchy endosperm during grain development; they survive milling and, once flour meets water, they are brought together into one continuous network. Cereal chemistry sorts them by solubility into the **Osborne fractions** — albumins, globulins, gliadins and glutenins — and gluten is built from the last two.
>
> The two fractions do different work. **Gliadins** are monomeric, hydrated they have little elasticity and are poorly cohesive: they lower the viscosity of the network and let it extend. **Glutenins** are polymeric, linked by intermolecular disulfide bonds and, in wheat, in a wide range of molecular weights; they are cohesive and elastic and are what makes dough strong. The **high-molecular-weight subunits of glutenin** are the elastic backbone of the network, and their amount and type are the strongest single determinants of breadmaking quality. The glutamine-rich repetitive sequences at the centre of those subunits form long arrays of interchain hydrogen bonds, which is one proposed route by which the network stores elastic energy.
>
> Gluten exists across the **Triticeae** — wheat, barley and rye — but wheat is the only cereal whose gluten gives dough of baking quality. The composition of the network, not just the presence of storage proteins, is what carries the property.
>
> Two uses of the name are distinct and should not be conflated. **Gluten as substrate** — the washed-out network just described — is what cereal chemistry measures. **Gluten as dietary exposure** — the storage-protein fraction of wheat, barley and rye that a person ingests — is what clinical medicine means, and it is the exposure behind gluten-related disorders. The gluten-related disorders are a clinical family, defined by consensus, not a single disease: coeliac disease is an immune-mediated enteropathy in genetically predisposed individuals, and non-coeliac gluten sensitivity is a distinct presentation in people without coeliac disease or wheat allergy. "Gluten intolerance" is a discouraged term; "gluten-related disorders" is the umbrella.

### What is the recursive instance decomposition of this natural instance?

> Boundary: the hydrated protein network as a material — its constituent proteins, the bonds and entanglements that hold it, the mechanical properties those produce, and the processing steps that build it from flour. The wheat grain that synthesizes the storage proteins, the dough as a whole, and the baker's ovens are associated, not parts.
>
> Stopping rule: a row is terminal when it names a protein family, a bond class, a measurable property, or a processing step whose own decomposition lives elsewhere.
>
> Identity: Instance Tree Path is the stable identifier of each row; every path is unique. Level of organization: the constituent proteins are **micro**; the network and its mechanical properties are **meso**; loaf-level baking performance is **macro**. Limitation checklist for the segment: **Many-Body coupling** (a network of thousands of chains), **Scale**, **Partial Observability** (single-molecule behaviour inferred from bulk rheology), **Non-Repeatability** (vintage, weather and cultivar enter every batch).
