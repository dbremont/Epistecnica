# Cellulose

> **Cellulose** is the structural polysaccharide of the plant cell wall: unbranched chains of β-D-glucose joined 1→4, associated by hydrogen bonds into microfibrils and those into fibres. It is the most abundant organic substance in the biosphere, and the one whose properties most clearly depend on organisation rather than chemistry — the same β-1,4 chain is the cotton of a shirt, the fibre of a sheet of paper, the wall of a wood cell and the dietary fibre that passes through the gut undigested.

## Formulation

### What natural element type does this cellulose instance belong to?

**Cellulose, read as a material, belongs to `Ontic Element (→ Kind) Substance (→ Kind) Substance / Matter` — a stuff-kind individuated by composition.** Readable again as a set of `Natural Object`s when the microfibril and the fibre are the units under study, since those are where the mechanical behaviour actually lives.

Readable secondarily as a `Building Block` when a single glucose unit or a single chain is the unit under study. Those readings are prose here, not second roots.

### What is this natural instance?

> The chain chemistry is simple and, at first sight, unpromising. Glucose units alternate in orientation along the chain, so every second residue is rotated by 180°; the chain is therefore straight, not coiled, and its hydroxyls project in a regular pattern on both faces. In the crystalline core water cannot penetrate and the chains are locked by a dense network of **interchain hydrogen bonds**. That is the whole of the mechanical story: cellulose is strong because its chains are straight, regular and mutually bonded, not because any single bond is remarkable.
>
> Against starch the difference is one bond. Starch joins its glucose α-1,4; cellulose joins it β-1,4. The α-linkage puts a bend at every unit and makes the chain coil into helices that pack loosely and are opened by water; the β-linkage leaves the chain straight and lets neighbouring chains register against one another. The consequence is that **human digestive enzymes cannot cleave β-1,4** — starch feeds us, cellulose passes through, and only the fermentative microbiota of the gut or of a rumen can take energy from it.
>
> The hierarchy matters more than any single level: a single chain is a molecule; chains bundle into **elementary fibrils**, fibrils into **microfibrils**, and these into the layered wall that makes wood and cotton what they are. Crystalline regions coexist with disordered regions, and the ratio of the two governs every practical property: the swellability of a paper pulp, the stiffness of a fibre, the accessibility of a surface to a chemical reagent, and the rate at which the material ages.
>
> Its historical importance is structural rather than merely material. Cellulose was the substance on which the case for macromolecules was won: Staudinger argued that the chains were covalently continuous and not aggregates of small units, and the viscosity and derivatisation behaviour of cellulose only made sense under that picture. Haworth's work settled the ring structure and the linkage, and the modern account of its crystal packing followed.

### What is the recursive instance decomposition of this natural instance?

> Boundary: the substance and the material it forms — its monomer and linkage, the chain, the fibrillar hierarchy, the bonds that hold the hierarchy together, and the properties the hierarchy produces. The plant cell that synthesises it, the products made from it, and the organisms that digest it are associated, not parts.
>
> Stopping rule: a row is terminal when it names a unit, a bond, a structural level, a reaction class, or a measurable property whose own decomposition lives elsewhere.
>
> Identity: Instance Tree Path is the stable identifier of each row; every path is unique. Level of organization: the glucose unit and the glycosidic bond are **micro**; the chain and the microfibril are **meso**; a fibre, a paper sheet or a wood wall is **macro**. Limitation checklist for the segment: **Many-Body coupling** (properties emerge from packing, not from the unit), **Scale**, **Partial Observability** (chain length inferred from viscosity and from end-group assays), **Non-Repeatability** (biological source and processing history set the result).
