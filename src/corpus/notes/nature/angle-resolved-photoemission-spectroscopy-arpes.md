# Angle-Resolved Photoemission Spectroscopy (ARPES)

> **ARPES** measures the **electronic band structure** of a solid: it shines ultraviolet or soft X-ray photons on a surface, collects the emitted photoelectrons as a function of their kinetic energy and emission angle, and reconstructs the occupied electronic states as energy versus crystal momentum, $E(\mathbf{k})$. It is the technique that made the band structure — long a theoretical construction inferred from transport and optics — a directly measured object.

## The principle

> The photoelectric effect gives the energy balance: a photon of energy $h\nu$ ejects an electron whose kinetic energy, measured by the analyser, fixes its binding energy below the Fermi level. The **emission angle** fixes the in-plane momentum: $k_\parallel = \sqrt{2mE_k}\,\sin\theta/\hbar$, so scanning the angle scans the Brillouin zone. The out-of-plane momentum $k_\perp$ is not conserved at the surface and is instead tuned by the photon energy, which is why synchrotron beamlines with tunable light dominate the field.

| Component | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Photon source** | Laser, gas-discharge lamp or synchrotron; sets the energy range and the $k_\perp$ probe |
| **Sample surface** | Must be atomically clean and ordered — the escape depth is a few ångströms |
| **Electron analyser** | Hemispherical analyser resolving kinetic energy and emission angle |
| **Detector** | Measures the count of electrons per energy–angle bin; modern instruments do this in parallel |

## What it gives and what it does not

> It gives the **occupied** band structure with energy and momentum resolution, the Fermi surface, the opening of a gap, the dispersion of a band and the self-energy of a quasiparticle. It does not give the unoccupied states (inverse photoemission does), it does not see below the surface escape depth, and it is acutely sensitive to surface contamination — which is why a "clean ARPES" result is a statement about a preparation as much as about a material.

> The technique's place in the catalogue is the interesting one: it is a **spectroscopy** by its instrument and a **diffraction-like** measurement by its output, because what it returns is a structure in reciprocal space rather than a spectrum of a sample.
