# 15.879 Building Serious System Dynamics Models from Scratch

> MIT OpenCourseWare, Spring 2025, Prof. Hazhir Rahmandad (Sloan School of Management). A graduate research seminar in which the whole syllabus is **one archetype per week, built from scratch**: stocks, an epidemic with vaccination, energy-efficiency co-flows, predator–prey, a drug pipeline, a Ponzi scheme, market growth, endogenous diffusion, and field growth. The method is the message — the models are not studied, they are constructed, until constructing them is the skill.

## Notes

> The pedagogy is the point. System dynamics is usually taught as a **notation** — here is a stock, here is a flow, here is a loop — and then applied. This course inverts it: each week takes one well-known behaviour and builds the smallest structure that produces it, so the student learns the mapping from **structure to behaviour** by making it, one archetype at a time. Weeks 10 to 12 are then the test: a model of one's own, built without a template.

> Three of the archetypes carry more than they appear to. **Co-flows** (week 3, energy efficiency) are the construct most often missing from casual SD use: a flow that carries an attribute — the efficiency embodied in a capital stock — so that the quality of what accumulates, not just its quantity, evolves. **The Ponzi model** (week 6) is where the method stops being accounting: solvency is generated and destroyed by the structure itself, and the collapse is not an external shock. **Endogenous diffusion** (week 8) refuses the exogenous adoption curve: adoption is produced inside the model, by the feedback between adopters and non-adopters, which is the difference between describing a diffusion and explaining one.

> The course's implicit claim is that **model-building judgment is not transferable by reading** — the errors that matter (a loop that should not close, a stock that is really a flow, a delay whose length changes the answer) only surface when one builds the thing and watches it misbehave. That is why the corpus pairs it with the [System Dynamics](/view.html?p=notes/epistemica/singular/system-dynamics.md) note, which classifies the framework epistemically; the course is what the classification looks like when it is practised.
