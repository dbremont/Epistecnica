# 2.25 Advanced Fluid Mechanics

> MIT OpenCourseWare, Fall 2013. The graduate-level treatment of fluid motion: the continuum viewpoint and the equations of motion, fluid statics, inviscid flow and Bernoulli, control-volume theorems, the equations of viscous flow, dimensional analysis, viscous-dominated flows, potential flow, vorticity and circulation, boundary layers, separation and drag, and surface tension. It is the course that turns the undergraduate "fluids" of pipes and drag coefficients into the field-theoretic formulation of the subject.

## Notes

> The course's spine is the **transition of viewpoint**: from the control-volume bookkeeping of undergraduate fluids to the differential equations of motion, and then to the three regimes those equations admit — inviscid (potential flow), viscous (exact solutions and lubrication), and boundary-layer (matched asymptotics). Dimensional analysis is placed in the middle because it is the bridge: it is what lets a solution of the equations apply to a scale it was not solved at.
