## Notes

> Property rights appear, Demsetz argues, when the gain from internalising an externality exceeds the cost of establishing and enforcing the right. The regime is an answer to a cost problem, not a moral discovery.

The worked case is the fur trade among the Montagnais: while the forest was abundant and the catch low, hunting rights were left undefined; as the commercial trade in furs raised the value of the catch, territory was demarcated and held. The claim is not that every regime is efficient, but that the *formation* of a right has a cost-benefit structure, and that regimes change when that structure changes.

For the corpus note this supplies the dynamic the decomposition otherwise lacks: the regimes branch says who may hold which incidents, and Demsetz supplies one account of why a society moves from one arrangement to another. It is an account of emergence, not a justification — which is the distinction the note's own question about the theories of property depends on.
