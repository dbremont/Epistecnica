## Notes

> A practitioner's statement of the tokenization thesis: converting a tangible or intangible asset into a token — "the digital representation of that asset" — lets ownership be split, transferred and settled on a network, so that a painting, a share or a footballer's training contract can be held in fractions by many holders at once.

The evidence offered is market projection and worked instance rather than argument. Boston Consulting Group's forecast of more than USD 16 trillion tokenized by 2030 — 10% of global GDP — is reported alongside the World Economic Forum's earlier date of 2027; the Sygnum case, in which the ownership rights over a 1964 Picasso were split into 4,000 tokens sold to over 50 investors, is given as the practical demonstration. The savings claimed are time and cost: seconds to settle on a chain against up to three days on the interbank system.

For the corpus note the piece is useful as an object of study rather than a source of fact — it states what the tokenization claim *is* while leaving out the part the institution supplies, namely what the token entitles its holder to and against whom. It also illustrates the drift the note describes: the register's promotion from evidence of the holding to its substitute.
