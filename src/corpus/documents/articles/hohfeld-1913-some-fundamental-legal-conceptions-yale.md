## Notes

> The argument is that legal vocabulary is systematically ambiguous: "right" stands for four different things, and until the four are separated no legal question can be stated precisely.

Hohfeld sets out the conceptions in correlative pairs — right and duty, privilege and no-right — and defines each by its correlate rather than by its content. A right is not a thing one holds but a claim that someone else's duty answers; a privilege is the absence of a duty in the holder, answered by the absence of a claim in everyone else.

The consequence for property is the one the corpus note builds on: ownership stops being a relation between a person and an object and becomes a set of relations among persons about an object. Once that move is made, "what does the owner own?" becomes "which incidents, held against whom?", and the answer can differ from object to object without the concept changing.
