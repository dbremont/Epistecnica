## Notes

> A property right, on this account, is the socially sanctioned ability to choose the use of a resource — and the paradigm's point is that the choice can be divided: what is owned is not the resource but a set of permitted decisions about it.

The article generalises from the economist's separate treatments of private, communal, collective and state property, and insists that all four be described in the same terms: who holds which decision rights, who bears the consequences, and how the holding is transmitted. The differences between regimes then become differences in the allocation of decision rights rather than different kinds of thing.

That is the economic counterpart of the legal decomposition in the corpus note: the incidents branch and this article's decision rights describe the same division from two sides. The article's weakness is the same as its strength — a description that fits every regime is in no hurry to explain why a particular one holds.
