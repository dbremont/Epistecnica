## Notes

> A comparative study of the currency unions that peg to an external currency — the CFA franc zones, the Eastern Caribbean Currency Union, and the other dollar- and euro-anchored arrangements — and of what that anchor does to the members' policy space. Van Riet's argument is that the formal symmetry of a currency union can coexist with an **asymmetric** relation to the hegemon whose currency provides the anchor: the union's monetary policy is effectively made elsewhere, and the adjustment burden falls on the periphery.

> The paper is the institutional counterpart to the optimum-currency-area literature. It asks not whether the members are sufficiently similar to each other, but whether the union as a whole is subordinate to an external monetary authority, and what that subordination costs in crises when the anchor tightens. The answer is a case for regional arrangements that reduce the dependence rather than reproduce it at a larger scale.
