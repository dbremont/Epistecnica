## Notes

> Kaps asks whether the Habsburg Monarchy had a **core–periphery structure** in the eighteenth century, and answers by reconstructing the trans-regional division of labour from trade data. The western lands — Lower Austria, Bohemia, Moravia, Silesia — exported manufactures and imported raw materials and agricultural produce from the eastern and southern lands, and the pattern hardened over the century. That is the economic content of the core–periphery relation.

> The methodological claim is the interesting one. Kaps argues that the economic pattern cannot be read off the trade flows alone: the same flows were interpreted through **cultural images** of the east as backward and the west as industrious, and those images were produced and circulated by the same commercial and administrative elite that benefited from the division of labour. The periphery was not only an economic position but a representation, and the representation helped to make the position durable.

> **Note on the title.** The title supplied in the request — “The emergence of a core-periphery dynamic between the West and the East in the Habsburg Monarchy: Trans-regional division of labor embodied by trade relations” — is a section heading inside the article. The entry records the article's own title.
