## Notes

> A compact history of postwar Japanese technology policy and a warning against the standard stereotype of a state that picks technological winners. Harayama traces the 1949 technology white paper, the 1966 Large-Scale Industrial Research and Development System, the 1995 Science and Technology Basic Law and the 1996 Basic Plan, and asks what the policy actually did to private R&D decisions.

> The paper is useful in the RIKEN corpus because it gives the policy frame around the institute's postwar public-corporation and Independent Administrative Institution phases. RIKEN is not its main case, but the argument applies: Japanese R&D is financed and performed mainly by industry, and the state's role is better described as institution-building and coordination than as direct technological command.
