## Notes

> A UN Economic Commission for Africa study of the European Payments Union and the Latin American Free Trade Association's payments mechanism, written to ask what an African payments union could borrow. The document belongs to the wave of proposals that followed African independence, when the question was how to expand intra-regional trade without the convertible-currency infrastructure the industrial countries already had.

> Its value now is documentary: it records the design options that were on the table — multilateral clearing, short-term credit, a unit of account, the sequencing from bilateral to convertible settlement — and the reasons the earlier experiments succeeded or failed. Read with the later literature, it shows that the payments-union idea in Africa is not a recent import but a long-standing proposal with a record.

> The record carries no publication date; the UN digital library attributes it to the Commission's work of the early 1960s. The entry keeps the archive's “n.d.” rather than assigning a year.
