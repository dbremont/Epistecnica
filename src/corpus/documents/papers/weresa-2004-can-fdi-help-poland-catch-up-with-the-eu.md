## Notes

> The question is the one the Polish transition actually posed: if the country's route to the EU was **foreign capital rather than domestic R&D**, does the capital bring the capability with it? Weresa's answer is a qualified yes — FDI is correlated with innovation effort across Polish industry, **except in the low-technology branches**, so the mechanism works where the absorptive capacity exists and fails where it does not.

> The channel she tests is the standard one: technology transfer and diffusion from the foreign affiliate to the host, raising the technological and innovative potential of the branch it enters. The regression across industrial branches is the evidence, and the sector qualification is the finding — it is the difference between an FDI policy that upgrades and one that merely installs capacity.

> Read beside [Domański & Gwosdz (2009)](/view.html?p=documents/papers/domanski-gwosdz-2009-embedded-production-system-automotive-poland.md), the pair is the corpus's FDI account: Weresa establishes the aggregate correlation and its sectoral limit; Domański and Gwosdz show the mechanism in the automotive supply chain — upgrading in production competences, much less in R&D — which is the same limit seen from the firm's side.
