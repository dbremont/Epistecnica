## Notes

---

> Information in the brain is carried by **populations** or clusters of cells rather than by single cells — the encoding strategy known as *population coding*. The review sets out the standard use of such codes for **encoding and decoding** information, chiefly through the tuning functions of the individual cells and the decoders built on them: the population vector and maximum-likelihood readout, and the basis-function networks that learn the mapping from activity to variable.
>
> It then asks what a population can **compute**, not merely represent: how population codes support noise removal and nonlinear mapping, the operations that sit between a sensory array and a decision.
>
> The closing part turns to a more radical proposal — that a population code may represent the **uncertainty** of the stimulus directly, in the shape of its activity distribution, rather than leaving uncertainty to be recovered by a separate estimator. This is the reading later named the *probabilistic population code*, and it is what makes the paper foundational for the line that treats neural activity as a probability distribution rather than a rate.
