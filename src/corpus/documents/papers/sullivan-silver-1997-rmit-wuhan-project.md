## Notes

> A short practitioner's report on RMIT's Wuhan project — one of the earliest foreign **joint-venture degree programmes** in the People's Republic of China. An Australian award delivered in China, with a Chinese partner institution, at a moment when the regulatory framework for foreign academic collaboration was still being written.

**It is documentary rather than evaluative.** The paper reports the negotiation, the curriculum arrangement and the institutional division of labour — who taught what, who awarded the degree, who owned the curriculum — and it is written by two of the people who ran it. That is its value now: it records the model in the form it had before it became an industry, and it names the problems that the sector has been managing ever since.

**The problems it names are the ones transnational education still has.** Accreditation of the partner and of the programme; equivalence of the award between two national systems; who controls the curriculum when the awarding body is offshore; and the quality-assurance question that follows from all three — whether an external examiner's visit is a sufficient guarantee of a degree delivered on another continent.

**In the corpus's terms**, the case is a knowledge-transfer channel of a particular kind: what diffuses through an education joint venture is not a technology but a **curriculum, a quality regime and a credential** — which is why the form spread faster than most industrial joint ventures, and why its output is measured in graduates rather than in products.

> The paper is the template that Australian transnational education scaled from. Read beside [China](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/public/polity/chn/prc/china.md)'s joint-venture reading, it supplies the education case for the same mechanism.
