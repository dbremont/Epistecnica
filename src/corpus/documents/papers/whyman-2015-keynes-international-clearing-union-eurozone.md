## Notes

> Whyman asks whether the Keynesian clearing union can be adapted to reform the euro area. The diagnosis is the euro's **asymmetric adjustment**: the single monetary policy and the fiscal rules put the burden of correction on deficit countries, while surplus countries face no comparable obligation. The prescription is a clearing mechanism that would recycle surpluses and impose symmetric adjustment.

> The paper is careful about the limits. A euro-area clearing union would have to operate inside an existing currency, so the exchange-rate instrument is unavailable and the clearing balances would have to be fiscal rather than monetary. Whyman presents the design as a reform direction rather than a blueprint, and the value of the paper is that it takes the institutional detail of the 1943 proposal seriously enough to ask what survives translation.

| Element | Keynes (1943) | Euro-area adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit | Bancor | Clearing balances in euro |
| Surplus adjustment | Interest on excess balances | Fiscal transfer or levy |
| Deficit adjustment | Overdraft limits | Conditionality reformed |
| Exchange rate | Adjustable | Not available |

> The article is dated 2015 by its volume and issue; it appeared online in 2014.
