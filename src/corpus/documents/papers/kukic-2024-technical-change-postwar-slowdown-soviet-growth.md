## Notes

> A long-run growth-accounting exercise over the whole Russian and Soviet record, from the late imperial economy to the post-Soviet period. Kukić decomposes output growth into the contributions of capital, labour and technical change, and asks whether the postwar slowdown was a Soviet anomaly or a stage in a longer trajectory.

> The answer is that the slowdown is best read as a **technical-change failure**, not as a savings or labour-supply failure. The Soviet system could mobilise capital and labour at high rates for decades; what it could not do was convert that mobilisation into sustained productivity growth once the extensive margins were exhausted. Set against the imperial and post-Soviet periods, the Soviet decades look like a distinctive growth regime with a distinctive terminal problem — which is exactly the link the corpus needs between the institutional accounts of the planning system and the empirical growth record.

> Note the dating: the article is assigned to the 2024 volume (77:2), though it appeared online in 2023.
