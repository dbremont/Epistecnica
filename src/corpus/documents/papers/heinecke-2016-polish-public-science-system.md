## Notes

> A historical-institutionalist account of what happened to Polish science after 1989, and the paper's finding is counter-intuitive in exactly the way the corpus cares about: while the economy was being transformed by **shock therapy**, the public science system changed **stepwise and late**, and it changed *differently in each of its three research-performing sectors*. Two decades of accretion produced a cumulative transformation that was never a policy.

**The three sectors and their three fates** — the analytic core:

| Sector | What happened | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Polish Academy of Sciences** (PAN) | Institutionally **reproduced** — the academy and its institutes survived the transition essentially intact | Reproduction / drift |
| **Governmental applied-R&D units** | Partly **dismantled**, partly **displaced** by private-sector R&D | Dismantling and displacement |
| **Higher education** | Strong **expansion** — new research activity and fields layered onto the teaching institutions | Layering |

> The paper's contribution is the *comparison within one country*: the same political shock produced reproduction in one sector, dismantling in another and layering in a third. That is the historical-institutionalist argument made empirically — institutions do not change at the rate of the politics around them, and the variation is structural, not incidental.

> Two methodological points worth carrying. The evidence is **macro-statistical** (R&D personnel, expenditure, publication and patent counts), so the paper measures the system's shape rather than its quality. And the period is the one *before* the big Polish reforms of 2010 (NCN, NCBR, parametric evaluation) and 2017 (the Constitution for Science) — which makes it the baseline against which those reforms should be read rather than a description of the present system.
