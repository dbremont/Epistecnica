## Notes

---

> In many regions of the brain information is represented by **patterns of activity distributed over populations of neurons** — the reading matters both for the computations behind brain function and for interpreting the signals a prosthetic device would have to read.
>
> The treatment is mathematical and deliberately narrow: neurons with **Poisson spike statistics**, where the information sits in the **mean firing rate**, and the code analysed through the **tuning functions** that describe each cell's behaviour. The review organises the field around three questions — *what* information is encoded in a population, *how* the brain computes with populations, and *when* a population is optimal.
>
> Answering them takes in the decoding methods used on experimental data, computation inside networks of interconnected populations, and the optimal design of a code — why a population takes the form it does and which variables it represents best. Its conclusion is that for Poisson-based codes the behaviour and computational properties of the whole follow from the **tuning properties of the individual cells**: the population adds a readout, not a new primitive.
