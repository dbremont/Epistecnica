## Notes

> The earlier of Yamanaka's two studies of Joji Sakurai, this one tracing Sakurai's wider programme for the promotion of science rather than the founding of RIKEN alone. It follows his education in England, his career at Tokyo Imperial University, and his work in the Institute of Physical and Chemical Research, the National Research Council and the Foundation for the Promotion of Scientific Research.

> The argument is that Sakurai's importance is not primarily as a chemist but as an institutional entrepreneur: he tried to build the conditions — funding, international exchange, research autonomy — under which creative science could be done in Japan.
