## Notes

> Gorton's account of the 2007 crisis as a **run on the shadow banking system**. The argument is that the instruments at the centre of the crisis — asset-backed commercial paper, repo, and the structured vehicles that issued them — were the shadow banking system's deposits, and that they were subject to the same panic dynamics as bank deposits: when the collateral lost its perceived safety, the holders ran, and the run transmitted the shock through the whole system.

> The paper's analytical move is to treat the crisis as a **classic panic** rather than a failure of risk management. The information problem was not that the assets were unknown, but that the market could no longer distinguish good from bad collateral, and the run was the rational response. That reframing matters for regulation: the remedy is a lender of last resort and deposit insurance for the shadow system, not better models alone.

| Element | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Run | Withdrawal from repo and asset-backed commercial paper |
| Instrument | Securitised claims as the shadow system's deposits |
| Information | Collateral values became opaque; good and bad could not be told apart |
| Remedy | Liquidity backstop, not only capital rules |
