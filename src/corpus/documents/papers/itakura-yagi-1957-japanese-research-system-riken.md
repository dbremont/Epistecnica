## Notes

> The foundational account of how the Japanese research system produced RIKEN, published in two parts in *Kagakushi Kenkyu* in 1957 and translated as a chapter in *Science and Society in Modern Japan* (1974). Its argument is institutional rather than biographical: the institute is read through the research system that made it possible — the weakness of permanent state funding, the reliance on private industrial patronage, the importation of the Kaiser Wilhelm model, and the attempt to combine pure science with industrial application.

> The chapter is the source to use for the founding coalition around Takamine and Shibusawa and for the early organisational debates. It also explains why the institute's financial base was precarious from the start, which is the condition that later produced Okochi's laboratory reforms and the Riken Konzern.
