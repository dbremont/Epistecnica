## Notes

> The clearest short account of how structured finance works and why it failed. Coval, Jurek and Stafford explain the **tranching** technology: a pool of loans is sliced into claims of different seniority, the senior tranches are rated AAA because they absorb losses only after the junior tranches are exhausted, and the junior tranches carry the risk. The engineering is sound in the model; the failure was in the inputs and the correlations.

> The paper's central point is that the ratings were **model-based, not market-based**. The tranche ratings depended on assumed default correlations, and when house prices fell together those correlations rose toward one, so the diversification that made the senior tranches safe disappeared exactly when it was needed. The instruments were designed for idiosyncratic defaults in a boom, not for a common shock.

| Layer | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Pool | Loans aggregated and their cash flows assigned |
| Tranching | Seniority ordering of claims on the pool |
| Rating | Model-based estimate of tranche default probability |
| Failure | Correlation assumptions broke in the downturn |
| Lesson | Model risk in the tail is systemic, not idiosyncratic |
