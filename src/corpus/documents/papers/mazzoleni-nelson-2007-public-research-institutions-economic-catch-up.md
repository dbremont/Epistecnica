## Notes

> The central claim is institutional: economic catch-up is not produced by private firms alone, and it is not simply a matter of importing machinery or codified technology. **Public research institutions** — universities, public laboratories, agricultural experiment stations and similar bodies — have been central where latecomers built technological capability, because they create knowledge, train researchers and engineers, solve problems that firms cannot solve alone, and diffuse what they learn into the wider economy.

> Mazzoleni and Nelson read that claim through historical cases of catch-up and institution-building, including the United States, Germany, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Brazil, with particular attention to agricultural research and to the relation between public science and industrial learning. The paper is a corrective to innovation accounts that treat public research as a peripheral input and to catch-up accounts that treat institutions as background conditions rather than as the thing being built.

> For the RIKEN corpus the paper supplies the general argument that the institute's history illustrates: a public research institution can be a catch-up institution, not merely a prestige laboratory. The Riken Konzern, the chief-scientist system and the later large-scale facilities are different answers to the same problem — how a latecomer builds, retains and diffuses research capability.
