## Notes

> The problem is the one post-training creates for itself. Reinforcement learning is how a modern model is pushed on a *specific* capability — mathematics, code, tool use, instruction following — and specialised RL produces specialised models that are each good at one thing and worse at the rest. Putting the capabilities back into one model is the hard part, and the existing answers all fail in a specific way:

| Method | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| **Off-policy finetune** on teacher outputs | The student is trained on states the *teacher* visited; at inference it visits its own, so errors compound (exposure bias) |
| **Mix-RL** — one RL run over the union of domain rewards | The rewards interfere; optimising the mixture degrades the individual capabilities |
| **Cascade RL** — sequentially on each domain | Later domains overwrite earlier ones; the sequence order becomes a hyperparameter with no principled setting |
| **Parameter merging** | Combines weights without combining behaviour; the merged model is often worse than any parent on every domain |

> **MOPD** separates the stages. First train one RL teacher per domain, independently — they never interact. Then distill all of them into the student **on the student's own rollouts**: at each step the student generates, the teachers score the state the student actually reached, and the student is pulled toward the teachers' distributions there. That is the DAgger fix for exposure bias, applied at the scale of whole domains, and it produces a **dense** signal (a distribution per token) rather than the sparse reward of RL. On Qwen3-30B-A3B it outperforms all four baselines and inherits nearly all of each teacher's capability.

> Its structural consequence is the part worth keeping: because the teachers are trained independently and combined only at distillation, capability development becomes **parallel and modular**. Domains can be added without retraining the others, and the integration step is separate from the specialisation step — a division of labour the monolithic RL run cannot express.

> The caveat: a single base-model family at one size (a 30B mixture-of-experts), benchmark-measured "inheritance", and a cost profile that pays for k teachers plus the distillation pass. It is a strong result about *how* to compose, demonstrated on one composition.
