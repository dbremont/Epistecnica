## Notes

> A reconstruction of Soviet labour productivity over the whole Soviet period, built to separate three regimes that are usually conflated: the pre-war build-up, the war and its demographic shock, and the postwar recovery and slowdown. Harrison's series shows the shape that the growth-accounting literature has since confirmed — rapid productivity growth while the economy was absorbing surplus labour from agriculture and while wartime destruction was being repaired, then a decline in the growth rate once those sources were exhausted.

> The methodological point matters for the corpus: the "Soviet slowdown" is not a single event but a transition between sources of growth. The early gains came from reallocating labour and capital to industry and from recovery; the later stagnation came when the same institutions had to generate growth from technical change, which is exactly the margin the planning system handled worst. The paper is therefore the empirical bridge between the institutional accounts and the long-run technical-change analysis.
