## Notes

> A formal model of the technology gap between a innovating North and a imitating South. New products are introduced in the North; over time their production migrates to the South as the technology becomes standardised and the wage advantage dominates; the North must keep innovating to sustain its income. The steady state is a **moving frontier**: the composition of trade turns over continuously even though the aggregate pattern is stable.

> The model is the cleanest statement of the proposition that technical leadership is a *flow*, not a stock. A country that stops generating new products loses the high-wage activities to the imitators, however good its existing technology. Read against the Soviet case, it gives the growth-accounting result a trade-theoretic form: the system that could adopt and diffuse an existing technology but could not generate the next one was structurally exposed, and its income position depended on a frontier it was not moving.

| Element | Content |
| --- | --- |
| North | Innovator: introduces new products, pays higher wages |
| South | Imitator: takes over standardised products, competes on cost |
| Transfer | The technology becomes routine and production relocates |
| Steady state | A constant *rate* of innovation, not a constant set of products |
| Implication | Income leadership requires continuous innovation, not a technology stock |

> The corpus keeps this as the general model; the Soviet note uses it to frame the technology-transfer and innovation-failure literature rather than as a claim about Soviet trade itself.
