## Notes

> The paper asks how a command economy organised invention, and answers with a case study of Soviet jet propulsion before and during the Second World War. The institutional form was neither a market nor a pure hierarchy: design bureaus and research institutes competed for resources, sponsorship and the attention of the political leadership, and the resulting allocation behaved like a **quasi-market** — rival projects, patrons, and a currency of political priority rather than price.

> Harrison's finding is that the quasi-market could mobilise effort and generate inventions, but selected them badly. Patronage rewarded projects that promised spectacular results rather than incremental reliability, duplication was endemic, and the transition from a working prototype to a producible engine was the weak link. The paper is the best short account of why Soviet R&D was strong at the invention end and weak at the diffusion end — the asymmetry that the growth literature treats as the core of the slowdown.

> **Caveat on the year.** The requested reference gives 2004; the volume (23) and the DOI's year segment place the article in 2005. The entry records the volume year and leaves the discrepancy visible rather than silently choosing.
