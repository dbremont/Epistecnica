## Notes

> An empirical test of whether the West African Monetary Zone — the English-speaking countries outside the CFA franc — meets the conditions for a monetary union. The paper examines nominal and real convergence, trade integration, the symmetry of shocks, and the readiness of the institutional framework, and concludes that the zone is not yet ripe: convergence is partial, the shocks are not sufficiently symmetric, and the fiscal and statistical infrastructure the union would require is uneven.

> The finding is the empirical version of the argument that a monetary union is a **political commitment with economic preconditions**, not a technical arrangement that can be adopted when the numbers look close. It is the counterpart to the official convergence criteria, which measure the nominal variables but not the symmetry that makes a common monetary policy bearable.
