## Notes

> A study of the social and institutional conditions under which Japanese nuclear physics developed before the war, with RIKEN and Nishina's laboratory at its centre. Hirosige is interested in the research school as a social form: who could get instruments, who could travel, how a laboratory reproduced itself, and how the boundary between pure physics and military application was negotiated.

> The article is the necessary complement to the institutional histories of RIKEN. It explains why Nishina's laboratory could become the Japanese centre of nuclear physics, and why the same laboratory was drawn into the wartime uranium project. It is reprinted in the Nakayama–Swain–Yagi collection.
