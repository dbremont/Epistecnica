## Notes

> The best empirical account of what FDI actually did to Polish industry, built on a survey of **more than 550 automotive suppliers** plus company interviews. The finding is more interesting than the headline: foreign assemblers became embedded in supply networks **they largely created themselves**, and local suppliers were pulled into a Europe-wide production system — but the upgrading is **asymmetric**.

**What upgraded, and what did not:**

| Competence | Upgrading |
| --- | --- |
| Product quality | Strong |
| Cost efficiency | Strong |
| Adaptability and fast response | Strong |
| **R&D and design** | **Far weaker** |
| **Local brands and own intellectual property** | **Thin** |

> The mechanism the paper names is **localized capabilities**: competences produced by the dynamic interplay between the foreign firms' activity and the changing local environment — suppliers, labour, institutions, infrastructure. Because those capabilities are specific to the place, they become elements of the firm's **sunk costs**, and that is what embeds the production system: the investment is hard to move because the capability is not portable.

> The constraint is stated plainly and it is the part worth keeping: dependence on decisions and innovations from abroad, plus limited local design and brands, may **cap** the semi-peripheral role Poland can reach. The same FDI that produced the catch-up may also set its ceiling — which is the firm-level version of [Weresa's](/view.html?p=documents/papers/weresa-2004-can-fdi-help-poland-catch-up-with-the-eu.md) sector qualification.
