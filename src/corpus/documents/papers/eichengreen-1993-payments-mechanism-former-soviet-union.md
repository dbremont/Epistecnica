## Notes

> Written while the ruble zone was dissolving, the paper asks whether the **European Payments Union** of 1950–1958 offers a workable model for a payments mechanism among the former Soviet republics. The EPU cleared bilateral balances multilaterally through a central account, extended limited credit to deficit members, and made the transition from bilateral clearing to convertible currencies gradual. Eichengreen's answer is that the precedent is relevant but partial.

> The relevant part is the technique: a clearing union can keep trade flowing when the members lack convertible currencies and cannot yet settle bilaterally. The partial part is the politics — the EPU worked because the members were committed to liberalisation and because the United States underwrote the system through the Marshall Plan. A post-Soviet clearing union would have lacked both, and the paper's conclusion is correspondingly cautious.

| Element | EPU (1950–58) | Post-Soviet case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clearing | Multilateral through the union | Would need a new clearing institution |
| Credit | Limited, conditional | No equivalent underwriter |
| Path | Gradual convertibility | Collapse of the ruble zone was immediate |
| Politics | Shared liberalisation commitment | Diverging national interests |
