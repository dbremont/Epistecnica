## Notes

> A policy paper arguing that African integration should begin with a **payments union** rather than a monetary union. The distinction is the argument: a payments union clears trade balances multilaterally and extends short-term credit to deficit members, while leaving each country its own currency and its own monetary policy. It is the Keynesian clearing-union design, scaled to a region that is not an optimum currency area.

> Pettifor draws the lesson from the European experience in both directions: the European Payments Union of 1950–58 is the model that worked, and the euro is the model that imposed a single monetary policy on economies that had not converged. The proposal is a way to capture the trade gains of integration without the adjustment costs of a common currency — and a way to avoid subordinating African monetary policy to an external anchor.

| Element | Payments union | Monetary union |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Currencies | National currencies retained | Single currency |
| Adjustment | Exchange rates remain available | Internal adjustment only |
| Credit | Short-term clearing credit | No equivalent |
| Politics | Coordination without surrender | Sovereignty pooled |
