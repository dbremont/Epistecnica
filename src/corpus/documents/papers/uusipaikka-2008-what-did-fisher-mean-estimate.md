## Notes

> A historical and methodological paper on what R. A. Fisher meant by “estimate”. Uusipaikka argues that Fisher's method of maximum likelihood was not, in Fisher's own understanding, a procedure for producing a point estimate: it was a procedure for constructing **likelihood intervals or regions** from the likelihood function, and an estimate was the statistic that summarised the evidence, not a single number to be plugged in.

> The paper matters to the corpus because it separates two things that later textbook statistics merged: the **estimate** as a statistic with a distribution, and the **estimate** as a point guess. Fisher's insistence on the former is the origin of the interval-first reading of inference, and it explains why his later work on fiducial inference and on the design of experiments is continuous with the 1922 paper rather than a departure from it.

| Reading | What “estimate” means |
| --- | --- |
| Fisher (as argued here) | A statistic; the construction of a likelihood interval or region |
| Later textbook | A point value, with a separate confidence interval |
| Consequence | Inference is interval-first, not point-first |
