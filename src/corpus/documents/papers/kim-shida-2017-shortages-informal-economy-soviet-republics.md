## Notes

> A quantitative account of the second economy across the Soviet republics in the last quarter-century of the USSR. Kim and Shida use household survey evidence — the Soviet interview and budget data — to estimate the size of informal activity and to relate it to the incidence of shortage, region by region and over time.

> The finding that matters for the corpus is the **regional variation**: shortage and informal activity were not uniform across the union, and the republics differed in how much of the shortfall the second economy absorbed. That variation is the empirical link between the institutional theory of shortage and the political economy of the union's disintegration — where the informal economy was larger and the plan less able to deliver, the system's legitimacy was thinner.

> Note the correction to the requested citation: the article appears in volume 70, issue 4 (not issue 1), at pages 1346–1374.

| Variable | What the paper measures |
| --- | --- |
| Shortage | The gap between planned allocation and realised household consumption |
| Informal economy | Private-gain activity outside the plan, estimated from survey data |
| Regional variation | Differences across the Soviet republics, 1965–89 |
| Implication | The second economy as an absorber of shortage, unevenly distributed |
