## Notes

> An empirical assessment of whether the existing African monetary unions — the CFA franc zones — should be widened into a larger regional union. The paper applies the **optimum currency area** apparatus to African data: trade integration, symmetry of shocks, factor mobility, fiscal transfers, and the credibility of the common central bank, and asks which countries would gain and which would lose from joining.

> The result is a qualified yes for some candidates and a no for others, with the qualification doing the work. The gains from a common currency depend on the depth of trade with the union and on the symmetry of the shocks the members face; the costs fall on countries whose economies are dissimilar and whose adjustment would have to run through wages and migration rather than the exchange rate. The paper is the quantitative counterpart to the political argument for a wider African monetary union.
