## Notes

> A policy paper on the **plumbing** of European finance: the integration of capital markets, the supervision architecture, the savings-and-investment union, and the specific measures a legislator could take rather than the single-market rhetoric. Its value for the corpus is that it names the steps and their order — which is the difference between a strategy document and a legislative programme.

> The recurring obstacle it has to address is political rather than technical: the single market in capital requires **common supervision and a common safe asset**, and both transfer authority that member states have kept. The paper's argument is that the cost of not acting — a fragmented market that cannot finance the scale of the transition — is now larger than the sovereignty it protects.

> Read against the corpus's [productive policy](/view.html?p=notes/social/collective/governance/action/instrument/policy/productive-policy.md) notes, this is the **finance-side** of industrial policy: a capital-market union is the instrument that lets European savings fund European firms, which is what industrial policy needs when it stops subsidising and starts financing.
