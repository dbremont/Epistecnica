## Notes

> A business-history account of the Riken Konzern, written against the older view that treated the group as a simple fund-raising device for the institute. Saito reconstructs how the companies were formed, how Okochi governed them, and how the group related to the institute's research budget. The article is the best short account of RIKEN's industrial organisation as an organisational form, not just a list of subsidiaries.

> It is also a corrective to the RIKEN legend: the Konzern was not a peripheral commercial adventure but the financial condition that allowed the institute to keep doing research. At its height the group had more than sixty companies, and its profits supplied most of RIKEN's research budget in the late 1930s.
