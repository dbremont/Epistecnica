## Notes

> A study of the Lombardo-Venetian Kingdom under Habsburg rule between the Congress of Vienna and the second war of Italian independence. Pichler reconstructs the Austrian administration's economic policy — tariffs, infrastructure, agriculture, credit — and asks whether Lombardy's relatively advanced economy grew *because of* or *despite* the imperial framework.

> The answer is qualified: the Habsburg state provided order, a customs union with the rest of the monarchy, and investment in roads and waterways, but its fiscal demands and its protection of Austrian industry constrained Lombard manufacturing. The article is a case of the core–periphery relation inside the empire, and of the difficulty of separating the effects of policy from the effects of geography and prior endowments.
