## Notes

> A compact statement of how the shadow banking system creates **leverage and maturity mismatch** outside the regulated banking sector, and why that matters for regulation. Adrian and Shin describe the chain: loans are originated, packaged into securities, funded in the repo market, and held by institutions whose balance sheets expand and contract with the value of the collateral. The system performs a banking function — maturity transformation — without the deposit insurance and the lender of last resort that make banking stable.

> The paper's analytical contribution is the **balance-sheet view of leverage**. When asset prices rise, the collateral supports more borrowing and leverage is procyclical; when prices fall, margin calls force deleveraging, which pushes prices down further. The instability is built into the funding structure, not into the character of the participants, and the regulatory implication is that capital and liquidity rules must follow the activity rather than the institution's charter.

| Feature | Shadow banking | Traditional banking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Funding | Repo and commercial paper | Deposits |
| Backstop | None | Lender of last resort, insurance |
| Leverage | Procyclical, collateral-driven | Regulated |
| Run | Collateral run | Deposit run |
