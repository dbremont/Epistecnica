## Notes

> A proposal for the measurement infrastructure that macroprudential regulation needs. The authors argue that the crisis was not only a failure of models but a failure of **data**: the supervisors could not see where risk was accumulating because the relevant exposures were off balance sheet, in the shadow system, and reported only in aggregate. “Risk topography” is the programme of mapping those exposures — who holds what, at what maturity, against what collateral — in something close to real time.

> The paper is a design document rather than an empirical study. Its contribution is to specify what a systemic-risk regulator would have to know: the maturity mismatch of each sector, the collateral chains, the concentration of exposures, and the prices at which positions could be liquidated. The proposal anticipates the later work on systemic risk dashboards and on the measurement of shadow-banking leverage.

| Requirement | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Sectoral balance sheets | To locate the maturity mismatch |
| Collateral chains | To see where a run would transmit |
| Exposure concentration | To identify the nodes that matter |
| Liquidation prices | To distinguish solvency from fire-sale illiquidity |
