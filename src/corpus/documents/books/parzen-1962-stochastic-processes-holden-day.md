## Notes

> Holden-Day's probability-series text: the general theory of stochastic processes built from Markov chains outward — stationarity, the spectral density, and estimation — rather than from measure theory inward.

The order of the book is its argument. It starts with the notion of a stochastic process and with second-order (covariance) descriptions, goes to the Markov chain and the Markov process, and only then to stationarity and spectral analysis, closing with estimation and hypothesis testing for random functions. A reader gets the working machinery before the measure-theoretic foundations, which is why the book remains usable as a first text and why it is still cited for the classical treatment of stationary processes and their spectra.
