## Notes

> The standard anatomy of the Soviet economy as it actually operated, written by the economist who did most to make the subject readable. Nove describes the planning hierarchy — Gosplan, the ministries, the enterprise — the rules of the game that governed each level, and the gap between the formal plan and the informal practices that made it work.

> Its chapters on the enterprise are the best short account of the incentive structure that [Berliner](/view.html?p=documents/books/berliner-1957-factory-manager-ussr-harvard.md) documented in detail: the bonus attached to gross output, the ratchet, the hoarding of inputs, the resistance to innovation, and the central role of the *tolkach* and of informal supply networks. Nove's judgement is neither apology nor denunciation; the system is described as a working machine with characteristic strengths (mobilisation, full employment, rapid extensive growth) and characteristic failures (quality, innovation, shortage).

| Layer | What Nove describes |
| --- | --- |
| Gosplan and the ministries | Target setting, balancing, and the annual plan cycle |
| The enterprise | The director's incentives, the bonus system, and informal adjustment |
| Agriculture | The collective farm, the private plot, and the procurement system |
| Investment | The priority sectors, the construction lag, and the unfinished-investment problem |
| Reform | The Kosygin reforms and the limits of decentralisation within the plan |

> Nove's book is the descriptive baseline against which the analytical accounts — Kornai's shortage theory, Harrison's agency model — can be read.
