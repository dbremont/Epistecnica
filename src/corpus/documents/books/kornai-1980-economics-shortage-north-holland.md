## Notes

> Kornai's two-volume treatise is the founding statement of the **shortage economy**: a system in which the binding constraint is not demand but supply, and in which firms and households routinely queue, wait, hoard and substitute because the goods they want are unavailable. The book's central mechanism is the **soft budget constraint** — the state's willingness to bail out a loss-making enterprise through subsidies, soft credit and administrative price adjustment — which removes the financial consequence of failure and therefore removes the incentive to adjust.

> The consequence is a systematic **quantity drive**. Because the firm cannot be made to bear the cost of over-spending, it demands more inputs than it needs, hoards them, and invests until the investment is absorbed by shortages elsewhere. Shortage is not an allocation error that better planning would fix; it is the equilibrium behaviour of an economy in which the budget constraint does not bind. Kornai's later work — [Growth, Shortage and Efficiency](/view.html?p=documents/books/kornai-1982-growth-shortage-efficiency-california.md) and [The Socialist System](/view.html?p=documents/books/kornai-1992-socialist-system-princeton.md) — turns the diagnosis into a macro model and then into a general theory of the socialist system.

| Concept | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Soft budget constraint** | The enterprise's survival does not depend on revenue; losses are absorbed by the state |
| **Shortage** | Excess demand at administered prices, persistent rather than transient |
| **Quantity drive** | The firm's drive to expand output and inputs, unconstrained by profitability |
| **Investment hunger** | The same drive at the level of capital allocation, producing a permanent investment boom |
| **Paternalism** | The state as owner treats firms as children: protects, disciplines, and cannot let them fail |

> The book is also the clearest statement of the corpus's distinction between a **planned** economy and a **shortage** economy: planning is the instrument, shortage is the outcome, and the two are not the same claim.
