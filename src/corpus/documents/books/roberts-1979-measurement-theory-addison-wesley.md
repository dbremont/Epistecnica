## Notes

The standard monograph of the representational theory, written for readers who
will use it: the numerical representations are derived, the uniqueness theorems
are stated with their admissible transformations, and the applications to
decision-making and the social sciences are worked rather than gestured at.

It sits exactly one level below [Quantification](/view.html?p=notes/epistemica/measurement/quantification.md).
That note decomposes the *scheme* — a scale, a unit, a numeral — and takes the
four scale levels as given. Roberts supplies the machinery under them: the
algebraic axioms of a qualitative structure, the homomorphism into the reals
that licenses treating the structure as numbers, and the uniqueness of that
homomorphism up to the class of transformations that defines the scale (ordinal
→ monotone, interval → affine, ratio → similarity). "Interval scale" stops being
a label and becomes a theorem.

Two things it fixes that the note's readers will want:

- **Meaningfulness.** Which numerical statements carry empirical content is
  settled by uniqueness, not by convention: statements invariant under every
  admissible transformation are the meaningful ones. This is why `42 °C` and
  `42 K` admit different operations.
- **The set of unit systems is not arbitrary.** Where the note asserts that an
  external unit fixes what the numeral counts in multiples of, Roberts shows
  the conditions under which an additive unit — and hence a concatenation
  operation — can exist at all, and what happens to the social sciences when it
  cannot.
