## Notes

The *Istorie fiorentine* was commissioned in 1520 by Cardinal Giulio de' Medici, the future Clement VII, and it is the strangest commission in Renaissance letters: a history of Florence written by the man the Medici had tortured and exiled, for the family that had returned to power. Machiavelli took the money and wrote a republican analysis anyway — the book's subject is not the Medici but the *disorder* that made them possible.

> Its method is the one the *Discourses* had already set out. Florentine history is the record of two humours, the nobles' and the people's, and the republic's instability follows from the unresolved contest between them; the factions, the exiles, the foreign interventions are symptoms of a constitution that never solved the question of who holds power. It is the first city history written as an analysis of internal conflict rather than as a chronicle of deeds.

> The book runs from the fall of the Roman Empire to the death of Lorenzo the Magnificent in 1492, in eight books. It was published posthumously in 1532, five years after the sack of Rome and the fall of the last Florentine republic Machiavelli had served.

> Read beside [Italy](/view.html?p=notes/social/koinomia/polity/instance/ita/italy.md), it is a source for the political economy of the city-states as much as for the events of Florence: the relation of the *arti*, the guilds, to the *signoria*, the use of foreign mercenaries, and the fragility of a republic that could not arm its own people.
