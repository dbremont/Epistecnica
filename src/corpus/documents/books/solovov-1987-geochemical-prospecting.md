## Notes

> The systematic account of **geochemical prospecting** from the Soviet school: the dispersion halo as the object of search, and the three media in which it is read — **lithochemical** (soil and rock), **hydrochemical** (stream and ground water), and **biogeochemical** (plants). The method is to sample a medium, measure the trace-element pattern, and interpret the anomaly against a background established for the terrain.

> The book's value is that it is *operational*: sampling density and grid design, the choice of indicator elements for a target type, the analytical methods available, the treatment of threshold and background, and the ways an anomaly misleads (transported overburden, contamination, a halo displaced from its source). It is the technique behind every geochemical survey map.

> Read beside [Lindgren](/view.html?p=documents/books/lindgren-1919-mineral-deposits.md) and [Kužvart & Böhmer](/view.html?p=documents/books/kuzvart-bohmer-1978-prospecting-exploration-mineral-deposits.md), it is the **trace layer**: the classification tells you what the deposit would look like, the manual tells you how to prove one, and this book tells you how to see the chemical footprint of one that is buried.
