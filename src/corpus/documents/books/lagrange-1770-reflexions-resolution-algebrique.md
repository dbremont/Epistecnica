## Notes

> The Berlin memoir in which the theory of equations was reorganized around its own mechanism: Lagrange's *Réflexions* — read to the academy across 1770–1771 — take every earlier solution of the algebraic equation (del Ferro, Cardano, Ferrari, the elimination arts), read them all as instances of one principle, and state the principle: an equation is solved through auxiliary equations whose roots are combinations of the intended roots, and the solvability question reduces to how many distinct values such combinations can take under permutation of the roots. The resolvent program is born here — the direction the century's algebra traveled to Galois, and the analysis that explained *why* the cubic and the quartic yield to radicals before returning the quintic unanswered.

> The scanned-and-transcribed text below is the **Œuvres rendering** (Gauthier-Villars, 1868), the wikisource transcription of the third volume under its *Mémoires extraits des recueils de l'Académie royale de Berlin* part — the original Berlin printing lies behind the academy volume; the corpus's exposition works the memoir's cubic case through its arithmetic.
