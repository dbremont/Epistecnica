## Notes

> The book that made the Soviet enterprise legible as an organisation rather than a box in a plan. Berliner, working from interviews with former Soviet managers and engineers who had emigrated, reconstructed how a factory director actually behaved under a target set from above: the informal network of suppliers and protectors, the "pusher" (*tolkach*) who obtained scarce inputs, the reciprocal favours that kept a plant running, and the reporting practices that made the plan appear to be met.

> Its analytical contribution is the claim that the manager's problem is not production but **information and insurance**. The plan assigns output in physical units, the bonus depends on meeting it, and the manager's rational response is to understate capacity, hoard inputs, resist innovation that threatens the current target, and negotiate the target downward — the "ratchet". These behaviours are not corruption of the system; they are the system working as its incentives require.

| Theme | What Berliner documents |
| --- | --- |
| Target setting | The annual bargaining over the plan, and the ratchet that punishes a good year with a harder one |
| Input supply | Hoarding, *tolkachi*, and informal exchange between enterprises |
| Reporting | Falsification and the gap between the reported and the actual output mix |
| Innovation | Resistance to new products, because a new product resets the learning curve and threatens the bonus |

> Read with [Kornai](/view.html?p=documents/books/kornai-1980-economics-shortage-north-holland.md), Berliner supplies the firm-level behaviour that Kornai's macro theory of shortage predicts; read with [Harrison](/view.html?p=documents/papers/harrison-1998-prices-planners-producers-agency-problem.md), it supplies the agency problem that Harrison formalises.
