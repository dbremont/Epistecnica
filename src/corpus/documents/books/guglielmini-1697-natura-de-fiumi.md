## Notes

> Domenico Guglielmini's treatise set the river, not the conduit, as the object: the relation of slope to velocity and bed, the sizing of a section by its discharge and fall, scour, sediment and the equilibrium a stream strikes between its gradient and its banks. Written for the Bologna institute whose first chair of hydraulics he held, it is the founding physical-mathematical treatise of river engineering — the discipline the Italian canal engineers worked from before Bernoulli and du Buat.

> The 1697 Bologna edition is scanned, and a 1821 printing of the tract beside it.
