## Notes

> The English translation of Fourcroy's *Élémens d'histoire naturelle et de chimie* — the treatise that carried the chemistry and natural history of the 1780s–90s into one bound course — made from its **fourth and last French edition**, in four volumes, by **R. Heron** (London: J. Murray and S. Highley; Edinburgh: G. Mudie and Son, 1796). It is the standard English rendering of the work: the treatise chemistry students in the anglophone world could read directly, in it the *Philosophy of Chemistry* — the work's conceptual opening — prefixed before the chemistry proper.

> The French original's third edition, the *Élémens …* of 1789 (the treatise's *tome cinquième* among its volumes), was translated separately in 1790 — a two-volume edition for which the translator is not named in the record; the fourth edition's Heron translation is the run through which the English world generally met Fourcroy. Whether the fifth tome's natural-history branch maps whole into Heron's four volumes, given the French fifth volume's 438 pages, is left open above.

This entry covers the **Heron translation, 1796**, all four volumes scanned: the archive.org copies are linked per volume below.
