## Notes

> Locke's fifth chapter answers a question the corpus note lists among its QA: by what right does anything become anyone's? His answer is labour — a man owns his own person and his work, so mixing his labour with a thing removes it from the common state in which nature left it.

The argument is doing two jobs at once. It grounds property before government, which is what makes government's protection of it a duty rather than a grant; and it sets a limit, since one may take only what leaves "enough and as good" for others. The later chapters convert that limit into a proviso no modern economy satisfies.

For the corpus note, Locke is the labour justification as a position rather than a slogan, and the reason the note's justification question cannot be answered by description: the incidents describe what a property right is, and none of them says why it should exist.
