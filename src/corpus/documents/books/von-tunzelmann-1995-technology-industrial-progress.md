## Notes

> The book asks the question the growth literature usually assumes away: **is technology the cause of industrial progress or its symptom?** Von Tunzelmann sets the theoretical answers side by side — the neoclassical residual, the evolutionary and Schumpeterian accounts, the demand-pull and technology-push positions, the long-wave and Kondratieff readings — and then tests them against the empirical record of the major industrialising nations, industry by industry and period by period.

> Its value is exactly that it is a **survey rather than a thesis**. Where a note in this corpus needs to know what the evidence supports and what it does not — whether the British lead was science, skills, markets or empire; whether the American system of manufactures was a technology or an organisation; whether catch-up is imitation or a different path — this is the volume that says what is settled and what is contested, with the sources attached.

> The limits are the obverse of the virtue: the survey is dense, the position is cautious to the point of reticence, and the 1995 vantage predates the endogenous-growth formalisation that absorbed much of the question. Read as a **map of the evidence** rather than as a theory to adopt.
