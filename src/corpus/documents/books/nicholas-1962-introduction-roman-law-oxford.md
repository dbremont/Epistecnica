## Notes

> Nicholas sets out the Roman vocabulary the modern law still uses: *dominium* as the owner's relation to the thing, *possessio* as factual control protected in its own right, *usufructus* as use and enjoyment without disposition, and *servitus* as a burden on another's land that travels with the land rather than the person.

The distinctions are not antiquarian. The separation of ownership from possession is what allows a thief to be protected against a stranger; the separation of usufruct from ownership is what allows an object to support two holders at once; the servitude is the ancestor of every easement and covenant that runs with land.

The corpus note's title branch is essentially this vocabulary, and the note's opening claim — that the same field can carry ownership, lease, easement and mortgage held by four parties — is a Roman arrangement before it is a modern one.
