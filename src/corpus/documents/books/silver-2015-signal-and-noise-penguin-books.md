## Intro

> Silver treats prediction as the disciplined separation of signal from
> noise: the forecaster's problem is not to eliminate uncertainty but to
> represent it honestly. The book moves through elections, baseball,
> weather, earthquakes, economics, terrorism, poker, chess, and climate,
> reading each as a case of the same failure modes — overfitting, base-rate
> neglect, narrative bias, and the confusion of confidence with accuracy —
> and argues that Bayesian updating, explicit error, and feedback are what
> separate forecasts that improve from forecasts that merely sound certain.

### Which are the feedbakcs loops on the econonomy?

| Feedback loop | Causal mechanism | Economic consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Credit–asset price | Credit → asset prices → collateral → credit | Financial booms and busts |
| Investment–growth | Investment → productive capacity → output → profits → investment | Economic expansion |
| Income–consumption | Income → consumption → aggregate demand → production → income | Demand-driven growth |
| Wage–price | Wages → production costs → prices → wage demands | Inflationary dynamics |
| Productivity–investment | Investment → productivity → profitability → investment | Long-term growth |
| Inventory–production | Inventory → production decisions → supply → inventory | Business-cycle fluctuations |
| Expectations–investment | Optimism → investment → economic growth → optimism | Self-reinforcing booms |
| Debt–deflation | Falling prices → rising real debt burden → defaults → credit contraction → falling prices | Debt-deflation crisis |
