## Notes

> The engineer's manual of French hydraulics in the first half of the nineteenth century. Aubuisson de Voisins covers the whole field as it stood before the mathematisation of the later century: the flow of water in channels and pipes, the measurement of discharge, the design of canals and aqueducts, the machinery of water-raising — pumps, water wheels, turbines — and the practical rules that linked the theory of Bernoulli and du Buat to the dimensions an engineer had to build.

> The book is a good index of what an engineer was expected to know in 1840: the physics is the eighteenth-century one, the coefficients are empirical, and the authority is the accumulated practice of the ponts et chaussées rather than the laboratory. It sits in the corpus as the **hydraulic** counterpart to the machine and structural manuals of the same decades, and it documents the state of the art immediately before the great nineteenth-century advances in turbines and pipe flow.

| Part | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Flow in channels and pipes | Uniform flow, resistance, and the empirical formulas in use |
| Discharge measurement | Weirs, orifices, and the practical gauging of water |
| Water-raising machinery | Pumps, wheels, and the transmission of water power |
| Canals and supply | The design of channels, aqueducts and urban supply |
