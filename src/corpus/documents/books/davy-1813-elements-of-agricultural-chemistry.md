## Notes

> The book that made agricultural chemistry a discipline with a syllabus: Humphry Davy's lectures to the Board of Agriculture, arranging soils, manures, and the plant's nutrition under the new chemistry of his own making. Published from a manuscript the Board held for seven years, it established the genre — the chemical survey of the field — that Stöckhardt's field lectures and Johnson's treatises would later carry to the working farmer.

> The 1813 first edition is scanned twice below; the 1815 and 1821 printings show the demand it met through the decade before Liebig rewrote its chemistry.
