## Notes

The 1990 volume is the crop-level extension of Thornley's 1976 treatise, and the textbook that trained a generation of process modellers. It starts from the individual plant — light interception, photosynthesis, respiration, translocation, water relations, mineral nitrogen — and then assembles the canopy: the crop as a stand of plants exchanging light, water and nitrogen with the soil and the atmosphere, with the state variables and rate laws written explicitly enough to be integrated numerically.

> Its contribution is the **method made canonical**: a modelling grammar of state variables, rate equations, and the calibration data each equation needs, applied consistently from leaf to canopy. That is what makes the book the reference for the mechanistic crop-model tradition rather than a model of one crop.

> The distinction that later work had to confront is already visible here: the model extrapolates through mechanism, but the parameters are many and the data to estimate them are few. The book states the problem rather than hiding it.
