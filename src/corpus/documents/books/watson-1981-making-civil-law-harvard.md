## Notes

> Watson's argument is that legal systems change mainly by borrowing: rules, categories and whole institutions are transplanted from one system to another, and the borrowing is rarely driven by the social conditions that produced the original.

The civil law is his central case. The Roman categories survived the empire that made them, were recovered in the medieval universities, and were received into the codes of states whose societies had little in common with the one that produced them. Property law is the clearest instance: *dominium*, possession, usufruct and servitude reappear across jurisdictions that never shared a legal practice.

This is the mechanism behind the corpus note's contextual-robustness row. The concept travels well not because it is natural but because it is portable, and portability is a property of the vocabulary rather than of the societies that receive it.
