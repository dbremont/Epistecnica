## Notes

> Behaviour in a digital environment is regulated by four modalities at once: law, social norms, the market, and architecture. Code belongs to the fourth. What a system makes possible, makes costly, or makes impossible regulates as effectively as a statute, and does so without a court, a police force, or a published rule.

The book's argument is about cyberspace generally, but its most durable use in this corpus is narrower: in a digital register the architecture is the enforcement apparatus. Exclusion is executed by an access check rather than by a bailiff; transfer is a signed message rather than a deed; and the sanction for a bad transfer is a rejected transaction rather than an action in court.

Read against property, that substitution cuts both ways. It makes holdings cheap to protect and cheap to verify, which is what the tokenization literature promises; and it moves the real constitution of the holding into a technical artefact that the holder does not control and cannot litigate against in the ordinary way.
