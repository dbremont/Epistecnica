## Notes

A late-nineteenth-century laboratory manual: the period instrument of the
quantification scheme, before the scheme had a name. Kohlrausch sets out, method
by method, how a magnitude is actually bound to a number — the instrument, the
observing procedure, the correction terms, the care of the standard, the
combination of individual errors.

Read against [Quantification](/view.html?p=notes/epistemica/quantification/quantification.md),
it is the *practice* side of the note. The note abstracts the scheme to a scale,
a unit and a numeral; Kohlrausch shows what each of those costs. A unit is a
physical object kept somewhere and compared against, so the numeral's accuracy
turns on the care of that object. A scale's admissible operations are constrained
by what the instrument can do to the observable. And the uncertainty that the
note treats formally arrives here as worked arithmetic on the corrections —
what the [International Vocabulary of Metrology](/view.html?p=notes/epistemica/quantification/isu.md)
would later call the definitional uncertainty of a realized unit.

Worth reading for the contrast it draws with the modern system: the 1894 manual
realizes units through artefacts and reproducible experiments, whereas after
2019 every SI base unit is fixed by an exact value of a constant of nature (see
[International System of Units](/view.html?p=notes/epistemica/quantification/isu.md)).
The procedure changes; the structure the note decomposes does not.
