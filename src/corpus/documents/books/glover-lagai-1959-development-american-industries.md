## Notes

> An edited volume with one chapter per American industry, each tracing the sector's technology, organisation and economic weight together. It is the **industry-as-unit** method: the unit of analysis is the sector, and the questions are the same across chapters — what the technology is, how production is organised, how the industry is financed and regulated, what it contributes to the economy.

> That uniformity is the book's value now. Because the chapters ask the same questions of each sector, the volume is a **comparative** record of American industrial structure in the mid-twentieth century, and it is one of the sources from which the corpus's own industry profiles — the product space, the actors, the technical decomposition — can be calibrated: a sector note that cannot say what the industry's economic significance was in 1959 is a note without a baseline.

> Its limits are those of its genre: it is descriptive and celebratory in tone, thin on failure and on labour, and its statistics are the ones the trade associations supplied. Read as a **photograph** of American industry at a moment rather than as an analysis of how it got there.
