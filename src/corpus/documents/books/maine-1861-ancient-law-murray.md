## Notes

> Maine's thesis is a movement: early societies are organised by status, and the progressive ones move toward contract — the individual's position ceasing to be fixed by the group he was born into and becoming something he can arrange.

Property belongs to that movement. In his account the earliest holding is collective and family-bound, and individual ownership emerges as the group's claims are separated, registered and made transferable. The famous sentence about progress "from status to contract" is therefore also a sentence about property: the thing held and the person holding it become distinct legal subjects.

The claim has been criticised for reading a Roman sequence as a universal law, and the corpus note's temporal-stability row registers that. It remains the account that made property a historical object rather than a natural one, which is what the note's history paragraph needs.
