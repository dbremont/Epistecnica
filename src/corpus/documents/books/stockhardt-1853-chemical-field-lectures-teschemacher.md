## Notes

> Julius Adolph Stöckhardt — the Saxon agricultural chemist whose *Chemische Feldpredigten* ("field sermons") were written, deliberately, in the language of the field rather than the laboratory — read plainly to farmers what chemistry knew of soils, manures and crops. James E. Teschemacher's translation brought the form to America two years after the first Leipzig printing: the working farmer's agricultural chemistry, a generation after Davy's Board lectures and two decades before the experiment stations made it institutional.
