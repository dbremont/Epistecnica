## Notes

> The essay is the young Restrepo's survey of the province of Antioquia: its geography, its productions, its industries, and its population. It accompanied the first technically surveyed map of the province and was published in the *Semanario del Nuevo Reino de Granada* edited by Francisco José de Caldas.

> Its best-known argument concerns mining. Restrepo contrasts the ephemeral prosperity of the Spanish metal economies with the durable wealth of England and Holland, which he attributes to agriculture, the arts and commerce. The passage is an early statement of what later becomes the resource-curse thesis: a mineral endowment is not the same thing as a productive economy.

### The passage

> Las que producen las minas no deslumbran al ciudadano juicioso. Ningún pueblo ha conseguido con la explotación de los metales si no es una efímera prosperidad. Las ricas venas de plata de Méjico y del Perú, el oro y la platina del Nuevo Reino de Granada, de ningún modo han hecho más opulenta a la España, cuando antes de que las poseyese estaba al frente de las potencias europeas. Por el contrario, la Inglaterra y la Holanda han sido las naciones más ricas de la Europa, sostenidas únicamente por su agricultura, sus artes y su comercio: éstos son los verdaderos manantiales de la prosperidad; y éstos los que deben cultivar las naciones que pretenden figurar en el sistema político del mundo.

> **English gloss.** The mines do not dazzle the judicious citizen. No people has achieved anything but ephemeral prosperity through the exploitation of metals. The rich silver veins of Mexico and Peru, the gold and platinum of the New Kingdom of Granada, have by no means made Spain more opulent, when before possessing them it stood at the head of the European powers. On the contrary, England and Holland have been the richest nations of Europe, sustained solely by their agriculture, arts and commerce: these are the true sources of prosperity, and these are what nations aspiring to figure in the world's political system must cultivate.
