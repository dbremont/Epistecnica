## Notes

> The founding treatise of the drill-husbandry movement: Jethro Tull's argument that tillage — the frequent, minute pulverization of the soil between horse-drawn rows — feeds the plant by comminuting its food, and that the horse-hoe makes it labor-cheap. Its theory of plant nutrition reads wrong to every later eye, yet its practice reorganized English field culture, and its polemical voice set the register for a century of improvement literature.

> The copy scanned on archive.org is the second edition (1733); the fourth (1751) carries the author's addenda and his name, and the 1829 printing shows the work's afterlife in the new agricultural science. All are linked below.
