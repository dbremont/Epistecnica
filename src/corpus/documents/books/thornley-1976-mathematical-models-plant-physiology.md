## Notes

Thornley's book is the founding statement of plant physiology as a **modelling** discipline rather than a descriptive one. Its move is to write the plant as a system of state variables and rate equations — carbon fixed by photosynthesis, lost by respiration and translocation, allocated to growth, with water and mineral nitrogen as the other two pools — and then to ask what the equations imply rather than what the tissue looks like.

> The method it teaches is the one later crop models inherited: define the state variables, write the rate laws, calibrate against the few measurements available, and read the behaviour off the integrated system. It is a book about *how to build the model*, not a catalogue of models.

> The companion volume [Thornley & Johnson (1990)](/view.html?p=documents/books/thornley-johnson-1990-plant-and-crop-modelling.md) carries the same method up from the plant to the canopy and the crop.
