## Notes

> Commons writes property into the economy as a working arrangement rather than a legal ornament: what an economy is, he argues, depends on which transactions the law will enforce, and every transaction is a transfer of rights rather than of things.

His unit is the transaction — bargaining, managerial and rationing — and his claim is that classical economics took the legal framework for granted and then mistook its own abstractions for nature. Property in this account is not a static title but a going concern: a set of expectations the courts will protect, priced by the market and revised as the courts revise.

For the corpus note, Commons is the bridge between the legal decomposition and the economic one. The incidents branch says what the relation contains; Commons says why the content matters — because investment, credit and price all rest on expectations that the regime will hold.
