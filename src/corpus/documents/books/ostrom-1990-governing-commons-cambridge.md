## Notes

> Ostrom's subject is the resource that no single holder can exclude others from without cost — a fishery, a pasture, a groundwater basin, a forest — and her argument is that the people who use such a resource often govern it themselves, successfully, without either private title or state ownership.

She separates four arrangements that the older literature ran together: open access, where no one holds a right and the resource is depleted; private property; state property; and common property, where a bounded community holds the resource under rules it makes and enforces. Her design principles — boundaries, rules matched to local conditions, collective choice, monitoring, graduated sanctions, conflict resolution — describe what makes the fourth work.

For the corpus note this is the corrective to the note's own QA question about the tragedy of the commons: common property is a regime in the decomposition, and open access is the absence of one. Confusing them is the error the note's extensional-fidelity verdict names.
