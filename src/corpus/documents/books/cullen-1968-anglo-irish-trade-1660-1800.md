## Notes

> The quantitative and institutional history of the trade between Ireland and Britain from the Restoration to the Act of Union. Cullen reconstructs the composition and direction of exports and imports — cattle and provisions, then linen, then colonial re-exports — and sets them against the legislative frame: the Cattle Acts, the Navigation Acts, the embargoes, and the partial free trade conceded in 1779–80.

> The book's analytical contribution is to show that the **legislative restrictions did not simply suppress Irish trade**; they redirected it. Ireland specialised in provisions and linen for the British market and in re-exports through British ports, and the relationship became complementary to British needs at the same time as it was subordinate to British policy. That is the economic background against which the union of 1800 was argued.

| Period | Trade regime |
| --- | --- |
| 1660–1688 | Cattle Acts and the exclusion of Irish wool; provisions to the colonies |
| 1689–1714 | War, embargoes, and the restrictions on Irish shipping |
| 1715–1779 | Linen encouraged, other manufacturers restricted; the "colonial" trade through Britain |
| 1779–1800 | Partial free trade, the Volunteers, and the argument over union |
