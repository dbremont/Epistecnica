## Notes

> The textbook that organised the study of ore deposits by **genesis** rather than by geography or commodity. Lindgren's classification — magmatic segregation, pegmatitic, contact-metamorphic, hydrothermal (with its depth and temperature subdivisions), sedimentary, residual, and secondary enrichment — became the frame in which economic geologists described a deposit for most of the twentieth century.

> Its significance is taxonomic in the strict sense: it fixed the **classes** into which a new find is placed, and therefore what questions are asked about it. A deposit classified as hydrothermal is expected to have a particular alteration halo, a particular set of associated minerals and a particular depth range, and the exploration programme follows from the classification before any drilling is planned.

> The book is also the record of the **magmatic-hydrothermal** debate of its time — the argument over how much of an ore body is deposited from cooling magmatic fluids versus circulated meteoric water — and it is useful precisely because it predates the isotope geochemistry that resolved much of it.
