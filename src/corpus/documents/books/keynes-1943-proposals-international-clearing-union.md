## Notes

> The British Treasury's plan for the postwar monetary order, drafted by Keynes and published as a white paper in 1943. Its instrument is the **International Clearing Union**: a multilateral clearing house in which each member holds an account denominated in an international unit, the **bancor**, and settles its balance with the union rather than with each trading partner bilaterally.

> The design's political point is symmetry. A country in deficit is not forced to deflate alone: the union may charge interest on overdrafts, and a country in persistent surplus is also obliged to adjust — its balances are taxed or its exchange rate is raised. Keynes's target was the deflationary bias of the gold standard and of any system in which the burden of adjustment falls only on debtors. The plan was not adopted: the Bretton Woods settlement of 1944 took the American stabilisation-fund model instead, and the clearing union survives as the reference design for later proposals — the [European Payments Union](/view.html?p=documents/papers/eichengreen-1993-payments-mechanism-former-soviet-union.md) and, later, the euro-bancor literature.

| Element | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Unit of account | The bancor, held only by central banks |
| Settlement | Multilateral clearing through the union, not bilateral balances |
| Adjustment | Symmetric: debtors and creditors both obliged to correct |
| Governance | A board with power to charge interest on excessive balances |
| Fate | Rejected at Bretton Woods in favour of the stabilisation-fund model |
