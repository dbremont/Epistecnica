## Notes

> Blackstone gives the classical common-law statement: property is "that sole and despotic dominion which one man claims and exercises over the external things of the world, in total exclusion of the right of any other individual in the universe."

The phrase is quoted as often as it is misread. Blackstone states it in order to qualify it: the dominion is bounded by the law that grants it, and he goes on to describe the estates, tenures and servitudes through which English law had already divided exactly what the sentence seems to concentrate. The absolute owner is a limiting case, not the ordinary one.

For the corpus note the sentence is the best statement of the reading the note rejects — property as a relation between a person and a thing — and Blackstone's own following chapters are the evidence that the common law never actually worked that way.
