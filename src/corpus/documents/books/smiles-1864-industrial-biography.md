## Notes

> Samuel Smiles's Industrial Biography — the iron workers and tool makers told as careers from within the trade: the foundrymen and the forge masters, and the machine-tool founders — Henry Maudslay and his screw-cutting lathe, Joseph Bramah and his lock, James Nasmyth and his planer and hammer — carried from apprentice to employer in their own words and those of the shops that trained them. The volume is the record of the trade's men at the point their papers and drawings were still in private hands, and it stands beside the autobiographies of Nasmyth himself.

> Honest record: Maudslay's own drawings and shop papers are not verified in an archive scan here; the biography is their nearest printed account.
