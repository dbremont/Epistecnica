## Notes

> Edmund Gurney's study of sound — the single large nineteenth-century English treatment of what hearing does to a person: the continuity of sound-sensation and its aesthetic power, music's effect written by a founder of the Society for Psychical Research. More philosophy of hearing than acoustic manual; entered because the discipline's literature-runs include the reader at its end as much as the apparatus at its start.
