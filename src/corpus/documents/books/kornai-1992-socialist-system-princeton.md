## Notes

> The synthesis. Kornai treats the socialist system as a coherent whole — an **affinity** between communist party rule, state ownership, bureaucratic coordination, soft budget constraints and shortage — rather than as a set of separate policy choices. Each element supports the others: the party needs the ownership structure to govern, the ownership structure produces the soft budget, the soft budget produces shortage, and shortage legitimises further central coordination.

> The book is deliberately comparative: it sets the socialist system against the capitalist one on the same analytical dimensions, and it treats the system's *successes* (rapid extensive growth, mobilisation, full employment) as seriously as its failures. Its final part analyses the disintegration of the system, arguing that reform within it was constrained by the affinity — partial market reform, in his account, produces neither plan nor market but a hybrid with the weaknesses of both.

| Part | Content |
| --- | --- |
| The system's anatomy | Power, ownership, coordination, and the socialist firm |
| Shortage and growth | The dynamics inherited from the earlier books |
| The system's limits | Innovation, efficiency, and the reform dilemma |
| Disintegration | Why the system's own reforms could not stabilise it |

> For the corpus this is the reference statement of the **socialist system** as an object of the social ontology, and the natural companion to the Soviet case notes.
