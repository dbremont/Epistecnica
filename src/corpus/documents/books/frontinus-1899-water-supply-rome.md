## Notes

> Frontinus, the water commissioner of Rome, measured his city at the ends of the system: the calyx gauges at the aqueduct intakes, the standard of pipe sections, the apportionment of the supply among public basins, imperial works and private users, and the frauds by which the flow was stolen. The treatise is the one classical administrative document of an engineered water service — the supply stated as a regime of channels, gauges, quotas and legal enforcement.

> The 1899 translation is by Clemens Herschel, the consulting hydraulician behind the Venturi meter — an engineer reading the Roman intake, rationing and measurement as his own trade.
