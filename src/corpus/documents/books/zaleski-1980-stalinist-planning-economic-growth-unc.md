## Notes

> A reconstruction of what the Soviet plans actually said. Zaleski worked through the five-year plans, the annual plans and the unpublished plan documents of the Stalin period to establish the targets, the revisions, and the relationship between the plan and the reported outcome. The result is the reference source for the planning side of Soviet growth: not the ideology of planning, but the numbers the planners wrote down and the frequency with which they changed them.

> The book's central finding is the **plan as a moving target**. Targets were revised repeatedly, annual plans frequently replaced the five-year figures, and reported success was measured against targets that had been adjusted to the outcome. This is why Soviet growth statistics are contested: the plan is both the instrument of allocation and the benchmark of success, and the two roles pull the record in opposite directions.

| Period | What the plans show |
| --- | --- |
| Second Five-Year Plan (1933–37) | Consumption promises abandoned in favour of heavy industry |
| Third Five-Year Plan (1938–41) | Rearmament and the drift into war |
| War and reconstruction (1941–50) | Plan as mobilisation and as recovery accounting |
| Fifth Five-Year Plan (1951–55, planned under Stalin) | The postwar target structure inherited by his successors |
