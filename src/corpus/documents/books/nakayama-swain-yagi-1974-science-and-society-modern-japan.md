## Notes

> A source collection on the social history of modern Japanese science. Two chapters are central to the RIKEN corpus: Itakura and Yagi's "The Japanese Research System and the Establishment of the Institute of Physical and Chemical Research" (pp. 158–201) and Hirosige's "Social Conditions for Prewar Japanese Research in Nuclear Physics" (pp. 202–220). The volume also carries Nakayama's history of universities, Yagi's statistical approach to the history of science, and Kaneseki's account of the elementary-particle theory group.

> The Itakura–Yagi chapter is the English version of the two-part 1957 *Kagakushi Kenkyu* article. Read together with Saito's business-history study of the Riken Konzern, it supplies the institutional argument the RIKEN note needs: the institute was not simply a government laboratory, but a private foundation whose research system, industrial group and wartime role were shaped by the absence of a stable state-funding model.
