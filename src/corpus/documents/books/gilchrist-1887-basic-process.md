## Notes

> Percy Carlyle Gilchrist — with his cousin Sidney Gilchrist Thomas — turned the basic-lining furnace into steelmaking's second revolution: the 1877 patent papers and the process papers, the phosphorus of the pig iron taken into the basic slag where the acid Bessemer process had to refuse it. The steel industry, which had been confined to haematite ores, opened to the phosphoric deposits; the patent-engineering literature documents both the invention and its contested priority.

> Honest record: no archive.org scan of the Gilchrist process papers verified here; the patent literature lives in institutional collections. The basic open-hearth's process literature of a generation later does circulate.
