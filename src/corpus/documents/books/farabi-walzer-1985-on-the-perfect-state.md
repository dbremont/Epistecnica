## Notes

> Walzer's edition is the standard scholarly instrument for the *Virtuous City*: a **critical Arabic text with a facing English translation and a commentary**, not a popular rendering. That form is the reason to hold the scan — the argument can be checked against the Arabic on the same opening, and Walzer's notes record the Greek and Neoplatonic sources line by line.

> The work's architecture: a **cosmology first**, a politics second. The First Cause emanates the chain of intellects down to the Active Intellect; the human soul ascends by knowing that order; and the **virtuous city is the political analogue of the cosmic hierarchy** — its parts ordered as the spheres are, its health the health of a body. The classes of citizens, the ranks of happiness, and the ruler as the **philosopher–prophet–Imam** who both knows the truth and can present it to those who cannot take it demonstrated: religion appears here as the symbolic presentation of philosophy, which is the move the whole later Islamic political tradition argues with.

> For the corpus the edition is the **primary source underneath the secondary literature**: the [al-Fārābī persona](/view.html?p=notes/social/actor/private/persona/al-farabi.md) and the Orwin paper both rest on this text, and the regime typology — virtuous, the four lesser cities, the democratic city among them — is a technical instrument for classifying polities, not a moral ranking alone.

> **Bibliographic note:** the archive record gives the date as **0950** — the work's composition — not the edition's. This is the Walzer edition of **1985** (Clarendon Press, ISBN 019824505X), reprinted in 1998 by Great Books of the Islamic World / Kazi.
