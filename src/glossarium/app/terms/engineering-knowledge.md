# Engineering Knowledge

> **Engineering Knowledge** is organized knowledge used to analyse, design, construct and optimize technical objects and systems.

> It is a species of [technical knowledge](/glossarium/?t=technical-knowledge), distinguished by its organization: engineering knowledge is codified, calculable and transmissible through formal instruction, and it is the knowledge a design office can apply without the craftsperson who holds the feel of the material.

Vincenti's cases show that engineering knowledge is not applied science: it includes criteria and specifications, theoretical tools, quantitative data, practical considerations and design instrumentalities, none of which is reducible to a physical law. Its characteristic form is the design rule — a statement of what works within a range, derived from theory, experiment and accumulated practice together.

The distinction from technical knowledge is a distinction of organization and scope, not of worth: engineering knowledge is what can be taught in a curriculum and applied in an office, while technical knowledge includes the craft, the tacit and the local that the curriculum cannot carry.
