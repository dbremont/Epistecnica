# Technical Element Life Cycle

> The **Technical Element Life Cycle** is the sequence of developmental states and transitions through which a technical element passes during its existence, from emergence to discontinuation.

> Its phases are **analytical**, not universal or strictly sequential stages: an element may skip commerce, loop back from operation to design, or persist indefinitely in a niche long after its replacement exists.

The phases used here are: conception, invention, research, design, development, production, deployment, operation, maintenance, modification, obsolescence and discontinuation. The states that name the element's position in the sequence — emergence, development, maturation, deployment, persistence, replacement, discontinuation — are defined as separate terms because a state is what a decomposition or an institutional record can observe, while a phase is what an account of the work can narrate.

Two warnings belong with the concept. The cycle is not a law of nature: a craft element can persist for centuries without a design phase in the modern sense, and a software element can be modified continuously without ever re-entering production. And the cycle is not a value judgement: discontinuation is a normal terminal state, not a failure.
