# Antagonistic Interaction

> **Antagonistic interaction** — a relation in which two elements, **X** and **Y**, exert *opposing influences* on a shared variable or outcome. For a state variable *s*, X tends to raise it and Y to lower it (or the reverse); the observed outcome is the resultant, which may settle at an equilibrium, oscillate, or resolve toward one side.

> The term is deliberately **neutral, formal and domain-general**: it names the oppositional structure alone, without the teleology, the metaphysical commitment or the historical charge carried by **dialectic** (see [Dialectic](/glossarium/?t=dialectic)). It describes; it does not prescribe.

## Opposition and its resultants

| Relation | What it asserts | What it leaves open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Antagonistic interaction** | Two influences oppose each other on a shared variable | Whether the opposition is productive, historical or resolvable |
| **Dialectic** | An opposition that drives a movement toward a higher unity (*Aufhebung*) | Whether every opposition does so |
| **Contradiction** | Two propositions cannot both be true | Any relation between forces or variables |
| **Competition** | Two agents pursue the same scarce resource | Opposition on a shared variable that is not a resource |

> The distinctions matter because the three are routinely conflated. A logical contradiction is a relation between propositions; an antagonistic interaction is a relation between influences; a dialectic is a historical claim about what oppositions do. Only the second is what the formal term names.

## Why a neutral name

The formal term exists to avoid three loads that "dialectic" carries:

1. the **Hegelian** commitment that every opposition resolves into a higher unity;
2. the **Marxist** charge of class antagonism as the engine of history;
3. the conflation of **contradiction** (logical) with **opposition** (causal or quantitative).

Antagonistic interaction keeps the opposition and drops the rest. It is the description a control engineer, a population ecologist and a economist can share without agreeing on a philosophy of history.

## Examples

| Domain | X / Y | Opposed influence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physics | two forces | opposing vectors acting on one body |
| Biology | predator / prey | opposing effects on population size |
| Economics | supply / demand | opposing pressure on price |
| Logic | p / ¬p | mutually exclusive truth values |
| Social | reform / reaction | opposing pressure on a policy outcome |
| Control | heating / cooling | opposing action on a controlled temperature |
