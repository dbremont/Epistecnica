# Sociotechnical System

> **Sociotechnical System** is the joint system of technical and social elements whose performance cannot be explained by either side alone: the machinery, the work organization, the roles and the rules are designed, and fail, together.

The concept originates in the Tavistock studies of coal mining, where a change in technology alone degraded a work organization that had been finely tuned, and a change in organization alone could not recover the lost performance. The lesson is that the technical and the social are not two systems that interact; they are one system with two analytically separable aspects.

In the corpus, the technical facet reads such a system through its transformation mechanism, while the social facet reads the same configuration through coordination, authority and meaning.
