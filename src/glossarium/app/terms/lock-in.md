# Lock-in

> **Lock-in** is the state in which an incumbent technical option persists because the cost of switching — in infrastructure, skills, standards and expectations — outweighs the advantage of its alternatives, even when the alternatives are better.

Lock-in is path dependence become a constraint: the system is not stuck because nobody knows better, but because the whole configuration has been built around the incumbent. Its characteristic signs are a dominant design, a trained workforce, a standards stack and a supply chain that all assume the same choice.

Breaking lock-in is a transition problem, not a design problem: it requires aligning niches, regimes and landscape pressures, not merely producing a superior artifact.
