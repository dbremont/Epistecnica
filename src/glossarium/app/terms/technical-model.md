# Technical Model

> A **Technical Model** is a simplified representation of a technical element or phenomenon, used to explain, predict or investigate its behaviour.

> A model is a representation with a purpose and a domain: it is valid inside the conditions it was built for, and its predictions outside them are extrapolations, not results.

The technical reading of the general [model](/glossarium/?t=model) is distinguished by its use: a technical model exists to answer a design or operation question — will it hold, how hot will it run, how long will it last, what happens at the limit. Its adequacy is therefore measured by fitness for that question, not by fidelity in general, which is why a coarse model can be better than a precise one.

Models are run as [simulations](/glossarium/?t=technical-simulation), stated as [blueprints](/glossarium/?t=technical-blueprint) or [specifications](/glossarium/?t=technical-specification) when prescriptive, and checked by [testing](/glossarium/?t=technical-testing) against the element they represent.
