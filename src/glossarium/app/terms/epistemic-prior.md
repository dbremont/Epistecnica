# Epistemic Prior

> **Epistemic Prior** (aka inductive bias) is a practical modeling assumption — not an affirmation — about the underlying segment of reality, informing artifact selection, solution, and validation. The term is re-used here and is not tied to Bayesian epistemology, though it bridges practical and Bayesian thought.

### Broken down:

- **Assumption, not claim**: A prior guides search; it is not itself asserted as true of the segment.
- **Consequence**: Priors decide which explanations are even considered — Gaussian smoothness, locality, simplicity preferences shape the hypothesis space before evidence arrives.
- **Auditability**: Good practice makes priors explicit so their influence can be tested by varying them.

### In different fields:

- **Machine learning / Statistics**:
Inductive bias and regularization; Bayesian priors as the formalized special case.
- **Epistemica**:
Priors are scaffold-status: they structure inquiry from the start and are revised only deliberately.
