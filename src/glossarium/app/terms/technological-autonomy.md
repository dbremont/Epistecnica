# Technological Autonomy

> **Technological Autonomy** is the thesis that technical development follows a dynamic partially independent of deliberate human direction.

> It is a claim about degree and locus, not a metaphysical assertion: how much of the direction of technical change is chosen, and by whom, is an empirical question the thesis keeps open.

The strong form — technique as an autonomous force with its own ends — is the classical critique of Ellul and Winner. The weak form is more defensible and more useful: the accumulated momentum of technical systems, the internal logic of their problem sequences, and the distribution of control among experts and firms make the direction of development partly self-propelling. The concept is the complement of [technological momentum](/glossarium/?t=technological-momentum) and the rival of [technological determinism](/glossarium/?t=technological-determinism).

See [Autonomous Technology] and [Technical Rationality](/glossarium/?t=technical-rationality).
