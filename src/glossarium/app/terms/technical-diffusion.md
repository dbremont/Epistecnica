# Technical Diffusion

> **Technical Diffusion** is the spread and adoption of technical knowledge, techniques and objects across individuals, organizations and societies.

Its mechanisms include learning-by-doing and learning-by-using, shallow and deep transfers, licensing agreements, FDI spillovers, consulting, formal education, apprenticeships and skill deepening. Its reverse is capability decay: the loss of a technique when the competence that carried it is no longer exercised.

Diffusion is what turns a local invention into a social capability; a technique that does not diffuse remains an event, not a technical fact of the society.


## Technical reading

In the realization chain the term names the spread of an element through a population of potential adopters — the population-level counterpart of [adoption](/glossarium/?t=technical-element-adoption), which is the single decision, and of [absorption](/glossarium/?t=technical-element-absorption), which is the capability that makes use possible. Diffusion can occur without absorption, which is how a technology spreads while the capability to develop it does not.
