# Homo Faber

> **Homo faber** is the human read as maker: the being whose relation to the world is constituted in part by making, using and maintaining technical objects.

It is a philosophical figure rather than a technical-element type. It names the anthropological reading of the technical facet: technique is not something the human does on the side, but one of the ways the human is constituted — the hand, the tool and the made world as a condition of experience.

The figure has a history: Bergson's *homo faber* against *homo sapiens*, Simondon's technical object as a mode of existence, and the corpus's own reading of technicity as an intrinsic technical dimension of life.
