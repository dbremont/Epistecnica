# Social Construction of Technology

> **Social Construction of Technology** (SCOT) is the thesis that the content of a technology is shaped by relevant social groups and their interpretations: what an artifact *is* is settled through the meanings and conflicts of the groups that use and design it.

The SCOT programme follows three moves: interpretative flexibility (the same artifact supports different readings), closure (a reading stabilizes and the artifact's identity is fixed), and the wider context (the stabilized reading is explained by the groups' interests and power). Its central case is the bicycle: the ordinary and the safety bicycle were not successive approximations to one truth but different answers to different users.

SCOT is the empirical companion of technical code and the rival of technological determinism: it shows the social inside the technical, not merely around it.
