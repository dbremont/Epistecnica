# Technological Determinism

> **Technological Determinism** is the thesis that technology drives social change independently of social choice: that a technique, once available, imposes the social arrangements that fit it.

The thesis comes in strong and weak forms. The strong form — technology as an autonomous force — is rarely defended today; the weak form — technology as a constraint that narrows the space of viable arrangements — is widely used and hard to avoid. Its critics point out that the same technology is adopted differently in different societies, and that the direction of causation runs both ways.

Determinism is the position against which the social-construction reading defines itself; the technical facet needs both, because neither the constraint nor the contingency can be explained away.
