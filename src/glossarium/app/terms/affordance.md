# Affordance

> **Affordance** is the possibility for action that an object or environment offers an agent — a relation between the object's properties and the agent's capacities, not a property of either alone.

> A step affords climbing to a walker of the right size; the same step is an obstacle to a crawler and a seat to a child. The affordance is in the relation, which is why it cannot be read off the artifact's geometry.

The concept entered design through Gibson's ecological psychology and Norman's design writing, with a shift of emphasis between them: Gibson's affordances are properties of the environment taken relative to an organism, Norman's are the perceived possibilities that make an interface usable. The corpus uses the relational reading, because it is the one that survives transfer between agents — the same artifact affords different actions to a novice, an expert, a maintainer and a machine.
