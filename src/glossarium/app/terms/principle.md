# Principle

> A **principle** is a fundamental truth, law, or assumption that serves as a foundation for a **system of beliefs, behaviors, or reasoning**.

> Note: The word first principles is idiotic; because principles already means first.

Key Question:

- What evidence support this `principle`?
- What is the nature of  `'principles'`? Principles are **fundamental statements about how reality works**. The aspects they capture may concern **structure, state, dynamics, constraints, or regularities** within a domain. They illuminate the **space of possible configurations** of reality, clarifying both its **scope and its limitations**.
    - Note: The term **principle** can also be used in **agentic domains**—such as problem solving—to denote **a normative guideline that directs action or decision-making**.

### Key aspects of a principle:

- **Fundamental:** It underlies more complex ideas or systems.
- **General:** Applies broadly across situations or fields.
- **Guiding:** Helps explain, predict, or regulate phenomena or actions.
- **Often foundational:** Used to build theories, methods, or practices.

## Kinds of Principle

> Classify a principle primarily by the **domain in which its validity conditions are established**, not by every domain in which it can be applied. A principle's domain of validity is not its domain of application: a principle grounded in reality can be applied in inquiry or practice without thereby becoming epistemic or technical.

| Kind | Domain | Fundamental concern | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ontic Principle** | Reality | What exists, how it behaves, and what constraints or relations characterize it | Conservation of energy; least action; thermodynamic principles |
| **Epistemic Principle** | Knowledge and inquiry | How claims are established, knowledge is produced, and representations are evaluated | Falsifiability; evidential adequacy; reproducibility |
| **Technical Principle** | Technical activity | How agents produce, maintain, control, and transform things | Feedback control; modularity; redundancy in safety-critical systems |
| **Normative Principle** | Action and institutions | What ought to be done, permitted, required, or valued | Equal cases treated equally; non-refoulement |

> A principle may also be classified along three further axes: **functional character** (descriptive, explanatory, constitutive, regulative, prescriptive, evaluative); **scope** (universal, domain-general, domain-specific, context-dependent); and **justificatory status** (empirically established, theoretically derived, logically necessary, methodologically adopted, normatively justified, heuristically useful). A single principle can carry one primary domain, several functional characters, a particular scope, and multiple grounds.

### Principle versus its neighbours

| Concept | Defining characteristic |
| --- | --- |
| **Law** | A proposition expressing a purportedly general relation or regularity. |
| **Rule** | A specification governing permissible actions, transitions, or operations. |
| **Method** | An organized procedure for accomplishing an epistemic or practical objective. |
| **Criterion** | A condition or standard used to evaluate or classify something. |
| **Axiom** | A proposition adopted as a starting point within a formal or theoretical system. |
| **Heuristic** | A strategy that guides problem-solving without guaranteeing success. |

## Origin of *Principle*

- Comes from **Latin** *principium*, meaning **“beginning,” “origin,” or “first part.”**
- *Principium* derives from *princeps* (genitive *principis*), meaning **“first,” “chief,” or “leader.”**
    - *Princeps* itself comes from *prim-* (meaning “first”) + *capere* (meaning “to take” or “to seize”).
- Entered **Old French** as *principe*, retaining the meaning of a fundamental rule or starting point.
- Adopted into **Middle English** as *principle*, carrying the meaning of a fundamental truth or rule.
