# Deskilling

> **Deskilling** is the separation of conception from execution that degrades craft competence: the work is decomposed into tasks that can be prescribed, and the knowledge of the whole is transferred to management or to the machine.

Deskilling is not simply mechanization; it is a reorganization of the labor process in which the worker's judgment is removed and reattached to a planning office. Its classic study is Braverman's analysis of Taylorism and the machine tool; its contemporary form is the automation of judgment itself.

The concept matters for the technical facet because it shows that a technique can be decomposed in more than one way: the same operation can be organized as a craft or as a sequence of prescribed steps, with different distributions of competence and power.
