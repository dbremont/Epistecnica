# Technē

> **Technē** is knowledge and capacity associated with making and producing — the reasoned disposition that guides a maker's work, as distinct from the theoretical knowledge of what is and the practical wisdom of how to act.

> It is not mere routine. Technē is the kind of knowing that can give an account of why the making is done this way, which is why it can be taught and why it can fail intelligibly.

In the Aristotelian scheme, technē is the intellectual virtue of the productive domain, as *epistēmē* is of the theoretical and *phronēsis* of the practical. The distinction is not a hierarchy: a technique without technē is procedure, and technē without technique is talk. The corpus uses the term to name the knowledge dimension of [technique](/glossarium/?t=technique), the one that makes a technique transmissible rather than merely repeatable.
