# Technical Element Realization Chain

> The **Technical Element Realization Chain** is the sequence of transformations through which a technical possibility is carried into a working element: conception, invention, research, design, development, production and deployment.

> The chain is analytic, not chronological dogma. Its steps can overlap, iterate and repeat — a field failure sends an element back from operation to design — but each step answers a different question, and collapsing two of them hides the work that the other does.

The chain is the forward half of the [life cycle](/glossarium/?t=technical-element-life-cycle); the life cycle adds operation, maintenance, modification, obsolescence and discontinuation. Its *transactions* — development, transfer, commercialization, industrialization, diffusion, adoption, integration and absorption — are what moves an element between positions in the chain, and they are defined separately because each has its own failure mode.

Read the chain as a realization claim: a principle that has not been designed is not yet an element, and an element that has not been deployed is not yet in operation. The chain says what must have happened for the claim *this exists as a technical element* to be true.
