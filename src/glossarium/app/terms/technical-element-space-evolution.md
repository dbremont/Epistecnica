# Technical Element Space Evolution

> **Technical Element Space Evolution** is the change over time of the set of technical elements available in a domain — through emergence, combination, displacement and obsolescence — and of the relations that make that set a space rather than a list.

> One element's history is a trajectory; the space's history is the changing landscape of what can be conceived, combined and selected. The two are not the same object of study, and confusing them makes a population question look like a biography.

The [technical element space](/glossarium/?t=technical-element-space) is the landscape a practice selects from: what exists, what is compatible, what is reachable by combination. Its evolution is driven by the same events that drive element trajectories — inventions, transfers, failures — but its unit is the **variety** of the space: which kinds are present, how densely they interconnect, which regions empty out when an element is displaced.

The concept is the technical-facet counterpart of an evolutionary account of technology: variation (new configurations), selection (adoption and failure) and retention (standards, infrastructure, skills) act on a population of elements, not on a single one.
