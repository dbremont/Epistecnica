## Of the Origin of Ideas

> **Impressions:** Lively, vivid perceptions (e.g., sensations, emotions) from direct experience.
> 

> **Ideas:** Faint copies of impressions, formed in thought or memory.
> 

> **Principle of Copying:** All **ideas derive from prior impressions** ("No idea without an antecedent impression").
> 

**Association of Ideas:** Ideas connect via three natural principles:

- **Resemblance** (e.g., a portrait reminds you of a person).
- **Contiguity** (e.g., thinking of a school leads to memories of classmates).
- **Cause/Effect** (e.g., seeing lightning makes you expect thunder).

**Simple vs. Complex Ideas**

- **Simple ideas**: Basic units (e.g., "red," "sweet") copied directly from impressions.
- **Complex ideas**: Combinations of simple ideas (e.g., a "golden mountain" = gold + mountain).

**Critique of Abstract Ideas**

- Hume follows Berkeley in rejecting innate or abstract ideas (e.g., "justice" is just a collection of related impressions).
