## Introducing Organizations as Social Systems Conducting Experiments

> In this book we model organizations as **`Organizations as Social Systems Conducting Experiments`** with their survival.
> 

> In this chapter we expand on this idea; we elaborate on principles for the design of organizations, enabling them to survive; to continue **conducting experiments**.
> 

> **Aristotle** seeks to penetrate the essence of phenomena, examining their fundamental characteristics—elements that can only be negated if the entire phenomenon itself is negated.
> 

### Observations

→ **Strategy Formulation By A Group of Managers**: The director of the enterprise discusses a vision for the expansion of the enterprise in foreign markets, a **social activity** in which departments/heads of departments **communicate** and **reason together** to create new goals for the `org`; a critical task because it affects the survival probability of the `org.`

→ **A Worker in Trouble**: A worker with work accumulated (several circumstances, machine failures, personal ill, etc.); the boss is pressing and has to communicate to realize its goals. Usually, **disturbances** and **regulatory actions** are made to solve these issues. There are two ways: top-down (thinking about how things can go wrong and taking action to fix it) and giving the worker the tools to resolve the disturbances (autoregulation) …

### Organisations Archai

→ **Experimental Character**:  Make decisions regarding objectives, implement innovative regulatory actions to overcome disruptions, and achieve these goals,

→ **Social Systemic Character**: Interactions,  Expectations, and Communication by a set of people that define a group (organization) with a standard set of goals;
