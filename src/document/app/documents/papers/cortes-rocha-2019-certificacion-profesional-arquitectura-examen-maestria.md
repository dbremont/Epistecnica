## Notes

> [**Professional certification**] From the second half of the sixteenth century until the founding of the Academia de San Carlos, the guild system governed builders in New Spain. The master's examination was the only procedure that authorised professional practice: it tested the candidate's knowledge and ability, and passing it granted the Carta de Examen, accepted throughout the kingdom. The Ordenanzas de Albañilería set the legal frame and the Cabildo enforced it; a master gained the exclusive rights to keep a workshop, take commissions, and carry out appraisals. Architecture was treated as a mechanical art, with eighteenth-century attempts to have it recognised as a liberal one.

> [**Relevance**] Very high. It reconstructs the certification requirements and the rights acquired by master builders, and situates the guild examination within the wider regulation of architecture in New Spain.
