### II. Of Unproductive Labor

> How does labor impact wealth—or the capacity to satisfy the needs and desires of everyone?
> 
- Labor is the main input for production - but it can be used for other **non ‘production’** related activities. (What does he means by non production here?)
- **Productive labor means** labor productive of wealth.
- **Unproductive Labor** – labor that does not generate a net increase in social production. In other words, it is labor employed without producing more than it consumes. For example:
    - **Productive case:** Labor invested in saving the life of a worker—this preserves human capital that can continue contributing to production.
    - **Unproductive case:** Labor spent preserving obsolete machinery (e.g., an old mill no longer in use), which does not yield a net gain in production.
    - **Note: Social reality is complex**. Applying a strict “productive vs. unproductive” distinction can generate feedback effects that destabilize society, because not all labor outcomes are easily reducible to net production. Some activities may appear unproductive but carry indirect or long-term societal benefits.
- **Productive labor** can produce waste - be render unproductive - if the results are not consume.

### V.  On Circulating and Fixed Capital

> What is the taxonomy of capital? Which are the dimensions of that taxonomy? Which are the possibles taxonomy, that is taxonomy space? Which are the dimesions set to specify such taxonomy?
> 

- **Capital** that performs the entirety of its productive function within a single act of use is termed ***circulating capital***.
- **Capital** whose productive function is not exhausted in a single use, but persists through repeated production processes, is termed *fixed capital*.
