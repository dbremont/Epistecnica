> **Relativistic Brain Theory (RBT)** is a new conceptual and physiological model that tries to explain (ground) how huge networks of interconected brain cells - known as neuron - are capable of both generating complex brain functions, ranging from mundane pain, to the sense of self and even consciousness, and, in striking contrast, diverge into pathological mental states, as the result of multitude of neurological or phychiatric disorders that can devastate one’s existence by altering the way our brains build perception of ourselves and the world around us.
> 

> In this works (appendix 1) there is a detail set of experiments in order to test the theory.
> 

> This work challenges a competing theory—the computationalist hypothesis—which posits that the brain functions like a digital computer.
> 

> The book propose that the **central nervous system** generate, combine and store information about itself, the body and the external world through the recurrent dynamic interplay of a hybrid **digital-analog** **computational engine**.
> 

> This computational engine works as follows: the electrical firing produced by widely distributed networks of neurons flows thought a large variety oi ‘biological coils’, formed by  the nerve bundles of the brain’s white matter core, continously generating variable and complex electromagnetic fields (NEMFs).
> 

> **RBT** predicts that the manifold created by the continuos interferences of these NEMFs works as a ‘biological analog computer’, the neuronal space-time continuum, from which a “mental space” emerges to underlie most of the brain’s **higher order functions**.
