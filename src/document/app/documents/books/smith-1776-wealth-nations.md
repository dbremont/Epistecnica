> Adam Smith's critique of "mercantilism" is often directed at a constructed category rather than a coherent school of thought. What later became known as *mercantilism* encompassed a diverse collection of writers, administrators, merchants, and policymakers whose views frequently differed on important questions. As a result, it is often more informative to study figures such as Thomas Mun, Josiah Child, Jean-Baptiste Colbert, and Antonio Serra individually rather than treating them as members of a unified doctrine. One recurring concern among many of these thinkers was the **development of productive capacity, manufacturing, technical capabilities, and state-supported economic development**, but beyond this there was considerable variation.
> 
> 
> A similar issue appears in the work of Karl Marx. Like Smith's use of **"mercantilism," Marx's** concept of "capitalism" aggregates a vast range of institutions, historical arrangements, and economic systems under a single label. While Marx emphasized production, technology, and the organization of labor, critics argue that this framework underemphasized the role of distributed coordination mechanisms—especially markets, price systems, entrepreneurship, and decentralized decision-making. In this view, both "mercantilism" and "capitalism" function partly as broad abstractions that can obscure significant differences among the historical systems and actors grouped under those terms.

### Which is the epistemic goal set (Stated or Not) of the Smith  `*The Wealth of Nations`  Project?*

> **Epistemic Goal Set means - the collection of specific desired epistemic outcomes that guide inquiry, observation, modeling, validation, and reasoning within a given epistemic process. Goals define the target knowledge state that an agent seeks to reach regarding a reality section?**
> 

> This goal set is, quite frankly, excellent. The problem is not with the goals themselves, but with the incompleteness of Smith's attempt to fulfill them. In some respects, that incompleteness can be misleading and potentially dangerous.
> 

> Smith's project seeks to explain the causes of wealth, but the **DCESA** of *The Wealth of Nations* is disproportionately composed of artifacts describing allocation and exchange, while many of the artifacts needed to represent the generation and evolution of productive capacity are absent.

### Is the goal set achieve?

| Epistemic Goal | Evaluation |
| --- | --- |
| Explain the nature and causes of the wealth of nations | **Partially Achieved** — Smith identifies important causes of wealth, particularly specialization, exchange, and capital accumulation, but provides an incomplete account of technology, innovation, productive capabilities, and industrial transformation. |
| Account for the revenue of the people | **Substantially Achieved** — Smith develops a systematic treatment of wages, profits, and rents, though later economics would refine and revise many aspects. |
| Show why some nations are wealthier than others | **Partially Achieved** — Smith identifies several relevant factors but lacks a robust theory of technological leadership, capability accumulation, and structural transformation. |
| Provide a systematic explanation of market outcomes | **Substantially Achieved** — One of the book's strongest accomplishments. Smith develops enduring explanations for prices, competition, specialization, and exchange. |
