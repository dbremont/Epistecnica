## Notes

> Originally published in two volumes (1900–1901), *Logical Investigations* (*Logische Untersuchungen*) is one of the foundational works of 20th-century philosophy and the text that inaugurates **phenomenology** as a rigorous philosophical method.
> 

> Phenomenology take a ratoinalist premise of attributing identity to the self
> 

> Phenomenology arises as the rationalist attempt to recover the self’s foundation — but it collides with the self’s mutability. **Phenomenology** as a restauration of pure logic.
> 

> The `self` thought history has been described as ‘permanent - static’ - a huge mistake.
> 

> Which objects are permanent - “abstract mental objects” -  universals (categories) - concepts - etc.

## Guide

> How to study this book?
> 

> Which research problems? Which technical terminology? Which underlying meta-theory? Which underlying methods? How to validate Husserl results?
> 

**Research Problems:**

- **(Psychologism)** Can logic be reduced to psychology, or does it possess an objective, normative validity independent of human thought?
- **Intentionality** How do thoughts refer to objects, and what is the structure of consciousness that allows for this “aboutness”?
- **Essence of Logic** What is the essence of logic as a normative science, and how can its laws of validity be rigorously grounded?
- **Self as Subject** How can the self be given as a stable subject of knowledge, capable of providing a coherent standpoint for experience?
- **Universals** How do universal concepts—such as species, numbers, or essences—exist, and how can they be apprehended in consciousness?
- **Justification and Evidence** What justifies knowledge, and what makes it *evident*, rather than merely believed or assumed?
- **Temporal Constitution** How does consciousness constitute the flow of time, integrating past, present, and future in experience?

**Methods:**

- **Phenomenological reduction (Epoché)**: Suspend assumptions about the world to focus on consciousness itself.
- **Eidetic analysis**: Identify **essential structures** of experiences or concepts.
- **Intentional analysis**: Examine how mental acts are directed toward objects.

### **Part Two: The Idea of a Pure Logic**

**Chapter I.** Logic as a theoretical discipline

**Chapter II.** The idea of pure logic as a formal theory of science

**Chapter III.** Pure logic as the foundation of all sciences

**Chapter IV.** The systematic unity of pure logic

### **Part Three: The Fundamental Problems of Pure Logic**

**Chapter I.** Pure grammar and the logical forms of meaning

**Chapter II.** The pure theory of manifolds

**Chapter III.** The pure theory of inference and theory of knowledge

**Chapter IV.** The idea of a formal ontology

### **Conclusion**

- Logic as a normative and theoretical science
- The autonomy of pure logic
- The delimitation of pure logic from psychology and from theory of knowledge
