> The early atomists — mainly **Leucippus** and **Democritus** (5th century BCE), and later **Epicurus** and **Lucretius** — indeed created a **conceptual system** to explain all of reality in terms of **atoms** (indivisible particles) and **void** (empty space). They introduced this idea not from observation but as an **abstract explanatory principle** — an attempt to reduce the complexity of the world to a few fundamental, intelligible elements.
> 

> They then used that model to **explain phenomena** such as change, motion, perception, and even the soul, without invoking divine or teleological causes. For instance, they explained perception as the interaction of atomic “films” (eidola) with our sense organs, and change as the rearrangement of atoms.
> 

> They didn’t have empirical methods in the modern sense — their reasoning was **philosophical and speculative**, driven by logical consistency and the desire for a **naturalistic worldview**. So, while they *applied* the atomic theory to phenomena, it was at the level of **conceptual modeling**, not experimentation.
> 

Many forms of **irrational or pre-scientific thinking** indeed arise from **anthropomorphism** — the projection of human qualities (agency, purpose, intention, emotion) onto non-human entities or processes. Examples include:

- Believing that natural events (storms, diseases, earthquakes) happen *for a reason* or *to teach a lesson*.
- Attributing *intent* to random or mechanical processes (“the universe wanted this to happen”).
- Treating abstractions as moral or personal agents (“justice will punish them,” “nature is angry”).

Cognitive science explains this as a **byproduct of evolved social cognition** — our brains are specialized to detect agents, intentions, and social patterns, even when none exist. This “hyperactive agency detection” helped our ancestors survive but can misfire in abstract reasoning.

> Much irrational thinking stems from our tendency to anthropomorphize — to project human agency, purpose, and intention onto the impersonal or random — though other cognitive biases also play major roles.

Ontology:

| **Dimension** | **Category** | **Description** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Type of Ontology** | **Pluralism** | Reality is composed of *many* fundamental entities (atoms) rather than a single one (monism) or two (dualism). |
| **Metaphysical Stance** | **Materialism** | Only matter (atoms) truly exists; everything else (mind, soul, qualities) emerges from material interactions. |
| **Metaphysical Principle** | **Mechanicism** | All phenomena arise from the motion and interaction of atoms according to necessity, not divine purpose or teleology. |
| **Epistemological Approach** | **Rationalist + Empiricist tendencies** | Knowledge of atoms comes through reasoning from sensory appearances. The senses show the effects; reason infers the invisible causes. |
| **Causal Model** | **Efficient Causation** | Events are explained by physical interactions, not by purposes or final causes. |
| **Continuity of Being** | **Discrete Ontology** | Reality is composed of indivisible units (atoms) separated by void (empty space). |

| **Aspect** | **Atomist Position** |
| --- | --- |
| **Substance** | Atoms (indivisible, eternal, ungenerated, indestructible). |
| **Void** | Necessary for motion; the “space” between atoms. |
| **Change** | Explained as rearrangement of atoms. |
| **Qualities** | Secondary; arise from atomic configurations and motion. |
| **Being vs. Non-being** | Both exist: being = atoms, non-being = void (contra Parmenides) |
