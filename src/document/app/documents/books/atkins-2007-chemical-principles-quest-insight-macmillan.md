## Fundamentals

> Chemistry is the science of matter and the changes it can undergo.
> 

> Chemistry and Society:  Civilization is the art of transforming materials; and chemistry is the guide or field of research.
> 

Levels of Description of Chemical Systems

> A chemist think in the microscopy level;  conduct experiments at the macroscopy level and represent them both at the sybolic level.
> 
- **Macroscopy**:  Properties of large visible objects and systems.
- **Microscopy**:  How can we understand the properties of large objects in terms of lower level of reality (atomic and molecular)?
- **Symbolic**: How can we use symbols to represent chemical systems and phenomena?

How Science is Done

- Systems under investigations; experiments; data collection; modelling (qualitative and quantitative);  validation; hypothesis; phenomena; patterns,  laws.

The Branches of Chemistry

> Why is there a special branch of physics only for compounds of carbon? The reason **carbon compounds** merit a **special branch of science**—known as **organic chemistry** (and closely connected to **organic physics**, **physical organic chemistry**, and even **carbon-based materials science**)—is due to the **unique and exceptionally rich bonding behavior of carbon**, which enables an **extraordinary diversity of stable compounds and complex systems**.
> 
- Organic chemistry:  study the compounds of carbon,
- Inorganic chemistry: the study of all the others elements and their compounds; and
- Physical chemistry: the study of the principles of chemistry.
- biochemistry: the study of chemical compounds, reactions,  and other processes in living systems.
- analytical chemistry: the study of techniques for identifying substances and measuring their amounts.
- theoretical chemistry: the study of molecular structure and properties in terms of mathematical models,
- computational chemistry: the computation of molecular properties;
- chemical engineering: the study and design of industrial chemical processes, including the fabrication of manufacturing plants and their operations;
- medical chemistry:  the application of chemical principles to the development of pharmaceuticals; and’
- biological chemistry: the application of chemical principles to biological structure and processes;

Physical Properties:

- A **physical property** is any characteristic that we can observe or measure that does not change the identity of the substance (ej melting point) ???.
- A chemical property is the ability of one substance to change into another.
- Measurement System (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units).
- **Precision**: of a measurement is reflected in the number of significant figures justify by the procedure and depends on how close repeated measurement are to one another.
- **Accuracy**: A series of measurements is to closeness of their average value to the truth value.
- A **systematic error**: Is an error present in every one of a series of repeated measurements.
