### What is the ontological nature of the idea of ‘Will’?

> How does it relate with other deepest principle of reality?
> 

> Whatever underlies all reality at the deepest level — let’s call that Will. A **metaphysically rich total - but empirically empty.**
> 

> Schopenhauer’s “Will” is not an empirical hypothesis; it’s a **metaphysical postulate**.
> 

All the **laws of nature** and **causal relations** are **manifestations of the Will’s self-objectification** at different levels.

For example:

- Physical forces = Will objectified in the lowest grade (gravity, magnetism, electricity).
- Biological life = Higher grade of the same Will manifesting as instinct and striving.
- Human consciousness = The Will’s most reflective form, where it can become aware of itself.

Aspects:

- **Ontologically:** The Will is *the noumenal substrate of existence*, the ultimate reality beneath all appearances.
- **Epistemologically:** It is known directly only through inner experience (our own willing).
- **Cosmologically:** It manifests as all forces, processes, and beings in the phenomenal world.
- **Metaphysically:** It replaces the idea of God or rational order with a blind, irrational, striving force.

Relation:

| Principle | Schopenhauer’s Relation to It |
| --- | --- |
| **Reason / Intellect** | A secondary tool of the Will; evolved to serve its blind striving. |
| **Matter** | The phenomenal manifestation of Will — its “visibility.” |
| **Form (Space, Time, Causality)** | Conditions of representation, not of the Will itself; they structure appearance, not essence. |
| **Being / Becoming** | The Will is *being without becoming* (it does not change); becoming occurs in its manifestations. |
| **Unity / Multiplicity** | The Will is one; multiplicity belongs to its phenomenal appearances. |

Comparison:

| System | Deepest Principle | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Heraclitus** | Logos | Dynamic order through conflict |
| **Parmenides** | Being | Eternal, unchanging One |
| **Spinoza** | Substance (Deus sive Natura) | Rational unity of attributes |
| **Kant** | Thing-in-itself | Unknown, beyond representation |
| **Schopenhauer** | Will | Non-rational, striving essence underlying all phenomena |

### What does mean when we said “The world is my will”?

### Is Representation part of the Grammar of Representation?

> Representation itself is the object the grammar governs, not the grammar itself — but understanding “representation” conceptually is necessary to describe the grammar.
> 

### Is Representation the most abstract notion that there is?

> If we include representations of forms, structures, and elements in reality, then representation can be considered the most abstract notion, because it subsumes both the objects themselves and our perception or conceptualization of them. Representation is not just a mirror of reality—it is the framework through which any element, form, or pattern can be encoded, understood, or related to other elements.
> 

### How the idea of representation represent itself?

> Representation can reflect upon itself: by analyzing its own structure and mechanisms, we can construct a **meta-representation**—a representation of representation. This is a self-referential process, where the concept both **is** and **describes** the act of representing.
>
