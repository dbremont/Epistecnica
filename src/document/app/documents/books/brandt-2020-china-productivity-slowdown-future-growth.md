In the “**standard growth accounting** framework”,  growth  in output per worker can be allocated into contributions from changes in physical capital, employment, human capital (the education and skills of the labor force), and a residual, total factor productivity.

Assuming a **Cobb-Douglas production function**, output is a function of **capital**, **labor**, and **TFP**,    $Y = A K^{\alpha} (L H)^{1 - \alpha}$

- Y is output,
- $A$ is a constant that represents **TFP**,
- $K$ is capital: Typically refers to the physical or financial capital used in the production process,
- $L$ is labor,
- $H$ is human capital represents  educational attainment and the return to each additional year of education,
- $α$ is a parameter between 0 and 1 that indicates the share of capital in the production function.

Some simplifications:

$\frac{Y}{L}$ = $\frac{A K^{\alpha} (L H)^{1 - \alpha}}{L}$

Taking logarithms:

$\Delta \ln\left(\frac{Y}{L}\right) = \alpha \left[ \Delta \ln\left(\frac{K}{L}\right) \right] + (1 - \alpha) \Delta \ln H + \Delta \ln A$

> **Growth Meta Analysis**: Tian and Yu (2012) conduct a meta-analysis of 150 papers and find that the mean aggregate TFP growth is about 2 percent a year after 1978, accounting for about a fifth of GDP growth.
