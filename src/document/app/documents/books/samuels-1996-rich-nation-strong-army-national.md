## The Ideological Basis of Japanese Technonationalism

> The Japanese have the poorest spirit for cooperation. -  Nippon Oil President Naito referring to failed oil cartels in the 1920s.
> 

> Join Action for the common good apparently had little appeal to the Japanese mind. -  U.S. Strategic Bombing Survey, referring to the failure of Japanese aircraft manufacturers  to collaborate during World War II
> 

> Collaboration is everything - Organizing slogan  for the Japanese research program in very large scale integrated circuits in the 1970s.
> 

> **Note:** This chapter is about how Japan's technological and security ideologies underpin its national strategy from the Tokugawa period to the defeat in Second War II.
> 

Japan Strategy >> Technonationalism:

- **Import-substituting indigenization** – the identification and acquisition of foreign products and manufacturing design processes to stimulate local development.
- **Diffusion** – the distribution of this know-how throughout the economy.
- Nurturing – the development of a capacity to innovate and manufacture domestically.

> **Note:** The postwar consensus was based on these principles of (technonationalism). How to ‘proove this point?’ Look at the elite discourse.
