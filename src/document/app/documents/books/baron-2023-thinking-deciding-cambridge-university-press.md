- **🧭 Possibilities**: The set of available choices, possible paths, or candidate answers to the decision problem.
- **🎯 Goals**: The criteria or metrics used to evaluate the desirability or success of different choices.
- **🧠 Evidence**: Prior information, observations, or signals that indicate how likely a given choice is to help achieve the stated goals.
- **⚖️ Judgment**: The process of evaluating each choice in light of the available evidence and the defined goals.
- **🔎 Inference**: The active search for relevant and reliable evidence, aimed at improving the quality of judgments and ultimately leading to better choices.

1. **Naive Theories**
    - *Origin:* Evolve from cognitive biases, incomplete information, or cultural myths.
    - *Flaw:* Resistant to disconfirmation due to lack of falsifiability.
    - *Resolution:* Require evidence-based interrogation (e.g., experiments, counterexamples).
2. **Understanding**

    - **Functional Analysis:**
        - *Teleological:* Purpose in designed systems (e.g., democracy → fair representation).
        - *Emergent:* Function without intent (e.g., market equilibrium → supply/demand).
    - **Causal Mechanism:** Linking structure to function (e.g., "Voting systems → political accountability").
