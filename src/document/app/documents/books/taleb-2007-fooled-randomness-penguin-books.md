> The provided text outlines the **"Talent versus Luck" (TvL) model**, an agent-based simulation designed to quantify the impact of **randomness** on professional success. While Western societies operate under a **meritocratic paradigm** that rewards personal qualities like intelligence and hard work, the researchers argue that these traits follow a **normal distribution** while wealth follows a **power law**. This discrepancy suggests that **external factors**
 and "right place, right time" dynamics are the primary drivers of 
extreme achievement. Using computer simulations, the authors demonstrate
 that the **most successful individuals** are rarely the most talented, but rather those with **average ability and exceptional luck**. Conversely, highly gifted individuals often fail to reach their potential due to simple **misfortune**. Ultimately, the sources suggest that **funding strategies**
 prioritizing diversity and broad resource distribution are more 
effective than those rewarding past performance, which often confuses **luck with merit**.
