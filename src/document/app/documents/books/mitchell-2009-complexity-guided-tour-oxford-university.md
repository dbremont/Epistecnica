## Background and History

### What is complexity?

> Ideas thus made up of several simple ones put together, I call complex; such as are Beauty, Gratitude, a Man, an Army, the Universe. - John Locke, An Essay Concerning Human Understanding.
> 

> How do entities—such as humans and ants—cooperate to build larger structures that enhance their overall probability of survival? How do cells organize themselves to form complex organs like the eye or the brain? How does intelligence emerge from non-intelligent matter?
> 

> **Complex System:**   A system in which large networks of component with no central control and simple rules   of operation give rise to complex collective behavior, sophisticated information processing,  and adaptation via learning or evolution.
> 

> **Complex System Theory** refers in our **`framework`** to systems consisting of a large network of interconnected components (nodes) without central control, where simple rules of operation lead to complex collective behaviors, advanced information processing, and adaptation through **learning** or **evolution**.
> 

> **Emergent Behavior**: When interactions among a system's parts give rise to complex behaviors that are difficult to predict or derive from the behavior of the individual components.
> 

> **Self-organizing**: Systems in which **`organized behavior`** emerges spontaneously without the need for central control.
> 

> **How can complexity be measured?** As of today, there is no universally satisfactory definition for this question; many definitions and theories exist.
> 

**Case Studies:**

- **Insect Colonies (Individual** Simplicity  → **Collective** Sophistication**)**:  An **`individual ant`** is relatively simple, responding to chemical signals from nearby ants and driven by basic **`survival instincts`**. However, **`when ants work together as a group`**, they form a colony capable of achieving complex tasks far beyond the abilities of any single ant. These **`colonies`** can **build intricate structures** and demonstrate modular organization, accomplishing goals that no single ant could achieve alone.
- The Brain (**`Neurons`** → **`Intelligence`**): The brain is composed of **various types of cells**, but neuroscientists believe that **neurons** are the key ingredient from which thought, perception, and other  **`large scale activities`**  arise.
- The Immune System: (Cell → Human Defense System): A Collection of cells distributed across the body;   without a central coordination mechanism.
- [**Note this is really an out of equilibrium system**]  Economies (Actor - Action →  Self Organization of Economic Processes):  An economy is a system in which a set of actors (e.g., producers, consumers, and intermediaries), through primarily local interactions—such as buying, selling, and bargaining—collectively and often unintentionally give rise to global patterns of production and distribution of goods and services. This emergent behavior reflects the self-organizing nature of economic processes, where no central authority necessarily directs all activity, yet coherent outcomes such as supply chains, pricing, and allocation arise.

**List of Common Denominators of Complex Systems:**

- **`Emergent Collective Behavior`**: A system composed of individual components, each following simple rules, produces complex group dynamics **without centralized control**.
- **`Signaling & Information Processing`**: Components generate, transmit, and interpret signals to coordinate with one another.
- **`Adaptation`**: The system dynamically adjusts its behavior in response to feedback,
optimizing for survival, efficiency, or other key metrics.
