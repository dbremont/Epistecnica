Book Critics:

1. neither “Governing the Markets“ nor any other study proves that industrial policy - beyond market-friendly rules of property rights, net, to economic Growth in East Asia.
2. insofar as sectorial policy might have helped in Northeast Asia is was because of circumstances unique to the region; even in nearby fast-growing Southeast Asia sectorial industrial policy, to the extent that it was tried at all, failed;
3. the Asian financial crisis of 1997-98 showed that the kinds of relationships between state and big businesses fostered by the “development state” are prone to inefficiency and breakdown;
4. Taiwan, South Korea, China, and other countries of the region have been reforming themselves in line with the Anglo-American economic model, discarding the remnant of a “government market” system.
5. therefore, even if governed markets, sectorial-industrial policy, and the de developmental state had some validity in the early p postwar decades, that time is past; and WTO rules are making sure that it stays past. The only viable option for developing countries is some variant of the neoliberal Washinton Consensus Agenda - maximum integration in the world economy plus domestic reform to stabilize integration and make the domestic market more efficient.
