## Thermodynamic Systems

> A **thermodynamic state** is a macroscopic, equilibrium-based specification of a system using a complete set of state variables. It encodes all the information needed to determine the system’s energy, entropy, and response to external constraints—while abstracting away the chaotic microscopic details.
> 

> The state of a system in thermodynamics refers to a macroscopic description characterized by a set of thermodynamic variables. These variables—such as pressure, volume, temperature, and entropy—are not values of individual particles but represent ensemble averages over an enormous number of microscopic degrees of freedom. Consequently, the thermodynamic state is an aggregated abstraction that smooths out molecular-level fluctuations.
> 

> Let’s say our thermodynamic system is a **“system composed of a chemically homogeneous fluid”,**  we can measure the temperature $(t)$, volume $(v)$,  pressure $(p)$. These variables obey the **state equation**  $f (p, V , t) = 0$.
> 

> A **transformation** is defined as a process in which a thermodynamic system undergoes a change of state. During such a transformation, the system may **exchange energy** with its surroundings in the form of heat or work, thereby **affecting its environment**.
