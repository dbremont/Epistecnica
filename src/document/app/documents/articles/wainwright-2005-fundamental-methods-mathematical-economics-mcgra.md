## The Nature of Mathematical Economics

> **Economics uses mathematical models** to **** represent reality, but dismissing them as **'un-realistic'** simply because they are **models** is an **unproductive critique**. A more meaningful **critique** focuses on the presentation **capabilities** of these **models** and their ability to accurately capture the **underlying mechanisms**.
> 

## Economic Models

> **Economic theory** is an **abstraction** of the real world, and it is nearly impossible to describe social systems with complete granularity. Therefore, coarse-grained modeling, whether through verbal or mathematical approaches, remains essential in economics. However, with the advent of massive data and advanced computation, we now have the opportunity to refine and enhance these models.
> 

> **Variable**: an aspect that can change or not about the world.
> 

> **Endogenous Variables**: Elements that are in-corportated in the model.
> 

> **Exogenous Variables**: Variables outside in our model; that interact.
> 

> Parameter: A component of the **modeling framework** or **meta-model** that requires estimation. → Unbounded Variable; Partial Model?
> 

> **Notation**: Exogenous variables are denoted with a subscript of $0$; for instance, $P_0$ represents the exogenously determined price.

## Equilibrium Analysis in Economics

> Like many terms in economics, **`equilibrium`** is polysemic. However, it can broadly be understood as a constellation of interrelated variables that are so finely adjusted to one another that no inherent tendency to change exists within the model they form.
> 

> An **economic system** is in **`equilibrium`** when all forces within it are **balanced**, and no agent has the incentive or ability to change their **`behavior`**, resulting in a **`stable configuration`** of prices, outputs, and allocations.
