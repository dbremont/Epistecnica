## The Imitation Game

Setup:

Three participants are involved:

1. **Interrogator (C)** – a human, in a **separate room;**
2. **Subjects** - 
    1. **Man (A)**
    2. **Woman (B)**

Goal:

> The object of this game is for the **Interrogator (C)** to determine the sex of the participants.
> 

> The object of **B**  is to help the interrogator (C).
> 

Rules:

- Questions: Will **X** please tell me the length of his or her hair? If **X** is A then **X** must answer.
- The answers must be written;  so the voice can’t be use for identification.

## Contrary Views on the Main Question

> A set of arguments or objections against the existence of such “thinking machines”.
> 

### The theological argument

> Thinking is a function of man’s immortal soul.
> 

> …
> 

### The “Heads in the Sand”  Objection

> The **consequences of machine's thinking** would be **too dreadful**. **Let us hope** and believe that they cannot do so.
> 

> **Note:** This is not an objection to the possible existence of thinking machines; rather, it is a concern about the potential power of such machines.
> 

### The Mathematical Objection

> There are a number of results of mathematical logic which can be used to show that there are limitations to the powers of discrete-state machines.
> 

> How the limits of **formal systems in mathematics** are used to reason about the limits of **computation**?
> 

### The Argument from Consciousness

> Objection: Brain needs feeling.
> 

> Response: If a machine can functionally replicate consciousness or related phenomena in its outputs, then it should be considered equivalent.
> 

### Arguments from Various Disabilities

> Arguments of the form: “I grant you that you can make machines do all the things you have mentioned, but you will never be able to make one to do X”.
> 

### Lady Lovelace’s Objection

> The **machine** will only perform tasks it has been programmed for, meaning it will not exhibit any emergent behavior.
> 

> **Turing** did not use 'emergent' here, but the idea that computing the consequences of a given fact is not straightforward; it must be worked out, and surprises may arise.
> 

### Arguments from Continuity in the Nervous System

> The nervous system is not a discrete-machine
> 

> A small error in the information about the size of a nervous impulse impinging on a neuron,  may make a large difference to the size of the  outgoing impulse.
>
