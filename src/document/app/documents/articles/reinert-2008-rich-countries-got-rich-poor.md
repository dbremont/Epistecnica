## Note

### Which epistemic goals are pursued by Erik S. Reinert in *How Rich Countries Got Rich... and Why Poor Countries Stay Poor*?

The book is not merely an economic history. It is an attempt to reconstruct an alternative understanding of economic development that challenges the dominance of neoclassical economics and comparative-advantage explanations.

| Epistemic Goal | Description | Stated Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explain the origins of national wealth | Identify why some nations became rich while others remained poor. | Explicit |
| Recover the historical development tradition | Reconstruct the economic ideas of thinkers from the Renaissance through the 19th century that emphasized production, innovation, and industrialization. | Explicit |
| Critique equilibrium economics | Show the limitations of neoclassical models for understanding development and divergence. | Explicit |
| Explain persistent global inequality | Account for why international income gaps persist despite globalization and trade. | Explicit |
| Identify mechanisms of increasing returns | Explain how technological learning, innovation, and industrial specialization generate cumulative wealth. | Explicit |
| Diagnose development failure | Explain why many poor countries remain trapped in low-productivity activities. | Explicit |
| Reconstruct successful development strategies | Identify common features of historically successful development trajectories. | Explicit |
| Restore historical realism to economics | Replace abstract universal models with historically grounded analysis. | Mostly explicit |
| Reveal asymmetries in international trade | Explain why trade can generate unequal outcomes across countries. | Explicit |
| Develop a production-centered theory of development | Shift attention from exchange and allocation toward production capabilities and technological upgrading. | Implicit but central |

### Are there any relevant epistemic goals needed to build a better understanding of economic reality that are missing from *How Rich Countries Got Rich... and Why Poor Countries Stay Poor*?

Reinert's framework is powerful but not exhaustive.

| Epistemic Goal | Description | Why It Is Relevant? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formal causal modeling | Explicit representation of causal structures and mechanisms. | Reinert often argues historically rather than formally. |
| Quantitative predictive modeling | Construction of forecasting models. | The book explains well but predicts weakly. |
| Institutional microfoundations | Detailed modeling of state, firms, bureaucracies, and incentives. | Important for explaining variation among industrializers. |
| Innovation system modeling | Formal analysis of national innovation systems. | Technology is central but not systematically modeled. |
| Complexity dynamics | Explicit treatment of economies as complex adaptive systems. | Would strengthen cumulative-causation arguments. |
| Firm-level capability theory | Explanation of how productive capabilities emerge inside firms. | Industrialization ultimately occurs through firms. |
| Political coalition analysis | Modeling how development coalitions form and persist. | Many development successes depend on political alignment. |
| Measurement framework | Operational metrics for productive sophistication and capability accumulation. | Necessary for empirical implementation. |
| Economic network analysis | Mapping production and knowledge networks. | Modern industrial systems are networked structures. |
| Computational simulation | Agent-based or system-dynamics representations. | Could test hypotheses about divergence and catch-up. |

The DESA generated by Reinert's project consists of concepts, distinctions, mechanisms, historical narratives, and diagnostic frameworks.

| Artifact Layer | Category | Epistemic Artifact | Description | Role |

| Meta-Theoretical | Distinction | Production vs Exchange Focus | Wealth originates primarily in productive transformation rather than trade itself. | Foundational distinction |
| Conceptual | Concept | Increasing Returns | Activities become more productive through scale and learning. | Core explanatory mechanism |
| Conceptual | Concept | Diminishing Returns | Typical condition of many commodity sectors. | Contrastive mechanism |
| Conceptual | Concept | Productive Structure | Composition of economic activities within a nation. | Analytical object |
| Conceptual | Concept | Industrialization | Movement into higher-productivity sectors. | Central process |
| Conceptual | Concept | Synergy Effects | Interactions among industries create additional value. | Development mechanism |
| Conceptual | Concept | Cumulative Causation | Success reinforces further success. | Dynamic mechanism |
| Diagnostic | Framework | Rich vs Poor Activity Distinction | Some economic activities systematically generate greater learning and productivity growth. | Diagnostic tool |

| Historical | Historical Pattern | Development Canon | Recurring patterns across successful industrializers. | Comparative evidence |
