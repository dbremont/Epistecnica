> Catching Up Dynamics
> 

> This paper proposes an evolutionary view of economic catch-up, considered a long-run process of closing the gap in capabilities by promoting learning and innovation in interaction
with innovation systems (national, sectoral, or regional).
> 

> One central point that emerges from this perspective is the strong complementarity between domestic firms' learning and capabilities and the national, sectoral, and regional systems in which they operate.
> 

If cloning is present early in the catch-up process, and becoming familiar with what firms at the frontier are doing characterizes the first stages of catching up, successful catch-up at later stages may involve doing things differently compared to the leading firms or countries.

- Immigration -> Innovation: You just cannot, by definition, beat the front-runner following them; at some point, you have to do things differently (product, process, productivity, ...)
- This difference results from the fact that exact copying is almost impossible in practice. Knowledge is only partially codifiable and transferable; modifications and adaptations to local conditions are necessary.

> Alexander Gerschenkron - 1962 - first theories of catching-up process.
>
