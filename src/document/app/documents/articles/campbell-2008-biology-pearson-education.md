## The Chemistry of Life

### Chapter 01 - Introduction

> **Evolution** as a key property of living system;  and a **core principle guiding investigation of living systems**.
> 

**Key properties of living systems:**

- **Order**: Living organisms exhibit complex but organized structures—from cells to tissues to systems—maintaining internal coherence.
- **Regulation**: They maintain internal balance (homeostasis) through feedback mechanisms that control temperature, pH, metabolism, etc.
- **Evolutionary adaptation**: Over generations, populations change genetically, becoming better suited to their environments through natural selection.
- **Growth and development**: Living organisms grow in size and undergo programmed changes in form and function over time, directed by genetic information.
- **Response to the environment**: Organisms detect and react to environmental stimuli (e.g. light, heat, chemicals), ensuring survival and interaction with their surroundings.
- **Reproduction**: Living systems can produce new individuals, passing on genetic material to ensure continuity of life.

⚙️ **Guiding  Modelling Principles:**

> These principles are not **properties** of life per se, but rather **conceptual tools** to guide scientific thinking, modeling, and explanation.
> 
1. **Evolution by Natural Selection** Living systems evolve over time; variation, inheritance, and differential survival shape biological form and function.
2. **Structure–Function Relationship** Biological structures are shaped by their functions, and understanding one helps explain the other.
3. **Energy Transformation and Flow** Life depends on capturing, storing, and using energy—through processes like metabolism, respiration, and photosynthesis.
4. **Information Flow and Regulation** Genetic and molecular information is stored, transmitted, and regulated to coordinate life processes (e.g., gene expression, signaling pathways).
5. **Homeostasis and Dynamic Regulation** Systems maintain internal stability through dynamic **feedback mechanisms** that respond to internal and external changes.
6. **Systems and Emergence** Life is organized into systems (cells, organs, ecosystems), where new properties emerge from interactions among parts.
7. **Modularity and Hierarchy** Biological systems are built from nested, reusable modules (e.g., proteins, cells, organs), often organized hierarchically.
8. **Robustness and Adaptability** Living systems can maintain function despite perturbations and adapt over time through redundancy, plasticity, and learning.
9. **Development and Differentiation** Organisms arise from a single cell through regulated growth, differentiation, and pattern formation.
10. **Interaction with Environment** Organisms constantly interact with and adapt to their environments—ecologically, behaviorally, and physiologically.

Levels of Biological Organization:

1. **The Biosphere** All life on Earth and the environments in which life exists—land, water, and atmosphere.
2. **Ecosystems** All living organisms in a specific area plus the non-living components (climate, soil, water) they interact with.
3. **Communities** All the different species (populations) that live and interact in a particular area.
4. **Populations** Groups of individuals of the same species living in the same area, capable of interbreeding.
5. **Organisms** Individual living beings, capable of carrying out all life processes.
6. **Organs and Organ Systems** Organs are body parts made of different tissues with specific functions; organ systems are coordinated groups of organs (e.g., the digestive system).
7. **Tissues** Groups of similar cells working together to perform a specific function (e.g., muscle tissue, nervous tissue).
8. **Cells** The basic units of life; all organisms are made of one or more cells (prokaryotic or eukaryotic).
9. **Organelles** Specialized structures within cells that perform distinct functions (e.g., mitochondria, nucleus).
10. **Molecules** Chemical structures consisting of two or more atoms (e.g., DNA, proteins, water) that make up organelles and other cellular components.

Systems:

- A system is a collection of components that work together that function together.

Taxonomy:

- Usually, it uses the classical Linnaean system, but more modern techniques also exist.
