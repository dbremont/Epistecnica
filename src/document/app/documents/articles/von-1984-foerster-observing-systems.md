### On Self-Organizing Systems and Their Environments

> What is a self organizing system? There is not such as thing as a self-organizing system.
> 

> An **adiabatic process** is one in which **no heat is exchanged** with the surroundings.
> 

> Sometimes "adiabatic" is used metaphorically to mean **slowly changing**, **isolated**, or **without external interference**.
> 

Proof:

- Let’s assume that such a system exists,  assume a finite universe $U$,  which is enclosed in an **adiabatic** shell which separated this finite universe from any “meta-universe’ in which  it may be immersed.
- Assume that in this universe is divided into to parts: one occupied by a self-organizing system $S$, while the other part is occupied by the environment $E$ of $S$ ; so $S \& E = U$.
- If it’s organizing, the entropy must decrease  $\frac{\delta S_i}{\delta t} < 0$, which reads as: **the variation of $S_i$ with respect to time is negative**.  That is, as time progresses, the change in entropy must be negative. Note: This is not a derivative, it’s an **informal notation** used to denote small increments.
