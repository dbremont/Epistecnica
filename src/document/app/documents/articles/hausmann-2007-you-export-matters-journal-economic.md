- What is the technical definition of **knowledge spillover**?
- A Country’s **fundamentals**, namely its endowments of physical and human capital; labor; and natural resources along with the overall quality of its institutions, determine relative cost and the patterns of specialization that go with them.
- While fundamentals play an important role, they do not uniquely determine what a country will produce or export.
- **Specializing** in some products will produce more growth than others.
- The governments can alter this export structure; assuming they target the right **‘market failure’**.
- Does comparative advantage explain Swiss Watch Industry or Chocolate Industry?
- Explanation of specialization: comparative advantage,  increasing return to scale, network effects,  technological spillovers, thick-market externalities, or some combination.
- Some economic activities generates more growth than others.

Model:

- Rank  a set of products by the amount of wealth that they generate.
- Use the idea of ‘cost discovery’.
- Note:  When a firm tries to produce a good in a country for the first time even if the produce really comes with a technology ‘blueprint’  (there is almost no technical knowledge); only knowhow + others factors of the environment affects the process.
- If the pioneering firm succeeds, it establishes the truth that product X can be produced locally and effectively. Other firms can then emulate this success, creating a positive externality for the entire economy. However, if the project fails, the costs are borne entirely by the pioneering firm. This process of ‘price discovery’ is almost always a suboptimal investment—unless the government or industry develops mechanisms to share the burden of failure.
- The number of goods that an economy exports is not only determine by the ‘fundamentals’; but also by the amount of firms involved in ‘cost discovery activities’.
