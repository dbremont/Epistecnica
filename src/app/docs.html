What Epistecnica Is

Epistecnica joins two siblings under one framework: Epistemica, the structure of knowing, and Tecnica, the structure of doing. Together they model the two halves of purposeful human practice — the arts that produce knowledge, and the arts that produce transformation — as living knowledge graphs rather than static text.

The project is the complement of Philosophia Naturalis: where natural philosophy asks what the world is, Epistecnica asks what we do when we come to know it and when we act upon it.

The Theory

Technique and operation

Philosophia Artium Technicarum et Operis — the philosophy of the technical arts and of operation — analyzes purposeful (agentic) operation and its primary driver: technique, the organized method for achieving a desired transformation through purposeful action. Technical practice is the structured process by which an agent transforms, constructs, operates, and maintains material or informational artifacts to bring about desired states in the world.

Its most abstract formulation of operation:

  • Requirements → Technique → Product (good, service).
  • Desired Difference → Controlled Transformation → Realized Difference.

A companion aphorism anchors the dynamics: mechanism is to dynamics what program is to computation — function is what is achieved, mechanism is how, and process is the organized sequence of transformations a mechanism may coordinate.

The epistemic arts

Philosophia Artium Epistemicarum et Operis is its counterpart: the philosophy of epistemic practice. Where technique transforms reality, the epistemic arts transform ignorance into knowledge — the organized practices by which agents observe, model, validate, standardize, and transmit warranted belief. The two are joined: every epistemic practice is itself a technique, and every technique rests on knowledge gained epistemically.

From theory to graphs

Each ontology is the operationalization of one half of this theory: nodes are the elements the theory distinguishes, edges are the typed relationships that make practice intelligible. The full development of both philosophies lives in the notes corpus, most directly in Philosophia Artium Technicarum et Operis and Philosophia Artium Epistemicarum et Operis.

The Ontologies

Epistemica — the structure of knowing

An ontology of epistemic practice: — nodes across categories such as epistemic operators, acts, processes, constraints, blueprints, infrastructure, frameworks, standards, and artifacts, connected by typed relationships — agents, tools, methods, and the constraints under which knowledge is made. Explore it in the graph viewer: filter by category, search nodes, pan and zoom the canvas, and follow edges between practices. Edit it in the editor, which writes back through the sync server.

Tecnica — the structure of doing

An ontology of technical objects: — nodes organized into layered spaces — problem, capability, execution, artifact, governance, and evolution — with rich per-node specifications (functional roles, interfaces, implementations, failure modes, lifecycles). Explore it in the graph viewer and edit it in the editor.

Notes & Glossarium

The written corpus lives in the notes catalog: — searchable markdown notes behind both ontologies, organized by section, with a viewer for any note, self-contained live notes (interactive, hand-authored pages), and pins — star any note in the catalog to keep it at hand; pins persist server-side for everyone. Notes, courses, and documents all accept optional tags: [...] front matter, which surfaces as catalog facets and viewer chips.

The courses catalog holds one directory per course: a readme.md entry plus a set of lecture files. The course view renders the entry with its lectures; each lecture reads on its own page.

The documents catalog holds papers, articles, and books as markdown entries with summary, formulation, and references — the same catalog-plus-viewer shape as the notes.

The personas catalog is a new type of note: each entry carries a type: field (architecture, paper, …) that facets the catalog and chips the viewer.

Universal search lives in the hub header: one box over a pre-deployment snapshot (make search-index) covering both graphs plus all five corpora, grouped by surface and deep-linking into every viewer — including node focus via graph.html?node=<id>.

The glossarium is the lexical corpus: — terms and definitions, browsable alphabetically or by search. Selecting a word on the note pages pops its definition in place — consistencia, telos, technique — with a link to the full entry.

Architecture, for the Curious

One small server fronts everything; the browser never talks to the database. Data lives in CouchDB, is served through the endpoints below, and every page fetches relatively, so each surface also works under its own prefix or standalone server. There are no accounts: reads are open, saves and pins go through the server, and AI assistance in the editors is called browser-direct — model keys never touch the backend.

EndpointPurpose
GET /api/healthAggregate health of both dataset backends
GET /{ds}/api/healthPer-dataset health: CouchDB reachability, doc count
GET /{ds}/api/nodesThe ontology as a flat node array (proxied from CouchDB)
GET /{ds}/api/layoutPrecomputed node positions for the viewer
POST /{ds}/api/graph/saveEditor saves, proxied to the database
GET/POST /note/api/pinsNotes-catalog pins (read / toggle)

{ds} is epistemica or tecnica. When the database is unreachable, the proxies fail loudly (HTTP 502) rather than serving stale snapshots — what you read is the live store.

Design

Every surface shares one visual system: serif display type for titles, monospace for machine voice, a dark/light theme toggle (your choice is remembered), and the ambient constellation behind each page. The corpus pages — notes, courses, documents, personas, and glossarium — are plain markdown and generated indexes; nothing you read here is hand-maintained twice.
